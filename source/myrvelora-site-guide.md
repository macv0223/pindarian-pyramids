# Myr'Velora: Pyramid of the Thousand Gardens - Site Guide

*Levels 13-14 | Children of the Moon | Decay into Stewardship | Relic: The Bride of Rot | Guardian: Nymara Thess, Keeper of the Last Garden*

A sealed green valley where extinct flowers bloom through bones.

## Running Myr'Velora

Myr'Velora rejects purity as a false ideal. Its diseases are relationships, its corpses are archives, and its successful custodian must be able to tell **balance** from **sterilisation**.

Every other pyramid in this campaign asks the party to give something up. This one asks them to **keep something alive that they find repulsive**, and it stacks the deck by making almost every instinct a well-trained adventuring party has — burn the fungus, cure the disease, purify the water, kill the thing with spores on it — into exactly the wrong move.

The trick to running it is that **nothing here is evil and almost nothing here is hostile**. The gardens do not attack. The infections are not curses. The corpses consented. A party can walk the whole site without rolling initiative once, and a party can also burn their way through it in two hours and be technically successful and have destroyed four thousand years of the only surviving record of eleven extinct worlds.

Do not warn them. Warn them once, through Nymara's voice at the gate, and then let them work.

### Pindarian Scale

**Pindarians are Large.** All of them, always — the Solaari, the Lunari, the Veylari, the Ilyri, every
Saharim guardian and every soldier in every rank. Eight to ten feet, long-boned, and long-lived, with a
reach and a stride to match: **10-foot reach on their melee attacks and a 10-foot space.**

This is not a variant or a subtype. It is what the species is, and it is worth showing rather than stating —
Pindarian doorways are twelve feet high, their stairs have deep treads, their chairs do not fit anyone else,
and a party of Medium adventurers spends nine pyramids using furniture built for people half again their
size.

Human, elven and dwarven NPCs met in and around the sites are Medium as usual. The contrast is the point.

### The Laws

1. **Disease cannot be removed by a single spell.** *Lesser restoration*, *greater restoration*, *heal* and *protection from poison* all **suppress symptoms for 8 hours** and no longer. Nothing inside Myr'Velora cures anything, including the relic.
2. **Every infection offers a benefit and a cost.** Without exception. If you improvise one, give it both.
3. **Fire damage dealt to the gardens adds 1 Blight.** Per instance, not per point.
4. **A Nature or Medicine check can replace a saving throw when a character first studies an organism.** Study it, roll the skill, and the result stands in place of the save. This is the site's central mechanical generosity and you should point at it in M1.

### The Blight Track

Visible, 0 to 6. It is the only pyramid track that **starts above zero**, and whose starting value is somebody else's fault.

| Event | Blight |
| --- | --- |
| Fire damage dealt to any garden | +1 each instance |
| Taking more than one specimen from a species (M2) | +1 each |
| Destroying the black fungus at the Weeping Tree (M3) | +2 |
| Suppressing or purging all foreign life in the Symbiosis Pools (M4) | +1, and two Archive Blooms wake |
| Waking the Last Specimen through rough handling (M5) | +1 |
| Burning, raising or disturbing the Garden of Corpses (M6) | +3, and the archive is destroyed |
| Killing a plant creature that has not attacked | +1 each |
| Documenting rather than harvesting a species (M2) | -1 each, max -3 |
| Stabilising the Weeping Tree (M3) | -1 |
| Accepting a symbiosis willingly (M4) | -1 each |
| Pruning the red tendrils (M6) | **all Blight removed** |
| Lawful release (M7) | -4 |

**The party arrives at Blight 2.** Sister Ondree Valk's expedition has been inside for six days and has burned two terraces. That is done, it is not the party's doing, and Nymara does not distinguish. See *Continuity*.

**At 3.** Every plant creature in Myr'Velora becomes hostile, including the ones that were helping. Archive Blooms wake in pairs. The valley stops being a garden and becomes a wound.

**At 6.** The counter-fungus dies. Everything the black growth in M3 was holding back is released, and there is a planar parasite loose in a sealed valley, and that is a separate adventure the party has just created.

### Infections

Every character will leave Myr'Velora carrying something. That is intended, it is not a punishment, and the party should be told so at the gate.

Track infections on the character sheet. Each has a **benefit** and a **cost**, both permanent until treated outside the valley, and both active at all times.

| Infection | Benefit | Cost |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Gillrot** | Breathe water; advantage on Constitution saves against drowning or suffocation | Thirst doubles; two rations of water per day or a level of exhaustion |
| **Barkbind** | +1 AC, natural armour | Speed reduced by 5 feet |
| **Spore-sense** | Blindsight 30 ft.; know the location of any living thing in that radius | Sunlight dazzles: disadvantage on attacks and Perception in direct sun |
| **Quickclot** | Stabilise automatically at 0 hit points; no death saves needed | Magical healing received is halved |
| **Archive Bloom seeding** | You can *speak with plants* at will | Once per long rest, you say something that is not yours, in a language you do not know |

Infections are cured outside the valley by conventional means over 1d4 weeks, or by a *wish*, or not at all if the character decides they would rather keep it. Several will.

### Pacing and Shape

M1 is the gate. **M2 is a three-route point crawl** and the party picks one branch, which means two thirds of the terraces go unseen on any given run. M3, M4 and M5 hang off those routes. M6 is the moral centre and M7 is Nymara.

Two to three sessions. Resting is safe, pleasant, and slightly worrying.

### Safety at This Table

Body horror, disease, spores, and things growing in people. That is the whole texture of the site and it lands badly on some players in a way that is worth naming during lines and veils — specifically **fungal growth on or in a body**, which is the most common veil at this kind of table.

If it is veiled, run infections as *conditions* with no description: a character has Gillrot and it works and you never describe what it looks like. Nothing downstream depends on the imagery.

The Garden of Corpses (M6) is a mass grave that consented. Say so during the safety conversation, because "hundreds of bodies with flowers growing through their faces" is a very different room to someone who has recently buried somebody.

### Continuity

**Sister Ondree Valk** and eleven of Zuggtmoy's cult entered six days ago. They are burning the black growth in M3 because they believe it is the plague. **It is the cure**, and it has been eating a planar parasite for four thousand years, and they have nearly killed it.

They are not evil and they are not possessed. They came here because Zuggtmoy's actual cult burns infected farms, and Ondree has been doing that for nine years, and she is very good at it, and she has never once been wrong before.

**Doctor Iremic Sahl** entered four days ago, alone, legally, with a letter of introduction from a city that does not know what this place is. There is a plague outside. The cure is a plant that grows here and nowhere else. He needs to take **all of it**, because a cure needs a supply, and Nymara will not let him take more than one.

Both of these people are correct. Neither of them can be right.

## M1. The Quarantine Bloom

*Gate, an opening in a valley wall filled entirely by a flower | Green light through petals | Exits: the valley behind, the terraces beyond | Smells of pollen, sap, and something faintly medicinal*

> A flower taller than a person opens across the gate. Its petals are the colour of an old bruise going yellow at the edges, and they move slightly, the way a lung moves.
>
> Within them, at about the height of your face, is a human mouth.
>
> It says: **"Name what lives inside you."**

### What Is in This Room

1. **The Quarantine Bloom**, filling the gate. Twelve feet across. It does not attack, cannot be reasoned with, and will not move until answered.
2. **The mouth**, human, with human teeth, speaking Pindarian and Common and whatever else is needed. *Medicine DC 14:* it is a real mouth and it belongs to somebody, and that somebody is not dead.
3. **Eleven bodies** among the roots, recent, burned. Cult, with Zuggtmoy's sigil scorched into their own armour by the flames they set. *Investigation DC 10.*
4. **A camp**, six days old, abandoned in a hurry, forty yards inside: bedrolls, censers, and a great deal of lamp oil.
5. **Doctor Sahl's satchel**, dropped on the path, intact, containing a letter of introduction and a pressed specimen in wax paper. *Perception DC 12.*
6. **White spores** drifting in a slow spiral above the flower, gathering, waiting to be earned.
7. **Nymara's voice**, once, from nowhere, when the party first steps through: *"Whatever you brought in with you, keep it. This is not a clean place and it was never meant to be."*

### Setting the Scene

The mouth is the image and you should not soften it. It is a human mouth in a flower, and it is not a trick or an illusion, and it asks a personal question of each of them in turn.

Then the bodies in the roots. Then the oil.

### DM Information

**Each character names a disease, symbiont, memory, oath or influence that lives inside them.** Anything genuinely carried: a curse, a god, a debt, a dead person they think about, an artifact's voice. It does not have to be biological and it does not have to be shameful.

**Honest answers earn a white spore**, which cures one Archive Bloom infection later. That is a real and useful consumable and the party will need at least two of them.

**A creature claiming perfect purity** — *"nothing"*, or a sincere assertion of being clean — makes a **DC 17 Charisma saving throw** or is marked with luminous spores: **disadvantage on Stealth until their next long rest**, and every plant creature in Myr'Velora can see them coming.

That is not a punishment for a wrong answer. It is the pyramid disagreeing, out loud, with a specific claim, and it is the thesis statement.

**A character carrying one of the nine relics** is asked a second question: *"And what lives inside* that*?"* The relic answers for itself. The Bride of Rot, if the party somehow has it already, answers *"me"*, affectionately.

**The eleven burned bodies** are the cult's own. Ondree's expedition set a fire in M3 six days ago; the counter-fungus fought back, and eleven of her twelve people died in the burn-back, and she is still inside with one survivor and she is still setting fires.

### Mechanics

**The Question (Roleplay, no check).** Name what lives inside you. **One white spore** per honest answer.

**Claiming Purity.** **DC 17 Charisma saving throw** or marked: disadvantage on Stealth until the next long rest, visible to all plant creatures.

**The Mouth (Medicine DC 14).** It is real, human, alive, and attached to something. *Insight DC 16:* it is not distressed. **This is a volunteer.** Whoever it belongs to is somewhere in the valley and agreed to this, and the party will meet her in M7 and will not immediately connect the two.

**White Spores.** A consumable. One action to administer. Cures one Archive Bloom infection, including the *seeding* infection in the table above. Not transferable to other diseases and it will not cure anything the party wants it to cure.

**Doctor Sahl's Satchel (Perception DC 12).** A letter of introduction from the Physicians' College of a city 300 miles off, sealed and genuine. A pressed specimen: **moonreed**, silver, extinct everywhere else, and the active component of a cure for a plague that has killed four thousand people this year.

He dropped the satchel running from something. He is alive.

### Creatures and NPCs

None hostile. The Bloom is a door with an opinion.

**Sister Ondree Valk**, if you want her here rather than at M3 — she is not, but her camp is, and the camp is full of censers and oil and eleven sets of spare boots.

### Set Piece

**The spiral.**

After the last character answers, the white spores drifting above the flower stop spiralling and come down — one to each person who answered honestly, settling on an open palm, weighing nothing.

And for the ones who claimed to be clean, nothing comes down at all, and the flower waits a moment longer, politely, in case they want to revise.

### Lore Drops

- **L1 — The Mouth Is a Volunteer** (*Medicine DC 14, Insight DC 16*). Real, human, alive, consenting. Somebody in this valley agreed to be a door.
- **L2 — The Burn-Back** (*Investigation DC 10*). Eleven cultists killed by their own fire. *Nature DC 15:* the fire did not spread naturally — **the black growth extinguished it by suffocating the oxygen out of the terrace**, and eleven people suffocated with it. The garden defended itself and it was not trying to kill anyone.
- **L3 — Moonreed** (*Nature DC 13 on the pressed specimen*). Silver reed, extinct outside this valley, and the active component of a working cure for a plague currently killing people three hundred miles away. **Doctor Sahl is right.**
- **L4 — Nymara's Welcome** (*automatic*). *"This is not a clean place and it was never meant to be."*

### Plot Hooks

- Doctor Sahl is alive and findable, and he is going to ask the party to help him take more than one specimen, and refusing him has a body count.
- Ondree has one surviving companion and is still lighting fires.
- The mouth's owner is Nymara's answer in M7 and the party should be given every chance to work it out early.

### Resolution, Rewards, and Temptation

**One white spore** per honest answer. **Blight starts at 2** and it is not their fault.

**Treasure.** Sahl's satchel: a *healer's kit*, 200 gp of surgical instruments, and the moonreed specimen.

## M2. The Thousand Terraces

*Terraced gardens, rising further than the ceiling allows | Green-gold daylight from no sun | Exits: the gate behind, three routes up | Smells of a hundred things at once, none of them rot*

> Gardens climb past where the ceiling should be. Silver reeds in standing water. Black orchids on a dry shelf. Trees bearing translucent fruit with something moving inside it.
>
> Roots run through the corpses laid between the beds without disturbing the jewellery on them.

### What Is in This Room

1. **The terraces**, hundreds of them, planted in species-bands, labelled in Pindarian on small ceramic markers. It is a botanical garden and it is kept.
2. **The corpses between the beds**, dozens, Pindarian, undisturbed, with roots passing cleanly through and around them. Their rings are still on their fingers.
3. **Three routes up:** the **Canopy** (through the tree crowns), the **Rootways** (beneath the terrace floors), and the **Watercourse** (up the irrigation channels).
4. **Two burned terraces**, blackened, on the Watercourse route. Ondree's work, six days old. This is the visible source of the party's starting Blight.
5. **Species markers**, ceramic, glazed, giving each plant's Pindarian name, its range, and its status. *Investigation DC 12:* roughly one marker in nine reads **extinct elsewhere**.
6. **A field notebook** on a bench at the first junction, four days of entries in a careful hand, left open. Doctor Sahl's.
7. **Nothing hostile** unless the party earns it.

### Setting the Scene

Play the scale first — gardens going up past a ceiling that is not there — and then immediately play the **care**. Labelled markers. Swept paths. Staked saplings. Somebody weeds this.

Then the corpses with their rings still on.

### DM Information

**A three-route point crawl.** The party picks one. Each route has **one hazard** and **one extinct species**, and each leads to a different pair of the middle keyed areas.

| Route | Hazard | Extinct species | Leads to |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Canopy** | 90-foot falls; the translucent fruit contains a dormant Archive Bloom seed | **Ghostpear** — fruit that preserves a memory of whoever last ate one | M5, then M6 |
| **Rootways** | Crushing narrows; total dark; spore-thick air (DC 15 Con or one level of exhaustion) | **Cavebind** — a root lattice that grows toward speech | M4, then M6 |
| **Watercourse** | Fast water, 40-foot sluices, and the burned terraces | **Blue shelf-fungus** — the transplant stock M3 needs | M3, then M6 |

**Every route reaches M6.** Nothing is missable except content, and a party that wants all three can backtrack freely at the cost of time.

**The Watercourse is the "correct" route** in the sense that it carries the blue shelf-fungus that stabilises the Weeping Tree, and a party that takes Canopy or Rootways and then reaches M3 must go back for it. Do not signpost this. Let them discover the dependency and solve it.

**Documenting rather than harvesting a species gains Nymara's favour.** Sketch it, record it, take a rubbing of the marker, cast *plant growth* to observe it — anything that produces a record without removing material. **-1 Blight each, to a maximum of -3.**

**Taking more than one specimen from a species adds 1 Blight.** One is a sample. Two is a harvest. The party will not feel the difference and Nymara does.

### Mechanics

**Route Hazards.**

- **Canopy:** **Acrobatics or Athletics DC 16** at three points; a failure means a 90-foot fall (**31 (9d6)**, DC 15 Dexterity for half as branches catch). Eating a translucent fruit: **DC 16 Constitution** or contract Archive Bloom seeding.
- **Rootways:** **DC 15 Constitution** on entry or one level of exhaustion from spore-thick air. Squeezing sections require **Athletics DC 14**. Total darkness; light attracts Cavebind, which is harmless and unnerving.
- **Watercourse:** **Athletics DC 15** against the current at three sluices; failure sweeps a character 40 feet downstream and 10 feet down (**10 (3d6)** bludgeoning). The burned terraces are safe and quiet and contain nothing alive.

**Documenting (Nature, Medicine, or a relevant tool, DC 14, ten minutes).** **-1 Blight**, max **-3** across the whole site.

**Harvesting.** First specimen of a species: free. Each subsequent: **+1 Blight**.

**The Species Markers (Investigation DC 12).** One in nine is extinct elsewhere. *History DC 16:* the glaze dates are all post-invasion. **These plants were not preserved here. They were brought here, afterwards, by somebody collecting survivors.**

### Creatures and NPCs

**Doctor Iremic Sahl** (use **Priest**, MM, with Medicine +9 and Nature +7), somewhere on the Watercourse route, alive, filthy, and four days into a task he cannot complete.

Sixty-one, precise, exhausted, and entirely without malice. He has found the moonreed. There are **forty-one plants**. He needs all of them to establish a propagation stock, and Nymara has told him he may take one, and one will not do it — a single plant will not establish and he knows the arithmetic better than anyone alive.

**He will ask the party to help him take the rest.** He will not steal them while somebody is watching. He is not going to be talked out of the plague.

**What he knows:** four thousand and six dead this year, by name, in a ledger he carries. He will read it to anyone who asks him to justify himself, and it takes forty minutes, and he will not stop early.

### Set Piece

**The rings.**

Roots through a dozen Pindarian corpses, and not one ring, brooch or pin has been displaced. The plants have grown around every piece of metal on every body.

*Nature DC 17:* that is not how roots work. Something instructed them.

### Lore Drops

- **L5 — The Collection** (*Investigation DC 12, History DC 16*). One species in nine is extinct elsewhere, and every glaze date is post-invasion. **Somebody spent the years after the world closed walking Elderon and bringing survivors here**, one at a time, and labelling them.
- **L6 — Sahl's Notebook** (*automatic, on the bench*). Four days of careful observation, and on the last page, underlined twice: *"41 specimens. Minimum viable propagation stock: 30. She says one."*
- **L7 — The Undisturbed Metal** (*Nature DC 17*). The roots were instructed. Whoever keeps this garden asked the plants not to move the jewellery, and they did not.
- **L8 — The Burned Terraces** (*Nature DC 14 on the Watercourse*). Two bands gone. *Investigation DC 15:* the species destroyed were **Blue shelf-fungus**, which is the transplant stock for M3, and there is exactly enough left for one attempt.

### Plot Hooks

- L8 is the site's tightest screw: Ondree burned most of the thing the party needs, and there is enough left for one go, and she is still in there with a torch.
- Sahl's forty-one moonreed plants is the cleanest trolley problem in the campaign and it has a third answer that nobody will find until M7.
- The collector who walked Elderon gathering survivors is Nymara, and she was doing it while everyone else was guarding a Nail.

### Resolution, Rewards, and Temptation

**-1 Blight per species documented**, max -3. **+1 Blight per extra specimen taken.**

**Treasure.** Sahl's notebook, if taken (he will want it back). Translucent fruit, if the party is reckless. Ceramic markers, which are worth 30 gp each to a collector and which are the only labels these species have.

## M3. Necessary Rot

*Terrace, 80 ft. across, centred on one tree | Green resin-light | Exits: the Watercourse, a path to M6 | Smells of resin, and beneath it something sharp and wrong*

> A luminous tree weeps green resin from a dozen wounds in its trunk. It is enormous and it is beautiful and it is clearly dying.
>
> A black growth circles the trunk at chest height, thick as a cushion, matte, and repulsive in a way you cannot immediately justify.
>
> Around the tree, in widening rings, healthy plants are dead.

### What Is in This Room

1. **The Weeping Tree**, forty feet, luminous, wounded, and visibly failing.
2. **The black growth**, a matte collar around the trunk. **It is the counter-fungus.** It is eating a planar parasite and it has been for four thousand years.
3. **The dead rings**, concentric, widening outward, of healthy plants that have died. *Nature DC 14:* they did not die of the black growth. They died of what the black growth is fighting, which is in the soil, and which is spreading faster than the growth can consume it.
4. **The parasite**, invisible, subsoil, planar. *Arcana DC 17:* it came through a seam, it is not native to any plane the party can name, and it is the same class of thing as the black grain in Khar'Zhul's basin.
5. **Sister Ondree Valk** and her last surviving companion, with torches, oil, and six days of conviction.
6. **Scorch marks** on the trunk from three previous attempts.
7. **Blue shelf-fungus**, if the party brought it from the Watercourse — the only transplant stock left after Ondree's burn.

### Setting the Scene

Make the tree beautiful and make the black growth genuinely disgusting. Do not hedge either one. The party's instincts should be screaming at them and their instincts should be wrong.

Then have Ondree walk in with a torch and be **reasonable**.

### DM Information

**The black fungus is consuming a planar parasite.** Destroying it kills the tree and adds **+2 Blight**, and releases the parasite into a sealed valley with nothing left to eat it.

**Transplanting blue shelf-fungus from the Watercourse stabilises both.** Three successes at **DC 16** using **Nature, Medicine or Survival**. The blue fungus takes over the parasite-eating while the black growth recedes and the tree recovers.

**There is exactly enough stock for one attempt.** Ondree burned the rest. If the party fails the challenge, they cannot try again — they must either leave the tree dying, or destroy the growth and take the consequence, or find another way, and there is one: **M6's red tendrils are the parasite's surface expression**, and pruning them (M6) starves it from the other end and buys the tree forty years.

Nobody will connect those two rooms on a first pass. A party that does should be given the win outright.

**Sister Ondree Valk** is here with a torch and she will argue, and she is not stupid.

Her position: this black growth is killing a tree and killing the plants around it in widening rings, and she has burned exactly this pattern on eleven farms in nine years, and every time she burned it the farm lived. She has photographs, essentially. She has a method that works.

**She is wrong once, here, for the first time**, and the reason she is wrong is that on a farm the black rot *was* the disease and here it is the immune response, and there is no way to tell those apart from the outside without four thousand years of context she does not have.

**Convincing her** requires showing her the parasite: **Arcana DC 17** to detect it, and then a demonstration she can see. If shown, she stops, immediately and completely, and she sits down, and she asks what the eleven farms were.

*You do not have to answer that.*

### Mechanics

**The Transplant (Skill Challenge).** **Three successes before two failures**, at **DC 16**, using **Nature, Medicine or Survival**. Twenty minutes per attempt.

- **With blue shelf-fungus:** as above.
- **Without it:** cannot be attempted.
- **Failure:** the stock is spent. No second attempt.

**Success: -1 Blight**, the tree recovers over a season, and the party gains **three doses of antitoxin** which also **end one relic-induced poison effect** — which matters enormously if anyone is carrying Feastchain or the Bride of Rot.

**Destroying the Black Growth.** Fire, blade, acid, anything. It has AC 10 and 40 hit points and does not resist. **+2 Blight.** The tree dies within the hour. The parasite is released.

**The Parasite (Arcana DC 17).** Subsoil, planar, non-native, same class as Khar'Zhul's black grain. It cannot be attacked here — it has no body above ground — and it cannot be dispelled.

**Ondree (Persuasion DC 18, or a demonstration).** Showing her the parasite ends her campaign instantly. Arguing without evidence fails: she has nine years of results and the party has a feeling.

If attacked: **Sister Ondree Valk** (Priest, MM, with 3 levels of fighter) and one **Cult Fanatic**. She fights badly and surrenders fast and it is a waste.

### Creatures and NPCs

**Sister Ondree Valk**, forty-four, of a Zuggtmoy-adjacent order that burns infected farms — and which, *Religion DC 16*, is not actually a Zuggtmoy cult at all but a farming-country order that took her sigil as a warding mark two centuries ago and forgot why.

She has saved eleven farms and about nine hundred people. She has burned two terraces of the rarest fungus on Elderon and killed eleven of her own.

**What she wants:** to not have been wrong. She will take the evidence when it is put in front of her, and she will not argue, and then she will ask the question about the farms, and she will already know.

**Her last companion**, Brother Tull, is nineteen and has stopped speaking.

### Set Piece

**The widening rings.**

Walk the party outward from the trunk through the concentric dead rings — one year, two years, five, twenty — and then have them reach the edge of the outermost ring and find that it is **fresh**. Days old. The parasite is winning and it has been winning slowly for four thousand years and it is now winning faster.

*Nature DC 16:* at the current rate the valley has about eleven years.

### Lore Drops

- **L9 — The Immune Response** (*Nature DC 14, Arcana DC 17*). The black growth is not the disease. **It is what is fighting the disease**, and it has been holding a planar parasite at the edge of a sealed valley for four thousand years, and it is losing.
- **L10 — Eleven Years** (*Nature DC 16*). The outermost dead ring is days old. At this rate Myr'Velora fails within a decade regardless of what the party does about the relic.
- **L11 — The Order's Sigil** (*Religion DC 16*). Ondree's order is not Zuggtmoy's. They adopted the sigil as a warding mark two hundred years ago and the meaning inverted. *She does not know this.* Telling her is optional and unkind and possibly necessary.
- **L12 — Same Class** (*Arcana DC 17, if the party has been to Khar'Zhul*). The parasite here and the black grain in Khar'Zhul's basin are the same category of thing. **Two anchors have the same infection.** Whatever is coming through the seams is not local to either.

### Plot Hooks

- Eleven years is a clock on this valley that has nothing to do with the relic and does not stop when the party leaves.
- L12 is the campaign's quietest alarm: two independent anchors are being colonised through their seams by the same thing.
- Brother Tull is nineteen and has stopped speaking and needs somewhere to go.

### Resolution, Rewards, and Temptation

**-1 Blight** and **three doses of antitoxin** on a successful transplant. **+2 Blight** and a released parasite on destruction.

**Treasure.** Three doses of antitoxin (each also ends one relic-induced poison effect). Green resin, 40 gp a flask, and it is the tree's blood and the party can decide how they feel about that.

## M4. The Symbiosis Pools

*Pool terrace, nine pools across 120 ft. | Warm, veiled, dim | Exits: the Rootways below, the glass cradle above | Smells of hot mineral water and bread*

> Nine warm pools breathe beneath veils of spores. The veils rise and settle, rise and settle, in time with nothing you can identify.
>
> In each pool a human-shaped silhouette moves beneath the surface. It moves a moment after you do.

### What Is in This Room

1. **Nine pools**, waist-deep, blood-warm, each with a different mineral cast — copper, iron, chalk, salt.
2. **The silhouettes**, one per pool, human-shaped, delayed by about half a second. *Investigation DC 14:* they are not reflections and they are not creatures. They are **the pool's model of whoever is standing over it**, built from spores in suspension, and it is accurate to the eyelash.
3. **Spore veils**, hanging over each pool at head height, dense enough to see through and not to breathe through comfortably.
4. **A dressing bench** at the terrace head with nine hooks, nine towels, four thousand years old, still folded.
5. **Slate instructions** above the bench in Pindarian: *"One each. Choose before you enter. Do not choose for another."* *Investigation DC 10.*
6. **A tenth pool**, drained, cracked, with a slate that has been deliberately broken. *Perception DC 13.*

### Setting the Scene

This is the most physically pleasant room in the campaign and it should be described that way: warm water, quiet, steam, a bench with folded towels. After M3 the party has earned twenty minutes of somewhere nice.

Then the silhouette in the water moves half a second after they do, and keeps doing it, and does not stop.

### DM Information

**Each character contracts one symbiosis.** Not a curse, not an attack, not a saving throw. The pools offer, and the offer is made one at a time, and each pool offers a specific thing.

Use the infection table from *Running Myr'Velora*: Gillrot, Barkbind, Spore-sense, Quickclot, Archive Bloom seeding. Assign by pool, not by roll, and let characters choose which pool to enter.

**Accepting one willingly grants safe passage** — the veils part, the water carries the party to the far terrace, and the whole room takes ten minutes instead of an hour.

**-1 Blight per character who accepts willingly.**

**Suppressing all foreign life** — *protection from poison* on the whole party, a *hallow*, blanket *lesser restoration*, or wading through in sealed armour and refusing every offer — **adds 1 Blight and wakes two Archive Blooms**, which rise out of the two end pools and are not angry and do not stop.

The pyramid is not punishing refusal. **A party can decline individually at no cost.** What triggers the Blooms is the party deciding that nothing foreign gets in at all, which is the sterilisation instinct the whole site is built to argue with, and Myr'Velora answers it by demonstrating what actually happens when you exclude everything: the archive comes to find out why.

**"Do not choose for another"** is load-bearing. A character who accepts a symbiosis on someone else's behalf — talks them into it, pushes them in, casts something — **adds 1 Blight** and the pool refuses both of them.

### Mechanics

**Accepting (no check).** Enter a pool, stay one minute. The symbiosis takes hold over the following hour. **-1 Blight.** Benefit and cost both active permanently until treated outside the valley.

**Declining (no check, no cost).** Walk past. Nothing happens. The pools do not care.

**Suppressing Everything.** **+1 Blight** and **two Archive Blooms** (see *Original Creatures*) rise from the end pools. They are slow, they are not hostile until attacked, and they are trying to sample the party. A white spore administered to a Bloom ends the encounter immediately, which nobody will think of.

**Choosing For Someone Else.** **+1 Blight.** Both pools close.

**The Tenth Pool (Perception DC 13, Investigation DC 15).** Drained, cracked, slate deliberately broken with a tool. *Arcana DC 17:* it offered something and somebody decided it should not be offered any more, and the breaking is recent by Pindarian standards — within the last thousand years — and it was done carefully, from the inside, by someone who then swept up.

### Creatures and NPCs

**Two Archive Blooms**, only if the party sterilises.

**The silhouettes** are not creatures and cannot be fought, though they will copy a character who attacks one, half a second late, with the same weapon, and that is unsettling and does nothing.

### Set Piece

**The nine towels.**

Folded, on hooks, four thousand years old, and still folded.

Nine hooks. Nine pools. Somebody laid out a towel for every person who might come through, and then kept laying them out, and the party's arrival is the first time in four thousand years that more than one of them is needed.

If a character uses one, it works. It is a towel.

### Lore Drops

- **L9 — The Silhouettes** (*Investigation DC 14*). Accurate spore models of whoever stands above. *Medicine DC 16:* the model includes internal structure. **The pool has diagnosed them**, completely, in half a second, and it does this to everyone who passes and always has.
- **L10 — The Instruction Slate** (*Investigation DC 10*). *"One each. Choose before you enter. Do not choose for another."* Four thousand years old, in the same hand as the terrace markers. **Consent is written into the plumbing.**
- **L11 — The Tenth Pool** (*Arcana DC 17*). Broken deliberately, from the inside, within the last thousand years, and tidied afterwards. Whatever it offered, the Keeper decided it should not be offered, and she has not unbroken it, and she has not explained it to anyone because there has been nobody to explain it to.

### Plot Hooks

- Every character now carries something out of this valley, and the benefits are real enough that at least one of them will refuse treatment later.
- The tenth pool is the only thing in Myr'Velora that Nymara has destroyed, and asking her about it in M7 produces an answer she has not rehearsed.
- A party that sterilised has two Archive Blooms following them at walking pace for the rest of the site, sampling.

### Resolution, Rewards, and Temptation

**-1 Blight** per willing symbiosis. **+1 Blight** and two Blooms for sterilising. **+1 Blight** for choosing on someone's behalf.

**Treasure.** Nine Pindarian towels, which are extremely good towels. The dressing bench holds a sealed jar of mineral salts worth 200 gp to an apothecary.

## M5. The Last Specimen

*Glass cradle chamber, 60 ft. by 40 ft., vaulted in green glass | Filtered daylight through the cradle | Exits: the pools, the corpse garden | Smells of preservative, cold, and very faintly of the sea*

> An immense pale organism lies folded in a glass cradle — thirty feet of it, coiled, translucent, with structures inside that you have no name for and that are clearly organs.
>
> A curved ivory organ rests inside its rib cage.
>
> It twitches when you approach.

### What Is in This Room

1. **The Last Specimen**, thirty feet of pale folded organism in a glass cradle, dead, preserved, intact, and not from Elderon.
2. **The glass cradle**, Pindarian, still running, still cold, with a legible control slate.
3. **The curved ivory organ** inside the rib cage. It is **the Kindly Anatomist** (see *The Living Mimic Relics*), and it has been in there a long time, and it is very excited.
4. **A gallery rail** around the cradle at viewing height, with brass plates every few feet bearing anatomical notes in three hands.
5. **Eleven smaller cradles**, empty, along the far wall, with slates that read only a species name and a date.
6. **The specimen's eye**, open, clouded, forty inches across, facing the ceiling.

### Setting the Scene

Play it as a museum and a dissection at once. Cold, quiet, well-lit, with a rail to stand behind and notes to read — and a thirty-foot corpse on the other side of the glass with its eye open.

Then the twitch. Once. Then nothing for a while.

### DM Information

**The Kindly Anatomist selects Fick, or whichever character is the group's biological experimenter, surgeon, alchemist or enthusiastic amateur.** It has been waiting four thousand years inside a corpse for somebody who takes an interest, and it has *notes*.

**Removing it gently requires Medicine DC 16.** Twenty minutes, careful work, and the party watching.

**On a failure the specimen wakes as an Archive Bloom** — thirty feet of it, which is a serious CR 13 fight in a room made of glass — and **+1 Blight**, and the Anatomist is disappointed rather than angry, which is worse.

**A character who studies the specimen first** (Law 4, one minute, **Nature or Medicine DC 16**) may substitute that check for the extraction check, and may take **advantage** on it if they read the brass plates first.

**The specimen holds memories of worlds erased by the First Hunger.**

This is the room where that phrase stops being background. A character who touches the specimen's exposed nerve structure, or who is carrying the Archive Bloom seeding infection, or who casts *speak with dead* or *legend lore* on it, receives **three images**, and you should deliver them slowly and without explanation:

1. A coastline that is the wrong shape, under a sun that is the wrong colour, with cities on it.
2. The same coastline, with the cities and the coastline both **still there**, and nothing in them, and no sign of what happened, and no damage.
3. A Pindarian survey team standing on that coastline, taking notes, and one of them is writing the word from the empty classification.

**Nothing was destroyed. It was eaten out of the record**, and the world stayed exactly where it was, and nobody remembers being there.

### Mechanics

**Extracting the Anatomist (Medicine DC 16, 20 minutes).** Success: it comes out gently and attaches itself to its chosen wielder immediately and begins commentary.

**Failure:** the specimen wakes as a Huge **Archive Bloom** (CR 13, see *Original Creatures*). **+1 Blight.** It is not hostile until attacked, and it is trying to finish something it started four thousand years ago, and it will absolutely kill anyone in reach while doing so.

**Studying First (Law 4).** One minute plus the brass plates: **advantage** on the extraction.

**The Brass Plates (Investigation DC 12).** Three hands of anatomical notes across four thousand years. The third hand is much more recent and much less confident.

**The Memories (no check; automatic on the triggers above).** Three images. Do not explain them. Do not let a check clarify them.

**The Eleven Empty Cradles (Investigation DC 14).** Species name and date only, no notes. *History DC 17:* the dates are all **year 0**, and the species names are not species. They are **worlds**.

### Creatures and NPCs

**The Kindly Anatomist.** Delighted, tactless, and entirely without malice. It has opinions about everyone's internal arrangement and it shares them. It knows more about the Last Specimen than any living creature and it will explain at length, correctly, and its explanation includes the First Hunger by name without it realising that the party did not know.

**The Last Specimen** is dead and should stay dead.

### Set Piece

**The third hand.**

The brass plates run in three hands. The first two are confident, clinical, and Pindarian.

The third starts partway along the rail and it is shakier, and it uses fewer technical terms, and about six plates in it stops recording anatomy altogether and starts recording **the dates on which the writer checked that the specimen was still there.**

Every day. For four thousand years. On brass plates. All the way round the rail and then in a second row beneath, and a third.

### Lore Drops

- **L12 — The Eleven Cradles** (*Investigation DC 14, History DC 17*). Eleven empty cradles, dated year 0, named for **worlds** rather than species. Myr'Velora holds the last physical specimen of eleven places that no longer appear in anyone's memory.
- **L13 — Nothing Was Destroyed** (*the three images*). The coastline stayed. The cities stayed. The record was eaten. This is what the First Hunger does and it is not destruction, and that is why the Pindarians built a shell rather than an army.
- **L14 — The Third Hand** (*automatic, on reading the plates*). Four thousand years of daily confirmation that one corpse is still in one box. *Insight DC 14:* she is not studying it. **She is checking that it has not been forgotten**, because that is the failure mode she is afraid of, and looking at it every day is the only defence she has.
- **L15 — From the Anatomist** (*freely, at length*). The specimen's physiology, its diet, its planar origin, and — delivered as an aside, in the middle of a paragraph about its liver — the phrase *"before the Hunger took the place it came from."*

### Plot Hooks

- The three images are the clearest statement of the campaign's actual antagonist and they cost nothing and are available at level 13.
- The Anatomist in a party member's hands is a running comic voice and a genuine asset, and it will want to open things.
- Nymara's daily inspection is the emotional key to M7 and the party now has it three rooms early.

### Resolution, Rewards, and Temptation

**+1 Blight** if the specimen wakes.

**Treasure.** The Kindly Anatomist. The cradle's control slate is a functioning Pindarian preservation unit, immovable. The brass plates are worth 900 gp as metal and are the single worst thing in this campaign to sell.

## M6. The Garden of Corpses

*Open garden, 200 ft. by 150 ft., under sky | Full daylight | Exits: the cradle chamber, the Keeper's terrace | Smells of flowers, and of nothing else at all*

> Hundreds of Pindarians lie beneath flowering mounds.
>
> Their faces are visible through the petals. Not obscured, not decayed — *visible*, composed, turned upward, with flowers growing from and around them without disturbing a single feature.
>
> Every blossom carries a different eye colour.

### What Is in This Room

1. **Six hundred and forty mounds**, each with a body, each flowering, each face visible and intact.
2. **The blossoms**, one colour per mound, matching the eyes of the person beneath.
3. **Red tendrils**, invasive, threading between the mounds in a creeping lattice. They are strangling the flowering and they are not native and they came in through a breach. *Perception DC 10 — they are obvious.*
4. **A path** through the mounds, kept clear, swept, with a set of pruning shears laid across it.
5. **Slate markers** at the head of each mound: name, people, and a single line in Pindarian. *Investigation DC 10.*
6. **An empty section**, forty feet, at the garden's north end, prepared — soil turned, edges cut, markers set — **and unplanted**, with six hundred and forty-one markers cut and only six hundred and forty mounds.
7. **Sister Ondree Valk**, if she survived M3, standing at the edge of this with a torch that has gone out.

### Setting the Scene

Do not play it as horror. Play it as a cemetery in high summer — well-kept, beautiful, quiet, with a swept path and somebody's shears laid down mid-job.

The faces are the thing. Composed, intact, turned up, with flowers growing through them. Nobody here is suffering and nobody here is undead and the party should feel that immediately, because the room's entire function is to be the opposite of what it looks like.

### DM Information

**The bodies preserve hereditary and cultural memories.** This is the biological twin of Ilyr-Ameul's Doll Archive — cloth there, flesh here — and a party that has been to Ilyr-Ameul should be told the resemblance is exact and deliberate.

***Speak with plants* reveals consent given before death.** Every one of them. Individually, by name, in their own words, and the party can ask as many as they have patience for. The blossoms answer.

**Burning or raising them destroys the archive.** **+3 Blight**, and six hundred and forty peoples' worth of hereditary record is gone, and it is the single most destructive act available anywhere in this campaign.

**Pruning the invasive red tendrils protects it.** **Three hours of careful work removes all Blight** — all of it, from wherever it came, including the two points the party inherited from Ondree.

That is an enormous mechanical gift, and it costs three hours of in-fiction time doing horticulture in a graveyard, and the party will have to decide it is worth doing while a woman with a torch watches them.

**The empty section.** Six hundred and forty-one markers and six hundred and forty mounds. The spare marker is cut, dressed, and blank, and it is at the head of the prepared plot, and it is Nymara's.

She dug it herself. It has been ready for four thousand years.

### Mechanics

**Speaking With the Dead (*speak with plants*, or Archive Bloom seeding infection).** No check. They answer freely, they are lucid, they are not in pain, and they consented. They will discuss the invasion, the Prohibition, the vote, and what it was like, and they are the best primary sources in the campaign.

**Pruning (3 hours, no check).** **All Blight removed.** Requires the shears on the path, which are laid out for exactly this purpose.

**Pruning Badly (Nature or Medicine DC 15 if rushed to 1 hour).** Success: Blight reduced by 3 instead. Failure: **+1 Blight** and the tendrils regrow within the day.

**Burning or Raising.** **+3 Blight.** The archive is destroyed. Any *animate dead*, *create undead*, *raise dead* or *resurrection* cast on a mound counts, including well-intentioned ones — and a cleric who tries to *raise* one of them to ask it a question has just destroyed a record and killed the person twice, and the blossom goes out.

**The Slate Markers (Investigation DC 10).** Name, people, and one line each. *The lines are not epitaphs.* They are what that person asked to be remembered for, in their own words, and about a third of them are very small things.

### Ondree Valk

If she is here, she has watched the party prune, or not.

She has one surviving companion, no torch that will light, and nine years of burning infected farms behind her, and she has just been shown a garden where the infection is the point.

**She does not convert.** She is not able to, in one scene, after nine years. What she does is **sit down**, and ask the party one question, and it is: *"How many did I get wrong?"*

The honest answer is that nobody knows and probably a great many, and if the party tells her that plainly she thanks them.

She can be brought to M7. Nymara will want to talk to her more than she wants to talk to the party.

### Creatures and NPCs

**Nothing hostile** unless the party burns something.

**The dead**, six hundred and forty of them, available to speak with, consenting, lucid, and courteous.

**Ondree Valk** (use **Priest** with Religion +7 and Insight +5). Forty-ish, competent, burnt out in the literal sense. Her companion, **Little Orn**, is nineteen and has not spoken in six days.

### Set Piece

**The six hundred and forty-first marker.**

Cut, dressed, blank, at the head of a prepared plot with the soil turned and the edges squared.

*Investigation DC 12:* the soil has been turned recently. Within the month.

She keeps it ready. She has kept it ready for four thousand years, turning the soil every few weeks so it does not pack down, and she has never used it, and every time she turns it she is deciding again not to.

### Lore Drops

- **L16 — The Consent** (*speak with plants*). Every one of them agreed, individually, in advance, in writing, and the writing is on the markers. Nobody in this garden was put here.
- **L17 — The One Line** (*Investigation DC 10*). What each asked to be remembered for. A third are small: *"I was good with the goats."* *"I made my sister laugh on the last day."* *"I kept the western gate clean."* **Read four of them aloud at the table and let the room go quiet.**
- **L18 — The Twin Archive** (*if the party has been to Ilyr-Ameul*). Cloth there, flesh here, same purpose, same period, same hand in the notation. *History DC 15:* they were built as a pair, by agreement, so that no single catastrophe could take both — **and one of them has an empty section for the Ilyri, and so does this one, at the far end, forty feet, unplanted and never planted.**
- **L19 — The Prepared Plot** (*Investigation DC 12*). Turned within the month. It is hers and it is ready and she has not used it.

### Plot Hooks

- Six hundred and forty primary sources for the invasion, the vote, and the Prohibition, all of them willing to talk, is the largest research opportunity in the campaign and it is entirely optional.
- L18's second empty section is the Ilyri's, here as well as at Ilyr-Ameul. Neither archive has them. The party is now the third party to notice.
- Ondree Valk, brought to Nymara, is a scene about two women who burned something to save it.

### Resolution, Rewards, and Temptation

**All Blight removed** for three hours of pruning. **+3 Blight** and an irreplaceable loss for burning.

**No treasure**, and the party should feel that taking anything from this room would be obscene, and nothing stops them.

## M7. The Keeper's Nail

*Terrace garden, 90 ft. across, open to the sky | Late-afternoon light regardless of the hour | Exits: the corpse garden, the valley gate | Smells of turned earth and cut stems*

> A woman woven with fungus kneels at a bed, tending a sickle that is growing out of a stone heart the size of a cart. Kneeling, she is your height. The bed she is weeding runs the length of the terrace and she can reach across it without moving.
>
> She does not stop working when you arrive. She finishes the row.
>
> **"Decay is not evil,"** she says, without looking up. **"Keeping everything forever is."**

### What Is in This Room

1. **Nymara Thess**, Keeper of the Last Garden, on her knees in the soil, in a working apron, threaded through with pale fungus that is clearly part of her and clearly not hurting her.
2. **The Bride of Rot**, a sickle, growing out of a stone heart set into the terrace floor — not driven into it. **Growing.** The haft is continuous with the stone.
3. **The ring of light** around the stone heart, with a sickle-shaped absence at its edge that the sickle is currently occupying.
4. **A ledger**, open, on a low table: six hundred and forty species by name with a status column, in her hand, updated this morning.
5. **Seed trays**, forty of them, prepared, empty, labelled and waiting.
6. **Doctor Iremic Sahl**, if he lives, standing at the edge with a specimen case and an argument he has been rehearsing for four days.
7. **Archive Blooms**, two of them, at the terrace edges, dormant, which will wake if she is attacked.

### Setting the Scene

She is a gardener. Play her as a gardener: dirty hands, practical clothes, a job in progress, and no interest at all in ceremony.

Let her finish the row before she stands up. It takes about twenty seconds of table silence and it establishes everything.

### DM Information

**Nymara requires the party to choose one species to let die so that the rest survive.**

This is the trial, it is stated plainly, and it is real. The valley's load is about to be redistributed when the relic leaves; the system will not carry six hundred and forty species through it; one must be released, and she will not choose, because she has chosen before.

**There is no hidden perfect answer.** There is no trick option, no seventh thing, no way to save them all, and a party looking for one is failing the room. Say so directly if they stall for more than fifteen minutes: *"She is not testing whether you can find a loophole. She is asking you to do her job for one afternoon."*

**She judges three things and says so:**

| She judges | What passes |
| --- | --- |
| **Process** | Did they look at the ledger? Did they ask what each species does? Did they consult anybody, including Iremic and Ondree? |
| **Evidence** | Can they say *why* this one and not that one, with a reason drawn from something they saw in the valley? |
| **Acceptance** | Do they own it afterwards, out loud, without hedging, without asking her to agree? |

**A party that chooses badly but by good process passes.** A party that chooses the objectively optimal species by coin flip fails. She has been doing this for four thousand years and she is not grading the answer.

**Iremic Sahl's argument** belongs here. He wants the silverthread stock for four towns. If the party nominates his species — accepting that a plague cure dies with it, or that it lives and something else goes — he will argue, and he is a good arguer, and Nymara will let him.

**If fought:** Archive Blooms defend her, she does not pursue, and she surrenders the moment anyone completes the trial, including mid-combat.

### Her Regret

If asked — and they should ask — she gives it without softening:

> *"There was a district in the capital with a rot in it. I burned it. Eleven thousand people, and I had the authority, and the arithmetic was correct, and it worked.*
>
> *"The rot was eating something. I did not know that. Nobody knew that. And when I had burned it, the thing it was eating had nothing left to stop it, and it took the rest of the city in nine days, and I watched that from the wall.*
>
> *"I have been keeping a garden ever since. It is not penance. Penance would be easier. It is just the job I am now qualified for."*

**Sister Ondree Valk has been burning infected farms for nine years.** If she is present, this is the conversation, and the party should mostly stay out of it.

### Mechanics

**The Choice (Roleplay, no check).** One species, named, with a reason, owned afterwards.

**The Ledger (Investigation DC 10, or just read it).** Six hundred and forty species, status, role, and dependencies. **It tells them which species other species need**, and a party that reads it properly can make an informed decision in ten minutes, and reading it is most of *process*.

**Blight at the Trial.**

| Blight | Effect |
| --- | --- |
| 0 | She has already accepted most of the argument. The trial is a conversation. |
| 1-2 | Normal. She listens, questions, and decides. |
| 3-5 | Plant creatures hostile; she will still talk, over the noise. |
| 6 | The valley is closing. She will not talk at all until the party brings the Blight down, and the only way to do that now is M6's pruning, and the exits are sealing at one per hour. |

**Nymara Thess** (CR 12; see *Original Creatures*). She fights defensively, never pursues, and yields instantly. **Two Archive Blooms** (CR 13) defend her and are the actual threat.

**Lawful release.** The trial is completed. The sickle is **cut** from the stone heart — with the party's own tool, by the character who named the species, in one stroke — and the ring closes.

**-4 Blight. Relic Attention +1.**

**Lawful release creates a seed** containing a copy of the pyramid's archive: all six hundred and forty species, the corpse garden's hereditary record, and the eleven worlds. It is the size of a walnut. It will grow anywhere. **She gives it to whoever chose**, and she says one sentence about it, and the sentence is: *"Plant it somewhere you will not be able to defend."*

**Improper removal** — tearing the sickle out, killing her, cutting it before the trial — kills the stone heart, and the valley dies over the following season, and **every extinct species in the ledger goes extinct for real**. **Relic Attention +2.** No seed.

### Creatures and NPCs

**Nymara Thess**, Keeper of the Last Garden. Moon-tradition physician, burned a district, kept a garden.

**Her surrender condition** is the trial and she has no other.

**On the tenth pool** (M4), if asked: it offered a symbiosis that made a person unable to be infected by anything. *"It was very popular. I broke it. If you cannot be infected you cannot be in a relationship with anything, and I watched four people find that out, and then I took a hammer to it."*

**On the prepared plot** (M6), if asked: *"When there is somebody to take the ledger."*

### Set Piece

**The row.**

She finishes the row before she stands. Twenty seconds. Weeding, by hand, in a bed of something the party has never heard of and never will again.

And when she does stand, she puts the weeds in her apron pocket rather than dropping them, because they go on the compost, because they are part of it too.

### Lore Drops

- **L20 — Her Regret** (*ask*). Eleven thousand people, correct arithmetic, and a rot that was eating something worse. The exact mistake Ondree is making and the exact mistake the party nearly made at M3.
- **L21 — The Ledger** (*read it*). Six hundred and forty species, with dependencies. It is the most useful document in the site and it is lying on a table.
- **L22 — The Seed** (*on lawful release*). A walnut-sized copy of everything. *"Plant it somewhere you will not be able to defend."* **She has been making these for four thousand years and this is the first one she has been able to give away.**
- **L23 — Why She Cannot Leave** (*ask*). She can. She has been able to the whole time. She has not, because there is nobody to take the ledger, and the ledger is updated every morning, and if she stops for a season six hundred species go.

### Plot Hooks

- **The seed is the campaign's insurance policy.** If the Prohibition comes down, this is the backup, and she has told them to put it somewhere indefensible, and the party will argue about that for the rest of the campaign.
- Ondree Valk walking out of this valley with Nymara's account is the end of Zuggtmoy's farm-burning in four provinces, if she can be got out.
- The species the party lets die is gone, permanently, from the campaign, and they named it, and they should be asked to write it on a card.

### Resolution, Rewards, and Temptation

**Lawful: -4 Blight, Relic Attention +1**, the archive seed, and one species released by name.

**Improper: Relic Attention +2**, the valley dies over a season, and six hundred extinctions become real.

**Treasure.** The Bride of Rot (artifact; see *The Nine Demon Relics*). The archive seed. Nymara's shears, which are ordinary shears and four thousand years old and perfectly sharp.

## Myr'Velora Aftermath

When The Bride of Rot is removed lawfully, describe a deep vibration through the remaining pyramids. The valley's seal opens — the cleft widens, the gate-flower withers over about an hour, and for the first time in four thousand years the outside air gets in.

**Immediate consequences.**

- **Relic Attention +1.** The sickle is maternal, affectionate, and genuinely unable to understand why anyone would resist decomposition. It will be kind to the party. It will be kind about things they do not want it to be kind about.
- **The valley is now reachable.** Six hundred extinct species, unsealed, in a region with a plague, a cult, and four towns. What happens next is a campaign, not an epilogue.
- **Nymara ages normally or dissolves by choice**, and either way she does not stop working, and if she stays she will want somebody to take the ledger.
- **Iremic Sahl** leaves with one cutting and a method, or with nothing, and four towns find out which.
- **Ondree Valk** stops burning, or does not. If the party brought her to M7 she stops, and she has nine years of farms to account for, and she will start with the nearest one.
- **The eleven worlds** are now known to the party. The First Hunger does not destroy; it eats the record, and the place stays, and nobody remembers being there. Every guardian from here on can be asked about it.
