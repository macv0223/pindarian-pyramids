# Ilyr-Ameul: Pyramid of Two Truths - Site Guide

*Levels 10-12 | Children of the Moon | Conflict into Trust | Relic: The Two That Are One | Guardian: Vaelis and Vaelith, the Concordant Twins*

A mirrored ravine where one cliff is always in daylight and the other always in moonlight.

## Running Ilyr-Ameul

Every other pyramid asks the party a question. This one asks them a question **about each other**, and it does it by making them unable to agree on what the room looks like.

The site is built on a single conceit and everything in it serves that conceit: **the party is split into two perspectives at the door, and both perspectives are true.** Not one true and one illusion. Not a puzzle where the right answer is to work out which is real. Two complete, accurate, mutually incompatible accounts of the same physical space, and the only way through is to act on information you cannot verify, given to you by someone you cannot check.

Run it wrong and it is a frustrating hour of players arguing about a floor plan. Run it right and it is the best session in the campaign.

### Pindarian Scale

**Pindarians are Large.** All of them, always — the Solaari, the Lunari, the Veylari, the Ilyri, every
Saharim guardian and every soldier in every rank. Eight to ten feet, long-boned, and long-lived, with a
reach and a stride to match: **10-foot reach on their melee attacks and a 10-foot space.**

This is not a variant or a subtype. It is what the species is, and it is worth showing rather than stating —
Pindarian doorways are twelve feet high, their stairs have deep treads, their chairs do not fit anyone else,
and a party of Medium adventurers spends nine pyramids using furniture built for people half again their
size.

Human, elven and dwarven NPCs met in and around the sites are Medium as usual. The contrast is the point.

### The Split

At I1, divide the party into **Sun** and **Moon**. Two and two is ideal; three and one is playable and puts enormous weight on the one; five and one is not, so reassign.

**Both groups are in the same physical room at all times.** They occupy the same squares. They can touch each other. What differs is terrain, hazard, lighting, and what the furniture is doing.

| | Sun perspective | Moon perspective |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Light | Red stone, heat shimmer, hard shadow | Blue glass, frost, no shadow at all |
| Hazards | Physical: heat, collapse, weight, edges | Conceptual: doubt, delay, reflection, forgetting |
| Statues | Kneeling, surrendering, open-handed | Standing, armed, mid-swing |
| Doors | Where the hinges are | Where the handle is |
| Creatures | What they are doing | What they intend |

Neither list is the "real" one. Write that on an index card and put it where you can see it, because you will be tempted.

### Cross-Perspective Speech

**Communication across the split becomes metaphor.** Players may say anything they like; what the other side *hears* is an image.

- **Exact numbers become quantities.** *"Three of them"* is heard as *"a handful"*, *"more than I want"*, *"one for each of us and one over."*
- **Directions become relations.** *"Twenty feet north"* is heard as *"past the cold part"*, *"behind you and to the hand you write with."*
- **Creature identities become impressions.** *"It's a doppelganger"* is heard as *"it's wearing somebody."*

**Do not translate for the players.** Ask the speaking player to say it, then ask *them* to rephrase it as an image, and let the other side work with that. Players are better at this than you will expect and it is the most enjoyable part of the whole site.

**Within a perspective, speech is normal.** Sun can talk to Sun freely.

### The Discord Track

Visible, 0 to 6.

| Event | Discord |
| --- | --- |
| Declaring another character's perception false | +1 each |
| Forcing a door, gate or seam rather than solving it | +1 |
| Acting on your own perspective alone where both were available | +1 |
| Burning or destroying the Doll Archive (I4) | +2 |
| Coercing, tricking or forcing a character at the Trust Fall (I6) | +2 |
| Choosing a twin's faction at the Nail (I7) | +2 |
| Asking a cross-perspective question and acting on the answer | -1 each |
| Completing a paired repair at the Echo Forge (I5) | -1 |
| Repairing a doll (I4) | -1 each |
| Completing the Trust Fall (I6) | -2 |
| Stating aloud why you trust another character (I7) | -1 each |

**At 3.** A character who declares another's perception false is **confused until the end of their next turn** (as the *confusion* spell, one roll). The pyramid stops arguing and starts demonstrating.

**At 6.** The perspectives fully desynchronise. Characters **can no longer see each other at all** — Sun sees Moon's gear floating unsupported, Moon sees Sun as a heat-shimmer with a voice. All cross-perspective speech remains metaphor, and now so does *pointing*. This is survivable and it is horrible, and the only way back is I6.

### Concord Tokens

A silver-and-gold coin, warm on one face and cold on the other.

A character may spend a Concord Token to **see through another character's perspective for one round** — both accounts at once, superimposed, perfectly clear. It is the single most powerful thing in the site and there are not many of them.

Tokens are earned for acts of trust: accepting a cross-perspective answer without arguing, letting someone else make a call you cannot verify, completing the Trust Fall. Maximum three per character. They do not leave the pyramid.

### Pacing and Shape

Seven areas, roughly linear, two to three sessions. I1 splits the party, I2 through I5 teach them to work split, I6 is the emotional fulcrum, I7 is the guardians.

**There is no safe rest site**, but resting is not penalised here — Ilyr-Ameul is not trying to exhaust anybody. It is trying to make them talk.

### Safety at This Table

This is the one pyramid with a genuine table-safety consideration built into its premise. **A game that repeatedly tells a player their perception of reality is wrong can land very badly** on someone with a history of being gaslit, and you should say so explicitly during lines and veils rather than discovering it in session three.

The protections are structural and you must hold them:

- **No player is ever wrong.** Every perspective is accurate. The pyramid never lies to anyone, and neither do you.
- **No character is ever secretly replaced, possessed, or played by you without the player knowing.** Doppelgangers appear in the suggested creature list; if you use one, it replaces an NPC, never a PC.
- **The Trust Fall can always be declined** and never has to be attempted by a specific person.

If a player is not enjoying the split, collapse it for them — put them in whichever perspective their friends are in and run the site as a coordination puzzle instead. It loses something. It loses much less than losing the player.

### Continuity

**Vharo Sellick**, the six-fingered broker, has been to Astra and Khar'Zhul, has measured two Nail sockets, and is measuring a third. He is not in this pyramid. He is on the **daylight cliff**, outside, with a theodolite and a folding chair, and he waves.

## I1. The Bifurcation

*Entry passage, 80 ft. long, splitting around a blade-thin wall | Red heat on one side, blue frost on the other | Exits: the ravine mouth behind, the gallery ahead | Smells of hot stone and cold glass, simultaneously, from the same direction*

> The passage divides around a wall so thin you cannot see its edge from straight on. On one side, heat shimmers above red stone. On the other, frost silvers blue glass.
>
> Your companions choose different paths.
>
> They choose them while standing beside you. You watch them walk away to the left while their shoulder stays against yours, and neither of those things stops being true.

### What Is in This Room

1. **The blade wall**, 60 feet long, of a thickness that has no measurement. *Investigation DC 14:* it does not divide the passage. It **annotates** it — the passage is one corridor and the wall is a note in the margin explaining that.
2. **Two floors**, occupying the same floor. Sun sees red basalt, tilted, warm underfoot. Moon sees blue glass over a drop, frost-fogged, with something moving under it.
3. **The Cantor's alcove**, halfway along, in both perspectives, containing a shade.
4. **Two hundred handprints** on the blade wall, both faces, at the height of somebody steadying themselves. *Perception DC 12.* They are all left hands on one face and all right hands on the other, and *Investigation DC 16* confirms they are the same two hundred people.
5. **Your companions' second bodies.** If using a battle map, place **two tokens per character**: one where Sun sees them, one where Moon sees them, usually 5 to 15 feet apart. Both are them.
6. **A cold seam** running the length of the floor where the two accounts do not quite meet. *Perception DC 15.* A coin dropped on it lands on its edge and stays there.

### Setting the Scene

Describe the corridor **twice**, back to back, in the same breath, without saying which is which. Let the players sort out who heard what.

Then split them. Physically, at the table if you can — Sun players on one side, Moon on the other — and from that moment run every description in the room to one half of the table while the other half listens to something they cannot use.

This is the moment to establish the tone: **nobody is being tricked.** Say it out loud, once, in your own voice as the GM. *"Both of these are true, all the way through, and I will never tell either of you that you were wrong."* Then never say it again.

### DM Information

**Assign perspectives randomly.** A die, a coin, anything visible. Do not assign by class, by player, or by what would be dramatically convenient, because the party will spend three sessions looking for the pattern and there must not be one.

**The Moon Cantor shade** in the alcove is the tutorial and the thesis. It is called **Ithuel**, it is a fragment rather than a person, and it has one lesson and about a minute of coherence in which to give it:

> *"Different is not false. You will want it to be. It would be so much easier if one of you were lying."*

Then it asks each character, separately, to describe the alcove. It listens to both answers. It says *"yes"* to both. Then it goes out.

**The handprints** are the site's first hard lore: two hundred people came through here and steadied themselves on the wall, and every one of them used the hand on the side they were standing — which means the pyramid has been splitting visitors since it was built, and it has been doing it to Pindarians.

### Mechanics

**The Split (no check).** Random assignment. Both perspectives active from this point until I6 or the party leaves.

**Crossing the Wall.** A character may walk through the blade wall at any time. It costs nothing, takes one round, and **does not change their perspective** — they simply arrive on the other side still seeing what they saw. The first party member to try this will feel cheated and should be told, gently, that they have learned something.

**Speaking Across (no check).** Metaphor, as above. **-1 Discord** the first time a character asks a cross-perspective question and acts on the answer without arguing with it.

**The Cold Seam (Perception DC 15).** A coin stands on edge. *Arcana DC 17:* the seam is where the two accounts fail to overlap, and it is one-sixteenth of an inch wide, and **it is wider than it was four thousand years ago.** Ilyr-Ameul is slowly coming apart along its own premise.

**Ithuel (no check).** It speaks to both perspectives simultaneously and correctly, which nothing else in the site does. Each character who answers its question honestly gains **one Concord Token**.

### Creatures and NPCs

**Ithuel, Moon Cantor shade** (use **Moon Cantor**, non-hostile, at one quarter hit points; it will not fight and cannot be made to). A fragment of a Pindarian teaching-order that dealt in perspective as a discipline rather than a trick.

**Roleplaying it.** Patient, faintly amused, and running out of time in a way it finds more interesting than distressing. It answers questions for as long as it lasts, which is about a minute, and then it is simply not there.

**What it will tell them,** if asked within that minute: the pyramid does not create the split; it *reveals* one. Two people in a room have always been in two rooms. Ilyr-Ameul only removes the polite fiction.

### Set Piece

**The shoulder.**

The first thing each player experiences is watching a friend walk away down a corridor while still feeling that friend's shoulder against theirs. Describe it physically — the warmth, the weight, the small shift as they breathe — while describing them receding.

Do it once. Do not do it again.

### Lore Drops

- **L1 — The Handprints** (*Perception DC 12, then Investigation DC 16*). Two hundred people, all steadying themselves, all using the hand on their own side. The same two hundred appear on both faces. **The pyramid split Pindarians too**, and they came through in pairs, and they came through often enough to wear the stone.
- **L2 — The Annotating Wall** (*Investigation DC 14*). The wall does not divide the corridor. It is a margin note on a single corridor, explaining that it is two. *History DC 16:* this is Lunari notation, the same convention used in a Moon Cantor's teaching hall, which means **the entrance to this anchor is a classroom.**
- **L3 — The Widening Seam** (*Arcana DC 17*). One-sixteenth of an inch and growing. The two accounts are drifting apart. At the current rate they separate entirely in something under two centuries, and when they do the anchor fails — not violently, just into two halves that no longer touch.
- **L4 — Ithuel's Lesson** (*freely*). *"Different is not false. You will want it to be."*

### Plot Hooks

- The widening seam is Ilyr-Ameul's failure mode and it is nobody's fault, which makes it a different problem from every other anchor in the campaign.
- Two hundred pairs of Pindarian handprints implies a training programme. Somebody was sending people in here on purpose, repeatedly, and Ithuel was the instructor.
- Ithuel goes out after a minute. Whether it can be restored is a question for a party that has been collecting Moon Cantor lore since the sourcebook.

### Resolution, Rewards, and Temptation

**One Concord Token** per character who answers Ithuel honestly. **-1 Discord** for the first accepted cross-perspective answer.

**No treasure.** The party walks into the galleries split, talking in pictures, and about to be attacked by something only half of them can see properly.

## I2. The Paired Galleries

*Gallery, 120 ft. by 40 ft., colonnaded | Perspective-dependent | Exits: the bifurcation behind, the door chamber ahead | Smells of dust and, faintly, of blood that has not been spilled yet*

> Statues line the hall, forty of them, in two facing rows.
>
> **Sun:** they kneel. Every one of them, weapons laid down, palms open and upturned, faces raised. It is a hall of surrender and it goes on for a hundred and twenty feet.
>
> **Moon:** they stand. Every one of them, mid-swing, weight forward, blades at the top of the arc. It is an ambush frozen one second before it lands.

### What Is in This Room

1. **Forty statues**, twenty a side, each in both postures. *Investigation DC 13 in either perspective:* the stone is unworked at the joints — they were not carved kneeling or standing. They were carved **in between**, and each perspective is completing the gesture.
2. **The floor**, a grid of 5-foot tiles. **Sun** sees some tiles glowing red-hot. **Moon** sees some tiles as thin glass over a void. *They are not the same tiles.* Roughly a third of the floor is safe in both.
3. **Two hundred and forty tiles** total. A tile is **Sun-hazard**, **Moon-hazard**, **both**, or **neither**. Only *neither* is safe.
4. **Bones on the floor**, in both perspectives, in the same places: sixteen bodies, scattered, none of them recent. *Medicine DC 12:* all sixteen died of burns **and** of falling, which is not possible.
5. **A tally scratched on the ninth column**, in Pindarian: four marks and a fifth struck through, repeated, twenty-two times. *Investigation DC 12.*
6. **Nothing hostile** unless the party earns it.

### Setting the Scene

Read both descriptions aloud, to the whole table, one after the other. This is the first room where the players hear each other's version in full, and the contrast is the scene.

Then put a hazard under somebody's foot and let the other half of the table watch them almost step on it.

### DM Information

**The statues attack only a character who acts on an assumption without checking the other perspective.** That is the whole rule and it must be enforced consistently and visibly.

Concretely: a character who **moves onto a tile without asking their counterpart whether it is safe** triggers the nearest statue. A character who asks, receives a metaphor, interprets it wrong, and steps on a hazard anyway takes the hazard damage — **but no statue animates**, because they did the right thing and the answer was imperfect.

**That distinction is the entire pyramid.** Punish the failure to ask. Never punish an honest attempt that went wrong.

**Players may ask each other sensory questions.** *"Is it hot where I'm about to be?"* *"What colour is the floor under my left foot?"* Answers arrive as images, never as exact statements — *"where you're going is the colour of a kettle"* — and that is enough, and the players will get good at it within ten minutes.

**The safe path exists.** Roughly a third of the tiles are safe in both accounts, and they form a continuous, winding, slightly ridiculous route the length of the hall. Comparing perspectives reveals it. Nothing else does.

### Mechanics

**Tile Hazards.**

| Tile | Sun perspective | Moon perspective |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Sun-hazard | Red-hot: **22 (4d10) fire**, DC 16 Dexterity for half | Ordinary blue glass |
| Moon-hazard | Ordinary red basalt | Thin glass: **DC 16 Dexterity** or fall 30 ft. for 10 (3d6) bludgeoning and prone |
| Both | Both effects | Both effects |
| Safe | Nothing | Nothing |

A character only suffers the hazard **of their own perspective**. A Sun character walking on Moon-glass walks on solid basalt, and their Moon counterpart watches them stroll across a sheet of ice over a pit.

**Triggering a Statue.** Moving onto any non-safe tile **without having asked** animates the nearest statue as a **Concordant Echo** (CR 11), which copies the last action used against it. **+1 Discord.**

**Concordant Echo.** AC 17, 150 hp, immune to psychic. *Mirror the Blow:* whenever it takes damage from an attack or spell, it may use its reaction to make that same attack or cast that same spell back at the attacker, using its own modifiers (+9 to hit, DC 17). *Both-Sighted:* it exists identically in both perspectives and can be fought by everyone, which makes it the one thing in the site the party can co-ordinate against cleanly.

Killing an Echo with a single overwhelming attack is the worst possible plan and a party will do it exactly once.

**Sensory Questions (no check, no limit).** Ask freely. Answers are images. **-1 Discord** per character the first time they act on one.

**Declaring a Perception False.** Any character who says a version of *"no it isn't"*, *"you're wrong"*, or *"that's not what's there"* gains **+1 Discord**. Rule it kindly — the trigger is an assertion that the other account is untrue, not an expression of confusion or frustration.

### Creatures and NPCs

**Concordant Echoes** only, and only if earned. They have no dialogue, no faces, and no motive; they are the room disagreeing with itself.

### Set Piece

**The sixteenth body.**

Among the bones, one skeleton lies half on a tile that is red-hot in one account and open void in the other. It is burned on the left side and broken on the right.

*Medicine DC 12* confirms both, and confirms they happened at the same moment, and there is no posture in which that is possible unless two things were true about that person at once.

Somebody's friend did not ask.

### Lore Drops

- **L5 — The Unfinished Statues** (*Investigation DC 13*). Carved mid-gesture and completed by the observer. *History DC 15:* Lunari teaching sculpture. These are not guards; they are **exam questions**, and they have been marking for four thousand years.
- **L6 — The Tally** (*Investigation DC 12*). Twenty-two sets of five, scratched on the ninth column. One hundred and ten. *Insight DC 14:* it is not a body count. The marks are in two hands, alternating, and somebody was keeping score against somebody else, and they stopped at exactly one hundred and ten.
- **L7 — Burned and Broken** (*Medicine DC 12*). Sixteen bodies, each killed by both hazards simultaneously. **Perspective is not a metaphor here.** It is load-bearing physics, and a body can be subject to both accounts at once if the person inside it stopped believing either.

### Plot Hooks

- The tally in two hands is the first sign of the twins, two rooms early. Vaelis and Vaelith kept score in this gallery for a very long time and then stopped, and a party that asks them why gets the whole story.
- One hundred and ten is also a number the party can ask Ithuel about, if anyone thought to keep it alive.

### Resolution, Rewards, and Temptation

**-1 Discord** per character who acts on a cross-perspective answer. **+1 Discord** per statue triggered.

**Treasure.** The sixteen bodies carry 340 gp between them, a *cloak of displacement* (which the party will find very funny and then very useful), and a Lunari teaching token — a silver-and-gold coin, warm on one face, cold on the other. **It is a Concord Token.** There are three in the room if anyone searches properly (*Investigation DC 15*, ten minutes).

## I3. The Contradictory Doors

*Antechamber, 40 ft. square | Both perspectives agree on this room, which is itself unsettling | Exits: the gallery behind, two doors facing each other | Smells of nothing at all*

> Two doors face each other across an empty room. Both perspectives show the same thing, for the first time since the entrance, and the sameness is worse than the split was.
>
> The left door reads: **ONLY THE TRUTH OPENS ME.**
>
> The right door reads: **EVERY TRUTH CLOSES ME.**

### What Is in This Room

1. **The left door**, Pindarian bronze, inscribed **ONLY THE TRUTH OPENS ME**, currently shut.
2. **The right door**, identical, inscribed **EVERY TRUTH CLOSES ME**, currently shut.
3. **The empty room** between them — no furniture, no marks, no dust, and *Perception DC 10* confirms there is no dust, in a four-thousand-year-old sealed chamber, which is the room's only warning.
4. **The floor**, which is one continuous slab. There is no seam here. **Both perspectives match exactly.**
5. **The passage beyond**, visible through neither door until both are open, and both doors lead to the same corridor.
6. **Scuff marks** in front of each door, *Investigation DC 14* — hundreds of pairs of feet, always two people, always standing one at each door, always facing each other.

### Setting the Scene

The absence of the split is the tension. For two rooms the party has been living in two accounts, and now they are standing in a room where everyone sees the same thing, and it should feel like a held breath rather than a relief.

Read both inscriptions. Then stop and let them work.

### DM Information

**Both inscriptions are correct and both doors are honest.**

- **The left door** opens for a **true statement** and is closed by anything else.
- **The right door** is closed by a true statement and opens for a **sincere uncertainty** — *"I don't know if we're going to survive this"*, *"I'm not sure I trust him"*, *"I can't tell if I'm doing this right."*

**Both must stand open simultaneously**, which means **two characters must speak at the same moment**: one a truth, one an uncertainty. They must count each other in. They must mean both.

This is a coordination puzzle whose solution is two people saying vulnerable things in unison, and it is much harder at a table than it sounds, and it is worth every minute it takes.

**A statement must be sincere, not clever.** *"I am standing here"* is true and opens the left door and Khar'Zhul would have accepted it; Ilyr-Ameul accepts it too, and the door swings, and nothing else happens, because the right door needs somebody to actually not know something and say so.

**Most parties solve the left door in thirty seconds and the right door in twenty minutes**, and the twenty minutes is the room.

### Mechanics

**Speaking (no check).** Say a thing. The relevant door responds. There is no roll, no DC, and no limit on attempts.

**Simultaneity.** Both statements must be spoken in the same moment. At the table: count three, both players speak. If one hesitates, both doors reset. Let them try as often as they like.

**Forcing a Door.** Left door: AC 18, 80 hp. Right door: the same. Breaking either one **creates a Concordant Echo** (CR 11) and adds **+1 Discord**, and the broken door's corridor leads to the same place, so the party gains nothing but a fight and a mark on the ledger.

**Divination.** *Zone of truth*, *detect thoughts*, *augury*, *commune* and similar all function normally and all confirm that both inscriptions are accurate. They do not help. A party that spends a 5th-level slot confirming a paradox should be allowed to, and told plainly that the paradox is real.

**-1 Discord** for each character who speaks a sincere uncertainty aloud, whether or not it works.

**One Concord Token** to each of the two characters who open the doors together.

### Creatures and NPCs

None, unless a door is broken.

### Set Piece

**The dustless room.**

Four thousand years sealed, and no dust. *Perception DC 10*, freely given if nobody asks.

Because the room is swept. Because somebody sweeps it. Because there is a custodian in Ilyr-Ameul as there is in Astra and Vhal'Kathar, and this pyramid's custodian has two sets of hands and has been arguing with themselves about the correct sweeping direction for four thousand years, and the party will meet them in I7 and will not immediately connect the two.

### Lore Drops

- **L8 — The Scuff Marks** (*Investigation DC 14*). Hundreds of pairs, always two people, always one at each door, always facing. **Nobody has ever done this alone**, which means nobody ever could.
- **L9 — The Matching Room** (*automatic*). The only chamber in Ilyr-Ameul where both perspectives agree completely. *Arcana DC 16:* the agreement is manufactured — this room has been **forced** into concordance, at cost, by something maintaining it. It is the one place in the pyramid where the two accounts are held together by hand.
- **L10 — No Dust** (*Perception DC 10*). Somebody sweeps.

### Plot Hooks

- The forced concordance in this room is a working demonstration of what the twins do to themselves continuously, and a party that measures it (*Arcana DC 18*) learns the effort is roughly what it would take to hold a building up.
- Both doors lead to the same corridor. A party that notices this before opening them has understood the pyramid and should be told so.

### Resolution, Rewards, and Temptation

**-1 Discord** per sincere uncertainty spoken. **One Concord Token** each to the pair who open the doors.

**+1 Discord** and a Concordant Echo for forcing.

**No treasure.** Both doors open onto the same corridor, and the corridor goes down.

## I4. The Doll Archive

*Archive, 100 ft. by 60 ft., shelved floor to ceiling | Perspective-dependent, but only in detail | Exits: the corridor behind, the forge stair | Smells of clean linen, old wool, and cedar*

> Shelves hold cloth figures. Thousands of them, in rows, each about the length of a forearm, each stitched with enormous care in the dress of a people whose name you do not know and cannot find.
>
> They are not toys. Nothing about the room suggests toys.
>
> A single black needle drags a red thread between the shelves whenever no one is looking directly at it. You have not seen it move. You have seen where it has been.

### What Is in This Room

1. **Four thousand and six dolls**, shelved, catalogued, in cloth, wool, hide and grass, representing peoples across four thousand years. *Investigation DC 12:* the stitching records lineage — seam length, thread colour, knot pattern. Each doll is a family.
2. **Two hundred and eleven damaged dolls** on a repair table: torn seams, lost stuffing, faded thread. Somebody has been mending them and has not finished.
3. **The black needle**, moving only when unobserved, trailing red thread between shelves. It is the **Seam Ripper** (see *The Living Mimic Relics*), and it has been here a long time, and it is bored.
4. **An empty section**, forty feet of shelving, bare, with the mounting holes still in the stone. *Perception DC 11.* The label plate is still attached and the name has been scraped off it.
5. **A catalogue lectern** with an index in Pindarian listing every doll by people, region and period. **The index includes the empty section.**
6. **A repair kit** at the table: needles, thread in ninety colours, scissors, and a pair of spectacles sized for someone with a very small face.
7. **The perspective difference here is small and horrible.** Sun sees the dolls as cloth. Moon sees, inside each one, the shape of the person it records — not a ghost, not alive, just the shape, the way a mould holds a shape.

### Setting the Scene

Play it as a library, warm and quiet and well-kept, and let the party work out on their own what they are standing in.

Then have a Moon character look closely at a doll and describe what is inside it.

### DM Information

**The dolls are genetic and cultural records.** Not magic, not soul-jars, not phylacteries. The Pindarians built a physical archive of every people on Elderon, recorded in stitching, because they were about to seal the world and they were not certain the world would survive it.

**Repairing three dolls reveals this** — the third repair exposes the inner seam, which carries a legible Pindarian notation of lineage, language group and last census.

**The empty section is the site's central lore.** Forty feet of bare shelving, label scraped, and the index still lists it:

> **THE ILYRI — Children of the Fourth. Pop. 1,104,000. Final entry: year 0.**

There were **four** Children of Pindaria, not three. Sun, Moon, Void — and Ilyr. And the Ninefold Prohibition required three traditions working together, and it is named for nine anchors, and this pyramid is called **Ilyr**-Ameul, and there is nothing on the shelf.

What happened to them is not answered here. It is answered in I7, by two people who were there.

**The Seam Ripper** chooses a wielder from among stitched, constructed, shapechanging or doll-adjacent characters — **Dolly**, if she is in your campaign, and it will be insufferable about it. Otherwise it goes to whoever most recently repaired something.

**Burning the archive adds 2 Discord and erases a Pindarian people from history**, permanently, in the campaign, retroactively. Nobody will remember them. The party will, because they were holding the match.

### Mechanics

**Reading a Doll (Investigation DC 12).** Lineage, region, period. Ten minutes each.

**Repairing a Doll.** **Dexterity (weaver's or leatherworker's tools) DC 14**, or **Intelligence (History) DC 16** to reconstruct the correct pattern before sewing, granting advantage. Twenty minutes each.

**-1 Discord per doll repaired.** After the third, the inner seam is legible and the archive's purpose is clear.

**The Empty Section (Perception DC 11, then read the index).** See above. No check required to read the index; it is open on the lectern.

**The Seam Ripper.** It selects a wielder when first observed directly — which requires a character to **look at it on purpose and keep looking**, a **DC 15 Wisdom saving throw** to maintain. On a success it is caught, and it is pleased, and it starts talking.

**Burning or Destroying the Archive.** **+2 Discord.** One Pindarian people is erased from history. Choose a people the party has heard of, and then never mention them again, and if a player asks about them later, look blank.

**The Carrionette Option.** If you want a fight here, animated dolls work well and the suggested list includes Carrionettes. **They should be defending the shelves, not attacking the party**, and they stop the moment the party stops reaching for something.

### Creatures and NPCs

**The Seam Ripper** (see *The Living Mimic Relics*). A black needle and an opinion. It is dormant until observed and it has been waiting four thousand years for somebody interesting, and it will make that everyone's problem.

**The mender.** Two hundred and eleven dolls on the repair table, a half-finished seam in the top one, and spectacles sized for a small face. Nobody is here. The thread on the needle in the half-finished doll is still under tension.

They left in a hurry, recently, and they are coming back.

### Set Piece

**The label plate.**

Forty feet of bare shelf, the mounting holes still in the stone, and a bronze label plate still screwed to the end panel with the name scraped off — not chiselled, *scraped*, by hand, over what must have been a very long time, by somebody who kept stopping.

The index on the lectern still has the entry. Whoever scraped the plate never went to the lectern.

*Insight DC 15:* they could not make themselves finish the job.

### Lore Drops

- **L11 — What the Dolls Are** (*three repairs, or Investigation DC 18*). A physical census of every people on Elderon, recorded in stitching, made because the Pindarians were about to seal the world and were not confident it would hold.
- **L12 — The Ilyri** (*read the index*). **Children of the Fourth. Population 1,104,000. Final entry: year 0.** A fourth Pindarian tradition, absent from every other source in this campaign, with an empty shelf and a scraped label and a surviving index entry.
- **L13 — Year 0** (*History DC 16*). Pindarian dating runs from the completion of the Ninefold Prohibition. **The Ilyri's final entry is the year the barrier went up.** They did not die in the invasion. They ended when the shell closed.
- **L14 — The Half-Finished Seam** (*automatic, at the repair table*). Thread still under tension, spectacles set down, two hundred and eleven to go. Somebody has been doing this job for four thousand years and stepped out.

### Plot Hooks

- The Ilyri are the campaign's biggest single thread and they belong to you. The Prohibition took three traditions to build. The obvious and worst answer to what the fourth one was for is that it was what the barrier was made *of*.
- The mender is in I7, and the party will not realise it.
- A party that repairs dolls is doing archival conservation work in the middle of a dungeon crawl and should be given the time and the quiet to enjoy it.

### Resolution, Rewards, and Temptation

**-1 Discord per doll repaired**, uncapped, and a party that spends two hours in here can walk into I7 at zero.

**+2 Discord** for destroying the archive, and a people gone.

**Treasure.** The repair kit (a masterwork weaver's tools). Ninety colours of Pindarian thread, worth 400 gp to the right buyer and priceless to a historian. The spectacles, which are lenses of *comprehend languages* for written text only, once per day. The Seam Ripper, if it takes an interest.

## I5. The Echo Forge

*Forge, 70 ft. by 50 ft., double-vaulted | Perspective-dependent | Exits: the archive stair, the wall chamber | Smells of hot iron in one account and cold iron in the other*

> Two anvils ring though no hammer falls. They are not ringing in time.
>
> On each anvil lies the same spear. Not a matched pair — **the same spear**, and you can see this, and you cannot explain how you can see it.
>
> It is broken in a different place on each.

### What Is in This Room

1. **Two anvils**, 30 feet apart, ringing irregularly. Sun sees one; Moon sees the other; **each perspective can only see and touch its own**, and each can see the *other* group standing at nothing.
2. **The spear**, on both anvils, the same object, broken at the shaft in the Sun account and at the socket in the Moon account.
3. **A forge**, cold in both accounts, with tools laid out neatly for two smiths.
4. **A quenching trough**, full, and the water is warm on one side and frozen on the other, and it is one trough.
5. **Work notes** pinned above the forge in Pindarian, in two hands, arguing. *Investigation DC 12.*
6. **Eleven previous attempts** stacked in a corner: eleven spears, each welded wrong, each ruined by somebody repairing the break they could see.

### Setting the Scene

The rhythm is the hook: two anvils ringing out of time with each other, in the same room, and nobody is hitting anything.

Then the spear. Make sure both halves of the table understand that it is one object described twice, because the entire puzzle depends on that landing.

### DM Information

**Each perspective must repair what the other can see.**

Sun can see the shaft break and cannot touch the spear that has it; Moon can touch that spear and cannot see the break. Every repair action is therefore **performed by one character on instructions from another**, in metaphor, with no way to verify.

This is I2's lesson made into labour, and it should take twenty minutes and produce at least one very funny disaster.

**Four successes at DC 16**, using **Arcana, Athletics, smith's tools, or Insight** — the ability is whatever the player justifies, because the room does not care how they do it, only that they do it together.

**Each failure manifests a Concordant Echo** that copies the last action used against it, which means a party failing repeatedly builds itself an increasingly well-armed opponent out of its own best ideas.

**Success suppresses the Gaze of the Prince** during the first day of attunement to The Two That Are One — which is worth a great deal, because the Gaze's price is two contradictory impressions about something important, and the first day with an artifact is exactly when a party can least afford that.

### Mechanics

**The Repair (Skill Challenge).** **Four successes before three failures.** Each attempt is one character acting, guided by a character in the other perspective.

| Approach | Check | Notes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Smith's tools | DC 16 | The direct answer |
| Athletics | DC 16 | Heat, hold, hammer, hope |
| Arcana | DC 16 | Understanding why one object has two breaks |
| Insight | DC 16 | Reading what your partner actually means |

**The guiding character must describe the break in metaphor**, and the acting character must work from that description. If the guiding player gives an exact instruction, the acting player does not hear it — ask them to rephrase.

**A character may spend a Concord Token** to see both accounts for one round, which grants **automatic success** on one attempt. This is the correct use of a Token and a party that works that out should be rewarded for it.

**On a failure:** one **Concordant Echo** (CR 11) manifests and acts immediately. *Mirror the Blow* means the party's own best attack comes straight back at them.

**Four successes:** the spear is whole in both accounts simultaneously — the only object in the pyramid that is. **-1 Discord** for every character who contributed, and the Gaze suppression above.

**Three failures:** the spear is ruined and joins the stack of eleven. The party may rest and try again with a fresh spear that appears on the anvils; the Echoes remain.

### Creatures and NPCs

**Concordant Echoes**, earned only.

### Set Piece

**The eleven failures.**

In the corner, eleven spears. Every one of them is welded solid at one break and shattered at the other. Every one of them was repaired by somebody competent working alone, correctly, on the damage they could see.

*Investigation DC 13:* the welds are excellent. The smiths were not bad at this. They were bad at asking.

### Lore Drops

- **L15 — The Work Notes** (*Investigation DC 12, Pindarian*). Two hands, arguing, pinned above a cold forge. Not about metallurgy — about **method**. One hand insists the break must be understood before it is struck; the other insists it must be struck before it can be understood. Neither yields. The notes run to forty sheets and the last one, in both hands together, reads: *"Then we will do both, and we will do them at the same moment, and if it fails we will do it again."*
- **L16 — One Trough, Two Temperatures** (*Arcana DC 15*). Warm and frozen simultaneously, stable, indefinitely. The quench works perfectly in both accounts. **Ilyr-Ameul is not broken.** It is a functioning machine that operates on two states at once, and everything the party has found unsettling has been the machine working.
- **L17 — The Eleven** (*Investigation DC 13*). Excellent welds, ruined spears. Competence was never the obstacle.

### Plot Hooks

- The two arguing hands in the work notes are Vaelis and Vaelith, and the final sheet is the moment they stopped keeping score in I2.
- A suppressed Gaze on day one of attunement is a real tactical gift and will shape how the party handles the relic afterwards.

### Resolution, Rewards, and Temptation

**-1 Discord** per contributor on success. **Gaze of the Prince suppressed** for the first day of attunement.

**Treasure.** The repaired spear is not the relic — it is a *+1 spear*, whole in both accounts, and it is the only mundane object in the campaign that is provably in two states and stable in both. Any Pindarian scholar will pay a great deal for it. The forge tools are masterwork smith's tools.

## I6. The Trust Fall

*Divided chamber, 60 ft. by 40 ft., split by a solid wall | Both perspectives, separated | Exits: the forge behind, the Nail chamber ahead (sealed) | Smells of nothing, again, which by now the party should find ominous*

> A wall divides the chamber wall to wall and floor to ceiling. There is no door.
>
> On your side there is a lever.
>
> Above it, cut into the stone:
>
> **PULLING THIS KILLS THE ONE BEYOND.**

### What Is in This Room

1. **The dividing wall**, solid, seamless, impassable by any means the party has. *Passwall*, *dimension door*, *etherealness*, digging — all fail, politely, without damage.
2. **Two levers**, one on each side, identical, in perfect working order.
3. **The inscription**, identical on both sides.
4. **A speaking-grille** in the wall at head height, through which the two halves can hear each other — **normally**, without metaphor, for the first time since I1. *This is the room's cruelty:* the party can finally talk properly, and what they have to talk about is this.
5. **Bones on both sides.** Four skeletons on one, four on the other, all of them **beside the lever, not at it**, all of them having sat down.
6. **Nothing else.** No trap, no monster, no timer, no consequence for waiting.

### Setting the Scene

Split the table physically if you have not already — two sides of the room, or two rooms if your space allows. Let them talk through a doorway.

Read the inscription to both halves. Then say nothing for as long as it takes.

**There is no timer.** Tell them that explicitly if they ask, and mean it. A party may spend an hour of real time in this room and the pyramid does not care.

### DM Information

**The statement is false. Divination confirms it as true.**

Pulling a lever destroys the **projected body** the other side is standing in — not the character. Each of them reappears, unharmed, in the central chamber beyond the wall, with every possession and every hit point intact.

But *zone of truth*, *detect thoughts*, *augury*, *divination* and *commune* all return **true**, because by every measure those spells use, the thing beyond the wall dies. The party's most reliable tools for establishing fact will look them in the eye and confirm the worst reading.

**Both levers must be pulled simultaneously.** One alone does nothing at all — no click, no sound, no effect — and the character who pulled it has no way to know whether they have just killed their friend, and will not find out until the other one pulls.

**That gap is the trial.** A character who pulls first and then waits is doing the hardest thing this pyramid asks of anyone.

**A character who refuses can be reassured, never coerced.** The trial resets after honest conversation, as many times as needed. If a party tries to trick, bully, bribe or magically compel a reluctant character into pulling: **+2 Discord**, the levers seize, and the room does not open again until the coerced character has been apologised to and has freely agreed.

That is not a soft ruling. Enforce it exactly.

### Mechanics

**No checks. Not one.** No save, no skill, no Insight roll to determine whether the inscription is true. If a player asks to roll, tell them there is nothing to roll and that this is deliberate.

**Divination.** Any truth-detecting effect returns **true**. Do not soften this and do not hint. A cleric who burns *divination* on this and gets *"yes, it kills them"* has had an entirely fair and correct answer.

**Pulling Alone.** Nothing happens. The character has no feedback of any kind.

**Pulling Together.** Both bodies dissolve. Both characters reappear in the central chamber beyond, whole, with everything they carried, standing next to each other.

**-2 Discord for every character.** **One Concord Token each.** And if the party was at **6 Discord**, the desynchronisation ends here and only here — the perspectives resolve, the party can see each other again, and that relief should be described at length.

**Refusing.** No penalty, ever. The party talks. They try again when they are ready. There is no limit and no cost.

**Coercion.** **+2 Discord**, levers seize, room locks until a genuine apology and a free agreement.

### Creatures and NPCs

None. This is the only keyed area in the entire site with nothing in it.

### Set Piece

**The eight who sat down.**

Four skeletons on each side, and not one of them is at the lever. Every one of them is sitting with their back against the wall, beside the grille, facing away from the mechanism.

*Investigation DC 12:* they died of age. All eight. They sat down on either side of a wall, and they talked through a grille, and they never pulled, and they got old.

Nobody in this room was murdered. Eight people just decided the cost was too high and stayed.

The party can absolutely make that choice too. The pyramid will let them. There is another way to the Nail chamber and it takes nine days and the site does not object.

### Lore Drops

- **L18 — The Eight** (*Investigation DC 12*). Died of age, sitting, talking. **This trial has a survivable refusal**, and eight people took it, and there is a pile of empty ration tins beside each group that suggests they were supplied from outside for years.
- **L19 — What the Levers Do** (*Arcana DC 20, or from the twins afterwards*). The projected body is a Lunari teaching construct — the same technology as the split itself. Nobody has ever been at risk in this room. **The Pindarians could not build a version of this test that was honest and also safe,** so they built one that was safe and lied about it, and they wrote that decision down, and it troubled them.
- **L20 — The Grille** (*automatic*). The only place in Ilyr-Ameul where the two perspectives can speak plainly. It is in this room specifically, and that is a design choice, and the party should notice it.

### Plot Hooks

- Whatever a character says through the grille before pulling is the most quotable line the campaign will produce. Write it down.
- A party that refuses and takes the nine-day route arrives at I7 having failed the trial and knowing exactly why they failed it, and Vaelis and Vaelith will respect that far more than most tables expect.

### Resolution, Rewards, and Temptation

**-2 Discord and one Concord Token** per character on completion. **Desynchronisation at 6 Discord ends here.**

**+2 Discord** for coercion, and a locked room.

**No treasure.** The eight left 900 gp and eight sets of very old rations, and taking it feels wrong and is not penalised.

## I7. The Concordant Nail

*Nail chamber, 80 ft. diameter, domed | Both accounts, overlapping and visibly straining | Exits: the trust chamber, the ravine road | Smells of hot and cold iron together*

> Two guardians speak at once, and both of them are nine feet tall, and both of them are the same person.
>
> They stand on either side of a double-bladed spear driven point-down through a ring of light in the floor. One demands caution. The other demands violence. They are talking over each other and they are both talking to you.
>
> Halfway through the argument, without pausing, without a step, **they exchange positions.** The one demanding caution is now on the left and is now demanding violence, and neither of them has stopped mid-sentence, and neither sentence has changed.

### What Is in This Room

1. **Vaelis and Vaelith**, the Concordant Twins, arguing, exchanging, arguing.
2. **The Two That Are One**, a double-bladed spear driven point-down through a ring of light set into the floor.
3. **The ring**, with a spear-shaped absence at its centre, and the shaft occupying it.
4. **The dome**, painted in two schemes that occupy the same plaster: a sun map and a moon map, complete, neither obscuring the other.
5. **A broom**, propped against the wall behind them. Ordinary. Well-used. *Perception DC 10.*
6. **Forty feet of empty shelving** replicated in miniature around the chamber's rim — a model of the Doll Archive's empty section, made by hand, at one-twelfth scale, and there are **two hundred and eleven tiny unfinished dolls** stacked beside it.
7. **The Nail shaft**, below the ring, dropping out of sight, with the red-gold seam light of a working anchor coming up it.

### Setting the Scene

The exchange is the whole first impression and it should happen three or four times before anyone speaks to them. Mid-sentence. No movement. No transition. The argument continues perfectly.

Then let a player try to address one of them specifically and discover they cannot reliably tell which is which, and that the twins do not find this remarkable.

### DM Information

**The twins test whether the party chooses a faction.**

Everything about the encounter invites it. One argues for caution and one for violence; they are each persuasive; they will each take a character aside and make a private case; and **choosing either of them is +2 Discord and the wrong answer.**

**They release the relic only when two characters independently state why they trust the other.** Not why the *twins* should trust each other. Why **this character trusts that character** — specifically, concretely, and about something real that happened in this pyramid.

Two characters. Independently. Out loud. That is the entire condition and it is achievable in five minutes of good roleplay by a party that has spent three sessions learning to work split.

**They are one person.** *Insight DC 18*, or simply asking: Vaelis and Vaelith are not twins and never were. They are a single Pindarian custodian — the mender from I4, the sweeper from I3 — who has been holding the two accounts of this pyramid apart **by being both of them**, continuously, for four thousand years.

The exchange of positions is not a trick. It is what happens when one mind holding two perspectives loses track of which one is currently on the left.

**The Ilyri.** If the party asks about the empty shelf, they answer, and it is the campaign's biggest single piece of lore, and they give it plainly:

> *"We were the fourth. The Prohibition needed three traditions to build and a fourth to be the thing it was built out of. Sun gave the power and Moon gave the adaptation and Void gave the boundary, and Ilyr gave the — "*
> *" — material. One million, one hundred and four thousand people. It was a vote. It was an actual vote, and it carried, and I am the remainder."*

They are the last Ilyri. They have been mending dolls of everyone else for four thousand years and have not yet started on their own people's shelf.

### Mechanics

**The Statements (Roleplay, no check).** Two characters, independently, state why they trust another character. **-1 Discord each.** The twins step back from the ring.

**Choosing a Faction.** Any character who sides with one twin against the other: **+2 Discord**, and both twins stop talking, which is worse than the argument.

**If Combat Happens** — because the party attacked, or reached 6 Discord and never resolved it, or chose a faction three times:

**Vaelis and Vaelith** (CR 10 each; AC 18, and they **share a single hit point pool of 136**, Legendary Resistance 3/day shared, *Concordant Exchange* as a free action at will to swap positions, three attacks each round split between them as they choose).

- They never both act on the same initiative count; they act on **two** counts and each acts as half a creature.
- Damage to either reduces the shared pool.
- **Effects that target one apply to both.** A *hold person* on Vaelis holds Vaelith, forty feet away.
- At half the shared pool, they **stop exchanging** and one of them says, in both voices at once: *"We are going to lose track."*

**They will not kill anyone**, and they surrender the moment two characters do the thing described above, even mid-combat, even at 1 hit point.

**Lawful release requires two creatures to lift the relic together.** Two characters, four hands, one spear, at the same moment. There is no check. A single character cannot draw it at all — the shaft does not move, at any Strength, by any means.

**-4 Discord. Relic Attention +1.**

**Improper removal** is not available. There is no forcing this one. A party that kills the twins and cannot find two people willing to lift it together **leaves without the relic**, and Ilyr-Ameul stays sealed, and the load never redistributes, and that is a real and permanent campaign outcome.

### Creatures and NPCs

**Vaelis and Vaelith, the Concordant Twins.** One Ilyri custodian, four thousand years into holding a building together by disagreeing with themselves on schedule.

**Their regret:** they voted yes. They were part of the census that carried the motion, and they argued for it — one of them did, which is to say they did — and then they were the remainder, and they have had four thousand years to decide whether the argument they made was a good one.

**Their surrender condition:** seeing two people who can disagree completely and trust each other anyway. That is all. They have not seen it done since the vote.

**What they will do afterwards:** ask, very carefully, whether the party would be willing to help them start the Ilyri shelf. It is four thousand and six dolls behind. They have the thread.

### Set Piece

**"Which of you is which?"**

Ask it, or let a player ask it, and watch them try to answer.

They look at each other. Then they look at their own hands. Then one of them says *"I am Vaelis,"* and the other says *"I am Vaelis,"* at the same moment, and there is a pause of about two seconds that is the longest silence in the pyramid.

And then, quietly, in one voice: *"It has been a while since anyone asked."*

### Lore Drops

- **L21 — One Person** (*Insight DC 18, or ask*). Not twins. One Ilyri custodian holding both accounts apart by inhabiting both. The exchange is a slip, not a technique.
- **L22 — The Fourth Children** (*ask about the empty shelf*). **The Ilyri were the material.** One million, one hundred and four thousand people, by a vote that carried, converted into the substance of the Ninefold Prohibition in year 0.
- **L23 — The Vote** (*Insight DC 16 while they explain*). They argued **for** it. They have not decided whether they were right, and they will say so, and they will not accept comfort about it.
- **L24 — The Model Shelf** (*automatic*). A one-twelfth-scale replica of the empty section, and two hundred and eleven tiny unfinished dolls. They have been practising. For four thousand years they have been practising on everyone else's people because they cannot yet face starting their own.
- **L25 — The Widening Seam, Answered** (*from them, if the party raises L3*). They know. They have known for two centuries. When the accounts finally separate they will be in one of them, and the other half of them will be in the other, and neither half will remember there was ever a second one. **That is what they are afraid of, and it is not death.**

### Plot Hooks

- The Ilyri revelation reframes the entire campaign. The barrier is not made of magic. It is made of people, by consent, and there are eight more anchors.
- Vaelis and Vaelith released can leave. Whether one person who has been two for four thousand years can survive becoming one again is an open question and a superb ongoing NPC.
- The Ilyri shelf is four thousand and six dolls of work and the party can help with it between pyramids. Let them.
- Somebody else may know about the vote. Kathandar was there. Hestra Vaun at Khal'Vethar certainly was.

### Resolution, Rewards, and Temptation

**On lawful release:** the spear is lifted by two people together, the ring closes, and the load redistributes. **-4 Discord. Relic Attention +1.** Every character who spoke a reason for trust gains a permanent **Concord Token** that does not dissolve on leaving.

**Treasure.** The Two That Are One (artifact; see *The Nine Demon Relics*). The broom. The model shelf, if they are willing to part with it, which they are not.

**If the party cannot find two people to lift it:** they leave empty-handed, and Ilyr-Ameul remains sealed, and everything downstream of this anchor stays where it is.

## Ilyr-Ameul Aftermath

When The Two That Are One is removed lawfully, describe a deep vibration through the remaining pyramids. The ravine's split resolves: within a day, one cliff is in morning light and the other in afternoon light, like an ordinary valley, and it is the least dramatic aftermath in the campaign and the most unnerving.

**Immediate consequences.**

- **Relic Attention +1.** The spear has two minds. Aameul is impulsive and proud, Hethradiah calculating and contemptuous, and they agree only when violence validates both. The party now travels with an argument.
- **The perspectives collapse.** Every split ends. Characters who spent three sessions unable to verify each other can now do so freely, and at least one of them will find that harder than the split was.
- **The Ilyri are now known.** The party is carrying the fact that the Ninefold Prohibition is made of a million people who voted for it. What they do with that — tell the other guardians, tell Wahyrst, tell nobody — is the biggest open decision in the campaign so far.
- **Vaelis and Vaelith age normally or dissolve by choice**, and either way they have to decide first whether to do it as one person or two, and they will ask the party what they think.
- **Vharo Sellick has measured a third socket.** He is on the daylight cliff with his theodolite, and when the split resolves he stands up, folds his chair, and leaves without saying anything, because he now has the spacing of three anchors and can calculate the rest.
