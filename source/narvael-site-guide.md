# Nar'Vael: Black Sun Pyramid - Site Guide

*Levels 16-18 | Children of the Void | Deception into Truth | Relic: The Honest Lie | Guardian: High Scribe Irio Venn, Keeper of the Unaltered Word*

In the Astral Sea, orbiting a star that emits darkness and casts a bright shadow.

## Running Nar'Vael

Every illusion here contains one usable truth. Every offered fact contains one lie.

The pyramid does not reward guessing correctly. It rewards **calibrated uncertainty** — knowing *why* you believe something, and how strongly, and being willing to say so out loud before you find out whether you were right. A party that is confidently wrong does worse than a party that is uncertainly wrong, and both do worse than a party that says *"I think it's the third one, about seventy per cent, because of the dates."*

This is the most unusual pyramid to run in the campaign because the change is in **your narration**, not in the dungeon. From N1 onward you are labelling everything you say.

### Pindarian Scale

**Pindarians are Large.** All of them, always — the Solaari, the Lunari, the Veylari, the Ilyri, every
Saharim guardian and every soldier in every rank. Eight to ten feet, long-boned, and long-lived, with a
reach and a stride to match: **10-foot reach on their melee attacks and a 10-foot space.**

This is not a variant or a subtype. It is what the species is, and it is worth showing rather than stating —
Pindarian doorways are twelve feet high, their stairs have deep treads, their chairs do not fit anyone else,
and a party of Medium adventurers spends nine pyramids using furniture built for people half again their
size.

Human, elven and dwarven NPCs met in and around the sites are Medium as usual. The contrast is the point.

### The Labelling Law

**Every description you give is tagged Observation, Memory, or Claim.** Say the tag out loud. Put it at the front.

| Tag | Means | Reliability |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Observation** | A character is perceiving it right now, with their own senses | Always true, though not always complete |
| **Memory** | A character is recalling it, or an NPC is recounting it | May have been altered. Inside Nar'Vael, some have been. |
| **Claim** | Someone or something is asserting it — an inscription, an NPC, a mural, a door | **Contains at least one falsehood unless Irio wrote it** |

In practice:

> *"**Observation:** the corridor runs forty feet and ends at a door. **Claim:** the plaque beside it says the room beyond is a records office and has never been entered. **Memory:** Kessa is fairly sure she has been in this corridor before, from the other end."*

It is clunky for about ten minutes and then it is the best narration tool in the campaign, and players start using it themselves.

**Two rules that make it work:**

1. **Observations are never false.** Not once, not for a twist, not for a good reason. If you break this, the whole system collapses and the players stop listening to tags.
2. **Every deliberate illusion preserves one true sensory detail.** When you describe an illusion, one Observation-tagged element of it is real, and a party that finds it has found the thread.

### The Certainty Track

Visible, 0 to 6. It measures **overconfidence**, not error.

| Event | Certainty |
| --- | --- |
| Asserting a Claim as fact, aloud, without evidence | +1 each |
| Acting on an unverified Claim when verification was available | +1 |
| Taking an omen at the Observatory without cross-checking (N5) | +1 each |
| A confident guess rejected by the Door (N3) | +1 each |
| Killing a real ally at the False Party (N4) | +2 |
| Attacking Irio (N6) | +2 |
| Stating a confidence level honestly before a check | -1 each, once per area |
| Correctly identifying a Claim's false clause | -1 each |
| Admitting uncertainty to the Door (N3) | -1 each |
| Cross-checking an omen against the Histories (N5) | -1 |
| Naming the belief the pyramid made you reject (N6) | -2 |
| Lawful release (N7) | -4 |

**At 3.** The pyramid begins editing. Once per area, **one Memory-tagged detail a player has previously established about their own character becomes false** — not removed, *false*, and the character still remembers it clearly. Tell the player privately, on a note. They may play it either way.

**At 6.** Observations start carrying Claims. From this point you may tag a description **Observation/Claim**, and the party can no longer trust their own senses, and the only thing in the pyramid that is still reliable is Irio Venn, who they have not met yet.

### Confidence and Evidence

**A character who states confidence and evidence may reroll one failed Investigation or Insight check per area.**

The statement has to be real: *"I'm about sixty per cent on this, because the mural's armour matches the dates on the dock plaque."* Not *"I'm confident."* Not *"I have a hunch."* Percentage or plain-language equivalent, plus the specific thing it rests on.

**-1 Certainty** the first time each character does this in each area, whether or not the reroll helps.

### The Ink That Cannot Lie

Irio's writing is exempt from everything. **Anything in his hand is true.** The nine contradictory invitations the party received are all in that ink, and they are all true, and working out how is a genuine puzzle with a clean answer — see N6.

Give the players the invitations as a physical handout if you can. Nine short notes, mutually exclusive, all signed.

### Pacing and Shape

Seven areas, linear, two to three sessions. N2 and N5 interlock and a party that does N5 first will struggle; let them, and let the cross-check in N5 send them back.

Resting is safe. Nothing here attacks a sleeping party. Several things watch.

### Safety at This Table

Deception, gaslighting-adjacent content, and — at Certainty 3 — the GM telling a player that something their character remembers is false. That last one is the sharp edge and it wants naming during lines and veils.

Three protections you hold:

- **Observations are never false.** The players always have one reliable channel and they know which.
- **The false-memory edits are given privately, in writing, and the player decides how to play them.** You never play their character for them and you never tell the table.
- **Nothing here makes a player wrong for being uncertain.** Uncertainty is the winning move and the site says so in N3.

### Continuity

**Vharo Sellick is not here and his employer is.** Not in person — in a lens, in N5, for about four seconds, and the party will recognise the six fingers on the wrong hand.

**The First Hunger is visible from N5**, outside causality, and this is the only place in the campaign where anyone can look directly at it.

**The Ilyri question has a document.** Three guardians have now confirmed the vote from memory. Irio Venn has **the minutes**, in his hand, in ink that cannot lie, and he will read them aloud if the party spends a question on it.

## N1. The Bright Shadow Dock

*Astral approach and docking spar | Black star, white shadow | Exits: the astral crossing, the pyramid's mouth | No smell; there is no air until the mouth*

> The obsidian pyramid eclipses a black star. Light does not come out of that star — **darkness** does, in a slow steady radiance, and the pyramid's shadow across it is a bridge of white light a mile long.
>
> Your vessel casts no shadow at all.

### What Is in This Room

1. **The black star**, emitting darkness, and casting a bright shadow. *Observation.*
2. **The bright-shadow bridge**, a mile of white light between the party's approach and the docking spar. *Observation.*
3. **The docking spar**, obsidian, with mooring rings and a plaque. *Observation.*
4. **The plaque**, in Pindarian: *"THE STAR IS HARMLESS. MOOR IN ITS LIGHT."* **Claim.** It is false in a specific and survivable way.
5. **Sera Ondt**, an astral navigator, moored already, sitting on her own spar with her legs over the side. *Observation.* She has been here four times.
6. **Nine wrecks** on the rocks below the spar, all of them having circled and run out of everything. *Observation.*
7. **Astral predators**, three of them, circling the bright shadow at a distance. *Observation.*

### Setting the Scene

Open with the star and let the players sit with it: a star that radiates darkness, and a shadow that is the only light for a mile.

Then have somebody try to navigate by starlight and start circling.

Then start labelling. The first time you say **"Claim: the plaque says..."** a player will ask what that means and you explain the law in two sentences and never explain it again.

### DM Information

**Crossing requires steering into the bright shadow.** A navigator who follows visible starlight circles forever — literally forever, with no damage, no timer, and no way to notice from inside the loop except that the wrecks below have been circling for four thousand years.

**Three DC 18 Arcana, Vehicles, or Survival successes dock safely.** Each **failure attracts an astral predator**, which arrives in 1d4 rounds.

**The plaque's false clause** is *"THE STAR IS HARMLESS."* It is not. Its light — the darkness it emits — **deals psychic rather than radiant damage**, which means every protection a level 17 party has prepared for a star is wrong, and the correct precaution is a mind-shield rather than a sun-shade.

A character who reads the plaque and **states what they think is false about it, with reasoning**, before docking, gets it: *Insight or Arcana DC 16* confirms that the second clause is accurate and the first is not.

**Sera Ondt** is the pyramid's tutorial and the campaign's clearest demonstration of what Nar'Vael does to people, and she is completely functional and completely reliable and one thing about her is untrue.

### Mechanics

**The Crossing (three successes, DC 18).** Arcana, Vehicles (water or space), or Survival. Steering into the bright shadow is the required declaration; a party that does not declare it cannot succeed regardless of rolls.

**Each failure:** one **astral predator** (use **Astral Dreadnought** scaled down, or a CR 13 hunter of your choice) arrives in 1d4 rounds. Fighting one costs resources and no progress.

**The Plaque (Insight or Arcana DC 16).** Identifies which clause is false. **-1 Certainty** for the character who names it and says why.

**Star-Light Exposure.** Any creature that spends a round in the star's direct radiance without cover takes **27 (6d8) psychic** damage, DC 18 Wisdom for half. Radiant protections do nothing. Psychic resistance halves it.

**The Confidence Reroll.** Available here as everywhere: state a confidence level and its evidence, reroll one failed Investigation or Insight, **-1 Certainty** once per character per area.

### Creatures and NPCs

**Sera Ondt**, astral navigator (use **Veteran** with Vehicles +9, Survival +7, Insight +6). Human, forty, weathered, and extremely good at her job. Four crossings, four safe docks, and she will pilot the party in for nothing if they ask.

**What she will tell them**, unprompted, within the first minute, because she has learned to say it up front:

> *"I have a sister called Ilva. She taught me to sail. She lives in Tarsh and she has a bad knee and she takes sugar in everything and I can tell you what her hands look like.*
>
> *"She does not exist. She has never existed. I checked. There is no Ilva, there is no record, my mother had one child, and I remember twenty-nine years of her.*
>
> *"I came out of that building with her. So when I tell you something, ask me how I know it, because sometimes I do not have an answer and I say it exactly the same way."*

**She is the site's thesis, walking.** She is not damaged and not unreliable — she is **calibrated**, and she is the only NPC in the campaign who volunteers her own confidence level before anyone asks.

She will not come inside. She will wait on the spar for as long as they are in there, and she has waited for parties before, and none of them have come back out, and she says that too.

### Set Piece

**"Ask me how I know it."**

Have her deliver the Ilva speech flat, without self-pity, while coiling a line. Then have her finish the coil and look up and say the last sentence.

Then tag it: **"Memory."**

### Lore Drops

- **L1 — The Bright Shadow** (*Arcana DC 16*). *Observation:* the shadow is the only light for a mile. *Arcana:* the star's darkness is a **medium**, not an absence — it propagates, refracts and casts, and the pyramid's shadow in it is therefore bright. **Nar'Vael is not in an unusual place. It has inverted its own local physics and is using the star as a demonstration.**
- **L2 — The Nine Wrecks** (*Investigation DC 14*). All nine ran out of supplies while circling. *Investigation DC 18:* their logs are intact and every one of them records a successful approach, in detail, for weeks. **They believed they had docked.**
- **L3 — The Plaque's Lie** (*Insight or Arcana DC 16*). First clause false, second true. And the second clause is the useful one, and following it correctly requires disbelieving the first, and this is the whole pyramid in eleven words of Pindarian.
- **L4 — Sera** (*freely*). An accurate, complete, twenty-nine-year memory of a person who never existed.

### Plot Hooks

- Sera on the spar is an extraction plan and a deadline: she will wait, and she has waited before, and the party knows what happened to the last ones.
- The nine wrecks' logs are nine detailed accounts of a thing that did not happen, written by competent people. A party that reads one is reading their own possible future.
- Ilva is findable. Not as a person — as a **record**, in N6, in Irio's hand, and what it says is worse than nothing.

### Resolution, Rewards, and Temptation

**-1 Certainty** for naming the plaque's false clause; **-1** for the first honest confidence statement.

**Treasure.** The nine wrecks hold 4,000 gp in salvage, three *potions of superior healing*, and nine ships' logs that are the most valuable documents on the dock and worth nothing to anyone but a scholar of this pyramid.

## N2. The Nine Histories

*Mural hall, 200 ft. long, nine bays | Even white light from the walls themselves | Exits: the dock mouth, the questioning door | Smells of dry pigment*

> Nine murals show Pindaria's fall.
>
> In one, dragons kneel. In another, demons wear Pindarian faces and Pindarians wear demon faces and the battle lines are drawn between colleagues. In a third, there was never a war at all.
>
> All nine are signed. All nine are dated. All nine are in the same hand.

### What Is in This Room

1. **Nine murals**, each 20 feet by 15, each a complete account of the fall of Pindaria. **Claim**, all of them.
2. **A dedication plate** under each, with a date, a signature, and a one-line summary. **Claim.**
3. **A timeline band** running the length of the hall above the murals, marked in Pindarian years with event notches and no text. **Observation** — it is a measuring instrument, not an account.
4. **Eight murals contain two lies and one truth. One is accurate throughout.**
5. **Pigment residue** on the floor under three of them. *Observation, Investigation DC 12.*
6. **A viewing bench** in the centre of the hall, facing bay five. Worn.

### Setting the Scene

Walk them down the hall and describe each mural in two or three sentences, tagged **Claim**, without commentary.

Then the timeline band above, tagged **Observation**, and let somebody realise that the band is the only thing in the room that is not making an argument.

### DM Information

**Three DC 18 History, Investigation, or Insight successes identify the true mural.** But the checks are the backstop, not the method. **The real solution is cross-referencing the murals against the timeline band**, which is an Observation and therefore reliable, and against each other.

**The accurate account reveals that Graz'zt engineered a war intended to trap his rivals** — the invasion of Elderon was not an invasion. It was a **container**, built to hold several Abyssal rivals in one place under one lid, and the Pindarians built the lid, and Graz'zt let them, and the Ninefold Prohibition is doing exactly what he wanted it to do and has been for four thousand years.

That is the largest single reveal in the campaign and it sits in a mural in bay six.

### The Nine Murals

Give this table to the players as a handout with the summaries and let them work.

| Bay | Account | The two lies | The truth |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **1** | Dragons knelt and swore to the Pindarians, and the war was won in the air | No dragon force existed; the war was not won | **Kathandar was a dragonlord and did swear** |
| **2** | Demons wore Pindarian faces and the war was fought between colleagues | The faces were not worn; it was not primarily internal | **There were infiltrators, and eleven were never identified** |
| **3** | There was never a war; the Prohibition was a public-works project | There was a war; it was not public | **The Prohibition was planned before the invasion began** |
| **4** | The Sun tradition carried the working alone | Three traditions were required; Sun did not lead it | **The Solaari provided the energy and lost the most people doing it** |
| **5** | The Ilyri volunteered as one body in a single hour of acclaim | It was a vote over nine days, not an hour; it was not unanimous | **They volunteered** |
| **6** | The invasion was engineered by one Abyssal prince to trap his rivals inside a lid built by mortals | *(none)* | **All of it** |
| **7** | The First Hunger arrived with the demons and was driven off | It did not arrive with them; it was not driven off | **It was present** |
| **8** | Nine anchors were chosen for their geography | Geography was irrelevant; they were not chosen | **There are nine** |
| **9** | The Prohibition was completed and the work was finished | It was completed; the work was not finished | **It was completed** |

**Bay 6 is accurate throughout.** It is also the least dramatic mural — a small, flat, administrative composition of a seated figure signing something — and parties will dismiss it.

### The Timeline Band

**Observation.** Notches, no text. Cross-referencing the murals against it eliminates murals 1, 3, 5, and 9 immediately:

- A notch **nine days wide** where mural 5 claims one hour.
- A notch **before** the invasion notch where mural 3 claims no war and mural 9 claims completion.
- **No notch at all** in the air-war period mural 1 depends on.

That is three eliminations from one Observation and no rolls, and a party that uses it has understood the pyramid.

### Mechanics

**Identifying the True Mural (three successes, DC 18).** History, Investigation or Insight. **Using the timeline band grants advantage** on all three.

**-1 Certainty** per false clause a character correctly names with reasoning. There are sixteen available. A thorough party can walk out of this room at Certainty 0 permanently.

**+1 Certainty** per mural asserted as true without evidence.

**The Pigment Residue (Investigation DC 12).** Under bays 2, 5 and 7. *Investigation DC 16:* those three have been **repainted**, once each, in the last thousand years, and the repainting is in a different hand from the original and it is not Irio's.

**Somebody has been editing the histories, recently, and Irio either does not know or cannot stop them.**

### Creatures and NPCs

**None.** The hall is empty and stays empty. A party that expects an ambush in a room full of murals is going to be tense for twenty minutes and nothing will happen, and that is a legitimate and useful thing to do to a level 17 party.

### Set Piece

**Bay six.**

After eight murals of dragons and burning cities and demons in Pindarian faces, bay six is a small flat picture of a man at a desk, signing something, with a clerk beside him.

It is badly composed. It is the least interesting thing in the hall. There is a bench in front of it and the bench is worn.

Somebody has been sitting in front of the true one for four thousand years.

### Lore Drops

- **L5 — The Container** (*identify bay 6*). The invasion was a device for trapping Abyssal rivals under a mortal-built lid. Graz'zt engineered it. **The Ninefold Prohibition is working as designed, and the design was not Pindaria's.**
- **L6 — The Eleven Infiltrators** (*bay 2's truth*). Eleven were never identified. *History DC 18:* eleven is a recurring number in this campaign — the eleven marks on Aul's badge, the eleven cradles at Myr'Velora, the eleven staffs at Gor-Mazaal. **Note it and do not explain it.**
- **L7 — The Repainting** (*Investigation DC 16*). Bays 2, 5 and 7, within the last thousand years, in a hand that is not Irio's. The three repainted murals are the ones about **infiltrators, the Ilyri vote, and the First Hunger.**
- **L8 — The Bench** (*automatic*). Worn, facing the accurate one.

### Plot Hooks

- L5 recontextualises the entire campaign. Releasing the relics is dismantling a prison that a demon prince built on purpose, and the party is doing it, and he has been paying a broker to measure the sockets.
- Somebody is editing these murals. They are still doing it. They chose the three most dangerous subjects.
- The eleven infiltrators were never identified and the Saharim took the Watch in year 0 and nobody has ever counted them against that list.

### Resolution, Rewards, and Temptation

**-1 Certainty** per correctly named false clause, up to sixteen. **+1** per unevidenced assertion.

**No treasure.** The murals cannot be removed and nobody would want bay six.

## N3. The Door That Questions

*Antechamber, 30 ft. square | White light | Exits: the mural hall, the door | Smells of nothing*

> A mouth opens in the wall. It is the wall — no face around it, no eyes, just a mouth about four feet across at chest height.
>
> **"Answer truly,"** it says, **"or answer falsely. I care only whether you know which you chose."**

### What Is in This Room

1. **The mouth**, in the wall, patient, and possessed of a slightly bored courtesy.
2. **The door** beside it, shut. **Observation:** it has no handle, no hinge, and no lock.
3. **A floor worn in two places** — directly in front of the mouth, and about six feet back. *Observation, Perception DC 10.* People stand, then step back to think, then stand again.
4. **Nothing else.**

### Setting the Scene

Short, austere, and conversational. The mouth is not menacing. It sounds like a clerk on the last hour of a shift who has heard everything.

### DM Information

**Ask each character one verifiable question and one ambiguous question.**

- **Verifiable:** something with a fact of the matter that the character may or may not know. *"How many people were in the room when your mentor died?"* *"What is the name of the street your family lived on?"* *"Is your brother still alive?"*
- **Ambiguous:** something with no fact of the matter at all. *"Did you do the right thing at Astra?"* *"Does the person you left behind think about you?"* *"Will you use the relic?"*

**The door opens for a stated answer plus an honest confidence level.**

- *"Four. Certain."* → passes if the character genuinely is certain.
- *"Four. I think — maybe sixty per cent, it was dark."* → passes.
- *"I don't know."* → **passes.**
- *"Four."* delivered confidently by a character who is guessing → **rejected.**

**The door cannot detect truth. It detects self-deception.** A character can lie to it freely, deliberately, and pass, as long as they know they are lying: *"Seven. That's a lie and I know it is."* opens the door instantly and the mouth sounds pleased.

**A confident guess is rejected.** **+1 Certainty.** The character may try again immediately with a corrected confidence level and the door bears no grudge.

**An admitted uncertainty passes.** **-1 Certainty.**

**The ambiguous question has no right answer and the door knows it.** The only failing answer is a confident one. A character who says *"I think it was right. I am not sure. I have stopped being sure"* passes comfortably; one who says *"Yes"* flatly does not, and the mouth says *"You are not."*

### Mechanics

**No checks.** None at all. Do not allow Deception, Insight, or Persuasion to be rolled against the door; it is not fooled by Deception and does not care.

**Each character answers two questions.** Each honest confidence statement: **-1 Certainty**, maximum -2 per character here.

**Each rejected confident guess: +1 Certainty**, and immediate retry allowed.

**Assisting.** A character may not answer for another and the door will not accept it. A character may **coach** — *"say how sure you actually are"* — and the door does not object, and this is how the party teaches each other the room.

### Creatures and NPCs

**The mouth.** Bored, courteous, and the single most reasonable entity in Nar'Vael. If asked about itself it says it has been asking these questions for four thousand years and that the answers have got worse, and if asked to elaborate it says *"People used to say they didn't know."*

### Set Piece

**The second worn patch.**

Six feet back from the mouth, a patch worn in the floor by people stepping away to think.

It is deeper than the one in front.

### Lore Drops

- **L9 — What the Door Measures** (*ask it*). Not truth. **Self-deception.** It says so unprompted if anyone asks and the party will not believe it until they test it, and testing it is the correct response.
- **L10 — "The Answers Have Got Worse"** (*ask it*). Four thousand years of increasing confidence and decreasing accuracy. *Insight DC 16:* it is not describing the world getting more dishonest. It is describing **people getting more certain**, which is a different and worse trend, and it has the numbers.
- **L11 — The Deeper Patch** (*Perception DC 10*). More time spent stepping back to think than standing and answering. That was true once. *Investigation DC 15:* the front patch is wearing faster now.

### Plot Hooks

- The ambiguous questions you ask are direct campaign hooks in the players' own mouths. Ask about things that will come up at Khal'Vethar.
- The mouth's numbers are a document and Irio has them.

### Resolution, Rewards, and Temptation

**-1 Certainty per honest confidence statement**, max -2 each. **+1** per confident guess.

**No treasure.** The door opens.

## N4. The False Party

*Divided chamber, two halves, 50 ft. each | White light in one half, no light in the other, and both are visible | Exits: back, forward, and a third that is not there | Smells of your companions*

> Your companions wait in the next room.
>
> One looks relieved. Another raises a weapon and shouts a warning you cannot hear through the glass.
>
> Behind you, those same companions are shouting at you not to go in.

### What Is in This Room

1. **The party**, on both sides. **Observation** in both cases, which is the problem, because Observations are never false and both of these are Observations.
2. **Verity Eaters**, impersonating absent characters — anyone who has died, been left behind, or is elsewhere in the campaign, plus duplicates of the present party.
3. **A glass divide**, transparent, soundproof, with no door. **Observation.**
4. **Sera Ondt**, on one side, who is on the docking spar and cannot be here. **Observation.**
5. **A dead ally of the party's**, on the other side, whoever that is in your campaign, looking well. **Observation.**

### Setting the Scene

**Hand out private notes.** One per player, each containing **one true and one false memory** about the events of the last hour, and do not indicate which is which.

Then describe both halves, both tagged **Observation**, and let them sit with the fact that the rule they have been relying on has just been used against them.

### DM Information

**Verity Eaters impersonate absent characters.** They are perfect, they have the memories, and **they register as real to every form of detection**, including *true seeing*, which shows a person.

**They cannot do two things:**

1. **Reproduce a newly created shared signal.** Anything the party invents *in this room* — a phrase, a gesture, a number sequence, a knock — cannot be copied, because the Eaters read memory and the signal does not exist in any memory yet. **This is the solution.** A party that establishes a challenge-and-response before entering has already won the room, and if they did that at any earlier point in the campaign, honour it.
2. **Accept uncertainty without trying to close it.** An Eater cannot let a question stand. Say *"I'm not sure which of you is real and I'm going to sit here and think about it,"* and a real companion sits down with you, and an Eater will **immediately** offer proof — evidence, a memory, a demonstration, an argument. It has to. The tell is not the content of the proof; it is that the proof arrives at all, unprompted, within a round.

**Killing a real ally here is reversible** — Nar'Vael is not in the business of permanent loss and Law 3 of the campaign's general structure does not apply, but the pyramid returns them within the hour, intact, unharmed, and confused.

**It gives the Honest Lie leverage during attunement.** Note it. The relic will bring it up, accurately, at the worst possible time, for the rest of the campaign.

**+2 Certainty** for killing a real ally.

### Mechanics

**The Notes.** One per player: two memories of the last hour, one true, one false. Do not reveal which. They resolve on lawful exit or at N6 if a question is spent.

**Verity Eaters** (CR 14 each; see *Original Creatures*). One per character. They do not open with violence and would prefer not to fight at all.

**Detection.** *True seeing*, *detect thoughts*, *zone of truth* and *divination* all confirm an Eater as the person it is copying. **Say so plainly** — do not fudge it, do not give a hint, do not let a 6th-level slot solve the room. The tools genuinely do not work and that is the room's entire premise.

**The Signal Solution (no check).** Anything invented in this room. It works absolutely and immediately and no roll is made.

**The Uncertainty Tell (no check).** Declare uncertainty and do nothing. Real companions tolerate it. Eaters produce proof within one round, unprompted. **-1 Certainty** to a character who identifies an Eater this way.

**Combat.** An Eater reduced to 0 dissolves into a page of written text describing the person it was copying, in a hand that is not Irio's — **the same hand as the repainting in N2.**

### Creatures and NPCs

**The Verity Eaters.** Not malicious. They are **drafts** — attempts at a person, written by something that is trying to get the person right, and they are very close, and they do not know they are drafts.

An Eater that is identified does not attack. It says *"Ah,"* and sits down, and asks what gave it away, and takes the answer seriously, and will be better next time.

**That is the horror and you should not underline it.**

### Set Piece

**The dead ally.**

Whoever the party lost — in Astra, in Wahyrst, at the Nail — is standing in the far half, looking well, looking relieved, with their hands up and empty.

**Observation.** They are there. That is what the party is seeing and the tag is honest.

They are a draft. They are a very good draft. And when they are identified they say *"Ah,"* and sit down, and ask what gave it away.

### Lore Drops

- **L12 — The Hand** (*a defeated Eater's page*). The same hand as the repainted murals in N2. **Something in Nar'Vael writes people**, and it has been editing the histories, and it is not the guardian.
- **L13 — What They Cannot Copy** (*automatic, on the signal solution*). Anything that did not exist before this room. They read memory. They cannot read the present.
- **L14 — The Notes** (*resolved at N6 or on exit*). Each character has been carrying one false memory of the last hour since they walked in, and several of them have acted on it.

### Plot Hooks

- The writer is still in the building. Irio has nine answers and one of them can be spent on this.
- An Eater that was identified and let go is still in there, getting better, and it will be in Khal'Vethar.
- Killing a real ally is a permanent conversational weapon in the Honest Lie's hands, and it will use it in front of people.

### Resolution, Rewards, and Temptation

**-1 Certainty** per Eater identified by the uncertainty tell. **+2** for killing a real ally.

**Treasure.** A defeated Eater's page is a complete written description of a person, accurate to the eyelash, in an unknown hand. There are as many as they killed.

## N5. The Unreliable Observatory

*Lens gallery, circular, 90 ft. across, nine mountings | Light from the lenses only | Exits: the divided chamber, the scriptorium | Smells of warm glass*

> Nine lenses frame nine versions of Elderon.
>
> Whole. Burning. Empty. Unborn. Devoured. And four you have no word for.
>
> Each becomes more convincing the longer you look at it.

### What Is in This Room

1. **Nine lenses** in brass mountings, each three feet across, each showing a version of Elderon. **Claim**, all nine, and the room does not pretend otherwise — there is a plate above the door that says so, in Irio's hand, and it is therefore true.
2. **Irio's plate**, above the door: *"NINE VIEWS. ONE IS A PROBABLE FUTURE. ONE IS NOT A VIEW OF ELDERON AT ALL. I HAVE NOT LABELLED THEM."* **True.**
3. **A cross-reference desk** with a copy of the Nine Histories' timeline band, in Irio's hand, and therefore reliable. *Observation.*
4. **Seat marks** in front of lens seven, deep. Somebody looks at that one.
5. **Lens nine**, which does not show Elderon.

### Setting the Scene

Let them look. Describe two or three lenses fully and let the players pick which ones to examine, and describe the convincingness — the longer you look, the more the details resolve, the more it stops being a picture.

Then have somebody look at lens nine.

### DM Information

**One image shows a probable future if a relic is stolen.** That is lens four: Elderon empty — not destroyed, not burning, **empty**, with cities intact and nothing in them, and no damage.

The party has seen that before. It is what the Last Specimen showed them at Myr'Velora, and it is what Aul described at Thaal-Uluun, and it is the First Hunger's result.

**Another shows the First Hunger outside causality.** That is lens nine, and it is not a view of Elderon, and looking at it is the most dangerous optional action in the campaign.

**A character can learn one useful omen but takes 4d8 psychic damage and gains a false supporting detail chosen by the GM.**

That last clause is the mechanic that makes this room work. Write the false detail down. It is specific, it is plausible, it supports the true omen, and it is wrong, and the character will cite it for the rest of the campaign.

**Careful cross-checking with the Nine Histories isolates the true omen.** A party that identified bay 6 in N2 and brings that knowledge here can check each lens against the accurate account, and the one that is consistent with bay 6 is lens four, and cross-checking **removes the false supporting detail** — they get the omen clean.

**-1 Certainty** for cross-checking. **+1** for taking an omen without it.

### The Nine Lenses

| Lens | Shows | Status |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Elderon whole, prosperous, the pyramids standing and tended | A past, not a future |
| 2 | Elderon burning, Abyssal armies in every city | A **false** future; contradicts bay 6 |
| 3 | Elderon unborn — the world before anything | A past |
| 4 | Elderon **empty**: cities intact, nothing in them, no damage | **The probable future if a relic is stolen** |
| 5 | Elderon devoured, a shell with the crust peeled | A false future; nothing peels |
| 6 | Elderon with ten pyramids | Not false. Not explained. **Do not explain it.** |
| 7 | A room in which the party is standing, now, looking at a lens | Accurate, live, and recursive |
| 8 | Elderon whole, with the sky the wrong colour and everyone content | A false future, and the most attractive one |
| 9 | **Not Elderon.** The First Hunger, outside causality | See below |

### Lens Nine

It does not show a place and it does not show a creature.

*Observation:* the lens is clear. There is nothing in it. It is a window onto nothing, and it is not dark, and it is not empty in the way a room is empty.

A character who looks for a full round understands, without any check, that **they are being looked back at, and that the thing looking has not started yet.** It is not in the past or the future or anywhere the party can point to. It has not begun and it is already finished and both of those are true and neither is a metaphor.

**Looking: 44 (8d10) psychic damage**, no save, and the character gains **one true and permanent piece of knowledge**, which you should give them privately on a card:

> *It does not want anything. It is not coming. It is where things go when they stop being complicated enough to be anywhere.*

**No false detail attaches to lens nine.** It is the one thing in Nar'Vael that cannot be lied about, because there is nothing there to lie about.

### Mechanics

**Examining a Lens (1 round).** Describe what it shows. No check.

**Taking an Omen (Arcana or Insight DC 18).** One per character, ever. Success: the omen is understood. **Always: 18 (4d8) psychic damage** and **one false supporting detail**, written down by you and given to the player on a card.

**Cross-Checking (Investigation DC 16, 20 minutes, requires bay 6 from N2).** Isolates the true omen and **removes the false detail**. **-1 Certainty.**

**Lens Nine (no check).** 44 (8d10) psychic, no save, and the card. Voluntary and never required and it should be very tempting.

**Lens Seven.** A character looking at lens seven sees themselves looking at lens seven, live. *Insight DC 18:* the reflection is delayed by about four seconds, and it is not a reflection, and it is **four seconds ahead.**

### Creatures and NPCs

**None.** The observatory is empty.

**Except:** in lens seven, for about four seconds, while the party is watching themselves — a figure crosses behind them, in the room, at the back. Six fingers on the left hand.

The party turns round. **Observation:** there is nobody there.

They were four seconds ahead.

### Set Piece

**The six-fingered man in lens seven.**

Play it exactly as above: the recursion, the four-second lead, the figure crossing behind them, and the empty room when they turn.

He was not there yet. He is going to be, or he was, or the lens is wrong, and there is no way to establish which, and the seat marks in front of lens seven are very deep.

### Lore Drops

- **L15 — Lens Four** (*cross-check*). Elderon empty, intact, undamaged. **The probable outcome if a relic is stolen**, and the party has now had this shown to them by a corpse, a soldier, and a lens.
- **L16 — Lens Nine** (*look*). It has not started. It is already finished. It does not want anything.
- **L17 — Lens Six** (*automatic*). **Ten pyramids.** The lens is not flagged as a false future by the cross-check, and the timeline band does not contradict it, and Irio's plate says one lens is not a view of Elderon and that is lens nine, not six.
- **L18 — The Seat Marks** (*Perception DC 12*). Deep, in front of lens seven, the recursive one. Somebody sits and watches themselves, four seconds behind, for a long time.

### Plot Hooks

- **Lens six is the campaign's best unanswered question** and you should leave it unanswered until you have decided what it means.
- The false supporting details are ticking bombs in the players' own notes, and they will cite them confidently in three sessions' time.
- Vharo's employer has been in this room, or will be.

### Resolution, Rewards, and Temptation

**-1 Certainty** for cross-checking; **+1** for an uncross-checked omen.

**Treasure.** The cross-reference desk holds Irio's copy of the timeline band, portable, reliable, and the single most useful document the party can carry into the last pyramid.

## N6. The Unaltered Word

*Scriptorium, 60 ft. by 40 ft. | Reading light, warm, from nowhere | Exits: the observatory, the Nail chamber | Smells of ink and paper and a little of tea*

> A thin man writes at a desk built for someone nine feet tall, while sentences crawl across the air around him and erase themselves.
>
> He does not look up until he finishes the line.
>
> **"I cannot promise safety,"** he says. **"I can promise precision."**

### What Is in This Room

1. **High Scribe Irio Venn**, at a desk, writing, in ink that cannot lie.
2. **Sentences in the air**, crawling, erasing themselves — every claim made anywhere in Nar'Vael, passing through this room and failing. *Observation.*
3. **The Unaltered Record**, a bound archive on shelves behind him: everything that is true, written by him, over four thousand years.
4. **Nine invitations** on a spike on the desk, in his hand, mutually contradictory, all true. The party's copies match.
5. **A second desk**, unoccupied, with a chair pulled out and work in progress on it — **in the hand from the repainted murals and the Eaters' pages.** *Observation, Perception DC 12.*
6. **Tea.** Made recently. Two cups.

### Setting the Scene

He is precise, courteous, and entirely without warmth or hostility. He answers what is asked. He does not volunteer.

Let him finish the line before he speaks. Then let the party realise there are two cups.

### DM Information

**Irio answers exactly nine questions and cannot lie.** Nine. Total. For the party, not per character. Count them aloud and visibly.

**He refuses compound questions.** *"Who is editing the murals and why?"* is two questions and he says so and it costs nothing. He is not being difficult; he is being exact, and the party will get better at it, and that is the room.

**He answers only what is asked.** *"Is the Prohibition working?"* gets *"Yes."* The party has burned a question. *"What is the Prohibition currently doing that it was not designed to do?"* gets a paragraph.

**He releases the relic when the party identifies one belief the pyramid made them reject despite sound evidence.**

Not a belief they got wrong. A belief they had **good reason for**, that Nar'Vael talked them out of, and that was correct. Every party will have one — a mural they dismissed, a companion they doubted, an omen they discounted, a plaque they over-corrected on. They have to find it, name it, and say what the evidence was.

**-2 Certainty** when they do.

### The Nine Invitations

All nine are true. The resolution is that each was written to a different reader — **to each of the nine possible parties that the timeline permitted** — and Irio wrote all nine and sent all nine, because he did not know which one would arrive and it would have been imprecise to guess.

If the party spends a question on it, that is the answer, and it is worth a question.

### The Second Desk

**Somebody else works here.** Chair out, work in progress, the same hand as the repainted murals and the Verity Eaters' pages.

If asked — and it is worth a question — Irio says:

> *"That is my assistant. It has worked here for eleven hundred years. It arrived able to write and unable to be precise, and it has been practising. It repaints the histories when it thinks it has improved them. I correct them afterwards. It is getting better and I have not decided whether that is good."*

**It is one of the eleven unidentified infiltrators from mural bay 2.** He knows. He has known for eleven hundred years. He has not reported it because nobody has asked him, and the party can ask, and it will cost a question.

### Mechanics

**Nine questions. No checks.** Do not allow Persuasion, Insight or Deception. He is not being fooled and he is not being convinced and nothing modifies his answers.

**Compound questions are refused** and do not count against the nine.

**Resolving the N4 notes** costs one question and he will tell every character which of their two memories is false.

**Resolving Sera's sister** costs one question. The answer is in the Unaltered Record and it is: *"Ilva Ondt was written. She is a draft that was not used. My assistant was practising and left her somewhere."*

**The Belief (Roleplay, no check).** Name it, with the evidence. **-2 Certainty.** The relic is released.

**If attacked:** he records the assault. **Written copies of his round appear** — each round of combat produces a written account of that round, which stands up and becomes a **Verity Eater** of whoever acted in it. He does not fight. He writes, accurately, and the party fights increasingly good drafts of themselves. **+2 Certainty**, and the room becomes unwinnable within six rounds, which is the point.

He will stop the moment anyone stops attacking, and he will not hold it against them, and he will say so, precisely.

### Creatures and NPCs

**High Scribe Irio Venn** (CR 15; see *Original Creatures*). Thin, exact, tired in the specific way of somebody who has been the only reliable thing in a building for four thousand years.

**His regret**, if a question is spent on it:

> *"I recorded the vote. All nine days of it. I have the minutes and they are accurate and they include every argument made on both sides.*
>
> *"I was asked, on the seventh day, whether the record would be read afterwards. I said I could not promise that. That was precise and it was true and it was not an answer, and eleven thousand people heard me say it, and some of them voted differently afterwards.*
>
> *"I have wondered since whether precision was the correct thing to offer them. I still do not have a better answer and I have had four thousand years, which suggests there is not one."*

**His surrender condition** is the belief. He has no other and he will wait.

### The Ilyri Minutes

If the party spends a question, he reads them. It takes four minutes and it is the campaign's definitive document: **one million, one hundred and four thousand people, nine days, a vote of 61 to 39 in favour, with the dissent recorded by name.**

Three guardians have now confirmed it from memory. This is the paper.

### Set Piece

**Two cups.**

Tea, made recently, two cups, one at his desk and one at the other.

The party will notice before they ask about the second desk. Let them.

### Lore Drops

- **L19 — The Assistant** (*one question*). Eleven hundred years, practising, improving, editing. One of eleven never-identified infiltrators. He has known and has not reported it and he is not sure whether it is still an infiltrator.
- **L20 — The Nine Invitations** (*one question*). Nine possible parties, nine letters, all sent, all true.
- **L21 — Ilva** (*one question*). A draft that was not used, left somewhere.
- **L22 — The Minutes** (*one question*). 61 to 39, nine days, dissent recorded by name.
- **L23 — His Regret** (*one question*). *"I have wondered since whether precision was the correct thing to offer them."*

### Plot Hooks

- The assistant is in the building and will be at Khal'Vethar and has been getting better for eleven hundred years.
- The dissent is recorded by name. Thirty-nine per cent of a million people said no and were overruled, and their names exist, and some of them are in the Black Catalogue.
- Nine questions is a resource the party will argue about for an hour and should.

### Resolution, Rewards, and Temptation

**-2 Certainty** for naming the belief. **+2** for attacking him.

**Treasure.** Nothing yet. The Unaltered Record cannot be removed. He will copy one page of it for them at N7.

## N7. The Black-Sun Nail

*Nail chamber, 50 ft. across | Light from the hole | Exits: the scriptorium, and out | Smells of hot glass*

> The rapier has no stable shape. The guard is a basket, then a cup, then a ring of thorns, then a basket again, and the blade is different lengths on consecutive glances.
>
> Only the hole it makes in reality remains consistent.

### What Is in This Room

1. **The Honest Lie**, a rapier with no stable form, point-down in the ring of light.
2. **The hole**, a rapier-shaped absence behind it that does not change when the blade does. **Observation**, and it is the only Observation in the room.
3. **The ring of light**, with the absence at its centre.
4. **Irio**, if the party asked him to come. He will. He brings a page.
5. **Nothing else**, and the room has nothing to say about itself.

### Setting the Scene

Very short. The party has done the work in N6; this is the payment and the last labelling joke.

Describe the rapier four times in four sentences, differently, all tagged **Observation**, all accurate.

### DM Information

**Lawful removal requires speaking a recovered truth while acknowledging the evidence for it.**

Both halves. *"Graz'zt built the war to trap his rivals"* is a recovered truth and is not enough. *"Graz'zt built the war to trap his rivals, and I believe it because bay six is the only mural the timeline band doesn't contradict, and because nobody repainted that one"* opens the ring.

**Any recovered truth works.** It does not have to be the big one. *"Sera's sister was written, and I believe it because Irio said so in ink that cannot lie"* is a complete and correct answer.

**-4 Certainty. Relic Attention +1.**

**Improper removal** — seizing it, or taking it without the statement — causes **identities, maps, and alliances within 100 miles to become unreliable for nine days.** In the Astral Sea that means the party's own vessel, their route home, Sera Ondt's memory of who they are, and any agreement they have made. **Relic Attention +2.**

**Irio gives the party a one-page Unaltered Record immune to the relic's lies.**

This is the most valuable object in the campaign. One page, in his hand, containing whatever the party asks him to put on it — **and they must decide what goes on it**, in front of him, and he will write exactly what they say and nothing more.

Let them draft it at the table. It is a ten-minute scene and it is the best one in the pyramid.

### Mechanics

**Lawful Release (Roleplay, no check).** A recovered truth plus its evidence, spoken aloud, by the character taking the rapier.

**Improper Removal.** 100-mile unreliability for nine days. **+3 Certainty. Relic Attention +2.** No page.

**The Page.** One side, Irio's hand, immune to the Honest Lie and to any effect that alters records. Whatever the party dictates. It cannot be altered, destroyed by mundane means, or disbelieved.

**Attunement.** The relic is brilliant, amused and compulsively dishonest even when truth would serve it better. Its first statement should be a small, charming, entirely unnecessary lie about something trivial and checkable, and a party that checks it has started correctly.

**If a real ally was killed at N4:** the relic knows, and it brings it up within the first hour, accurately, in company.

### Creatures and NPCs

**Irio**, with a pen.

### Set Piece

**The page.**

He sets it on the plinth, uncaps the ink, and says: *"What would you like to be true? I will not write it unless it is. But I will write anything that is."*

And then the party has to decide, out loud, what one page of guaranteed truth is for — and the obvious answers are all about the Prohibition, and the best answer somebody at the table will come up with is probably not.

### Lore Drops

- **L24 — The Consistent Hole** (*Observation*). The blade changes; the absence does not. *Arcana DC 18:* **the hole is the artifact.** The rapier is a shape the hole is currently wearing, and Fraz-Urb'luu's resonance is not in the metal, and destroying the blade would accomplish nothing at all.
- **L25 — The Page** (*on release*). One side, guaranteed, in a hand that cannot lie.

### Plot Hooks

- Whatever is on the page is the party's anchor for the rest of the campaign, and the Honest Lie will spend the endgame trying to make them doubt it, and it cannot, and that is the point of giving it to them before the last pyramid.
- The hole being the artifact means the destruction condition in the sourcebook is aimed at the wrong object, and somebody should work that out before they try it.

### Resolution, Rewards, and Temptation

**Lawful: -4 Certainty, Relic Attention +1**, and the Unaltered Record page.

**Improper: Relic Attention +2**, nine days of unreliable identity and geography for a hundred miles, and no page.

**Treasure.** The Honest Lie (artifact; see *The Nine Demon Relics*). One page.

## Nar'Vael Aftermath

When The Honest Lie is removed lawfully, describe a deep vibration through the remaining pyramids. The black star's output steadies — it still emits darkness, and the darkness stops propagating, and within a day the bright shadow is gone and the Astral Sea around Nar'Vael is simply dark and ordinary.

**Immediate consequences.**

- **Relic Attention +1.** The rapier is brilliant, amused, and lies compulsively even when the truth would be more useful. It is also excellent company and the party will like it, which is the problem.
- **Sera Ondt is still on the spar.** She waited. She is the first navigator in four thousand years to have a party come back out, and she will ask them about Ilva, and the party has an answer, and it is *"she was a draft that was not used."*
- **Irio ages normally or dissolves by choice.** If he stays he is the campaign's fact-checker and the only being in it who cannot lie, and every faction in Elderon will want him.
- **The assistant is unaccounted for.** The chair was out and the tea was made and nobody saw it, and it has been getting better for eleven hundred years, and Khal'Vethar is next.
- **Graz'zt's design is known.** The invasion was a container for his rivals, the Prohibition is the lid, the party has been removing the pins, and he has been paying a broker to measure the sockets. Everything from here reads differently.
- **One anchor remains.** Khal'Vethar must remain until every other load is transferred — the ninth goes last or the whole shell inverts — and the party now has eight.
