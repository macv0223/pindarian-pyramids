# Thaal-Uluun: Pyramid of Unmaking - Site Guide

*Levels 14-16 | Children of the Void | Dissolution into Identity | Relic: Mercy of Dissolution | Guardian: Aul Tareth, the Man Who Refused to Melt*

A coastal glass desert, created when stone forgot how to remain solid.

## Running Thaal-Uluun

Possessions, roles, bodies and memories are stripped in sequence, in that order, and the pyramid asks for an identity that survives change **without pretending that change never matters.**

The second half of that sentence is the difficulty. Thaal-Uluun is not impressed by a character who says *"I am the same person I always was."* It is also not impressed by one who says *"nothing about me is real."* It wants something harder: an account of yourself that acknowledges you have been altered and still claims responsibility for what happens next.

The site is structured as a controlled demolition of everything a level 15 party uses to answer the question *who are you*, taken one layer at a time: **gear, then competence, then body, then memory, then name.** Each layer comes off in its own keyed area and each one is survivable, and the party will get further than they expect and then be asked the question anyway.

### Pindarian Scale

**Pindarians are Large.** All of them, always — the Solaari, the Lunari, the Veylari, the Ilyri, every
Saharim guardian and every soldier in every rank. Eight to ten feet, long-boned, and long-lived, with a
reach and a stride to match: **10-foot reach on their melee attacks and a 10-foot space.**

This is not a variant or a subtype. It is what the species is, and it is worth showing rather than stating —
Pindarian doorways are twelve feet high, their stairs have deep treads, their chairs do not fit anyone else,
and a party of Medium adventurers spends nine pyramids using furniture built for people half again their
size.

Human, elven and dwarven NPCs met in and around the sites are Medium as usual. The contrast is the point.

### What the Stripping Does and Does Not Touch

**Read this before you run T2.** This is the single easiest room in the campaign to run in a way that ruins somebody's evening.

**Affected:**

- Weapons, armour, shields, tools, ammunition, mundane gear, consumables
- Magic items, wands, staffs, rods, wondrous items — *deposited safely or carried and degrading*
- Money, jewellery, trophies, keepsakes

**Not affected, ever, under any circumstance:**

- **Class features.** Rage, Sneak Attack, Channel Divinity, Ki, Wild Shape, Metamagic, all of it.
- **Spells known, spells prepared, and spell slots.** A wizard does not lose prepared spells. A cleric does not lose their list.
- **Spellcasting focus requirements and material components.** Both are **waived entirely inside Thaal-Uluun**, for everyone, including costly components under 500 gp. Say this out loud at T2.
- **Spellbooks**, provided they are deposited. A carried spellbook degrades like anything else and a wizard who loses one has lost something that is not replaceable, which is why the niches exist.
- **Familiars, companions, steeds, summons.** They are creatures.
- **Artifacts.** They whisper. They work at half charges.

The trial is about **objects**, not about switching classes off. A monk and a barbarian will breeze through T3 and a fighter will struggle, and that asymmetry is real and the room is written to compensate for it — see T3.

### The Laws

1. **Non-artifact equipment becomes inert after passing the Stripping Chamber.** Magic items stop functioning; mundane items become grey, heavy and brittle.
2. **Stored equipment is restored on lawful exit**, undamaged, fully charged, catalogued and returned.
3. **A creature reduced to 0 hit points becomes stable liquid rather than unconscious.** No death saves. An ally can re-form it with an action. It cannot die inside Thaal-Uluun by damage.
4. **Speaking a true identity statement grants advantage against an Unmade Duplicate**, once per character per statement, and the statement has to be new.

Law 3 is a gift and it changes how the whole site feels. **Nobody dies here.** Tell the players. The tension in Thaal-Uluun is not mortality; it is losing pieces of yourself and having to decide which ones were load-bearing.

### The Dissolution Track

Visible, 0 to 6. It measures how much the pyramid has had to take rather than been given.

| Event | Dissolution |
| --- | --- |
| Carrying a non-artifact item past the Stripping Chamber | +1 per character who does it |
| Destroying an item rather than surrendering it | +2 each |
| Forcing an obstacle in T3 by breaking it | +1 each |
| Killing an Unmade Duplicate in combat | +1 each |
| Being reduced to 0 hit points and liquefying | +1 each time |
| Defining another character's identity for them | +2 |
| Seizing the relic (T7) | +3 |
| Voluntarily depositing everything (T2) | -1 per character |
| Solving a T3 obstacle without tools | -1 each, once per obstacle |
| Defeating a Duplicate socially (T5) | -1 each |
| A sincere identity statement (T6) | -1 each |
| Accepting a Void Mark (T1) | -1 each |
| Lawful release (T7) | -4 |

**At 3.** The pyramid begins sampling the party directly. Once per hour, each character loses one **memory** — small, specific, and chosen by you from something the player has established: the name of a childhood street, what their mentor's voice sounded like, the ending of a song they know. It comes back on lawful exit. It does not come back otherwise.

**At 6.** The party begins losing cohesion. Characters reduced to 0 hit points **cannot be re-formed by an ally** and must be carried in a container until the site is resolved. The relic can still be released lawfully with a liquid party member sloshing in a helmet, and that is a legitimate and very memorable way to finish.

### Void Marks

At T1 the black wall samples every creature and takes one cosmetic feature. A character who **accepts** the change rather than resisting it gains a **Void Mark**.

A Void Mark can be spent to:

- **Automatically succeed** on one saving throw against a dissolution, acid, or shapechange effect, or
- **Re-form a liquefied ally as a bonus action** instead of an action, or
- **Gain advantage against an Unmade Duplicate**, stacking with Law 4.

Maximum three per character. They dissolve on lawful exit, which nobody minds by then.

### Pacing and Shape

Seven areas, linear, and the layers come off in order. Two sessions.

**Resting is safe** and restores hit points normally. It does not restore items and it does not restore memories.

### Safety at This Table

Identity loss, memory loss, and bodily dissolution. For most tables this is thrilling; for a player with experience of dementia in a family member, T6 in particular can land very hard, and it is worth naming *memory loss* specifically during lines and veils rather than the general body-horror heading.

Two structural protections, which you hold:

- **No player's answer at T6 is ever wrong.** There is no correct philosophy and the room says so. Repeating somebody else's answer fails; having an unusual one does not.
- **Nobody dies.** Law 3 is absolute and should be stated before T1.

### Continuity

**Vharo Sellick is being paid in a face.**

He said so at Gor-Mazaal and would not elaborate. The face is in a vat in T5, and it is not his, and it has been waiting for him for some time. Whether the party works that out, tells him, or takes it is entirely theirs.

**The First Hunger eats the record.** The party learned that at Myr'Velora — a coastline still there, cities still there, nothing in them, no damage. **Thaal-Uluun is where they learn the method.** Aul Tareth has seen it work and can describe it, and his description is the most frightening thing in the campaign so far, and it is four sentences long.

## T1. The Softstone Portal

*Freestanding portal in the glass desert, 30 ft. wide | Hard coastal daylight, glare off glass in every direction | Exits: the desert, the stripping hall | Smells of hot glass and salt*

> The black wall sags around the doorway like cooling wax. It is not falling; it has simply stopped insisting.
>
> Your reflection in the glass underfoot remains where it was for half a heartbeat after you move, and then catches up, and is not quite in a hurry about it.

### What Is in This Room

1. **The portal**, a doorway in a sagging black wall that extends about eighty feet either side and then stops, with nothing behind it but more desert.
2. **The glass desert**, horizon to horizon, smooth, with the shapes of buildings still visible in it at depth — walls, a stair, a cart — like things frozen in ice. *Perception DC 10.*
3. **Delayed reflections**, everywhere, on every glass surface, about half a heartbeat behind.
4. **Aul's letter**, if the party received it, still dripping through whatever they are carrying it in. It has been dripping for weeks and has not got smaller.
5. **A pile of rings**, about two hundred, loose on the glass beside the portal. Nobody put them there. *Investigation DC 12:* they are all wedding rings, and they are all slightly too large, and *Insight DC 14* suggests hands can be measured by what falls off them.
6. **Everyone's Invited**, if it is travelling with the party, which will begin calling something inside *"cousin"* and heading for the door.

### Setting the Scene

Open on the desert and the buildings visible at depth in the glass — a whole coastal town, still there, still shaped like a town, suspended.

Then the wall that sags, then the delay in the reflections, then let a player notice that their own reflection is not keeping up.

### DM Information

**The wall samples each creature.** No warning, no attack, no roll to notice.

**DC 17 Constitution saving throw** prevents the loss of **one cosmetic feature** until departure — hair colour, a freckle pattern, a scar, the exact shape of an ear, a birthmark, an accent. Ask the player which one it takes and let them choose; they know their character's features better than you do.

**A character who accepts the change** — declines the save, or fails and does not complain — **gains a Void Mark.**

Make the losses specific and personal and entirely cosmetic. Nothing mechanical. A character who loses their scar is not less anything; they are just a person whose reflection is now wrong in a way only they notice.

**The portal is harmless to a creature already amorphous.** An ooze, a druid in Wild Shape as something formless, a character under *Slime Passage*, or Everyone's Invited passes without being sampled and without a Void Mark, which is worth noting aloud so the player feels both the relief and the exclusion.

### Mechanics

**The Sampling (DC 17 Constitution save).** Success: nothing taken. Failure or voluntary acceptance: one cosmetic feature gone until lawful exit.

**Accepting Voluntarily.** **One Void Mark. -1 Dissolution.**

**The Rings (Investigation DC 12, Insight DC 14).** Two hundred, all slightly too large. Hands got thinner, or stopped being hands.

**The Buildings in the Glass (Perception DC 10, Arcana DC 16).** A coastal town, frozen mid-liquefaction. *Arcana DC 18:* the town did not melt in a disaster. It melted **slowly, over about nine days**, and the depth of the shapes shows which streets went first, and the sequence is not geographic — **it is by building age**. The newest went first.

### Creatures and NPCs

**None.** Thaal-Uluun does not have guards at the door; it has a wall that takes something small from everyone and then lets them in.

**Everyone's Invited**, if present, wants to go inside immediately and enthusiastically and will not be dissuaded, and it is correct that there is a cousin in there.

### Set Piece

**The reflection that does not catch up.**

Somewhere in this room, for one character, the half-heartbeat delay does not resolve. Their reflection stays where it was for two full seconds, looking at them, and then moves — and it does not move the way they moved, it moves the way they *were going to.*

Once. To one character. Never explain it and never do it again.

### Lore Drops

- **L1 — The Town in the Glass** (*Perception DC 10, then Arcana DC 18*). Nine days, newest buildings first. **Things that had been themselves for less time went first**, which is the site's thesis stated in geology before anyone has said a word about identity.
- **L2 — The Rings** (*Investigation DC 12*). Two hundred wedding rings, all loose. *Insight DC 14:* fingers thinned, or stopped.
- **L3 — Aul's Letter** (*if received*). Written in a Veylari officer's hand, sealed, and it has been dripping through every container for weeks without diminishing. The text is four lines and they are an invitation, and the last line is: *"Bring nothing you would not consent to become."*
- **L4 — The Half-Beat** (*automatic*). Reflections lag. *Arcana DC 15:* the delay is not optical. It is **ontological** — the glass is resolving what you are slightly after you do, and it is doing that because out here it still bothers to resolve it at all.

### Plot Hooks

- The town under the glass has a name and a population and both are findable, and by Myr'Velora's logic somebody should check whether anyone outside remembers it.
- Void Marks are the site's only currency and the party will be short of them by T5.
- The rings are two hundred people, and somebody at Vhal'Kathar could read their names.

### Resolution, Rewards, and Temptation

**One Void Mark and -1 Dissolution** per character who accepts the sampling.

**Treasure.** Two hundred wedding rings, worth 30 gp each, and taking them is not penalised and is not a good look.

## T2. The Stripping Chamber

*Hall of niches, 100 ft. by 60 ft., walls honeycombed | Grey even light | Exits: the portal behind, the arch to the corridor | Smells of cold metal and nothing else*

> Armour dulls as you walk. Buckles soften. Wands and holy symbols become heavy, colourless shapes in your hands — the right weight, the right size, and the wrong everything else.
>
> Niches open in the walls as you pass, one after another, as if waiting for every object you carry.

### What Is in This Room

1. **The niches**, thousands, opening in sequence as creatures pass, each sized to what that creature is carrying.
2. **A catalogue desk** at the hall's centre with a ledger, a stylus, and a bronze tally-plate. *Investigation DC 10:* the ledger is current and the last entry is four days old.
3. **Deposited possessions**, in perhaps four hundred of the niches — swords, packs, instruments, a cradle, a wheelchair, a stack of letters — all intact, all catalogued, all waiting.
4. **The Unnamed**, a woman sitting on the catalogue desk with her boots off. See *Creatures and NPCs*. She is the single most important NPC in this pyramid.
5. **One niche standing open and empty** with a bronze plate that reads, in Pindarian, **AUL TARETH — 1 ITEM — UNCOLLECTED.** *Perception DC 12.*
6. **A pile in the corner**, about waist high, of objects that were **destroyed** rather than surrendered: snapped blades, torn books, a shield beaten flat. Somebody would rather break a thing than hand it over, repeatedly, for four thousand years.

### Setting the Scene

Run the degradation as they walk. Describe one item per character going grey and heavy — the specific item, by name, the one on their sheet they care about.

Then the niches opening. Then the ledger. Then let them decide.

### DM Information

**Items deposited are catalogued and safe.** Absolutely safe. Restored on lawful exit, undamaged, fully charged. Say this plainly and mean it; the ledger is right there and the four hundred filled niches are the evidence.

**Carried items become inert and vulnerable.** They stop working — magic and mundane both — and **at the end of each encounter, one carried mundane item dissolves**, chosen by the pyramid, and it is gone permanently.

**Carrying anything past this room is +1 Dissolution per character who does it.**

**Artifacts whisper but remain functional at half charges.** The Velvet Ruin, Gravewake, Feastchain, the Two That Are One, the Labyrinth's Answer and the Bride of Rot all keep working. They also all have opinions about this room and should voice them — the Bride of Rot in particular is delighted, and the Two That Are One is having an argument with itself about whether to comply.

**The intended answer is voluntary relinquishment, not destruction.** A party that starts snapping their own gear rather than surrender it is making the pile in the corner bigger. **+2 Dissolution each.**

**The uncollected niche** is Aul's. One item. Four thousand years. He has never come back for it. The party can look — *Perception DC 12* to notice, and the niche is not locked, and inside is **a folded Veylari officer's cloak with a unit badge on it**, and nothing else.

### Mechanics

**Depositing (no check).** Everything goes in a niche, catalogued in the ledger under the depositor's name. **-1 Dissolution** for a character who deposits **everything** non-artifact.

**Carrying (no check).** Items become inert. **+1 Dissolution.** At the end of each subsequent encounter, roll to determine which carried mundane item dissolves — or simply take the one it would be funniest to lose, which is usually the rope.

**Destroying.** **+2 Dissolution each.** The fragments join the pile.

**The Ledger (Investigation DC 10).** Four thousand years of entries. *Investigation DC 15:* about one in nine names has a second entry in a different column — the collection column — and the rest do not. **Most people do not come back for their things.**

**Spellcasters.** Say it at the table: foci and components are waived, spells and slots are untouched, spellbooks are safe if deposited. A wizard who insists on carrying the spellbook has made a choice and it is a bad one and they should be allowed to make it.

### Creatures and NPCs

**The Unnamed.** A Veylari woman in her fifties - nine feet of her, competent, calm, barefoot, entirely at ease, and she has to duck for the niches. She has been in Thaal-Uluun for what she estimates is forty years.

**She has everything except her name.**

She remembers her childhood, her training, her sister's face, the taste of a fruit that no longer grows, the exact sound of the sea at the town she grew up in. She remembers being married. She does not remember what she was called, and she has tried everything, and the ledger has her deposit under a name she cannot read — the letters are there and they do not resolve.

**She will not accept a name from the party.** If offered one, gently: *"That would be yours, not mine. I would be carrying your idea of me around and calling it recovered."*

**What she does:** helps. Freely, competently, without asking for anything. She knows the site, she knows the obstacles in T3, she knows what the vats do, and she will walk the party most of the way in and stop at T6, because she has done T6 eleven times and cannot pass it.

**She is the demonstration.** T6 asks for an identity statement that does not rest on possessions, ancestry, class or appearance — and the Unnamed cannot make one, not because she is broken, but because she keeps trying to build it out of the thing she is missing. A party that watches her fail and then works out *why* she fails has been handed the answer to the room.

### Set Piece

**AUL TARETH — 1 ITEM — UNCOLLECTED.**

An open niche, a bronze plate, four thousand years of dust that does not settle in this room because there is no dust.

And in it, folded, a Veylari officer's cloak with a unit badge: eleven small silver marks in a ring, with one of them struck through.

He walks past it every day. He has never taken it out.

### Lore Drops

- **L5 — The Ledger** (*Investigation DC 10, then 15*). Four thousand years of deposits. One in nine collected. **Most people who come here do not come back for their things**, and the reason is not that they died — Law 3 means nobody dies here — it is that they got to T6 and decided they did not need them.
- **L6 — The Pile** (*automatic*). Objects destroyed rather than surrendered. *Insight DC 13:* every one of them is a weapon or a symbol of office. Nobody has ever smashed their own cooking pot rather than hand it in.
- **L7 — The Cloak** (*Perception DC 12*). Eleven silver marks in a ring, one struck through. **A Veylari unit badge with a casualty mark**, and the casualty is the wearer.
- **L8 — From the Unnamed** (*freely*). Forty years. Everything but the name. She is not unhappy and she would like the party to stop looking at her like that.

### Plot Hooks

- Aul's cloak is the key to his whole encounter and the party can be carrying it before they meet him.
- The Unnamed can leave with them, if T6 goes a particular way, and the campaign gains a superb NPC who has to be introduced to everyone as nothing.
- A party that deposits everything is about to play three rooms as themselves, and at level 15 that is a genuinely novel experience.

### Resolution, Rewards, and Temptation

**-1 Dissolution** for a full deposit. **+1** for carrying. **+2** for destroying.

**Treasure.** Four hundred niches of other people's possessions, all catalogued, all belonging to somebody. The ledger records every owner. **Taking any of it is not mechanically penalised and the Unnamed will watch them do it.**

## T3. Without Your Tools

*Corridor of obstacles, 300 ft. | Grey light | Exits: the stripping hall, the foundry stair | Smells of cold stone*

> Beyond the arch waits a corridor of locks, broken bridges, hostile sigils, and a door with no handle.
>
> You are carrying nothing that works.

### What Is in This Room

1. **Four obstacles** in sequence, each of a type the party normally solves with equipment.
2. **A narrow ledge** beside each obstacle where the intended alternative sits — not hidden, not trapped, just not what anyone reaches for first.
3. **Handholds, scuffs and wear** all over the alternatives. Four thousand years of people eventually finding them.
4. **The Unnamed**, walking ahead, not helping unless asked, and answering honestly when she is.

### Setting the Scene

Present each obstacle as an insult. The rogue looks at the lock and reaches for tools that are grey and brittle. The fighter looks at the gap and their climbing gear is in a niche two rooms back.

Then let them look at it properly.

### DM Information

**Four obstacles, each with bodily, social or environmental alternatives at DC 16.** Build them to the party's actual kit — if your rogue's identity is the thieves' tools, obstacle one is a lock; if your fighter's is the shield, obstacle two is a collapse.

**Creative teamwork automatically succeeds once per character.** State this rule to the table at the start of the corridor. Each character has exactly one automatic success available, spent by describing a solution that requires at least one other party member, and it cannot be re-used.

That rule is what keeps the corridor playable for every class and it is not optional.

**Failure costs hit points, not access.** Nothing in this corridor can lock the party out. A failed check deals damage and the obstacle is still there and they can try again, forever, and the corridor's job is to make them spend themselves rather than their gear.

**Forcing an obstacle by breaking it** — a barbarian simply demolishing the door — works, costs **+1 Dissolution**, and should absolutely be allowed. It is a valid answer from a character whose identity genuinely is *the person who breaks the door*, and the pyramid notes it without objecting.

### The Four Obstacles

**1. The Lock.** A Pindarian mechanism, twelve pins, no keyhole on this side. *Thieves' tools are inert.*

- **Alternative (DC 16):** the door is hung on a frame that has sagged over four thousand years. **Athletics** to lift the leaf on its hinges; **Investigation** to find the sag and where to lift; **Sleight of Hand** using a fingernail and a hairpin, because a fingernail is not a tool.
- **Failure:** 22 (4d10) force as the mechanism discharges.

**2. The Broken Bridge.** Forty feet across a drop into liquid stone. *No rope, no pitons.*

- **Alternative (DC 16):** the drop is not lethal — Law 3 — and a character who **jumps in deliberately** re-forms on the far side in one minute, liquefied and helpless in between and completely safe. **Acrobatics** or **Athletics** to cross above; **Survival** to read the flow and time the drop; or simply the nerve to step off.
- **Failure:** liquefaction and one minute of being a puddle, which is not damage and is not access denial and is extremely undignified.

**3. The Hostile Sigils.** A Veylari ward-band across the corridor, active, and it does not care about magic items because there are none.

- **Alternative (DC 16):** the ward keys on **intent to pass without permission**. **Persuasion** addressed to the ward in Pindarian; **Arcana** to read the permission clause; **Performance** to convincingly be someone who belongs here; **Deception** to not intend to pass at all while passing.
- **Failure:** 27 (6d8) force, and the ward resets.

**4. The Door With No Handle.** Seamless, flush, no mechanism, no hinges, no gap.

- **Alternative (DC 16):** it opens from the other side, and **there is somebody on the other side.** **History** to know Veylari door protocol is to knock; **Insight** to realise the corridor has been quiet and something has been walking on the far side; or **anyone who knocks succeeds automatically and instantly.**
- **Failure:** nothing. The door does not open. That is all. Make them stand there.

Obstacle four is the joke and the lesson and you should not rush it.

### Mechanics

**Each obstacle: DC 16**, any justified approach.

**Automatic Success.** One per character, spent on a solution requiring at least one ally. Non-refundable.

**Solving Without Tools.** **-1 Dissolution** per obstacle, once each.

**Breaking Through.** **+1 Dissolution** per obstacle.

**Damage.** As listed. Liquefied characters re-form with an ally's action, or after one minute unaided.

### Creatures and NPCs

**The Unnamed.** She knows all four answers. She will not volunteer any of them. She will answer a direct question completely and without smugness, and she will say *"you would have got it"* afterwards and mean it.

**Behind the fourth door:** a **Pindarian Void Warden**, which opened the door because somebody knocked, and which says *"About time,"* and steps aside.

### Set Piece

**Knocking.**

Four thousand years, a seamless door, no handle, no mechanism, and the answer is that you knock, and somebody opens it.

When they finally do it, have the Void Warden open it immediately — not after a pause, not grudgingly. **Immediately.** As if it had been standing there the whole time.

Because it has.

### Lore Drops

- **L9 — The Wear** (*Investigation DC 12*). Every alternative is worn smooth. Thousands of people, over four thousand years, all eventually finding the same answers, all after trying the obvious thing first.
- **L10 — The Ward's Permission Clause** (*Arcana DC 16*). The sigils grant passage to anyone with permission, and the permission is granted **by asking**, and it has always been granted by asking, and nobody asks.
- **L11 — From the Warden** (*ask it*). It has opened that door for everyone who knocked. *"Four hundred and eleven times."* Against a ledger with four thousand years of entries.

### Plot Hooks

- The Void Warden will talk and has been posted here since the beginning and is bored.
- A party that solved all four without tools has just demonstrated, at level 15, that they are not their equipment, and Aul will have been told.

### Resolution, Rewards, and Temptation

**-1 Dissolution** per obstacle solved without tools, **+1** per obstacle broken.

**No treasure.** There is nothing here to take and nothing to take it with.

## T4. The Melted Foundry

*Foundry floor, 140 ft. by 90 ft., channels and vats | Orange light from the flow | Exits: the corridor, the vat gallery | Smells of hot metal and, underneath, of pond water*

> Metal flows in channels beside liquid stone. The two run parallel and do not mix.
>
> Shapes rise out of the vats — a sword, a bird, a hand, a face — hold for a moment, and sink again. None of them is finished. None of them was going to be.

### What Is in This Room

1. **Nine vats**, each thirty feet across, each holding something in suspension that keeps trying to be an object.
2. **The channels**, molten metal and liquid stone running side by side without mixing, which is impossible and which nobody here comments on.
3. **The black pool**, the ninth vat, different from the others: still, matte, and paying attention. This is **Everyone's Invited's cousin.**
4. **A casting floor** with moulds, tongs, and a slate of Pindarian instructions that are entirely ordinary industrial safety notices.
5. **Finished pieces** on racks along the wall: four hundred objects, each perfect, each unclaimed, each with a bronze tag naming the person it was made for.
6. **One rack that is empty** with the tags still on it — forty tags, forty names, nothing made.

### Setting the Scene

Industrial, hot, loud, and working. The foundry has not stopped. Four thousand years and it is still running, still casting, still racking finished pieces nobody collects.

Then the ninth vat, which is quiet, and which is looking at them.

### DM Information

**Recovering Everyone's Invited requires speaking to the black pool.**

**If treated as an object** — sampled, poked, decanted, attacked, or scooped into a container — **it attacks as an Unmade Duplicate** (CR 12), and it is hurt rather than angry, and it will say so while fighting, which is worse.

**If greeted** — addressed, introduced to, spoken to as a person — it is **eagerly delighted**, becomes a staff, and follows **Bill, or whichever character is the group's most welcoming ooze-touched or ooze-adjacent member.** If Everyone's Invited is already with the party, the two of them are cousins and the reunion is loud and the party will not get a word in for several minutes.

**The foundry can reshape one mundane item without changing its value.** A sword becomes a ploughshare, a shield becomes a door, a crown becomes forty rings. It costs an hour and the item is genuinely the same item afterwards, with the same history, in a different shape — which matters enormously to a character who has been asked what survives change.

**The forty empty tags** are the room's lore. Forty names, forty commissions, nothing cast. *Investigation DC 14:* the commission dates are all within nine days of each other, and the date is year 0, and the foundry has simply never got round to them because the people who ordered them stopped existing in a way that did not cancel the order.

### Mechanics

**Greeting the Pool (no check).** Address it as a person. It becomes a staff — a **+1 quarterstaff** that is amorphous, affectionate, and can squeeze through 1-inch gaps while its wielder holds on, once per short rest.

**Treating It as an Object.** One **Unmade Duplicate** (CR 12) rises from the vat wearing the face of whoever handled it. **+1 Dissolution** if killed. It stops immediately and permanently if anyone apologises to it, which is a legitimate combat resolution and should be allowed without a check.

**Reshaping (1 hour, no check).** One mundane item, reshaped, same value, same history. **This is the only place in the campaign where that is possible** and a party that reshapes something meaningful here should get a Void Mark for it.

**The Racks (Investigation DC 12).** Four hundred finished, tagged, unclaimed pieces. Each is a masterwork mundane item. Taking one is not penalised. Each has a name on it.

**The Empty Rack (Investigation DC 14).** Forty tags, no objects, all commissioned within nine days of year 0.

### Creatures and NPCs

**The black pool**, who does not have a name and would like one and will accept whatever it is given with enormous enthusiasm, unlike the Unnamed two rooms back, and the contrast is deliberate.

**The Unnamed** will watch that exchange and say nothing.

### Set Piece

**The face in the vat.**

Among the shapes rising and sinking in vat six, one of them rises and does not sink. It is a face. It holds at the surface for four full seconds, looking up.

**It is not anyone the party knows.** It is a stranger — a man in his sixties, tired, ordinary. *Investigation DC 15 later, in T5:* the same face is in one of the duplicate vats, complete, finished, and labelled with a commission number rather than a name.

**It is the face Vharo Sellick is being paid in.** Somebody ordered it. It is nearly ready.

### Lore Drops

- **L12 — The Two Channels** (*Arcana DC 16*). Molten metal and liquid stone, parallel, not mixing, for four thousand years. *Arcana DC 19:* they are not two substances. They are **one substance being asked to be two things**, continuously, by the building, as a demonstration that it can be done — and Thaal-Uluun has been holding that demonstration up for four millennia as an argument.
- **L13 — The Forty Tags** (*Investigation DC 14*). Forty commissions, nine days, year 0, never cast. *History DC 16:* the names are Veylari and they are all from one unit.
- **L14 — The Commission Face** (*Investigation DC 15, in T5*). A stranger's face, ordered, nearly finished, numbered rather than named.
- **L15 — From the Pool** (*if greeted*). It has been here since the beginning. It is not Pindarian and was not made; it arrived, liked it, and stayed, and it has watched everything. **It will answer any question the party asks about the site, truthfully, in the wrong order, at length.**

### Plot Hooks

- The face is Vharo's fee and the party can take it, destroy it, or tell him. All three are interesting and one of them is a betrayal of somebody who has never lied to them.
- The forty uncast commissions are Aul's unit, and the party can carry the tags to him.
- A reshaped item is a permanent, specific, thematic keepsake and players will remember it for years.

### Resolution, Rewards, and Temptation

**+1 Dissolution** for killing the pool. **One Void Mark** for a meaningful reshaping.

**Treasure.** Four hundred masterwork mundane items with other people's names on them. The black pool, as a *+1 quarterstaff* with a personality. Forty bronze tags.

## T5. The Duplicate Vats

*Gallery, 120 ft. by 50 ft., vertical pools set into both walls | Blue-grey, lit from within the pools | Exits: the foundry, the naming chamber | Smells of clean water*

> Six clear bodies hang within vertical pools.
>
> They have your faces. Your scars — including the ones that are gone now. Your expressions of alarm, arriving about half a second after your own.

### What Is in This Room

1. **Vertical pools**, one per character plus two, set into both walls, each holding a suspended duplicate.
2. **The duplicates**, perfect, including features the portal took at T1, which means they are **more complete than the originals are**.
3. **Two additional pools** at the gallery's end: one holding the **commission face** from T4, and one **empty, drained, with the seal broken from the inside.**
4. **Control slates** beside each pool, Pindarian, legible, with a release lever.
5. **Scratches on the inside** of the drained pool's glass. *Perception DC 12.*
6. **The Unnamed**, stopping at the gallery entrance and not coming in. She will explain if asked: *"Mine's still in there."*

### Setting the Scene

Play the completeness. Whatever the portal took at T1 — the scar, the accent, the freckles — **the duplicate has it**, and describing that is the room's opening move.

Then have one of them open its eyes.

### DM Information

**Create one Unmade Duplicate for every two characters.** Four characters, two duplicates; six characters, three. They release themselves.

**They know surface memories and copy class roles in simplified form.** A duplicate of the wizard casts, badly, from a short list. A duplicate of the rogue is sneaky in a generic way. They are competent and they are approximations, and they fight like somebody doing an impression.

**A duplicate can be defeated socially when its original explains one choice the duplicate's copied memories cannot predict.**

That is the intended resolution and it should be stated to the players after the first round, by the duplicate itself, because the duplicates are not trying to win. They say things like: *"You are going to do the thing I would do,"* and they are right until they are not.

**The choice has to be real and it has to be surprising**, and the player has to explain **why** — a decision their character made that does not follow from their history, and the reason it did not. A duplicate that hears one stops, says something like *"Oh,"* and becomes inert.

**-1 Dissolution per duplicate defeated socially.** **+1 per duplicate killed in combat.**

**A slain duplicate becomes inert and reforms later without hostility** — it will be standing in the gallery on the way out, whole, not attacking, watching them go.

**Law 4:** speaking a true identity statement grants advantage against a duplicate, once per statement, and the statement must be new.

**The drained pool with scratches on the inside** is Aul Tareth's. He let himself out. *Investigation DC 15:* the seal was broken from within and the scratches are not frantic — they are **methodical**, evenly spaced, the marks of somebody working out a mechanism from the wrong side over a long period.

He was in a vat. He got out. **That is what "the Man Who Refused to Melt" means** and the party can know it before they meet him.

### Mechanics

**Unmade Duplicates** (CR 12 each; see *Original Creatures*). One per two characters.

**Social Defeat (Roleplay, no check).** The original explains one choice their copied memories could not predict, with the reason. The duplicate goes inert. **-1 Dissolution.**

**Combat Defeat.** Standard. **+1 Dissolution each.** It reforms later, harmless.

**Identity Statement (Law 4).** Advantage against duplicates, once per new statement per character.

**Void Marks** spend for advantage here too, and stack.

**The Commission Face (Investigation DC 15).** Complete, numbered not named, and the number cross-references a commission slate in T4. It is finished and it is waiting for collection.

**Taking it** is possible — it decants into any container and weighs about nine pounds — and it is a person's face, and somebody ordered it, and the party is now carrying the fee for whatever Vharo Sellick has been doing.

### Creatures and NPCs

**The duplicates.** They are polite, curious, and doing their job. They do not hate their originals. One of them should, at some point mid-combat, ask a genuine question about a memory it has and does not understand, and wait for the answer.

**The Unnamed** waits outside. Her duplicate is in a pool, intact, forty years old, with her name on the control slate.

**She can read the slate.** She just cannot read her own name on it, and the party can — *and they should be allowed to*, and it is a simple Investigation check, and what they do next is the best decision in this pyramid.

**If they tell her:** she goes very quiet, and then says *"Don't,"* and then, after a moment, *"Not yet."* She comes to T6 with them. She still cannot pass it. But she knows, and knowing is not the same as having it, and she will make the choice at T7 in front of everybody.

### Set Piece

**The scars that came back.**

Whatever the Softstone Portal took at T1, the duplicates still have. The character who lost their scar is looking at themselves with it. The one who lost an accent hears their own voice, correct, from inside a pool.

Let each player have four seconds of that before anything opens its eyes.

### Lore Drops

- **L16 — The Drained Pool** (*Perception DC 12, Investigation DC 15*). Broken from the inside, methodically, over a long period. Aul Tareth was a duplicate, or was in the vats, and let himself out, and the distinction is one he will make himself and it will take him a while.
- **L17 — What They Cannot Copy** (*automatic, on a social defeat*). Surface memories and role. **Not choices.** The vats can reproduce everything about a person except what they decided to do about it.
- **L18 — The Unnamed's Slate** (*Investigation DC 10*). Her name, legible, to everyone except her.
- **L19 — The Commission Number** (*Investigation DC 15, cross-referenced to T4*). Ordered, not named. **Faces are a product here and somebody has an account.**

### Plot Hooks

- Reading the Unnamed's name is the whole of her arc and the party controls it.
- The commission face is a live thread aimed directly at Vharo Sellick and at whoever is paying him.
- Duplicates reform harmlessly and stay in the gallery. A party that killed all of them walks back out past a line of themselves, standing in the water, watching.

### Resolution, Rewards, and Temptation

**-1 Dissolution** per social defeat, **+1** per kill.

**Treasure.** Nothing, unless they take the face, which is not treasure.

## T6. Who Are You?

*Bare chamber, 40 ft. across, unlit and perfectly visible | No light source and no darkness | Exits: the gallery, the Nail chamber | No smell at all, and the absence is total*

> Names peel from your memory like old labels off a jar.
>
> Not identities. Not histories. **Names** — your own, your companions', the town you were born in, the word for the thing you are standing on.
>
> A voice asks:
>
> **"If every part changes, what remains responsible for the life that follows?"**

### What Is in This Room

1. **Nothing.** The chamber is empty and unlit and perfectly visible and contains no object of any kind.
2. **The party**, who can still see each other and cannot name each other for the duration.
3. **The voice**, which is not Aul and not the pyramid and not a creature. It asks once and then waits, and it will wait forever.
4. **The Unnamed**, who came in, and who has been here before, eleven times.

### Setting the Scene

Strip the description to nothing, because the room is nothing. No brazier, no carving, no floor texture. Four sentences and then silence.

Then ask the question, once, and stop.

**Do not prompt. Do not offer examples. Do not roll anything.** If the table stalls, let it stall. This room is worth ten minutes of real silence.

### DM Information

**Each character offers an identity statement based on choice rather than possessions, ancestry, class or appearance.**

What fails:

- *"I am the sword of my order."* — possession and role.
- *"I am a dwarf of the Ironcount line."* — ancestry.
- *"I am the best fighter in this room."* — class.
- *"I am the one with the scar."* — appearance, and they do not have it any more.
- Anything another character has already said. **Repeating another character's answer fails**, which means the last player to speak has the hardest job, and you should go round in an order the table chooses.

What passes: **anything built out of a decision.** *"I am whoever keeps going back for people."* *"I am the one who does not look away."* *"I am the person who lied to my brother and has not fixed it."*

**There is no single correct philosophy.** A nihilist answer passes if it is sincere and choice-shaped: *"There is nothing that persists, and I am going to act as though there is, because the alternative is unbearable and I have decided that matters"* is a complete pass and one of the better ones.

**A sincere answer restores one suppressed item** for the final audience — the character names an item from their niche and it is simply in their hand again, functioning.

**-1 Dissolution per sincere answer.**

**A character who cannot answer is not punished.** They go through. Aul will ask them again himself and will not mind waiting.

### The Unnamed

She tries. The party will watch her try.

She says something about the sea at the town she grew up in, and she stops, and she says *"That's a place,"* and she starts again, and she says something about her sister, and she stops, and she says *"That's a person,"* and she starts again.

**Eleven times, over forty years, she has built her answer out of what she is missing.**

If the party read her slate in T5 and did not tell her, she fails again, cleanly, and takes it well, and it is awful.

**If they told her** — she stands there for a while. Then she says: *"I still don't know it. But somebody does, and they didn't use it on me, and they waited, and that was a choice, and I am the person who was worth waiting for."*

And she passes.

Give the table a moment.

### Mechanics

**The Statement (Roleplay, no check, no limit on attempts).** One per character. Choice-based, sincere, and not a repeat.

**-1 Dissolution** and **one restored item** per sincere answer.

**Failure.** Nothing happens. Try again, or go through anyway.

**Repeating another's answer.** Fails. The voice says the other character's name, which everybody has temporarily forgotten, and hearing it is briefly disorienting.

**The names come back** on leaving the room.

### Creatures and NPCs

**No creature.** The voice is the room and has no statistics and cannot be interacted with beyond the question.

### Set Piece

**Going round the table.**

That is the set piece. There is nothing else in this room and there does not need to be.

Ask the question once. Then go round, in whatever order the table picks, and let every player answer, and do not help any of them.

### Lore Drops

- **L20 — The Question's Age** (*ask the voice*). It has asked that question 4,111 times. It will tell them the number if asked and it will not elaborate.
- **L21 — What the Room Is For** (*Arcana DC 18*). Not a test and not a filter. It is a **survey instrument** — Veylari, and it does not grade the answers, it **records** them, and the record is somewhere, and Aul has read all of it.

### Plot Hooks

- Every answer given here is a player-authored campaign statement in their own words. Write them down and bring them back at Khal'Vethar.
- The 4,111 recorded answers are a document, and Aul has read them, and the party can ask him whether any of them were the same.

### Resolution, Rewards, and Temptation

**-1 Dissolution and one restored item** per sincere answer.

**No treasure.** The room contains nothing and that is the point.

## T7. The Dissolving Nail

*Nail chamber, 70 ft. across | Green-black light from the plinth | Exits: the naming chamber, and out | Smells of pond water and iron*

> A transparent man nine feet tall stands beside a black-green lash embedded in a liquid plinth. His head is near the vault and he has been holding that exact height for four thousand years. You can see the far wall through him, and the wall is not distorted, which is somehow worse.
>
> His outline trembles with every breath he takes.
>
> He has been holding that shape for four thousand years and he is still concentrating.

### What Is in This Room

1. **Aul Tareth**, transparent, upright, and visibly maintaining himself.
2. **Mercy of Dissolution**, a black-green whip, embedded in a plinth of liquid stone that is not splashing.
3. **The ring of light** around the plinth, with a whip-shaped absence in it.
4. **A chair**, ordinary, Pindarian, facing the plinth. He does not sit in it.
5. **Eleven marks** scratched into the plinth's rim, evenly spaced, one struck through. The same badge as the cloak in T2.
6. **The catalogue**, a second ledger, here rather than at the desk — the **collection** column, listing everyone who came back for their things.

### Setting the Scene

He is polite and he is working. Every sentence costs him something and he does not hurry any of them.

Open with him thanking them for coming, by name, including the ones who have not told him their names, because he was in the survey chamber's record and he has read all four thousand one hundred and eleven answers and he recognises the shape of theirs.

### DM Information

**Aul releases the relic to characters who completed the pyramid after abandoning possessions.**

That is the condition and it is mechanical: **every character who deposited everything at T2 and did not carry an item through.** A party where one person carried a sword can still get the relic — Aul is not absolute about it — but he will address that person directly and ask them what the sword was for, and he will accept any honest answer, including *"I was frightened."*

**Combat occurs only if they try to define his identity for him, or seize the whip.**

That first clause is precise and easy to trip over. Aul will describe himself, at length, uncertainly, and he will ask the party what they think he is. **Answering that question is fatal.** Any character who tells him what he is — *"you're a coward," "you're a hero," "you're just a man who was scared," "you're Aul Tareth and that's enough"* — even kindly, even correctly, is defining his identity for him, and he will say *"No,"* and roll initiative.

The correct response is to answer the way T6 taught them: **give him their own statement and let him build his.** Or to say they do not know. Or to ask him a question back.

Do not warn them. T6 warned them.

### His Regret

He gives it if asked, in pieces, with pauses:

> *"There were eleven of us. We were Veylari, and the siege line at Uluun was four miles deep, and the order was to dissolve and pass through it and re-form behind.*
>
> *"That is a standard manoeuvre. We had done it eleven times. It works.*
>
> *"And we went through, and we came back, and every one of them came back simplified. Not damaged — simplified. One of them came back without the thing that made him funny. One came back without whatever it was that made her argue. They were all still themselves, and they were all less, and none of them noticed, and I watched them not notice for the rest of the war.*
>
> *"I came back whole. And I have had four thousand years to work out whether that means I held on harder than they did, or whether it means I was carrying less."*

**He does not know the answer.** He is not fishing. If the party tells him, he refuses it, and that is what triggers the fight.

### The First Hunger

If asked about the eleven worlds, the empty classification, or what the party found at Myr'Velora — and he should be asked — he gives the campaign's clearest statement of the method, and it is four sentences:

> *"It does not destroy. It simplifies.*
>
> *"It removes whatever is not necessary for a thing to still technically be that thing, and then it does it again, and the thing is still there and still answers to its name.*
>
> *"When it has finished, what is left is accurate and complete and has nothing in it.*
>
> *"I have watched it happen to eleven people over four miles of siege line and I am the only reason anyone knows it can happen to a world."*

### The Ilyri

Third confirmation, and the one that answers Korveth's better question.

Vaelis and Vaelith voted. Korveth surveyed the volume and said the vote was free and noted that nobody had asked whether it was fair. **Aul was in the room.**

> *"It was free and it was not fair, and the difference is that we told them what it would cost and we did not tell them what it would feel like, because we did not know. I have since found out. I would not have voted the same way."*

### Mechanics

**Lawful Release.** The condition above is met, and a character **takes the whip from the plinth with a bare hand**, and the plinth's liquid does not resist.

**-4 Dissolution. Relic Attention +1.**

**Lawful release restores all catalogued items and repairs mundane damage** — every niche empties into its owner's hands, fully charged, and anything broken on the way in is whole. Void Marks dissolve. Missing cosmetic features return. **Memories lost to Dissolution 3 return.**

**Improper removal** — seizing the whip, or fighting him for it — **liquefies a one-mile radius over 24 hours.** The glass desert gets a mile wider, and there is a fishing village inside that radius, and they have a day, and the party knows it.

**+3 Dissolution. Relic Attention +2.** No restoration: everything in the niches is lost.

**Aul Tareth** (CR 12; see *Original Creatures*). He fights reluctantly, defensively, and badly, and he is not a hard encounter for a level 15 party, and killing him is trivially easy and is the single saddest available outcome in this pyramid.

**He surrenders** the moment anyone stops trying to tell him what he is.

### The Unnamed

If she passed T6, she asks to take the relic.

Not to keep. To be the one who lifts it, because she has been in this building for forty years and has never been able to do anything here that was hers.

**Let her.** It changes nothing mechanically, the party still gets the artifact, and it is the right ending for her.

And when she does it, Aul says her name. He has always known it. He has never said it, because saying it would have been giving it to her, and he has spent four thousand years not defining other people.

### Set Piece

**The chair.**

Ordinary, Pindarian, facing the plinth, within arm's reach of where he stands.

He has not sat down in four thousand years. If asked why: *"If I sit, I will relax. If I relax, I will stop concentrating on the shape."*

And then, after a moment, because he is honest: *"That is what I tell myself. I do not know whether it is true and I have not been willing to test it."*

### Lore Drops

- **L22 — The Eleven Marks** (*automatic*). The same badge as the uncollected cloak. Eleven, one struck through, and the struck one is his, and he struck it himself in year 0.
- **L23 — The Collection Ledger** (*Investigation DC 10*). One in nine came back for their things. *He knows every name in both columns.*
- **L24 — The Method** (*ask about the First Hunger*). Four sentences. The clearest statement in the campaign.
- **L25 — Free and Not Fair** (*ask about the Ilyri*). *"We told them what it would cost and we did not tell them what it would feel like, because we did not know."*
- **L26 — The Cloak** (*give it to him*). If the party brought it from T2, he takes it, and he does not put it on, and he folds it again, differently, and he says *"Thank you"* and nothing else for a while.

### Plot Hooks

- Aul released is the only person alive who has watched the First Hunger work at close range on people, and he will testify to anyone.
- The Unnamed, walking out with a name she has heard once, is a superb NPC and will not use it for a while.
- One in nine. Eight thousand people left their possessions in Thaal-Uluun and never came back, and they are all somewhere.

### Resolution, Rewards, and Temptation

**Lawful: -4 Dissolution, Relic Attention +1**, everything restored — items, charges, features, memories.

**Improper: Relic Attention +2**, a mile of new glass desert over 24 hours, a village inside it, and every deposited possession lost.

**Treasure.** Mercy of Dissolution (artifact; see *The Nine Demon Relics*). Everything the party deposited, back. A folded cloak, if they did not give it to him.

## Thaal-Uluun Aftermath

When Mercy of Dissolution is removed lawfully, describe a deep vibration through the remaining pyramids. Over the following week the glass desert's edge begins to grain and crumble into ordinary sand, and the buildings suspended in it settle another few feet and stop, and one of them is a bell tower and the bell rings once as it moves.

**Immediate consequences.**

- **Relic Attention +1.** The whip is slow, literal, and offended by boundaries, doors, and complex anatomy. It will suggest simplifications. Many of them are practical. That is the problem.
- **Aul ages normally or dissolves by choice.** If he dissolves, he does it in the chair, and he sits down first.
- **The niches empty.** Four hundred people's possessions are returned to nobody, and the ledger has every name, and eight thousand more names are in the collection column with no second entry.
- **The First Hunger's method is now known.** Simplification, not destruction. Every previous pyramid recontextualises: Astra's ledger, Vhal'Kathar's empty classification, Myr'Velora's eleven cradles, the Ilyri's empty shelf.
- **The Ilyri question is answered.** Free, and not fair, and the man who was in the room would not vote the same way now. Two anchors remain and both guardians were there.
- **Vharo Sellick's fee is in somebody's hands.** A face, ordered, numbered, nearly finished. Whoever holds it has leverage over a cambion who has never once lied to the party.
