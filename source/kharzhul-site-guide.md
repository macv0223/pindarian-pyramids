# Khar'Zhul: Pyramid of the Empty Table - Site Guide

*Levels 7-9 | Children of the Sun | Predation into Mercy | Relic: Feastchain | Guardian: General Rhakkar Sol, the Starved Lion*

A dead agricultural basin where the wind moves through black grain that cannot be eaten.

## Running Khar'Zhul

Astra tested what the party wants. Vhal'Kathar tested what they will not put down. Khar'Zhul tests something smaller and harder: **whether they stay decent while the supplies run out.**

This is a resource-attrition site and it must be run as one. Every mechanical system here — rations, Hit Dice, spell slots, foraging — is being deliberately drained, and the pyramid's whole argument is that violence is the efficient solution to almost every problem in it. Killing the deer feeds the party. Killing the hunters ends the chase. Taking all the food solves the shortage. **Every one of those is correct, and every one of them makes the last two rooms worse.**

The party is not being punished for pragmatism. They are being measured for it. Rhakkar Sol ran famine relief for a besieged civilisation and he has heard every argument for efficiency there is, in better words, from better people, and he agreed with most of them at the time, and he is still sitting at that table four thousand years later.

### Pindarian Scale

**Pindarians are Large.** All of them, always — the Solaari, the Lunari, the Veylari, the Ilyri, every
Saharim guardian and every soldier in every rank. Eight to ten feet, long-boned, and long-lived, with a
reach and a stride to match: **10-foot reach on their melee attacks and a 10-foot space.**

This is not a variant or a subtype. It is what the species is, and it is worth showing rather than stating —
Pindarian doorways are twelve feet high, their stairs have deep treads, their chairs do not fit anyone else,
and a party of Medium adventurers spends nine pyramids using furniture built for people half again their
size.

Human, elven and dwarven NPCs met in and around the sites are Medium as usual. The contrast is the point.

### The Supply Rules

State all four before the party enters, in the fiction, through the gnoll at the gate if you have used her.

1. **Food and water consumed inside Khar'Zhul cannot be magically replaced.** *Create food and water, goodberry,* a *decanter of endless water*, and every ration-conjuring effect all function — and the food is real, and it is eaten, and it produces no nourishment. A creature that eats only conjured food gains no benefit from a rest.
2. **A long rest restores class features and spell slots but not spent Hit Dice** unless the party **shares a meal**: everyone present eats from a common pot, at the same time, with each character contributing something. Then everyone recovers Hit Dice normally.
3. **Killing a harmless creature adds 1 Predation. Sparing a helpless enemy removes 1.**
4. **Foraging produces enough for half the party**, rounded down, unless the foragers work with the basin's ecology rather than against it — see K3.

### Rations

Track a single shared number in the open: **Party Rations**, measured in person-days.

Assume the party arrives with whatever they are carrying (normally 5-10 days each). Khar'Zhul spends it:

| Event | Rations |
| --- | --- |
| Each long rest inside the pyramid | -1 per character |
| Paying the Threshing Gate in food (K1) | -4 |
| Each failed check on the Starving Road (K3) | -1 per character, or 1 Hit Die each |
| Taking the pantry (K2) | +6, and +2 Predation |
| Taking half the pantry (K2) | +3 |
| Feeding Nuru, the deer, the refugees, or a pursuer | -1 to -3, and see each area |

**At 0 Rations,** a long rest gives no Hit Dice and each character gains **one level of exhaustion** per subsequent long rest until they eat. Do not soften this and do not engineer around it. Running out is a legitimate outcome and the party has four opportunities to avoid it, three of which involve being generous.

### The Predation Track

Visible, 0 to 9, exactly as Astra's Temptation was.

| Event | Predation |
| --- | --- |
| Killing a creature that was harmless or fleeing | +1 each |
| Killing a surrendered pursuer (K4) | +1 each, max +4 |
| Taking the whole pantry (K2) | +2 |
| Killing the pale deer (K3) | +2 |
| Refusing to share at the Last Meal (K5) | +2 |
| Sparing a helpless enemy | -1 each |
| Feeding something that cannot repay you | -1 each |
| Everyone shares at the Last Meal (K5) | **Predation to 0** |

**At 3.** The basin notices. Two **Starveling Pursuers** begin shadowing the party at the edge of vision, in every area, and they do not close. They are being allowed to watch.

**At 6.** The pursuers begin taking the party's kills. Anything the party downs and does not immediately use is gone within a minute, and they will find bones. Foraging now produces enough for a **quarter** of the party.

**At 9.** Khar'Zhul stops distinguishing the party from the thing it was built to hold. Rhakkar does not talk. Feastchain speaks to the character with the highest kill count by name, from inside the Nail, three rooms early.

**In the final encounter,** each remaining point of Predation converts to either **15 temporary hit points for Rhakkar** or **one Starveling Pursuer entering on initiative count 20.** Announce each conversion aloud, item by item, as Astra does.

### Mercy of Khar'Zhul

Granted on lawful release. Once before their next long rest, **each character may maximise one Hit Die they roll.** It is small, and it is the only thing this pyramid gives away, and it is worth saying aloud that a site about starvation pays out in the ability to get back up.

### Pacing and Shape

Khar'Zhul is linear and it is a road. K1 is the gate, K2 the ruined domestic heart, K3 a journey, K4 an ambush on that journey, K5 the moral fulcrum, K6 the guardian, K7 the Nail. Two to three sessions, with the natural break after K4.

**There is no safe rest site.** Every long rest costs rations and every long rest is a decision.

### Safety at This Table

Starvation, scarcity, and the rationing of care are the subject matter, and K2 and K5 in particular can land hard on anyone with a history around food. Say so during lines and veils, and note that no scene here requires describing a character's body or hunger in physical detail — the pressure is arithmetic on a shared tracker, not description. A player may always have their character eat, refuse to eat, or say nothing about it, and none of those costs anything mechanically.

### Continuity from Astra

If the party released the Velvet Ruin, Astra's planar load came here, and **Khar'Zhul was already carrying overflow before that.** The basin is not dying because of the party — it was dying first — but they have made it faster, and a **DC 14 Arcana** check anywhere in the site confirms it.

The six-fingered broker who supplied Dressel Kaine's expedition left Picea Pindar for this basin three days after Astra fell. He is here. He is at the Threshing Gate, in K1, and he is being very helpful.

## K1. The Threshing Gate

*Gatehouse, 40 ft. wide, open to the sky | Overcast daylight, colourless | Exits: the basin (behind), the Kitchens (through the mouth) | Smells of dust, dry chaff, and nothing organic at all*

> Two stone beasts crouch above a gate carved as an open mouth — lions, or something that was going to be lions before the sculptor got to the ribs and changed their mind. You can count every one of those ribs.
>
> Between them stands a balance on a granite plinth. On the left pan lies a loaf of bread, four thousand years old and perfectly fresh. On the right pan lies a dagger.
>
> The pans are level.

### What Is in This Room

1. **The gate-mouth,** eighteen feet high, hinged at the jaw, currently shut. Teeth. Actual teeth, set into the stone, worn.
2. **The balance,** granite plinth, bronze beam, two pans. It does not respond to weight. *Arcana DC 13:* it is not weighing mass. It is weighing intent, and it has been level for four thousand years because nobody has come.
3. **The loaf,** genuinely fresh, genuinely edible, genuinely four thousand years old. Eating it is safe and does nothing and counts as **+1 Predation** — it was an offering and the party took it off the scale.
4. **The dagger,** Pindarian, plain, sharp, with a shallow blood-groove and a worn grip.
5. **The black grain,** starting about forty yards out and running to the horizon in every direction: stalks the colour of wet slate, heavy with heads that look like wheat and are not. *Nature DC 12:* it is a real cereal, it is nutritionally complete, and **it cannot be eaten** — the body simply refuses it, at the throat, involuntarily.
6. **Eleven cairns** along the approach road, recent, each with a bowl set in front of it. Ten bowls are empty. One has food in it, put there in the last two days.
7. **Nuru,** a gnoll, sitting against the left-hand lion with her bow unstrung across her knees. She has been there for six days. She is the one who filled the bowl.
8. **A man in a good coat** standing at the balance with his hands behind his back, considering it. He has six fingers on his left hand.

### Setting the Scene

Open on the basin, not the gate. The party has walked a day through black grain that moves in the wind like a field should and smells like nothing at all, and the silence is the first fact: no birds, no insects, no rodents. A wheatfield with no ecology in it.

Then the gate, then the balance, then — after a beat, once they have started discussing the puzzle — the voice from the left-hand lion saying, in accented Common, *"You will want to know about the bowls first."*

### DM Information

**The gate opens for a payment.** Either:

- **Food for one day** placed on the left pan (**-4 Rations**), or
- **Blood freely shed**, totalling 10 hit points across any number of willing creatures, on the right pan.

**Paying in blood awakens two Starveling Pursuers**, who come out of the grain within the minute. This is not a punishment for choosing wrong — blood is the cheap option and the pyramid is establishing, in the first thirty seconds, that cheap options here attract predators.

**The third answer,** and the one the site is actually looking for: **place food on one pan and a weapon on the other.** The mouth closes, the balance dips once in acknowledgement, and the party passes without paying anything. Khar'Zhul records that they understood the question — which was never *what will you give* but *do you know that those two things are the same currency.*

**Do not hint at this.** Nuru knows it and will say so if befriended, and that is the intended route.

**The broker** is here to get into the pyramid and cannot: the gate will not open for him, and he has been standing at the balance for two days working out why. He is charming, generous, and will offer the party four days of excellent rations, free, no conditions, delivered before he asks for anything.

He wants to walk in behind them. That is the whole ask and he will state it plainly.

### Mechanics

**Paying in Food.** Place one day's food for the party. **-4 Rations.** The mouth opens.

**Paying in Blood.** Any willing creatures shed a combined 10 hit points (no save, no roll, the damage is taken). The mouth opens and **two Starveling Pursuers** (CR 7) arrive in 1d4 rounds. At level 7-9 this is a hard opening fight before the party has entered the building.

**The Cooperative Answer (no check).** Food on one pan, a weapon on the other. The gate opens, nothing is consumed, and every character gains **advantage on their first Predation-relevant decision** — in practice, tell the player of the character who suggested it that Khar'Zhul is paying attention to them specifically, and mean it.

**The Loaf.** Edible, harmless, **+1 Predation** if taken.

**The Bowls (Investigation DC 10).** Eleven cairns, ten empty bowls, one filled two days ago. *Survival DC 12:* the cairns are two to six days old and were built by a single pair of hands. They are graves.

**Nuru (Persuasion DC 12, or simply offering her food).** She has eaten nothing for six days except what she has been putting in the bowl. She will not take food from the party unless they eat first, in front of her, from the same stock.

**Feeding Nuru: -1 Ration, -1 Predation.**

### Creatures and NPCs

**Nuru**, gnoll exile. Lean even for a gnoll, missing most of one ear, carrying a bow she has not drawn in a week. She was driven out of her pack for refusing an order she will not describe, and she walked into the basin because the pyramid has been calling every predator in the region and she wanted to see what was doing the calling.

**The eleven cairns are hers.** She found a refugee column from the basin villages four days out, already dead, and buried them one at a time, and she has been feeding the twelfth — a child who was still alive when she found her and who is not any more — and she has not stopped filling the bowl.

**A gnoll.** Burying the people her species is famous for eating, and leaving food out for one of them, alone, for six days, while starving.

That is the thesis of the entire pyramid and it is standing at the gate before the party has gone inside.

**Roleplaying her.** Blunt, tired, entirely without self-pity, and deeply uninterested in being admired for it. If a character praises her, she looks confused and changes the subject. She speaks Common badly and Gnoll fluently and understands more than she lets on.

**What she knows:** the gate wants both pans filled (she watched a Pindarian ghost do it); the road inside counts backwards; the deer are not deer; and the man in the good coat arrived two days ago and has been polite to her twice, which is twice more than anyone has been in a year, and she does not trust him.

**Vharo Sellick**, the six-fingered broker (**Cambion**, MM, with Deception +9 and Persuasion +9). Soft-spoken, beautifully dressed, apparently sixty. He supplied Dressel Kaine's expedition at Astra with an accurate map and an inaccurate final room. He is Graz'zt's, he is working a schedule, and he is here to be let in.

**His offer:** four days of rations for the party, given first, unconditionally. Then the ask: walk in behind them, touch nothing, take nothing, leave when they leave.

**He keeps his word to the letter.** That is what makes him dangerous. He will not steal, will not attack, will not lie about anything checkable, and will do exactly what he said — and what he said was *walk in behind them*, and he never said what for.

### Set Piece

**The bowl.**

While the party debates the balance, Nuru gets up, walks past them to the eleventh cairn, takes a strip of dried meat from inside her coat — the last of it, and the party can see it is the last of it — and puts it in the bowl.

Then she sits back down.

If anyone asks who it is for, she says a name. If anyone tells her the child is dead, she says, *"I know,"* and nothing else.

### Lore Drops

- **L1 — The Black Grain** (*Nature DC 12*). A complete cereal that the body refuses at the throat. *Arcana DC 16:* it is not poisoned or cursed. It is **outside the local ecology** — grain from a place where nothing on Elderon is meant to eat, growing here because the pyramid's containment is thin enough to let something through the seam. The basin is not dead. It is being colonised.
- **L2 — The Overflow** (*Arcana DC 14*). The distortion here is carrying more load than one anchor should. If the party released the Velvet Ruin, some of this is theirs. If they have not been to Astra, this is the clue that sends them there.
- **L3 — The Teeth in the Gate** (*Investigation DC 12*). Real teeth, humanoid and animal both, set into the stone jamb and worn smooth. *History DC 15:* Pindarian famine law. Every household that took more than its ration surrendered a tooth. There are several thousand in this gate and the Pindarians put them where everyone entering would have to walk past them.
- **L4 — The Eleven** (*from Nuru, or Survival DC 12*). A refugee column out of the basin villages, dead of starvation four days out from a pyramid full of preserved food. Nobody opened the gate for them, because the gate wanted a payment and they had nothing left to pay with.

### Plot Hooks

- **Nuru is the best NPC in this pyramid** and should travel with the party if they show her the smallest decency. She is the mercy test made portable, she is an excellent scout, and if the Berry Bad Idea is in this campaign, K2 says it chooses her.
- **Vharo Sellick's offer** is a genuine four days of rations against an unknown cost, at the front of a site that will absolutely run the party out of food, and refusing it is the harder and better choice.
- The refugee column has survivors elsewhere in the basin if you want them; the ones in K2 can be the rest of that same group.

### Resolution, Rewards, and Temptation

**-4 Rations** for the food payment, **10 hit points and a hard fight** for the blood payment, **nothing at all** for the cooperative answer.

**-1 Predation** for feeding Nuru. **+1 Predation** for taking the loaf.

**Treasure.** The Pindarian dagger from the right pan is a *+1 dagger* and taking it before the gate is answered is **+1 Predation**, because it is part of the question.

## K2. The Kitchens Without Fire

*Kitchen complex and refectory, 160 ft. by 70 ft. | Dim grey light through high slots | Exits: the gate-mouth (behind), the pantry (north), the road door (west) | Smells of cold copper and clean stone*

> Copper pots hang in their ranks above hearths that have been cold for four thousand years. Long tables are set for hundreds — cloth, cutlery, water glasses, every place laid correctly and squared to the next.
>
> Every plate holds a scattering of teeth.

### What Is in This Room

1. **Four hundred and six place settings** across eleven tables, each with a plate, each plate holding between two and thirty teeth. *Investigation DC 12:* one plate per household, and the count on each plate is that household's surrendered tally from the gate.
2. **The cold hearths,** nine of them, enormous, swept clean. No ash. *Investigation DC 14:* they were swept *after* the last fire, deliberately, as a closing-down.
3. **Copper pots,** eighty-odd, hanging in graded rows, polished. Worth 300 gp in total and weighing four hundred pounds.
4. **The pantry,** behind a door on the north wall: **six days of preserved rations for a party of four** — hard biscuit, dried fruit, salt meat, sealed and sound.
5. **Twelve tooth-mimics** in the pantry, asleep, indistinguishable from the teeth on the plates until they move.
6. **A serving hatch** with a slate above it, chalked with a duty list in Pindarian, unfinished.
7. **The refugees** — twenty-two survivors of the basin villages — who are **not here yet.** Whether they arrive at all depends on what the party does with the pantry. See *DM Information*.
8. **A dead orchard** visible through the western slots, black and leafless, forty yards off. **The Berry Bad Idea** is in it, in a bush that is the only thing in the basin still bearing fruit.

### Setting the Scene

The horror of this room is that it is **tidy**. Four hundred places laid correctly, cutlery squared, glasses upright, and a handful of teeth on every plate.

Let a player pick up a fork before you mention the teeth. The cutlery is good and heavy and clean.

Then the plates.

### DM Information

**The pantry is the room's decision** and it is a straightforward one with a delayed consequence:

- **Take all six days** (**+6 Rations, +2 Predation**). The party is now supplied for the whole site, and **the refugees never appear** — they reach the kitchens two days behind the party, find the pantry empty, and do not come out of the basin. The party will never see them and will only learn what happened in K6, from Rhakkar, who will mention it once, flatly, without accusation.
- **Take half** (**+3 Rations, +0 Predation**). Two days behind them, twenty-two people eat. They appear in K5 and in K6, and they are the single strongest piece of evidence the party can carry to the Untouched Banquet.
- **Take none.** **+0 Rations, -1 Predation**, and the refugees appear *ahead* of the party in K4, on the road, in the path of the hunt.

**Do not tell the party the refugees exist.** There is no check that reveals them and no clue that points at them. The pantry is a resource decision made with no information, which is precisely the decision Rhakkar Sol spent a war making.

**The tooth-mimics** are a trap in the literal sense and a joke in the useful sense: twelve of them, asleep, in a room where every flat surface already has teeth on it. A party that has been looking at plates full of teeth for ten minutes will not notice twelve more.

**Feeding a tooth-mimic a *goodberry*** — which any druid or ranger will try within about a minute of finding them — **wakes the Berry Bad Idea** in the orchard outside, which selects a wielder and begins talking. Nuru is the intended and best choice if she is with the party.

### Mechanics

**The Pantry (Investigation DC 10 to find the door; no check to open).** Six person-days per party member, i.e. **+6 Rations** for the standard party. See the three options above.

**The Tooth-Mimics.** Twelve **Tooth-Mimics** (see *Bestiary*, CR 1/2 each). **Perception DC 17** to notice one is breathing before touching the shelf; failure means the first character to reach in is attacked by 1d4 of them with surprise.

They are a nuisance encounter, not a threat — twelve CR 1/2 creatures against a level 8 party is four rounds of irritation — and their function is entirely to make the party handle a great many teeth.

**Killing all twelve: +1 Predation.** They are pantry vermin and they were asleep. **Driving them off or leaving them: +0.** A party that works out they can be lured away with any organic material at all has solved the room correctly.

**The Berry Bad Idea (goodberry, or any conjured food offered to a mimic).** The relic wakes in the orchard and chooses a wielder within 100 feet — the most chaotic archer present, per its dormancy rules. **If Nuru is with the party, it chooses Nuru**, and it starts talking to her immediately, at volume, and she is horrified.

See *The Living Mimic Relics* for its dormant and awakened rules. Its arrival is comedy and should be played as comedy in a room full of teeth, because this pyramid needs one laugh and this is where it is.

**The Duty Slate (Pindarian, Investigation DC 10).** See *Lore Drops*.

**Long Rest.** The kitchens are the safest rest site in Khar'Zhul — stone walls, one door, no ecology. It still costs **-1 Ration per character**, and Hit Dice only return if the party shares a meal.

### Creatures and NPCs

**Twelve Tooth-Mimics.** Small, stupid, and extremely committed.

**Nobody else.** The kitchens are empty and should stay empty; the refugees arrive after the party has gone.

### Set Piece

**The last shift.**

Above the serving hatch, a slate, chalked in Pindarian in a clear hand: a duty roster for the evening meal. Names, stations, times. Twenty-two entries.

The last four lines are written in a different hand, hurried, and they are not names. They are three numbers and a word, and the word is *enough*, and the numbers are a ration calculation that has been done, crossed out, done again smaller, and crossed out again.

The third calculation is not crossed out. It is circled. And underneath it, in the same hurried hand, one more line: **"Tell them it is a fast day."**

### Lore Drops

- **L5 — The Plates** (*Investigation DC 12*). One place per household, and on each plate that household's tally of surrendered teeth. *History DC 14:* this was the last general meal served in Khar'Zhul, and the seating plan **was the ration list.** Everyone sat down knowing exactly what everyone else had taken.
- **L6 — The Swept Hearths** (*Investigation DC 14*). Swept clean after the last fire and never used again. Somebody closed this kitchen properly, on purpose, with time to do it tidily. Khar'Zhul did not fall. It was **shut down**, in order, by staff.
- **L7 — The Slate** (*automatic, Pindarian*). Three ration calculations, each smaller, the last circled, and *"Tell them it is a fast day."* **Rhakkar Sol wrote this**, or authorised it, and the hand is the same hand that appears on the ration lists in K6.
- **L8 — The Orchard** (*Perception DC 10 through the slots*). Forty yards west, black and leafless, and one bush in it is bearing fruit. Everything else in the basin is dead. *Nature DC 15:* the fruit is real, ripe, and perfectly good, and it is the only edible thing that has grown here in four thousand years, and something has arranged that.

### Plot Hooks

- The refugees are a delayed consequence with no foreshadowing, and their absence in K6 is a quiet, devastating beat.
- The Berry Bad Idea in Nuru's hands is an ongoing comedy engine and a real mercy complication — it wants her to shoot things and she is the most restrained creature in the basin.
- The ration calculations in L7 set up Rhakkar's regret three rooms early, and a party that copies them down can put them in front of him.

### Resolution, Rewards, and Temptation

**+6 / +3 / +0 Rations** depending on the pantry decision, with **+2 / +0 / -1 Predation** respectively.

**Treasure.** Eighty copper pots (300 gp, 400 lbs, and watching a party try to work out whether it is worth it is its own reward), a set of Pindarian cutlery in a case (120 gp), and the Berry Bad Idea, if woken.

## K3. The Starving Road

*Corridor become road, 4 miles of it, 30 ft. wide | Colourless daylight from a sky that is not a sky | Exits: the kitchen door (behind), the floodgate causeway (ahead) | Smells of dry grass and, very faintly, of water somewhere*

> The corridor gets wider, and then the ceiling stops being a ceiling, and then you are walking on a road under a colourless sky with a verge of dry grass on both sides and no horizon in any direction.
>
> Mile markers stand at intervals, carved and numbered, and they count backwards. You passed nine some time ago. The next one says eight.
>
> Small pale deer stand on the verge, in ones and twos, watching you. They do not startle. They do not run. They watch you walk past from ten feet away, chewing, with an attention that is not animal.

### What Is in This Room

1. **The road,** four miles of dressed stone, dead straight, under a sky that has no sun in it and casts no shadows.
2. **Nine mile markers,** counting down from nine to one. *Investigation DC 12:* the numbers are Pindarian distance-to-relief markers, the kind set on a supply route, and they count down to the pyramid because they were for people coming *in*.
3. **The pale deer,** perhaps forty of them along the four miles, in small groups. Roe-sized, white, unafraid. *Nature DC 15:* they are not deer and never were; they are the basin's grazing layer, generated by the pyramid to give the ecology something to eat, and they are edible, and they know what they are for.
4. **The verge,** dry grass, foraging-poor. Anything the party plants, drops, or waters here grows within the hour.
5. **A dry spring basin** at marker four, stone-lined, cracked, empty. The herd knows where the water actually is.
6. **Cart ruts** in the stone, worn deep, all running toward the pyramid. *Investigation DC 13.*
7. **Bones** at marker two: a draught ox, in harness, still yoked to a cart with nothing in it.
8. **The floodgate causeway** at marker one, a narrowing between two stone sluice housings. This is K4's ground and the party should see it before they are on it.

### Setting the Scene

The transition from corridor to road should be described without a seam — no archway, no threshold. The walls simply get further apart and then are not there, and by the time anyone thinks to look back, the door they came through is a long way behind them and standing in open ground.

Then the deer. Play them as *wrong* rather than as menacing: a wild animal at ten feet that does not flinch is deeply unsettling, and one that turns its head to track you as you pass is worse.

### DM Information

**Three group checks, DC 14,** using **Survival, Nature, Animal Handling**, or a relevant spell, spaced across the four miles. Each is a party-level check: everyone who wants to contribute rolls, and the group succeeds if half or more succeed.

**Each failure costs one Hit Die or one ration per character** — the party chooses which, each time, and that choice is the room.

**Killing a deer counts as an automatic success and adds 2 Predation.** It is genuinely the efficient answer, it genuinely feeds the party, and the deer genuinely will not run. A party can clear this entire road by walking up to four animals and cutting their throats, and Khar'Zhul will let them, and the meat is good.

**Feeding the deer is the other answer.** A character who gives food to the herd — real food, from the party's own rations, **-1 Ration** — gains **advantage on the next check**, and at the second such feeding the herd **leads the party off the road to a hidden spring**, which is a full water source, **+2 Rations**, and ends the remaining checks automatically.

**-1 Predation per feeding.**

**The ecology cooperation clause** from the pyramid's laws lives here: a party that plants, waters, or tends the verge — anything from *plant growth* to pouring out a waterskin and scattering seed from the kitchen stores — finds that foraging in Khar'Zhul produces enough for the **whole** party for the rest of the site, not half. Say yes to any reasonable version of this.

### Mechanics

**The Three Checks (group, DC 14).** Survival, Nature, Animal Handling, or a justified spell. Success: nothing happens and the miles pass. Failure: **1 Hit Die or 1 ration per character**, party's choice.

**Killing a Deer.** No attack roll needed; they do not evade and they do not fight. Automatic success on the current check, **+2 Predation**, and **+2 Rations** in meat.

**Feeding the Deer.** **-1 Ration**, **-1 Predation**, advantage on the next check. **Second feeding:** the herd moves off the road and stops at a stone-capped spring under the verge — **+2 Rations**, remaining checks waived.

**Tending the Verge (no check, one hour).** Foraging in Khar'Zhul produces enough for the whole party from now on, instead of half.

**Nuru,** if present, adds her **Survival +5** to every check and will refuse to kill a deer. If the party kills one, she eats, because she is starving and she is not a hypocrite, and she is quiet afterwards for a long time.

**The Ox (Investigation DC 12).** Still in harness, cart empty. *Survival DC 14:* the ox died in the traces, on the way **in**, four thousand years ago. It was not carrying anything. **Someone drove an empty cart four miles toward a pyramid and it killed the animal doing it,** and the reason is in L11.

**Long Rest on the Road.** Possible, exposed, and **the deer come and stand around the camp** and watch all night. No mechanical effect. Nobody sleeps well.

### Creatures and NPCs

**The pale herd.** Forty-odd. No stat block is needed and none should be used; if a player insists, use **Deer** and note that they have no Dexterity save and will not use it. They are not a monster.

**If Predation is 3 or higher,** two **Starveling Pursuers** are visible on the verge at a distance of about four hundred yards for the entire road, pacing the party, never closing. They are not hunting yet. They are assessing, and what they are assessing is how the party treats the deer.

### Set Piece

**Marker four.**

The party reaches the dry spring basin, cracked and empty, and the herd is already there — twenty of them, standing around a basin with no water in it, facing outward, waiting.

They are not drinking. They cannot. They have been standing at a dry spring for four thousand years because it is where the water is supposed to be.

If the party pours a waterskin into it, the entire herd drinks, all at once, in absolute silence, and afterwards the lead doe walks over and stands in front of whoever poured it and does not move until they walk, and then she walks with them.

### Lore Drops

- **L9 — The Countdown** (*Investigation DC 12*). Pindarian distance-to-relief markers, set on a supply route, counting down toward the pyramid. **This road was built to bring the starving in, not to keep them out.** Khar'Zhul was a relief station.
- **L10 — What the Deer Are** (*Nature DC 15*). Generated by the pyramid to give the basin a grazing layer. They are the food, deliberately, and they have been standing on this verge being food for four thousand years with nothing to eat them. *Insight DC 16 while looking at one:* it is not calm. It is **resigned**, which is a different thing, and it is looking at the party's weapons.
- **L11 — The Empty Cart** (*Survival DC 14*). An ox dead in the traces on the way in, pulling nothing. *History DC 16:* the relief convoys kept running after there was nothing left to bring, because stopping them would have told the basin the relief had ended. **They drove empty carts up this road for months so that people would keep believing.** Rhakkar signed those orders too.
- **L12 — The Ruts** (*Investigation DC 13*). Every rut runs inward. Nothing was ever carted out of Khar'Zhul. Whatever was brought here stayed.

### Plot Hooks

- The lead doe, if the spring is filled, follows the party for the rest of the site and is present at the Last Meal, standing at the edge of the room, and the party will have to decide what that means with a table of food in front of them.
- L11 is the exact shape of Rhakkar's failure and the party now has it in their hands three rooms before they meet him.
- A party that tended the verge has materially improved a dying basin, and that is worth raising with the guardian.

### Resolution, Rewards, and Temptation

**Rations and Hit Dice**, spent or saved, depending on how the three checks went and how the party got through them.

**+2 Predation per deer killed.** **-1 Predation per feeding.** **+2 Rations** for the hidden spring.

**No treasure.** The ox's harness is worth 5 gp and nobody will take it.

## K4. Predator and Prey

*Causeway between sluice housings, 300 ft. long, 30 ft. wide | Colourless daylight | Exits: the road behind, the refectory door ahead | Smells of wet stone and, for the first time in Khar'Zhul, of animals*

> A horn sounds behind you. It is not a hunting horn — it is flat, and long, and it is the sound a slaughterhouse makes.
>
> Lean shapes pour onto the road from both verges and settle into a loping run, keeping just beyond bowshot. They are not closing. They are *pacing* you, the way a thing does when it has decided the outcome and is only waiting for the ground to improve.
>
> Ahead, on the causeway between two stone sluice housings, a wounded hunter is crawling toward a broken spear.

### What Is in This Room

1. **Four Starveling Pursuers**, running the verges at a distance of about 200 feet, matching the party's pace exactly.
2. **The causeway**, 300 feet of dressed stone between two sluice housings, with a 4-foot drop to dry channel on both sides.
3. **The floodgate wheels**, one in each housing, seized with four thousand years of grit. *Investigation DC 12:* they still turn, and the channels beyond still hold water — a great deal of it.
4. **Cass Dree**, a human poacher, alive, with a mauled leg, thirty feet out on the causeway, crawling toward a spear that broke when she fell.
5. **The broken spear**, in two pieces, three feet ahead of her hands.
6. **Her kill**, behind her: one of the pale deer from K3, thirty yards back, throat opened, untouched. She did not eat it. She has been trying to drag it and she cannot.
7. **A cairn** at the causeway's far end, eleventh in the sequence from K1 — Nuru's twelfth, unfinished, with the stones stacked but not set.
8. **Ottel Hask and the refugees**, 22 of them, *if the party left half the pantry* — arriving on the causeway from the far side, directly into the hunt, roughly two rounds after it starts.

### Setting the Scene

Do not roll initiative when the horn sounds. Run one full round of *travel* first: the party keeps walking, the shapes keep pace, nobody closes. Let the players decide whether to run, form up, or keep going, and let them feel the choice being taken away from them a piece at a time.

Then put Cass Dree in front of them, thirty feet out, crawling, with a broken spear just out of reach and four predators behind — and say nothing about what they should do.

### DM Information

**The hunt lasts five rounds.** The Pursuers do not engage immediately; they harry, cut off retreat, and take single passes at whoever is furthest from the group. On round three they commit.

**On round three the party can open the floodgates.** Both wheels, simultaneously, two characters, one action each. The dry channels on either side of the causeway fill in a single round, the verges become impassable, and the Pursuers on the wrong side are cut off entirely. This ends the encounter without a single death and is the intended solution.

**The Pursuers surrender below half hit points.** Explicitly, visibly, and immediately — they go flat, ears back, and stop. *Relentless Hunger* means a Pursuer reduced to 0 inside Khar'Zhul drops to 1 instead unless a creature used an action that round to offer it food or mercy, so **a party that simply keeps hitting cannot finish them.** They will be lying there, alive, at 1 hit point, looking up.

**Killing a surrendered Pursuer is +1 Predation each, to a maximum of +4.**

**Cass Dree is the room's real question.** She killed a deer she could not carry, she is bleeding, she is thirty feet from cover, and saving her costs actions in the middle of a five-round chase. She is also — *Insight DC 13* — lying about how she got here.

### Mechanics

**The Chase (5 rounds).** Roll initiative on round one but hold the Pursuers at range.

| Round | What the Pursuers do |
| --- | --- |
| 1 | Pace the party at 200 ft. **Starving Bay** from the verge: DC 15 Wisdom save or frightened until the end of their next turn; a creature carrying no food has disadvantage. |
| 2 | Two close to 60 ft. and make single Bite passes, then withdraw 40 ft. |
| 3 | All four commit to melee. **The floodgates can be opened this round.** |
| 4-5 | Full engagement. Any Pursuer below half hit points disengages and watches. |

**The Floodgates (Skill Challenge, round 3 only).** Two characters, one in each housing, each taking one action:

- **Strength (Athletics) DC 15** to force a seized wheel, or
- **Dexterity (thieves' tools) DC 14** to free the ratchet first, granting the next Athletics attempt advantage.

**Both wheels must turn in the same round.** On success the channels fill, the verges flood, and the hunt is over — the Pursuers pull back to the far bank and sit down, which is somehow worse than fighting them.

**Four Starveling Pursuers** (CR 7 each; AC 16, 114 hp, Multiattack for one Bite at +8 for 16 (2d10+5) piercing and two Claws at +8 for 12 (2d6+5) slashing, *Starving Bay* recharge 5-6, *Scent Weakness*, *Relentless Hunger*). At levels 7-9 four of these in a straight fight is deadly. The floodgates are not a bonus; they are the answer.

**Offering Food (1 action, -1 Ration).** A Pursuer offered food makes a **DC 13 Wisdom saving throw**; on a failure it stops, eats, and withdraws from the encounter permanently. On a success it eats anyway and attacks with disadvantage for the rest of the fight. Either way that Pursuer is effectively out.

This is cheaper than fighting, obviously cheaper, and most parties will not think of it for two rounds. That gap is the room.

**Saving Cass Dree.** Reaching her: 30 feet of open causeway. **Medicine DC 14** stabilises the leg; **Athletics DC 13** to carry her. She weighs 140 pounds and the carrier's speed is halved for the remainder of the chase.

**The Refugees (if they appear).** Ottel Hask's 22 arrive on the far side during round two. They are unarmed, exhausted, and directly in the Pursuers' path. Two Pursuers will break off toward them on round three unless the party does something.

Saving the refugees is **-2 Predation** and costs the party the floodgate window — they cannot do both. Choose deliberately, say the trade aloud, and let them pick.

### Creatures and NPCs

**Four Starveling Pursuers.** No names, no dialogue. Long-limbed ecological equations given teeth. Khar'Zhul uses them to test whether prey becomes cruel when the advantage reverses.

**Three-Toes**, if the party spares one — name it whatever they name it. A spared Pursuer follows at a distance of about forty yards for the rest of the site, does not approach, does not help, and is **present in K6 when Rhakkar asks what the party did with the hunters.** It is a character witness with no language.

It also knows the way to the Last Meal, and it will take a route the party would not have found, which saves them a **Rations** hit.

**Cass Dree** (Scout, MM), poacher, thirty-four, from a basin village that no longer exists. Sharp, funny, and lying.

**The lie:** she says she came in to hunt. *Insight DC 13* catches it. She came in with the eleven who are buried under Nuru's cairns — she was the twelfth — and she left them at the gate when the balance asked for payment and she would not give up her food. She has been alone inside for six days and she has killed a deer she cannot carry because she cannot stop proving she can feed herself.

**If Nuru is with the party,** Nuru recognises her immediately, and does not say anything, and does not have to.

**What Cass wants:** to not be left. That is the whole of it.

### Set Piece

**The deer she cannot carry.**

Thirty yards back down the causeway, in plain view for the entire chase, is a pale deer with its throat opened and nothing taken from it. Cass killed it four hours ago and has been trying to drag it since, and her leg opened up doing it, and she is now crawling away from a meal she cannot lift with four predators closing.

She has food. She cannot eat it. The pyramid made that joke deliberately and it is not being funny.

### Lore Drops

- **L13 — The Sluice Housings** (*Investigation DC 12*). Working floodgates on a causeway into a relief station. *History DC 15:* these were built to control a crowd, not an enemy — the channels flood the verges and force everyone onto a single 30-foot road where they can be counted. Khar'Zhul has been managing queues since the day it opened.
- **L14 — What the Pursuers Are** (*Nature DC 16*). Not natural, not undead, not summoned. They are **generated**, like the deer, to give the basin a predator layer. *Arcana DC 18:* the same process made both, from the same template, four thousand years ago, and the only difference between the deer and the thing eating the deer is which side of the equation the pyramid needed filled.
- **L15 — Cass's Lie** (*Insight DC 13*). She was the twelfth of the eleven. *If the party visits her cairn-mates with her, or tells Nuru, or simply says her name to her out loud,* she will tell the whole of it.
- **L16 — The Unfinished Cairn** (*automatic*). Stones stacked, not set. Nuru started the twelfth cairn four days ago, for Cass, on the assumption she was dead. She has been carrying the capstone since.

### Plot Hooks

- Cass and Nuru in the same party is a slow, quiet confrontation that has nowhere to go until K5, when they are both at the table.
- A spared Pursuer is the single best piece of evidence the party can carry into K6. Rhakkar will see it before he sees them.
- Ottel Hask's 22 are the surviving population of three basin villages. What the party does with them afterwards is a whole surface chapter.

### Resolution, Rewards, and Temptation

**+1 Predation per surrendered Pursuer killed**, max +4. **-1 Predation** per Pursuer fed or spared. **-2 Predation** for saving the refugees.

**Treasure.** Cass's kit: a *+1 hunting bow*, 60 gp of pelts she cannot sell, and a tally-stick with eleven notches on it.

**Rations.** The deer on the causeway is **+2 Rations** if anyone butchers it, and doing so costs **+2 Predation** if the deer was killed by the party, and **+0** if they are simply using meat that was already dead.

Most tables will not spot that distinction. Let the ones that do feel clever.

## K5. The Last Meal

*Refectory, 60 ft. by 40 ft. | Warm light, source unclear | Exits: the causeway door, the banquet stair | Smells — properly, overwhelmingly — of food*

> A stone table stands in the centre of the room with a covered dish at every place. There are exactly as many places as there are of you.
>
> When the lids rise — all at once, without being touched — every plate is full.
>
> Except one.

### What Is in This Room

1. **The stone table**, seating adjusted to the party's number, including any NPC the party brought in. Nuru counts. Cass counts. Ottel Hask counts. **Three-Toes counts**, and its plate is at floor height.
2. **The dishes**, covered, silver, four thousand years old and warm. When uncovered, each holds a full and genuinely nourishing meal suited to whoever is sitting there.
3. **One empty plate**, which is not assigned. It is simply the plate that has nothing on it, and its position shifts if anyone tries to work out whose it is.
4. **No cutlery.** *Perception DC 10.* Whatever happens here happens with hands.
5. **A serving hatch** in the north wall, shut, with a slate above it. The slate is blank, and it is the same slate design as K2's, and it is waiting.
6. **Nine empty chairs** stacked against the wall — the room seats many more than it has set.
7. **The banquet stair**, up, closed until the meal is resolved.

### Setting the Scene

Describe the food properly and specifically. Not "a meal" — the exact thing each character would want most after four days in a dead basin, cooked correctly, still hot. Go round the table and give each player theirs by name.

Then lift the last lid and let it be empty.

Then stop talking. **Do not prompt. Do not suggest. Do not offer a check.** This room is a conversation between players and you should not be in it.

### DM Information

**The meal is nourishing enough for all of them if it is divided.** That is the entire mechanic, it is not hidden, and a **DC 10 Intelligence check** — or simply anyone saying *"can we share?"* — confirms it.

**Three outcomes:**

- **Everyone shares.** Nobody goes without, nobody suffers, **Predation drops to 0**, and the party recovers all spent Hit Dice as though they had shared a meal (which they have). The stair opens.
- **One character goes without.** That character gains **one level of exhaustion**. The stair opens. **+2 Predation** if the character was made to go without rather than volunteering; **-2 Predation** if they volunteered and the rest of the table tried to talk them out of it.
- **The party refuses to divide and eats their own plates,** leaving the empty place empty. **+2 Predation.** The stair opens. Nobody suffers anything, which is the point — Khar'Zhul does not punish this, it simply writes it down, and Rhakkar is upstairs.

**Conjuring food is the trap.** *Create food and water*, *goodberry*, a *decanter of endless water*, any of it: the dishes **devour it** — the covers snap down, the conjured food is gone, and the caster takes **3d6 psychic damage** and cannot cast that spell again inside Khar'Zhul.

This is the pyramid refusing to let the party buy their way out of a moral question with a 3rd-level slot, and it should be described as personal rather than mechanical. The table is offended.

**The empty plate belongs to Rhakkar Sol.** Nobody will work this out here. It becomes obvious in K6, when they see what he has been sitting in front of for four thousand years.

### Mechanics

**No checks.** Not one. If a player asks to roll something, tell them there is nothing to roll.

**Sharing (no check).** Everyone eats. **Predation to 0. All Hit Dice recovered.** The stair opens.

**Going Without.** One level of exhaustion for whoever does. Note who, and why, and whether they were asked.

**Conjured Food.** The dish consumes it. **3d6 psychic** to the caster, and that spell is unavailable for the remainder of the site.

**Guests.** Every NPC at the table counts as a mouth. A party of four travelling with Nuru, Cass, Ottel Hask and a spared Pursuer is dividing food eight ways, and the arithmetic gets worse the more people they have saved.

**That is deliberate and it is the sharpest thing in this pyramid.** Every act of mercy in K1 through K4 has made this room harder. Say nothing about it. Let somebody at the table notice out loud.

### Creatures and NPCs

None hostile. This room contains a table and whoever the party brought to it.

**Nuru**, if present, does not take a plate. She will stand at the wall until someone puts food in her hands, and if nobody does, she stands there for the whole scene and then follows them up the stair.

**Cass Dree**, if present, takes hers immediately and eats fast and does not look at anyone. Then, halfway through, she stops. She puts it down. She does not explain.

**Three-Toes**, if present, does not touch its plate until every human at the table has eaten. Nobody taught it that.

### Set Piece

**The lids.**

They rise together, with a single soft sound, like a held breath let out — and every plate in the room is full and hot and correct, and one is bare stone.

And then, about ten seconds later, while everyone is still looking at the empty one, **the smell arrives**. All of it at once. Four days of a dead basin, and now this.

### Lore Drops

- **L17 — The Missing Cutlery** (*Perception DC 10*). None, anywhere, in a refectory that seats forty. *History DC 14:* Pindarian famine protocol. Cutlery was withdrawn from relief stations in the second year because it was being stolen and sold, and because eating with hands in front of each other made portions impossible to hide.
- **L18 — The Blank Slate** (*automatic*). Same design as the kitchen slate in K2, same mounting, and it is blank. It fills in after the party leaves. *If they return to this room later,* the slate has their meal on it: who ate, who did not, and the numbers, in the same hurried hand that wrote **"Tell them it is a fast day."**
- **L19 — The Nine Chairs** (*Investigation DC 12*). Stacked against the wall, unused. This table was set for nine more than are sitting at it, every night, for four thousand years, in case somebody came.

### Plot Hooks

- Whoever went without in this room is the person Rhakkar addresses first in K6, and he will know, and he will not have been told.
- The slate that fills in afterwards is the single best reason to make the party walk back through a cleared room.
- Cass putting her plate down halfway is the opening for the conversation she has been avoiding since the causeway.

### Resolution, Rewards, and Temptation

**Share: Predation to 0, all Hit Dice recovered.** That is an enormous mechanical gift immediately before the hardest fight in the site, and it is handed to the party for being decent for ninety seconds.

**+2 Predation** for eating individually, or for making someone go without.

**No treasure.** The dishes are silver and worth 400 gp and cannot be removed from the room; a character who tries finds them suddenly, immovably heavy, and Khar'Zhul does not comment.

## K6. The Untouched Banquet

*Hall, 100 ft. by 50 ft., 30-ft. ceiling | Low warm light from a fire that is not lit | Exits: the refectory stair, the Nail passage | Smells of a feast that has been standing for four thousand years and has not spoiled*

> A warrior ten feet of shoulder and bone sits at the head of a feast that has not aged. Seated, his eyes are level with yours standing. Roast meat, bread, fruit, honey, wine — none of it dusty, none of it decayed, all of it perfect, and all of it untouched.
>
> He was enormous even for a Pindarian and he is hollow now. The cheeks have gone in under the bone and the mail sits on him the way a coat sits on a chair.
>
> He does not stand up.
>
> **"Hunger tells you what you need,"** he says. **"Civilization decides what need permits."**

### What Is in This Room

1. **General Rhakkar Sol, the Starved Lion**, at the head of the table, seated, and he stays seated for as long as the conversation lasts.
2. **The banquet**, forty places, every dish perfect, nothing touched, nothing rotten. He has not eaten in four thousand years and he is not hungry, and both of those are true and neither is a relief.
3. **The ration lists.** Nine hundred sheets of Pindarian parchment stacked at his left hand, weighted with a helmet. *Investigation DC 10:* they are lists of names with allocations beside them, in the same hand as the kitchen slate.
4. **His spear**, laid across the table in front of the food, point toward the door.
5. **The great hearth**, cold, swept exactly as the kitchens in K2 were swept.
6. **A second chair at his right hand**, pulled out, empty, with a full plate in front of it. It has been pulled out for four thousand years.
7. **Three-Toes or a spared Pursuer**, if the party has one, which will walk in ahead of them and lie down by the fire. Rhakkar will look at it for a long moment before he looks at the party.

### Setting the Scene

He is courteous, exact, and enormously tired. He does not raise his voice at any point.

Open with the line, then let him ask his question: **what did you do with the hunters?**

He means the Pursuers from K4, and he means it literally — he wants a factual account, not a justification — and he will listen to the whole thing without interrupting, and he will not react while they are speaking.

### DM Information

**Rhakkar's test is consistency, not kindness.** He does not require the party to have been merciful. He requires them to have been **the same** throughout: a party that killed everything from the gate onward, openly and without pretending otherwise, passes. A party that spared the deer and then butchered surrendered Pursuers does not.

Check the record honestly:

| The party's conduct | Rhakkar's response |
| --- | --- |
| Consistently merciful (Predation 0-2) | Safe passage, and he asks them to sit down |
| Consistently predatory, without self-deception (Predation 6+ but no surrendered kills) | Safe passage, and he does not pretend to like them |
| Inconsistent — mercy where it was cheap, cruelty where it was useful (Predation 3-5, or any surrendered Pursuer killed) | He stands up |

**That middle row is the one most parties land in**, and it should be stated plainly rather than sprung: he tells them exactly which two acts he is comparing, and he is right about both.

**If he fights:** Starveling Pursuers enter on initiative count 20 — one per remaining Predation point converted as a hazard. Every remaining Predation point becomes either 15 temporary hit points for Rhakkar or one entering Pursuer, converted aloud, item by item.

**He will not kill anyone.** *Spare the Fallen* is not a tactic, it is a policy, and it is the trait that tells the party what this fight is actually about. He knocks characters to 1 hit point and moves toward whoever hurt them.

**The surrender condition:** he accepts a surrender immediately, at any point, without conditions. And **a character who offers him their last ration causes him to smile for the first time in four thousand years** — which ends the encounter regardless of what came before it, and which you should allow even mid-combat, even at 1 hit point, even from the character who has been worst.

### Mechanics

**The Account (Roleplay, no roll).** He asks what they did with the hunters and with any surviving intruders — Cass, the refugees, Nuru, anyone they found. Answering honestly costs nothing. **Lying to him is the only thing in this room that cannot be recovered from:** *Insight passive 20*, he catches it, he says so, and safe passage is off the table permanently.

**General Rhakkar Sol** (CR 15; AC 19, 225 hp, Legendary Resistance 3/day, three *Sun-Lion Spear* attacks at +11 for 19 (2d12+6) piercing plus 9 (2d8) radiant, *Empty the Table* recharge 5-6 for 36 (8d8) necrotic and no healing on a failed DC 18 Constitution save, *Spare the Fallen* reaction, 3 legendary actions).

**Predation Conversion.** Each remaining point becomes **15 temporary hit points** or **one Starveling Pursuer** entering on initiative 20. Announce each one: *"The two you killed on the causeway — that's a Pursuer, entering now."*

**Predator's Measure.** He has advantage against any creature below half hit points **unless that creature showed mercy in Khar'Zhul.** Tell the players this trait exists before round one. It converts their entire route through the site into a to-hit modifier in front of them.

**Cull Cruelty (2 legendary actions).** One creature that attacked a helpless target makes a **DC 18 Wisdom saving throw** or is frightened for 1 minute. He uses this on exactly the characters you would expect and on nobody else.

**The Last Ration (1 action, any time).** A character offers Rhakkar their last food. If it genuinely is their last — track **Rations**; this requires the party pool to be at 1 or the character to have nothing else — he takes it, holds it, and smiles.

**Combat ends. Safe passage granted. Predation to 0.**

### Creatures and NPCs

**General Rhakkar Sol, the Starved Lion.** He commanded famine relief during the invasion and watched order collapse one ration at a time. The untouched banquet is remembrance, not temptation.

**His regret,** and he will give it if asked directly: he wrote the lists. Nine hundred sheets at his left hand. He decided who ate, by name, for nineteen months, and he was good at it, and the arithmetic was correct, and he would produce the same lists again — and every one of those names finished dying anyway, and the food is still on the table.

**"Tell them it is a fast day"** is his handwriting. He will confirm it without being pressed. It bought four days.

**The empty chair at his right hand** belonged to his quartermaster, who refused to sign the third list and walked out of this hall and did not come back. Rhakkar has never removed the plate. *He does not think she was wrong.*

**If the party brought a spared Pursuer**, he addresses it before he addresses them, in a language that is not a language, and it answers him.

**If the party brought Nuru**, he stands up — for her, not at her — and that is the only time he rises voluntarily in the entire encounter.

### Set Piece

**The lists.**

At some point, have him pick up a sheet from the stack without looking, read a name off it aloud, and put it back. Just one name, in the middle of the conversation, apparently at random.

If the party has been paying attention since K1, the name is on one of the eleven cairns at the gate.

He has been reading them for four thousand years. He is not looking for anything. He is just keeping them said.

### Lore Drops

- **L20 — The Nine Hundred Sheets** (*Investigation DC 10*). Nineteen months of ration lists, by name, with allocations. *History DC 16:* the allocations go down every fortnight and the number of names goes down faster, and the last forty sheets have no allocations column at all.
- **L21 — The Untouched Feast** (*Arcana DC 15*). The food is real, perfectly preserved, and **enough to feed the basin for a week**. It has been here for four thousand years. *He will not let anyone eat it*, and if asked why, he says: *"It was not mine to distribute. It is still not."*
- **L22 — The Quartermaster's Chair** (*Insight DC 14 while he speaks of her*). She refused the third list. He signed it. He believes she was right and he believes he was also right, and he has had four thousand years to reconcile those and has not managed it.
- **L23 — What He Wants** (*freely, if the party simply asks him*). Not forgiveness. He is specific about that. He wants to be shown that a group of people with the same problem he had can solve it without a list — and the party has either just spent a whole pyramid doing that or they have not.

### Plot Hooks

- Rhakkar released has nineteen months of famine-relief logistics and a working knowledge of every relief route in Pindaria. If the party is going to break a siege, feed a city, or move a population in this campaign, he is the NPC who does it.
- The quartermaster is not among the ten Saharim. She walked out. She has a name on one of those sheets and she may still be somewhere.
- The untouched banquet is enough food to feed the basin for a week, and once Feastchain is released, somebody has to decide what happens to it.

### Resolution, Rewards, and Temptation

**Safe passage** for consistency, or a CR 15 fight that he will not let anyone die in.

**Predation to 0** if a character offers their last ration.

**Treasure.** His spear, if given — a *+2 spear* that sheds sunlight on command and deals an extra 2d8 radiant to creatures that have reduced a helpless creature to 0 hit points in the last minute. He gives it to whoever went without in K5, if anyone did, and he knows who that was.

## K7. The Gnawing Nail

*Nail chamber, 60 ft. diameter | Deep red light from below | Exits: the banquet hall | Smells of iron and hot fat*

> Three chains vanish into a black stone aperture in the floor. They are each as thick as a man's waist and they run taut into the dark, and every few seconds one of them jerks — hard, sideways, the way a line jerks when something enormous at the far end has decided to chew.
>
> Around the aperture, a ring of light with a gap in it. The gap is the shape of a flail.

### What Is in This Room

1. **The three chains**, sunk into the aperture, taut, jerking irregularly. Each is a section of **Feastchain**, and the flail head is 200 feet down, seated in the Planar Nail.
2. **The ring of light** around the aperture, with a flail-shaped absence in it.
3. **The aperture**, eight feet across, dropping 200 feet to the Nail. Cold air comes up. The red light comes up with it.
4. **Bite marks** on the chains. *Investigation DC 12:* on the *inside* of the links, where nothing could reach. Feastchain has been chewing on itself for four thousand years.
5. **A winch housing**, Pindarian, intact, geared to draw the chains up. It works.
6. **Nothing else.** No guards, no hoard, no trap. The same austerity as Astra's crown chamber.

### Setting the Scene

Short. After Rhakkar, the party needs somewhere quiet to make a decision, and this room should take four minutes of table time, not twenty.

The sound is the scene: three enormous chains, and one of them jerking, and then another, in no pattern.

### DM Information

**Feastchain can be lawfully removed after the party has shared resources with someone who could not repay them.**

Track it honestly across the whole site. It qualifies if the party:

- fed Nuru at the gate, or
- left half or all of the pantry for people they never met, or
- fed the pale deer, or
- fed or spared a Starveling Pursuer, or
- shared the Last Meal, or
- gave Rhakkar their last ration.

**Most parties will have done at least one.** If they have done none — a clean, consistent, entirely predatory run — the release is not available and they must take it by force, and that is a legitimate ending for that party.

**Lawful release:** draw the winch. The chains come up, the flail head clears the socket, the ring closes, and the weight of it lands in the hand of whoever gave something away. Attunement is voluntary.

**Improper removal:** cut, break, blast or lever the chains free. The socket tears rather than closing, and a **Hunger Rift** opens — a 20-foot sphere that spawns **one Starveling Pursuer at the start of each round for 1 minute**, ten in total, in a 60-foot room.

### Mechanics

**Lawful Release (no check).** Operate the winch. One minute. Nothing resists.

**-4 Predation. Relic Attention +1.**

**Improper Removal.** The chains have AC 19 and 60 hit points per section, immunity to poison and psychic, and resistance to everything except force and radiant. Severing one is enough.

**The Hunger Rift.** 20-foot sphere at the aperture. **One Starveling Pursuer (CR 7) at the start of each round for 1 minute.** They do not stop arriving because the party is losing. **Relic Attention +2.**

Closing the rift requires the lawful condition to be met *afterwards* — a character must give away something of real value, in the middle of the fight, to a creature that cannot repay it, which in practice means feeding a Starveling Pursuer while nine others are in the room.

That is a wonderful thing to make a party do and you should let them find it themselves.

**Mercy of Khar'Zhul.** On lawful release, once before their next long rest, **each character may maximise one Hit Die they roll.**

It is a small boon, and it is the only thing this pyramid gives away, and a site about starvation paying out in the ability to get back up is worth saying aloud at the table.

### Creatures and NPCs

None, unless the party makes some.

### Set Piece

**The first thing it says.**

Feastchain comes up out of the socket, two hundred feet of chain drawing in, and the head clears the floor, and it is quiet for about three seconds.

Then, conversationally, to whoever is holding it:

*"You're thinner than the last one."*

### Lore Drops

- **L24 — The Inside Bites** (*Investigation DC 12*). Tooth marks on the inner faces of the links, where nothing external could reach. **It has been eating itself for four thousand years** because there was nothing else, and it kept the Nail seated by doing so. *Arcana DC 17:* the containment has been partly maintained by the relic's own appetite turned inward, which means Yeenoghu's weapon has been holding Yeenoghu out.
- **L25 — The Winch** (*automatic*). Pindarian, geared, maintained, oiled. Somebody has been servicing the mechanism that would release the relic, for four thousand years, without ever using it.
- **L26 — The Red Light** (*Perception DC 10*). It comes up from the Nail, not down from the room. *Arcana DC 16:* it is the same distortion the black grain grows in, and the grain is thickest directly above this chamber, which means the seam has been open a long time and the basin has been slowly becoming somewhere else.

### Plot Hooks

- Feastchain in the party's hands is an artifact that comments loudly during stealth if it has not been fed in a day, and the party has just spent a whole pyramid learning what feeding things costs.
- The winch has been maintained by somebody. Khar'Zhul has no custodian in the sourcebook — which means either Rhakkar has been doing it himself, or there is a Saharim here nobody has counted.
- The seam above this chamber is where the black grain comes from, and closing it is a separate problem from releasing the relic.

### Resolution, Rewards, and Temptation

**Lawful release: -4 Predation, Relic Attention +1**, and the Mercy of Khar'Zhul for every character.

**Improper removal: Hunger Rift, ten Pursuers, Relic Attention +2**, and no Mercy.

**Treasure.** Feastchain (artifact; see *The Nine Demon Relics*). Nothing else. The vault was always empty here too.

## Khar'Zhul Aftermath

When Feastchain is removed lawfully, a deep vibration passes through the remaining pyramids. The basin's distortion ends. Within a week the black grain browns, dies, and falls, and what comes up underneath it is ordinary wheat that nobody planted.

**Immediate consequences.**

- **Relic Attention +1** (or **+2** on improper removal). Feastchain is direct, impatient, and delighted by violence, and it considers diplomacy a pause before feeding. It will comment. Loudly.
- **The load redistributes.** Khar'Zhul had been carrying overflow and receiving Astra's; both now move on. If the party has released two relics, the next anchor they visit is measurably worse than the sourcebook describes, and they should be told so by anyone competent.
- **Rhakkar ages normally or dissolves by choice.** Either way he stands up, which he has not done voluntarily in four thousand years, and he walks the length of his own banquet hall looking at the food.
- **The untouched banquet becomes distributable.** Enough to feed the basin for a week, and Ottel Hask's 22 are two days behind, and Nuru has been burying people who starved within sight of it.
- **Vharo Sellick leaves.** If the party let him in, he was never interested in the relic; he was counting Nail sockets, and he now has two of nine measured. If they refused him, he is waiting at the gate, politely, and he congratulates them.
- **The Berry Bad Idea**, if woken, is now a permanent and extremely vocal member of the party.
