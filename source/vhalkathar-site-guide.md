# Vhal'Kathar: Pyramid of the Last Breath - Site Guide

*Levels 9-11 | Children of the Void | Death into Acceptance | Relic: Gravewake | Guardian: Kathandar, Elder Dragonlord*

Beneath the city of Wahyrst, on the snow-capped peak of Kharyl, buried under four thousand years of somebody else's history.

## Running Vhal'Kathar

Astra asked what you want. Vhal'Kathar asks what you refuse to put down.

This is an archive, not a tomb, and the distinction is the whole adventure. The dead here are articulate, catalogued, and available — and the pyramid's trial is whether a living creature can love someone who is gone without attempting to own them. Grief is permitted. Devotion is permitted. Command is not. Every mechanical system in this site measures the difference.

The site runs in three acts, and only the third is Pindarian.

**Act I — Wahyrst Under Siege.** A minotaur city on a mountain peak, surrounded by an army of ice giants, out of food and nearly out of will. The party's commander is murdered in front of them, the ruling council is massacred in front of them, and a masked figure announces a race for something beneath the city. Roughly one session.

**Act II — The Upper Necropolis.** The catacombs beneath Wahyrst: a yuan-ti cult, a Medusa Queen, and a mummy lord's hoard, none of which are Pindarian. These are squatters who tunnelled into the top of the pyramid and built a temple in its attic. Two to three sessions.

**Act III — The Pyramid of the Last Breath.** The actual site. Seven trials, one dragonlord, one mace driven through a disk of bone. Two sessions.

### Pindarian Scale

**Pindarians are Large.** All of them, always — the Solaari, the Lunari, the Veylari, the Ilyri, every
Saharim guardian and every soldier in every rank. Eight to ten feet, long-boned, and long-lived, with a
reach and a stride to match: **10-foot reach on their melee attacks and a 10-foot space.**

This is not a variant or a subtype. It is what the species is, and it is worth showing rather than stating —
Pindarian doorways are twelve feet high, their stairs have deep treads, their chairs do not fit anyone else,
and a party of Medium adventurers spends nine pyramids using furniture built for people half again their
size.

Human, elven and dwarven NPCs met in and around the sites are Medium as usual. The contrast is the point.

### The Laws of Vhal'Kathar

State these through play before enforcing them. All four are active from the moment the party crosses into Act III, and the first two are noticeable in Act II as strange local phenomena.

1. **Magic that returns the dead to life automatically fails inside the pyramid.** *Revivify, raise dead, resurrection,* and *true resurrection* all expend their resources and do nothing. A character who tries it feels the spell arrive, find the soul, and be politely declined.
2. **A dead creature can speak for 1 minute when its name is read aloud.** Any dead creature. The party will work out what this means somewhere around the third time they use it, and what it means is that the Black Catalogue is the most dangerous object in the campaign.
3. **Necrotic damage dealt by a creature also reduces that creature's hit point maximum by the same amount** until it finishes a long rest outside the pyramid. This applies to the party, to Gravewake, and to every undead in Acts II and III.
4. **Destroying a peaceful spirit adds 1 Refusal.** So does commanding the dead.

### The Refusal Track

Keep it visible, as with Astra's Temptation. It runs 0 to 6.

| Event | Refusal |
| --- | --- |
| Destroying a spirit that was not hostile | +1 |
| Animating, commanding, or compelling any dead creature | +1 |
| Taking a letter from the Archive (V3) | +1 |
| Taking treasure from a named grave | +1 each grave |
| Claiming aloud that the dead belong to the living | +2 |
| Lighting a candle in the Procession with a truthful memory | -1 |
| Reading a name aloud and answering what it says | -1 |
| Burying or interring a body properly, at cost | -2 |
| Leaving One More Day (V4) willingly | -2 |

**At 3.** The archive's defences wake. Processional Remnants begin forming in any room where a spirit was destroyed, one per hour, and they know the party's names.

**At 6.** The pyramid stops distinguishing the party from grave robbers. Every dead creature in Vhal'Kathar — including the yuan-ti's victims, including the mummies, including Wahyrst's own war dead two hundred feet above — rises at once and moves toward the Nail. Kathandar does not intervene. He has been waiting four thousand years for confirmation that the living cannot be trusted with the dead, and the party will have supplied it.

### Breath Tokens

A small cold weight in the palm, like a held breath. A character may spend a Breath Token to **automatically succeed on one death saving throw**, or to **speak one sentence to a creature that died within the last minute, and hear one sentence back.**

Tokens are awarded for acts of letting go: leaving V4 willingly, answering a name honestly at V1, completing an interment. They do not stack beyond three per character, and they do not leave the pyramid — an unspent token dissolves when the party surfaces, which is the correct and slightly cruel design.

### The Race Clock

Vhaskar Corr is also going down there, and he started first. Run an eight-segment clock in the open.

Advance it **one segment** each time the party takes a long rest, spends a session day in Wahyrst, or backtracks a full level. Advance it **two** if they take the safe route where a dangerous shortcut existed.

| Segments | What the party finds |
| --- | --- |
| 0-2 | Nothing. His people are still above them. |
| 3-4 | Fresh bodies in cleared rooms. Yuan-ti with crossbow bolts in their necks. |
| 5-6 | Two of the seven Pindarian trials already resolved, badly — a Procession with every candle lit and blown out, an Archive with drawers emptied into sacks. |
| 7 | Vhaskar reaches the Vigil. Kathandar is fighting him when the party arrives. |
| 8 | Vhaskar has the Black Catalogue. He does not have Gravewake. He does not need it any more. |

The clock is not a punishment for resting. It is a reason to make interesting choices, and at 7 it hands the party the best possible version of the finale: arriving to find the boss and the guardian already at each other's throats, and having to choose which one they help.

### Safety at This Table

This site is built on grief, and Act II contains imprisoned and murdered children by design. Confirm lines and veils before the session, name the content of rooms 11 and 17 explicitly during that conversation, and be prepared to run the necropolis with those rooms redressed — the yuan-ti larder works equally well full of adults, and the sacrificial chamber works with any captive population. Nothing downstream depends on the victims being children.

Act III asks players to speak to their characters' dead. Silence is always a valid answer and never costs anything mechanically.

## W1. Upon the Battlements

*Northern curtain wall, Wahyrst | Night, snow, firelight from below | Exits: wall stair to Lampborough, gatehouse to the north tower | Smells of woodsmoke, hot pitch, unwashed soldiers, and the particular iron smell of a city that has stopped burying its dead promptly*

> The northern wall of Wahyrst runs a mile along the cliff edge, and you have walked it every night for a week. Below, in the dark, the giants' fires make a second city — bigger than the first, and patient. Something huge is being winched into position out there. It has been being winched into position for three days.
>
> Your commander walks beside you with her helm under her arm. Aelied Axebiter is eight feet of scarred minotaur in a mail coat that has been repaired forty times, and she has the unhurried stride of someone who has already decided how this ends. She turns to you with complete seriousness and asks:
>
> **"So. Why should you never trust a minotaur?"**

### What Is in This Room

1. **Aelied Axebiter,** your commander, currently alive, currently telling a joke. She has a wineskin at her hip, a double-bladed battleaxe across her back, and a thin bronze disc on a thong around her neck that she touches when she is thinking.
2. **The wall itself,** forty feet of granite, twenty feet thick, with fighting steps, murder holes, and eleven scorpion emplacements along this stretch. Four of the eleven are broken. *Investigation DC 12:* they were not broken by giants. The torsion cables were cut, cleanly, from the inside.
3. **The watch,** sixty-odd soldiers on this mile of wall, half of them citizens in borrowed mail. They are exhausted and they are not deserting, and that is worth describing.
4. **The enemy fires,** three thousand of them, arranged in disciplined rings. *Survival or History DC 14:* ice giants do not camp in rings. Somebody is organising them.
5. **The winched thing** in the dark, half a mile out: a trebuchet the size of a temple, being assembled by giants who work in silence. *Perception DC 15:* the counterweight is a cage, and there are shapes inside it, and they are moving.
6. **The dead line,** a row of Wahyrst's war dead laid along the inner rampart under canvas, awaiting a pyre the city no longer has fuel for. Forty-one bodies tonight. *Perception DC 13,* or automatically if anyone walks the inner edge: three of the canvases have shifted since the last patrol, and nobody has been up here.
7. **Aelied's wineskin,** which she has carried for eleven years, which was filled this evening by a quartermaster's runner she did not recognise, and which she is about to drink from.
8. **The bronze disc.** Thin, circular, green with age, stamped with a nine-pointed figure. She has worn it since she was a cadet and has no idea what it is.

### Setting the Scene

Open with the joke and let the players answer it. *Because half of what they say is bull* is the intended punchline and a player will almost certainly get there; if one does, let them deliver it and have Aelied laugh like a landslide, genuinely, for slightly too long. If nobody gets it, she delivers it herself and is delighted with herself.

This matters. You have about ninety seconds of affection to establish before you kill her, and the joke is the whole of it. Do not rush past it to get to the poison.

Then let her be serious. She tells them about tomorrow's mission: the giants are being rallied by someone called **the Winged Shadow**, a small force will go out to find out who, and she will brief them properly in the morning. She is proud of them. She says so plainly, because minotaur officers do, and then she drinks.

### DM Information

Aelied is dead the moment she lifts the wineskin, and the party cannot prevent it. Be honest with yourself about this: it is a scripted death, and the compensation you owe the players is that everything *around* it is solvable. Who poisoned her, how they got into the quartermaster's stores, what the disc is, and why the scorpion cables were cut are all live questions with real answers.

**The poison is Pindarian,** not giant-made. *Medicine DC 16* or *Nature DC 18* identifies it as a plant-derived neurotoxin that has not grown on Kharyl in four thousand years, and *Arcana DC 17* notes that the victim's tears run red because the toxin is keyed to something in the blood of the Watch — it is a weapon designed to kill Saharim, being used on a mortal, which is enormous overkill and tells you the poisoner had it lying around.

**The killer** is a quartermaster's runner named Sella Vosk, who is not in this scene and can be found in Act I's aftermath. She is nineteen, she is not evil, and she did it because Vhaskar's people have her brother. She will confess to anyone who asks kindly. What the party does with her is one of the better moral questions in the act.

**The disc** is a Pindarian key-token: one of nine, distributed to the Watch's outer wardens, and the only thing that will open the final door in Act III. Aelied does not know this. She thinks it is a good-luck charm. It has been hanging around the neck of a minotaur sergeant's descendants for a hundred generations because the Pindarians gave the outermost keys to people, not to vaults.

### Mechanics

**The Joke (Roleplay, no roll).** No check. Give **Inspiration** to whoever lands the punchline.

**The Poisoning (Scripted).** Aelied drops in the round after she drinks. She is beyond saving: the toxin is a four-thousand-year-old assassination compound and *lesser restoration* fails against it, as does *protection from poison.* Say so plainly if a player asks, rather than letting them burn a spell and feel cheated.

**What a character can do** — and this is the point of the scene — is be with her:

- **Medicine DC 13** to ease the convulsions. She dies less badly. This is worth doing and you should say so.
- **Any character who takes her hand and speaks to her** rather than shouting for help: she manages one more sentence, and it should be the thing that player most needs to hear from an officer who was proud of them. Award **Inspiration.**
- **Perception DC 12** during the death to notice she is pressing the disc into someone's hand deliberately, not dropping it.

Her last instruction is clear and repeated: *"You must speak with the council. Give them this."*

**The Wineskin (Investigation).** *Investigation DC 12* on the skin finds a residue; *Medicine DC 16* or *Nature DC 18* identifies the Pindarian toxin; *Survival DC 14* backtracks the runner's boot prints in the snow to the wall stair and no further.

**The Cut Cables (Investigation DC 12).** Four scorpions disabled from inside, within the last two days. Somebody has been softening this wall. The northern wall is where the breach comes, tomorrow, and a party that reports this to a surviving officer can evacuate the Lampborough tenements before W2 — which changes who is in the orphanage.

**The Shifted Canvases (Perception DC 13).** Three of the forty-one war dead have moved. If a character pulls back a canvas: the body beneath is cold, four days dead, and its eyes are open and tracking. It does not attack. It does not speak. It is *listening,* and when the character leans close it says, in a voice like a held breath, one of its own names. Law 2 is already leaking upward through two hundred feet of rock, and this is the party's first and best clue that the thing under the city is awake.

### Creatures and NPCs

**Aelied Axebiter** (use **Veteran,** MM, with Athletics +7 and Persuasion +4; she will not fight in this scene). Eight feet, grey-brown, one horn capped in brass after a bad year. She is well-respected because she does not lose her head, and charismatic — well, for a minotaur. Her humour is her command style: she tells a joke before every hard thing so that people remember they are allowed to be people.

**Her regret,** if a character somehow reaches for it: she has sent one hundred and six people to the wall this week and she has written to none of their families, because she does not know what to say and she has been putting it off, and she is about to run out of time to put it off in.

**The watch on this stretch:** Corporal Bruhn Talloak (minotaur, Guard), who will take command badly; Sella Vosk is *not* here; and a human recruit named Petro who was standing close enough to see the runner's face and is too frightened to say so unless someone is gentle with him (*Persuasion DC 13,* or *Insight DC 12* to notice he is about to speak and then stop).

### Set Piece

**The wineskin.** She finishes the joke. She laughs. She drinks — a long pull, the last of it, with the satisfied smack of someone who has earned it — and she says something entirely ordinary about the cold.

Then the axe hits the stone. She has dropped it, which she has never done, and she is on her knees with both hands at her throat, and she is looking at the party with her eyes wide and absolutely clear, because the toxin does not take the mind. She knows exactly what is happening to her and she has about forty seconds and she spends thirty of them on the disc and the council.

She dies badly and undignified and she is aware of it. Do not narrate around that. Then have Corporal Talloak start shouting, and have the artillery resume, because the siege has not paused for this.

### Lore Drops

- **L1 — The Rings** (*Survival or History DC 14*). Ice giants raid in warbands and camp where they fall. Three thousand of them are camped in disciplined concentric rings around a mountain city, which is siege doctrine, which is not something giants do. *They are being commanded, and they are being commanded well.*
- **L2 — The Counterweight Cage** (*Perception DC 15*). The great trebuchet's counterweight is a cage full of moving shapes. *History DC 16:* giants weight their engines with stone. Whatever is in that cage is being loaded to be thrown into the city on purpose, and it is alive, or was.
- **L3 — The Pindarian Toxin** (*Medicine 16 / Nature 18 / Arcana 17*). Keyed to the blood of the deathless. Somebody has a Pindarian armoury, and they are spending assassination-grade Saharim poison on a wall commander, which means either they have a great deal of it or they knew exactly who Aelied's ancestors were.
- **L4 — The Disc** (*History DC 18, or from the Council in W3, or from Kathandar in Act III*). Nine-pointed, bronze, older than the city. One of nine outer-warden key-tokens. Wahyrst was built on top of a Pindarian site by people who had no idea, except that one minotaur family has been handing down the key for four thousand years and calling it luck.
- **L5 — The Listening Dead** (*Perception DC 13, then a closer look*). A four-day-old corpse with tracking eyes that says its own name when you lean in. The dead of Wahyrst have started to become articulate, and they started this week, and the siege started this week, and those two facts are the same fact.

### Plot Hooks

- **Sella Vosk** and her hostage brother are a complete side-plot that resolves in Act II — Vhaskar's people are holding him in the catacombs, in one of the yuan-ti's cages.
- **The cut cables** let the party affect W2's casualty count. Reporting them saves the Lampborough tenements; not reporting them means the orphanage is full when it comes down.
- **Aelied's unwritten letters** are in her quarters, one hundred and six names and no text. A party that writes them during a rest gets nothing mechanical and should get **Inspiration** each, and the names go into the Black Catalogue in Act III, and in Act III those hundred and six people can be called.
- **The counterweight cage** pays off in W2.

### Resolution, Rewards, and Temptation

The party leaves the wall carrying a bronze disc and an order. **No Refusal is gained or lost;** the track does not begin until the catacombs.

**Treasure.** Aelied's battleaxe, if they take it — a *+1 greataxe* sized for a minotaur, usable two-handed by a Medium creature with disadvantage unless they are Strength 17 or higher. Her wineskin, empty. The disc.

**Aelied's body** should be laid with the other forty-one under canvas, and the party will not have time to do more, and they should be made to notice that.

## W2. The Run to the Citadel

*Lampborough district, Wahyrst | Night, snow, burning | A one-mile run from the northern wall stair to the Citadel of Horns | Smells of granite dust, hot stone, wet ash, and burst bodies*

> You come down off the wall into a city being taken apart from the sky. Blocks of granite the size of carts come down at a slant and do not so much strike buildings as delete them. Between the stone come other things: ice that shatters into scything shards, and casks, and — as one comes apart forty feet above the Cooper's Row — a rain of rotten human corpses, four days dead and packed tight, bursting across the roofs and the street and the people in it.
>
> The Citadel of Horns stands a mile south, lit up, its doors mobbed. Between you and it is Lampborough.

### What Is in This Room

1. **The bombardment,** ongoing, roughly one impact every twelve seconds within earshot. Granite, ice, and corpse-shot. *Nature or Medicine DC 13:* the corpses are plague-carriers, deliberately rotted, and this is biological warfare — which giants also do not do.
2. **Lampborough,** a district of six-storey tenements built into the mountain's shoulder, with lamp-lighters' rails running along the eaves and steep stepped streets. Densely populated. Almost entirely civilian.
3. **The Kharyl Street orphanage,** four floors, ninety-one children and eleven staff, currently intact. It will not remain intact. See *Mechanics.*
4. **The crowd,** thousands of people moving south toward the citadel, which means the party is running with a current that is also an obstacle.
5. **A collapsed lamp-rail** across Fetter Lane, forty feet up, still burning, dripping oil — a hazard and, for anyone who thinks of it, a bridge.
6. **City guard and reserves** at every junction, shouting orders nobody can hear, doing their best, losing.
7. **The dead in the street.** Wahyrst's own, from earlier in the week, not yet collected. *Perception DC 12:* some of them are sitting up. Not attacking. Sitting up, and watching the party run past, and turning their heads to follow.
8. **A minotaur calf, perhaps six,** sitting in the middle of Anvil Steps beside an adult who is very clearly dead, not crying, not moving.

### Setting the Scene

Run this fast and out of order. Short sentences. Do not let the party stop to plan; the whole point of the sequence is that they are making decisions at a dead run with incomplete information.

Cut between the three saving throws and one human detail each time — the calf on the steps, a woman carrying a door with someone on it, a guard sergeant calmly directing traffic while bleeding from both ears. The bombardment is scenery. The people are the scene.

### DM Information

This is a chase-shaped hazard sequence with a moral fork in the middle, and it exists to do two things: establish that Wahyrst is dying, and give the party one chance to spend their own safety on strangers before the campaign starts asking them harder versions of that question.

The three saving throws from the original run **DC 17, then DC 13, then DC 9** — deliberately front-loaded, because the party is most tired and least oriented at the start. Keep that. It reads as the bombardment walking away from them as they move south, and it means the scene gets easier as it goes, which is the opposite of most chases and feels correct here.

**The orphanage comes down on the second save,** in view, forty yards off their line. What happens next is the fork.

### Mechanics

**The Run (Three Saves).** Each character makes three **Dexterity saving throws** in sequence, one per stretch of the run.

| Stretch | DC | On a failure |
| --- | --- | --- |
| The wall stair to Cooper's Row | 17 | 22 (4d10) bludgeoning from granite spall; knocked prone |
| Cooper's Row to Anvil Steps | 13 | 16 (3d10) slashing from ice shards |
| Anvil Steps to the Citadel approach | 9 | 11 (2d10) bludgeoning; and the character is carrying someone else's blood |

A character who succeeds on a save may instead **give up their success to pull an ally through** — the ally's failure becomes a success, and the volunteer takes the damage in their place. Offer this once, early, and then never mention it again.

**Alternative routes.** Reward thinking:
- **The lamp-rails** (*Acrobatics DC 15,* or *Athletics DC 15* to climb): forty feet up, out of the corpse-shot, but the burning oil deals **11 (2d10) fire** to anyone who fails. Skips the second save entirely.
- **The undercellars** (*History DC 12,* or automatically for any character from Wahyrst): brewery cellars run most of Fetter Lane. Slow — advance the **Race Clock by 1** — but skips all three saves.
- **Reported cut cables (from W1).** If the party warned an officer, the Lampborough tenements were emptied two hours ago, the crowd is thinner, and every DC drops by 3.

**The Orphanage (The Fork).** At the end of the second stretch, a granite block takes the Kharyl Street orphanage's northern corner off. The building does not fall at once; it has about ten minutes.

Going in costs **ten minutes** (advance the **Race Clock by 1**) and runs as a skill challenge: **four successes before three failures,** each attempt one round, using any of —

| Approach | Check | DC |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Hauling children clear | Strength (Athletics) | 14 |
| Shoring the stair | Dexterity (thieves' tools) or Intelligence | 15 |
| Keeping ninety-one terrified children moving | Charisma (Persuasion or Performance) | 15 |
| Finding the ones who have hidden rather than run | Wisdom (Perception) | 16 |
| Magic that lifts, shields, or carries | spell, no check | automatic success, one only |

**Four successes:** all ninety-one out. **Three failures first:** the party gets out with **1d20 + 40** children and the rest do not, and you should say the number aloud, and then say nothing else.

**Not going in** costs nothing and is a legitimate choice — the citadel is mobbed, the council is meeting now, and Aelied's last order was explicit. But the screams carry for the rest of the run, and they should be in the narration of the third saving throw, and they should be in the narration when the party reaches the citadel steps.

**The Sitting Dead (Perception DC 12).** No mechanics, no combat, no check to resist. They sit up, they watch, they turn their heads. If a character speaks to one, it says its own name and lies back down. This happens three times during the run and should never be explained.

### Creatures and NPCs

No combat. Resist the urge to add a giant; the sequence is about civilians and it does not survive an initiative order.

**Ordinary people, in this order:** a lamp-lighter still working her rails because nobody told her to stop; Sergeant Yola Vane (Guard), directing traffic at Anvil Steps with a collapsed lung, who will not leave her junction and does not need saving, thank you; the minotaur calf on the steps, who is called Dunnet, who will not speak, and who will hold onto whichever character picks him up with a grip that has to be prised off at the citadel doors.

**Dunnet** is worth carrying forward. He is silent through all of Act I and Act II. He speaks for the first time in Act III, in V2, because his mother is in the Procession — and what he says is the single best lever the party will have on Kathandar.

### Set Piece

**The corpse-shot.** A cask comes apart above Cooper's Row and it is not stone and it is not ice. Four days of the giants' own dead — or Wahyrst's, thrown back — burst across the street in front of the party at terminal velocity.

Then, in the wreckage, as the party runs through it: one of the bodies, split open and plainly beyond any question of life, lifts its head out of the slurry and says a name. Clearly. Conversationally. Its own.

And forty feet away, another one answers it.

### Lore Drops

- **L6 — The Rotted Shot** (*Nature or Medicine DC 13*). The corpses were rotted on purpose and packed to burst. Giants fling stone. Somebody in that camp is running a plague campaign, and somebody in that camp has been collecting the dead.
- **L7 — The Answering Bodies** (*automatic, during the set piece*). Two corpses exchange names across Cooper's Row. *Arcana DC 17:* this is not necromancy. Nothing is animating them. Something beneath the city is *reading a list,* and the bodies are simply responding to being read.
- **L8 — The Undercellars** (*History DC 12*). Wahyrst's brewery cellars are older than Wahyrst. The lowest three courses of the cellar walls are dressed stone of a type nobody on Kharyl has quarried in living memory, and there are nine-pointed marks on them at regular intervals.
- **L9 — Sergeant Vane's Count** (*ask her*). She has been counting impacts. Four hundred and ten today. She will tell the party, without being asked, that the artillery has not once struck the Citadel of Horns, the granaries, or the catacomb entrances. *The Winged Shadow is not trying to destroy Wahyrst. He is trying to empty it downward.*

### Plot Hooks

- **Dunnet** is a silent six-year-old dependent who becomes essential in V2. Give him to whichever player will take him most seriously.
- **L9** reframes the entire siege as a herding operation, and a party that works it out can stop running toward the citadel and start asking who benefits.
- The children saved from the orphanage are ninety-one witnesses, ninety-one mouths to feed in a besieged city, and — if things go very badly in Act III — ninety-one names in the Catalogue.
- Sergeant Vane dies at her junction tonight whatever the party does. Her name is findable, and it can be read aloud in Act III.

### Resolution, Rewards, and Temptation

**No Refusal.** No treasure. The party arrives at the Citadel of Horns bloodied, carrying someone else's blood and possibly someone else's child, having either gone into the orphanage or not.

Award **Inspiration** to every character who gave up a saving throw for someone else, and to every character who went into the building.

## W3. The Council of Horns

*The Citadel of Horns, Lampborough district | Amphitheatre council chamber, 150 ft. across, stained glass at height | Exits: main doors (guarded), servants' entrance (west), balcony stairs, and — later — nowhere useful | Smells of incense, cold stone, and, shortly, burnt blood*

> The Citadel of Horns rises out of Lampborough like a tooth. City guard and reserves are shouting orders into a crowd that has stopped listening, and the crowd is enormous, because the outer wall has been breached and everyone in the lower city knows it.
>
> Four minotaurs in golden plate stand across the citadel doors with double-bladed axes taller than most of you.

### What Is in This Room

1. **The Gilded Four:** Horn-Wardens Ossik, Brannagh, Teluun and Karr, in ceremonial gold plate that is not ceremonial tonight. They have orders to admit nobody. They have had those orders for six hours and they have been screamed at for all six.
2. **The crowd,** perhaps four thousand, pressing at the steps. Not yet a riot. Twenty minutes from being one.
3. **The servants' entrance,** western face, down a delivery stair behind the kitchen midden, unguarded because the Wardens do not think of it as a door.
4. **The amphitheatre,** a semicircular chamber with tiered seating for six hundred, currently holding twelve, ringed with statues of gods and of Wahyrst's great warriors, lit in shafts of colour through a plethora of stained glass.
5. **Twelve councillors** in a semicircle, mid-argument. Eleven minotaurs and one albino goliath without tattoos — which, *History DC 14,* means she is clan-stripped, which for a goliath means exiled, which means somebody sat an exile on the ruling council of a minotaur city and there is a story there.
6. **Barak, son of none,** Torchbearer of the Council of Horns, presiding: an old bull with a chipped horn, a bronze chain of office, and a spy network he is very proud of.
7. **The statues,** thirty-one of them. *Religion DC 13:* one is not a god or a warrior of Wahyrst. It is a crowned figure in funerary armour holding a mace, unlabelled, standing at the back, and it is four thousand years older than the citadel. Somebody built this chamber around it.
8. **A circular window** above the Torchbearer's seat, plain glass among the stained, admitting one shaft of white light onto the empty floor.
9. **Twelve repeater crossbows** on the second-floor balconies, in the dark, already loaded. The party cannot see them. They have been there since before the party arrived.

### Setting the Scene

Two very different rooms in one scene: the mob at the doors, loud and hot and panicking, and then the chamber, cold and echoing and full of people arguing about tactics while their city is deleted above them.

Make the councillors sound competent. The temptation is to write them as corrupt obstacles so that their massacre lands as justice; do the opposite. They are tired, frightened, serious people making impossible decisions, and one of them — the goliath, Vess Ashen — is the only person in Wahyrst who has already worked out that the giants are being herded, and she cannot get anyone to listen.

Then kill all twelve in eight seconds, in the dark, with no fight.

### DM Information

**Getting in.** The Wardens turn away anyone without authority. Presenting Aelied's bronze disc does not grant authority — none of them have ever seen it — but it is *obviously* a dead officer's token being delivered, and it earns the party a hearing.

**Eavesdropping.** The council's debate is the act's exposition and the party should get it one way or the other: by stealth from the balconies, by being admitted and listening, or by Vess Ashen repeating it afterwards. Do not gate it behind a single roll.

**What they are debating,** in order of how much support each option has:

- **Evacuation** down the southern switchbacks. Favoured by four. Impossible: the switchbacks are three days on foot and the giants have riders.
- **General levy** — arm every citizen. Favoured by three. Barak's preference. Buys four days and costs eleven thousand lives.
- **The dragon.** An ancient black dragon lairs on the adjacent peak, and Wahyrst has an old arrangement with it that nobody alive has invoked. Favoured by two. Requires an offering of appropriate weight.
- **The catacombs.** Go down, and bring up the Mageslayer Axe from the old tomb. Favoured by two, both of whom are treated as cranks.
- **Ask what the giants actually want.** Vess Ashen, alone, ignored.

**Barak's commission,** which he gives the party once they are in front of him: go into the catacombs, retrieve the treasure held in the old tomb complex, carry it up the north face as an offering, and beg the black dragon for aid. He also believes — correctly, and this is the important part — that **the artifacts in that tomb are what the enemy is after,** and that getting them out first is worth doing on its own.

**Then the lights go out.** Twelve soft thuds. See *Set Piece.*

**The Winged Shadow's monologue** is the hinge of the whole act and it should be delivered slowly, in full, while the party is looking down the shafts of twelve repeater crossbows. Vhaskar Corr does not gloat about power. He gloats about *bookkeeping.*

### Mechanics

**The Doors (Social).** A **Charisma (Persuasion)** or **Charisma (Deception)** check against **DC 16.** Presenting the disc grants **advantage.** Mentioning Aelied by name grants **advantage** (Ossik served under her). Carrying a rescued child from W2 grants **advantage** and, frankly, you should just let them in.

On a failure, the Wardens refuse and the party must find the **servants' entrance** (*History DC 12,* or *Investigation DC 14* circling the building, or free from any kitchen servant in the crowd). **This branch matters later** — see *The Guards Arrive.*

**Eavesdropping (Stealth DC 18).** From the balcony stairs. Success gives the party the full debate above, unfiltered, including Vess Ashen being dismissed. Failure is not a disaster: Barak calls out, *"Who dares disturb the council at such an auspicious moment? Declare your names and purpose,"* and the party gets the same information as an audience instead.

**The Old Statue (Religion DC 13).** The unlabelled crowned figure. See *Lore Drops.*

**The Massacre (Scripted, no saves).** The party cannot prevent it and cannot fight it. Twelve councillors, twelve bolts, eight seconds, total darkness. Do not roll initiative. If a player attempts to act, they may — and they find themselves moving through a darkness in which nothing is where it was, and when the light returns they are standing somewhere they did not intend to be, holding nothing.

**The Standoff.** Twelve **Shadowhand Executioners** on the balconies with loaded repeaters, plus Vhaskar. If the party attacks: the Executioners fire one volley (see stat block), Vhaskar spends his reaction on nothing at all, and on the party's second turn every one of the thirteen vanishes mid-action. There is no winning this and no losing it either; it costs the party hit points and a lesson.

**The Disc.** Vhaskar does not take it. He looks directly at whoever is carrying it, for slightly too long, and does not take it. Note the player's reaction; you will use it in Act III.

### Creatures and NPCs

**Barak, son of none, Torchbearer** (use **Minotaur** with Persuasion +6 and Investigation +5). Vain about his spy network, genuinely devoted to the city, and about to be beheaded by his own horn. His last act is to commission the party, which makes him — posthumously — the reason they go down there.

**Vess Ashen, councillor** (use **Gladiator** with Insight +6, Investigation +7). Albino goliath, clan-stripped, no tattoos. She has been tracking the giants' movements for a week and has concluded they are being driven, not led, and that their own dead have begun speaking to them. She is dismissed as a crank in the debate. **She is the only councillor who survives,** because she is in the stacks behind the chamber fetching her notes when the lights go out. Use her as the party's patron for the rest of the site.

**Horn-Warden Ossik** (Veteran) served under Aelied for six years and will weep, once, briefly, at the news, and then apologise for it.

**Vhaskar Corr, the Winged Shadow** — see stat block below. He is a Saharim: one of the original deathless of Vhal'Kathar, who took the Watch beside Kathandar four thousand years ago and then walked off the post. He wears a mask because his face is the face on the old statue at the back of the chamber, and somebody in Wahyrst would eventually notice.

**Roleplaying him.** Courteous, quiet, and administratively minded. He does not raise his voice. He speaks about the dead the way a clerk speaks about inventory, and about the living the way a clerk speaks about a queue. He finds the council's failure genuinely *irritating* rather than contemptible.

**His argument,** which he will make if the party engages him and which is the reason he is dangerous: *the dead are a resource that the living have agreed, collectively, to waste.* He is not wrong about the waste. He is wrong about everything else, and no character will be able to say precisely why until the end of Act III, which is the point.

### Set Piece

**Twelve soft thuds.**

Darkness takes the whole interior at once — not a dousing of lamps, an arrival of dark, saturating, absolute. Through it come twelve soft sounds, evenly spaced, like books being set down.

Then a single shaft of white light from the circular window above the Torchbearer's seat, and a figure standing in it, masked, cloaked, silhouetted in stark relief. Delighted laughter, echoing around an amphitheatre built for six hundred.

The dark clears. Twelve councillors lie broken across the pews with a single crossbow bolt in each neck and black corrupted veins bulging beneath the skin. Twelve more figures step out onto the second-floor balconies, repeaters levelled down at the party from every angle.

Vhaskar descends the steps unhurriedly. He takes Barak's head by the chipped horn, lifts it, and cuts the horn off with one stroke — black ichor, not blood — and he does it the way a man clips a coupon.

> *"This council failed to protect its citizens. It failed to protect each other. It failed, frankly, to protect itself from twelve men on a balcony, which I would call embarrassing if I thought any of them would have understood the criticism.*
>
> *"What I want is underneath this city, and it is not a weapon. It is a list. Every name that has ever finished dying on this world, catalogued, in order, by a civilisation that thought record-keeping was a moral act. And when I have read it aloud — all of it — there will be no one left in Elderon with the standing to object. Not your dragon. Not the thing kneeling under your feet. Not even me, I expect, by then.*
>
> *"It seems the race is on. Who will reach the Tomb of Kathandar first?"*

A click of his fingers. All thirteen are gone.

### The Guards Arrive

**If the party came in through the front doors:** the Horn-Wardens arrive within the minute, find the party standing among twelve corpses, and — because Ossik vouched for them at the door — question them at length rather than attacking. This takes an hour, ends in cooperation, and yields **the locations of all three catacomb entrances,** a requisition for supplies, and Ossik's insistence on coming with them as far as the first stair.

**If the party came in through the servants' entrance:** nobody vouched for anyone. The Wardens arrive, find four strangers who entered by a back door standing over the ruling council, and attack. **Four Horn-Wardens** (use **Veteran** with AC 18 and 2 Golden Greataxes at +7 for 12 slashing). They fight to subdue, not kill, and they will stop for any of: Vess Ashen emerging from the stacks; a successful **DC 18 Persuasion** with the disc in hand; or the party surrendering. A party that kills Horn-Wardens is wanted in Wahyrst for the rest of the site and must use the third catacomb entrance, which is in the sewers, which is worse.

### Lore Drops

- **L10 — The Old Statue** (*Religion DC 13*). Thirty-one statues; thirty are gods and heroes of Wahyrst; one is a crowned figure in funerary armour holding a mace, unlabelled, four thousand years older than the building around it. *History DC 17:* the citadel was not built and then decorated. It was built *around* this statue, on this spot, by people who found it here and did not dare move it. It is Kathandar, and the mace is Gravewake, and it has been standing in the city's seat of government the entire time.
- **L11 — Vess Ashen's Notes** (*from her, freely, after the massacre*). Seven days of giant movements. They are not besieging in any pattern that takes a city. They are *containing* one. And three nights ago her observers reported the giants' own funeral cairns on the lower slopes standing open, from the inside.
- **L12 — The Three Entrances** (*from the Wardens, or Investigation DC 15*). Tariel's cellar in the merchant quarter (the shortest, and the one Vhaskar used); the temple undercroft (guarded, formal, slow — costs a day and a **Race Clock** segment); and the sewer outfall on the north face (fast, foul, and it comes out past the yuan-ti's front door rather than through it).
- **L13 — The Black Catalogue** (*from Vhaskar's speech, automatic*). Every name that has ever finished dying on Elderon, catalogued in order. Combined with Law 2 — *a dead creature can speak for 1 minute when its name is read aloud* — the party has everything they need to work out what he intends, if they think about it for ten seconds. Let them have the ten seconds.
- **L14 — Twelve Bolts, Twelve Necks** (*Medicine DC 14 on any councillor*). The black corruption in the veins is the same Pindarian toxin that killed Aelied. He is not conserving it. He has a supply.

### Plot Hooks

- **Vess Ashen** becomes the party's patron, the acting authority in Wahyrst, and the only person above ground who believes them.
- **Barak's spy** — the one who infiltrated the giants and reported the Winged Shadow — is still alive, still out there, and can be found in Act II as a prisoner in the yuan-ti cages. He knows what is in the counterweight cage.
- **The statue in the chamber** means Kathandar's likeness has been standing over Wahyrst's every decision for four thousand years, and a party that returns here after meeting him will find it has changed posture.
- **The offering for the dragon** is a real commission and a real trap: the treasure Barak wants them to bring up is in the Medusa Queen's hoard and the mummy lord's chamber, and taking from the latter is cursed, and taking from named Pindarian graves below costs Refusal. The party has been sent to commit the exact sin the pyramid exists to test.
- **Vhaskar did not take the disc.** Why not is the best question in Act I and the answer is in V6: he cannot touch it. He forfeited his warden's right when he left the post, and the key-token would refuse him, and he would rather not have four adventurers watch that happen.

### Resolution, Rewards, and Temptation

**No Refusal yet.** The party leaves the citadel with a commission, three routes down, one silent child, one surviving councillor, one bronze disc, and a rival with a seven-segment head start.

**Set the Race Clock to 2.**

**Treasure.** Vess Ashen's requisition: 200 gp of supplies, four *potions of greater healing,* a *periapt of wound closure* from the citadel reliquary, and a sealed writ of authority that is worth precisely nothing in the catacombs and quite a lot on the way back up.

## Act II: The Upper Necropolis

Everything from here to N19 is a squat.

Four hundred years ago a yuan-ti cult tunnelled down out of a merchant's cellar looking for a dry place to worship, hit dressed Pindarian stone at ninety feet, and broke through into the top two courses of a structure they did not recognise and have never once investigated. They built a temple in the attic of a planetary machine. They have a kitchen in it. They have been eating people directly above the largest catalogue of the dead in existence, and the thing they worship — a Medusa Queen who calls herself divine and is merely very old — has no idea that the floor of her throne room is the ceiling of somebody else's archive.

This matters for how you run the necropolis. **None of the horror in Act II is Pindarian.** Vhal'Kathar did not build the cages in N17 and does not approve of them. When the party finally breaks through the floor of N19 into the Descent of Names, the change in register should be total: from a slaughterhouse to a library.

### What Is Different Down Here

**Law 1 is not yet in force.** Resurrection magic works in the necropolis. It stops working the instant the party crosses into Act III, and a party that has been relying on it will feel the floor go out from under them at exactly the right moment.

**Law 2 is leaking.** Read a name aloud — off a grave marker, off a prisoner's tag, off a yuan-ti's ledger — and roughly one time in three the dead thing answers. Do not roll for this in front of the players and do not explain it. Let it happen when it will be most unsettling.

**Law 3 is in force from N6 onward.** The blood in the garden basin is the boundary. Necrotic damage dealt reduces the dealer's own maximum, and the blood oozes in N7 are the party's introduction to that rule at a point where it is survivable.

**Refusal is live from N1.** The necropolis is full of the dead, most of them recent and none of them Pindarian, and how the party treats them is being recorded two hundred feet down.

### The Snake Count

Roll **d10** in any room you like from N1 to N18; that many **snakes** are present, using whatever profile suits the room (poisonous snake, giant poisonous snake, or constrictor at higher counts). They are not an encounter. They are infrastructure — the site is *infested*, in the way a granary is infested, and the yuan-ti simply live with it.

Use the roll for texture rather than combat. Snakes in the bedding. Snakes in the cook-pot. A snake unspooling out of a dead man's mouth when a character reads his name off the wall.

### Vhaskar's Trail

He is ahead of them and he is not being subtle. From the moment the Race Clock reaches 3, dress cleared rooms with his leavings: yuan-ti with single bolts in their necks and black veins under the scales; doors opened without being unlocked; and, in N8 and beyond, worshippers killed mid-prayer and left kneeling.

He is not clearing the dungeon for the party's benefit. He is clearing it because it is in his way, and the distinction should be visible in the corpse positions.

## N1. Tariel's Basement

*Cellar and rough-cut tunnel, 60 ft. by 40 ft., irregular | Dark | Exits: cellar stair up to the merchant quarter, twin oaken doors south | Smells of old wine, dry rot, and something underneath that is neither*

> The cellar has not been a cellar for a long time. The stone is plain, roughly worked, and the whole of it — floor, walls, the collapsed racks, the skeletons piled in the south-eastern corner — is furred in cobweb. The ceiling is not visible. There is simply a grey mass overhead where a ceiling ought to be. Rats squeak and skitter through the bones.
>
> At the far end, torchlight picks out a pair of heavy oaken doors with wrought-iron ring pulls. They stand at the bottom of a slope, and they are ninety feet below the street, and nobody in Wahyrst knows they are there.

### What Is in This Room

1. **The webbing,** dense enough to constitute terrain, filling the upper eight feet of the chamber entirely. It must be burned or cut to see anything above head height.
2. **The ceiling mosaic,** revealed by burning the webs or by a **DC 12 Intelligence (Investigation)** check from below: a serpent thirty feet long in white, green and gold tesserae, coiled around nothing. Pindarian workmanship. *History DC 15:* this was not a serpent when it was laid. The empty coil at its centre held a human figure, and the tesserae of that figure have been individually prised out and replaced with plain grey stone, recently, with care.
3. **The skeletons,** eleven of them, in the corner. Nine are four hundred years old and belong to Tariel's household. *Medicine DC 12:* two are eight days old, wearing Wahyrst guard leathers, and were shot through the neck.
4. **Wine racks,** collapsed, containing 1d6 bottles of intact four-hundred-year-old Kharyl red, worth 50 gp each to the right buyer and undrinkable.
5. **The oaken doors,** locked and trapped. See *Mechanics.*
6. **The rats,** hundreds, and they are all facing the doors. They have been facing the doors this whole time.
7. **Spiders.** Thousands, in the webbing, currently still.
8. **A chalk mark** on the left-hand door at knee height: a nine-pointed figure with a line struck through it. Fresh. *Investigation DC 10.*

### Setting the Scene

Start with sound. A cellar this full of web is acoustically dead; the party's own footsteps come back wrong, and the rats are audible and the spiders are not.

The ceiling is the hook. Do not mention a ceiling at all in the initial description — say the overhead is "a grey mass" and let a player ask. When they burn it, drop forty feet of falling, smouldering web and reveal a Pindarian serpent mosaic looking down at everyone, and let them understand that they are not in a cellar. They are standing on the lid of something.

### DM Information

N1 is the airlock. It establishes three things and then gets out of the way: that Vhaskar came through here (chalk mark, two shot guards, an open route), that the site below is Pindarian rather than yuan-ti, and that the dungeon's traps are lethal at this tier rather than decorative.

**The chalk mark** is Vhaskar's crew marking a cleared and disarmed route — except they did not disarm the door trap, they simply did not trigger it, because they opened the doors from a distance with a rope. The struck-through nine-point is their sign for *Pindarian, handled.*

**The spiders do not attack.** When either door opens, a hissing sound comes up out of the dark beyond, and every spider in the room goes over the party in a wave heading for the cellar stair and the open air. It is a stampede, not an ambush, and what matters is that something below made a noise and the spiders have been waiting four hundred years for an excuse.

### Mechanics

**The Webbing.** Burning it clears the chamber in one round and deals **7 (2d6) fire** to anything in the upper eight feet. Cutting through takes ten minutes and is quieter — a party that cuts rather than burns does not wake N4's occupant early.

**The Door Trap (Purple Worm Venom Darts).** **Investigation DC 15** to detect the dart ports in the frame. **Dexterity (thieves' tools) DC 15** to disarm; failure by 5 or more triggers it.

On a trigger, the creature opening the door makes a **DC 19 Constitution saving throw,** taking **42 (12d6) poison damage** on a failure or half on a success. This is a level 9-11 party and this trap can drop a rogue outright — that is intentional, it is detectable, and there is a rope in the room.

**Opening from a distance.** Any character who thinks of using a rope, *mage hand,* or a thrown grapnel bypasses the trap entirely. Say yes immediately and award **Inspiration** for the caution.

**The Lock.** **Thieves' tools DC 14,** or force with **Athletics DC 18.** Forcing it triggers the trap automatically.

**The Spider Wave.** When either door opens: every creature in the room makes a **DC 13 Dexterity saving throw.** On a failure, the character is knocked prone and takes **3 (1d6) piercing** as several thousand spiders go over and through them. No poison, no disease. It is purely horrible and lasts nine seconds.

**Reading the Guards' Names** (from their tags, *Investigation DC 10*): Corporal Weft and a recruit called Ilo Bramm. If a character reads either name aloud, there is a pause, and then one of the eight-day-old skeletons says, quite clearly, *"He went past me. He didn't even look."* Then nothing else, ever, no matter what is asked. **This is the party's first direct experience of Law 2** and it should be free, unprompted by any roll, and never explained.

### Creatures and NPCs

None hostile. **Swarms of spiders** as environmental effect only. **Roll the snake count** if you want a giant poisonous snake in the wine racks.

If you want a fight here — and at level 9 you may not need one — **two Giant Spiders** in the webbing over the doors will do, but they should be fleeing rather than hunting, and killing them should feel slightly unnecessary.

### Set Piece

**The ceiling.** Burn the webs and the whole grey mass comes down at once, smouldering, in sheets, and above it is a thirty-foot Pindarian serpent in gold and green looking straight down — and at the centre of its coil, where a figure obviously used to be, is a patch of blank grey stone with the old tesserae stacked neatly on a ledge nearby.

Somebody removed the person from the picture. Recently. And stacked the pieces, because they were not vandalising it. They were *editing* it.

### Lore Drops

- **L15 — The Edited Mosaic** (*Investigation DC 12, then History DC 15*). A Pindarian serpent coiled around a removed human figure. The tesserae were prised out with care and kept. *Whoever is in that mosaic is someone Vhaskar does not want identified,* and the obvious candidate is himself.
- **L16 — The Struck Nine-Point** (*Investigation DC 10*). A cleared-route mark. Vhaskar's crew have a standard notation for Pindarian obstacles, which means they have dealt with a great many of them, which means this is not their first pyramid.
- **L17 — Corporal Weft** (*read his name aloud*). *"He went past me. He didn't even look."* One sentence, and then silence forever. The dead of this mountain are answering and they are answering with grievances.
- **L18 — The Rats** (*Perception DC 10*). Every rat in the cellar faces the southern doors. They have been doing so for long enough that their runs are worn in that orientation. Something on the other side is worth facing.

### Plot Hooks

- Tariel's household skeletons are four hundred years old and all eleven are in one corner, which is not where people die. They were tidied. The yuan-ti tidy.
- The two dead guards mean Vess Ashen sent a patrol down eight days ago and nobody reported it, which means someone in the guard suppressed it.
- The missing figure in the mosaic is the single best identity clue in the site and pays off in V6, when Kathandar names him.

### Resolution, Rewards, and Temptation

**No Refusal.** The party descends knowing they are late.

**Treasure.** 1d6 bottles of Kharyl red (50 gp each). The guards' gear, and their tags, which should be carried out and returned, and which nobody will make them do.

## N2. The Chamber of Ten

*Gallery, 70 ft. by 30 ft. | Dark until entered, then green firelight | Exits: north door to N1's tunnel, south door (locked, keyed) | Smells of cold stone and scorched nothing*

> You cross the threshold and ten torches ignite at once in a blaze of green flame. They reveal ten statues, five against the west wall and five against the east, each exquisitely carved, each facing its opposite across the chamber.
>
> They are very good. They are better than good. Every one of them is caught mid-motion — a flail at the top of its arc, a bowstring at full draw, a mouth open on a word — and the carving of the cloth is so fine that it moves in the draught from the door.
>
> It is not carving.

### What Is in This Room

**West wall, north to south:**

1. **A human paladin in plate,** flail raised above his head in his right hand, left hand pointing directly across the chamber.
2. **A snake-like humanoid with six arms,** holding two curved swords, menacing the paladin opposite.
3. **An elven ranger,** bow drawn across the chamber, frozen against her foe.
4. **A beholder,** snarling, all eye stalks angled at the ranger.
5. **A human with a large backpack and a spear,** standing arrogantly, looking across the room, **with a key on a chain around his neck.**

**East wall, north to south:**

6. **A medusa,** hands on hips, staring across at the human with the key. Her head of snakes bends in all directions.
7. **A bare-chested dwarven barbarian,** an axe in each hand, roaring a challenge at an invisible foe.
8. **A strange humanoid resembling a wight,** frozen screaming at the ceiling.
9. **A halfling sorceress,** staff pointed across the room, face set in desperate concentration mid-cast.
10. **A basilisk,** reared back to strike at prey that is not there.

11. **The south door,** iron-bound, with a keyhole. It is the only way onward.
12. **The key,** around statue 5's neck — and made of stone, and therefore unusable and unremovable, because it petrified with him.
13. **Green torch-flame** that sheds bright light but no heat and does not gutter.
14. **Scuff marks** on the floor between the pairs. *Investigation DC 13:* the statues have been moved. Not much — a foot or two, in the last few centuries — and always to re-establish a clear line of sight.

### Setting the Scene

The reveal is the cloth. Describe the carving as superb, then describe a hem lifting in the draught, and let a player work out what they are looking at before you say it.

Play the room quietly. Nothing here is hostile, nothing here will move, and the horror is entirely in the arithmetic: ten people are alive inside these statues, in the middle of a second that has lasted four hundred years, and the party has the means to end that second for all of them.

### DM Information

**The puzzle is line of sight.** Each statue is petrified because it is looking at the thing opposite it — and each *petrifier* is itself petrified, gaze locked across the room. Break the sightline between a pair and the petrification on the victim ends.

The party will most likely work this out at pair 5-6, because that is the pair with the key, and because the medusa opposite is the most legible gaze-attacker in the room.

**Breaking a sightline** requires physically occluding it: a cloth over the medusa's face, a shield propped, a *wall of stone,* anything opaque between the two. Spells that create darkness work. *Greater restoration* also works on any individual statue and skips the puzzle for that one, which is fine at this tier — it costs a 5th-level slot per person and there are ten people.

**What happens when a statue is freed.** They come back into the middle of the second they left, at full speed, screaming. Then their four hundred years catch up. Each has a **death clock** of one round: they get one sentence, and then their body fails.

There is no saving them and the party should be allowed to try. *Lesser restoration, greater restoration,* a *heal* spell — none of it addresses a body that has been holding a breath for four centuries.

**Statue 5's last sentence,** the important one: *"Get out before it's too late — she will devour your soul!"* And then he collapses, and the key around his neck is flesh-warm bronze again, and it comes away in a character's hand.

**The others each get one sentence too,** and this is where the room earns its place in Vhal'Kathar. Each freed person says something unfinished, and each of them is a name that goes into the Catalogue in Act III, and a party that writes the names down can call every one of them at the Nail.

### Mechanics

**Spotting the Key (Perception or Investigation DC 14).** The key is around statue 5's neck and is stone.

**Occluding a Sightline (no check).** Any opaque barrier between a pair. One action. Say yes.

**The Revival (Scripted).** The freed creature acts immediately, gets one sentence, and drops at the end of that round. **No saving throw, no healing, no resurrection** — though resurrection magic *does* work in Act II, so a party that burns a *raise dead* on one of them should get that person back, permanently, confused and four hundred years out of date, and that is a wonderful complication rather than a problem.

**The Optional Fight.** If you want this room to bite: freed statues 1, 3, 7 and 9 — the adventurers — come back mid-combat and cannot tell the party from their original enemies. Run them as **Veteran, Scout, Berserker** and **Mage** respectively, each with **one round** before they die anyway. Four rounds of chaotic melee against people the party is trying to save, all of whom are doomed regardless, is an extremely Vhal'Kathar encounter.

**Refusal.** Killing a freed statue outright, rather than letting it fall: **+1 Refusal** each. They were not hostile in any sense that counts.

**Freeing a monster.** Statues 2, 4, 6, 8 and 10 can also be freed, and if freed they get a round and die like everyone else — except the **medusa** (statue 6), who has been petrified by her own reflection in the polished stone of statue 5's spear-head and who, if freed, has a full **Medusa** stat block and is furious and does not have a death clock, because she is not four hundred years past her own death. She was petrified last century and is fine.

Freeing her is a genuinely bad idea, entirely the party's fault, and **+0 Refusal**, because she immediately attacks and is therefore not peaceful.

### Creatures and NPCs

Nothing hostile unless the party creates it.

**The ten**, and their last sentences, which you should adapt to your campaign:

1. **Ser Alwen Roke,** paladin: *"Tell the chapterhouse the road is false."*
2. **Ssikathis,** six-armed yuan-ti: hisses one word in Abyssal — the name of the Medusa Queen — and it is not the name she uses now.
3. **Maerel Vint,** elven ranger: *"My brother is still on the stair."* (He is not. He has been dead four hundred years. He is in N4.)
4. The **beholder**: looks at each party member in turn, says *"No. No. No. No,"* and dies. It was counting something.
5. **Hallic Dray,** the man with the key: *"Get out before it's too late — she will devour your soul!"*
6. The **medusa**: see above.
7. **Brunna Ironcount,** dwarven barbarian: *"WHERE IS HE"* — and she is looking at the empty floor where statue 8's opposite number should be, and there are only nine pairs' worth of floor marks for ten statues.
8. The **wight-thing**: screams at the ceiling, continuously, for its entire round, and then falls apart. *Religion DC 16:* it is not a wight. It is a Pindarian, and the screaming is a name.
9. **Pell Orrow,** halfling sorceress: *"Did it work? Did it hold?"* — she was mid-*hold monster* — and if a character answers her honestly, **+1 Breath Token** to that character.
10. The **basilisk**: nothing. It is a basilisk.

### Set Piece

**The hem in the draught.** Before any check, before any reveal: as the party walks the line of statues, the elven ranger's cloak stirs in the air from the opened door. Stone does not do that.

And when they look closer, her eyes are wet.

### Lore Drops

- **L19 — The Missing Pair** (*Investigation DC 13*). Scuff marks show nine pairs of positions and ten statues. Brunna Ironcount's opposite number is not in the room, which means something was freed from this gallery already and walked out, and the yuan-ti have been carefully maintaining the sightlines of the remaining nine ever since.
- **L20 — The Queen's Old Name** (*from statue 2, if freed, and Abyssal*). The Medusa Queen has changed her name at least once. *History or Religion DC 18:* the old name belongs to a Pindarian priestess of the Void, which is not what a medusa should be, and which is the first crack in her divinity.
- **L21 — The Screaming Pindarian** (*Religion DC 16 on statue 8*). Not a wight. A Pindarian, four thousand years old, who was still walking around up here until somebody petrified him. The Saharim's deathless bodies do not decay — and one of them was loose in the necropolis.
- **L22 — Hallic Dray's Warning** (*automatic*). *She will devour your soul.* He is describing the Medusa Queen's regional effect, and he is being literal.

### Plot Hooks

- Any name the party writes down here can be spoken at the Nail in V7, and each of these ten has an unfinished sentence. Maerel Vint's brother in particular is findable in N4, which makes her one line into a small complete tragedy the party can close.
- The missing tenth statue walked out. Where it went is a question with an answer in N18.
- A *raise dead* spent on one of the ten produces a four-hundred-year-displaced NPC who knew Wahyrst before Wahyrst and who can identify the citadel statue on sight.

### Resolution, Rewards, and Temptation

The bronze key opens the south door and nothing else.

**+1 Refusal** per freed person killed rather than allowed to fall. **+1 Breath Token** for answering Pell Orrow honestly.

**Treasure.** Hallic Dray's large backpack contains four hundred-year-old rations, a *potion of superior healing*, 120 gp, and a journal whose last entry reads: *Day 9. The others have stopped talking about going back.* Ser Alwen's flail is a *+1 flail.* The rest is stone, and stays stone.

## N3. The Friendly Statue

*Mosaic corridor, 10 ft. wide, 90 ft. long, running south | Dark | Exits: north (N2), south (dead end at the statue), secret door west | Smells of dust and, faintly, of hot brass*

> Brilliant mosaics cover every inch of the walls and the vaulted ceiling, undimmed by the passing of centuries: minotaurs in Pindarian panoply fighting medusae and other serpent-things, panel after panel of it, the whole length of the corridor.
>
> The floor is plain except for a winding path of green tiles about two feet wide, snaking south. It ends at a bronze statue of a very serious-looking minotaur, who is holding his left hand out in front of him, palm forward, as if to say *halt.*

### What Is in This Room

1. **The mosaics,** forty-one panels of minotaurs fighting serpent-folk. *History DC 14:* these are not Wahyrst minotaurs. The panoply is Pindarian and the minotaurs are wearing Saharim funerary sigils, which means Pindaria had minotaur Watch, which means Wahyrst's founders were not the first minotaurs on this mountain — they were coming home and did not know it.
2. **The green path,** two feet wide, winding rather than straight, running the full ninety feet to the statue's feet. It does not correspond to any obstacle. It simply wanders.
3. **The bronze minotaur,** eight feet tall, left hand raised palm out, expression grave to the point of comedy.
4. **A secret door** in the west wall, forty feet back up the corridor from the statue, in the first passageway junction. *Perception DC 18* to find unaided; it opens on its own when the statue is greeted.
5. **Panel 37,** two-thirds of the way down: a minotaur in Saharim sigils standing *beside* a medusa rather than fighting her, both facing the same direction. *Investigation DC 15* to notice it is out of pattern.
6. **Wear on the statue's palm.** *Perception DC 12:* the bronze of the left hand is polished bright, and the rest of the statue is dull green with age. Thousands of hands have touched it.

### Setting the Scene

Play the corridor as a relief. The party has just come out of a room full of people dying at them; give them ninety feet of beautiful artwork and a joke.

The statue should be described with total sincerity — grave, dignified, monumental — precisely so the solution lands. He is asking for a high five. He has been asking for a high five for four thousand years, and everyone who has come down this corridor has walked up to him, read the room wrong, and turned back.

### DM Information

**The puzzle is the palm.** Touch the statue's raised hand and the secret door forty feet back opens. That is the entire mechanism. There is no trick, no cost, and no check.

**The green path is a red herring** and a good one. Players will assume it must be followed exactly, will treat stepping off it as lethal, and will spend twenty minutes edging along a two-foot ribbon of tile. Let them. Nothing happens either way. When they eventually ask, a **DC 14 Investigation** confirms the green tiles are simply a different quarry's stone used for a repair, and the winding is where the original floor cracked.

**The wear on the palm** is the fair clue, and it is worth making sure someone sees it.

**What the statue does** when touched: it smiles. Slowly, and far too wide, and with a great deal of bronze teeth. Then it lowers its arm, and from back up the corridor comes the sound of a heavy door swinging.

It does not smile in a friendly way. It is not unfriendly either. It has simply been holding that posture for four thousand years and its idea of an expression has drifted.

### Mechanics

**The High Five (no check).** Touch the palm. The west secret door opens. That is all.

**Finding the Secret Door the Hard Way (Perception DC 18).** Possible and pointless; the door is barred from the other side until the statue is greeted, so finding it early just tells the party where to look.

**Panel 37 (Investigation DC 15).** See *Lore Drops.*

**If the party attacks the statue:** bronze, AC 18, 80 hp, and it does nothing whatsoever while being destroyed. Destroying it permanently seals the secret door and the party must find another route — which means back through N1 and in via the sewer outfall, costing a day and **Race Clock +2.** No Refusal; it is not dead, it is a door handle.

### Creatures and NPCs

None. Roll the snake count if you like; snakes in a mosaic corridor read beautifully.

### Set Piece

**The smile.** A character puts their palm against the statue's. Nothing. Then, with the grinding of four thousand years of bronze, the minotaur's mouth widens — and keeps widening — into a grin of such enormous and unwarranted delight that it is genuinely unnerving, while the arm comes down, and somewhere behind them a door opens.

Then it holds the grin. It is still grinning when they leave.

### Lore Drops

- **L23 — Pindarian Minotaurs** (*History DC 14*). Minotaurs in Saharim funerary sigils, fighting for Pindaria, four thousand years ago. Wahyrst's founding myth says they came to Kharyl as refugees eleven hundred years back. Both can be true, and if both are true then somebody's ancestors were the Watch — which is why a bronze key-token has been hanging around a sergeant's family's neck ever since.
- **L24 — Panel 37** (*Investigation DC 15*). One panel out of forty-one shows a Saharim minotaur and a medusa standing together, facing the same enemy. *There was an alliance.* The serpent-folk were not always the enemy here, and the Medusa Queen in N18 is older than her cult believes.
- **L25 — The Polished Palm** (*Perception DC 12*). Thousands of hands. Whoever else has come this way, most of them figured it out.

### Plot Hooks

- L23 is the emotional argument the party will need in V6. Kathandar believes Pindaria's sacrifice bought nothing; a minotaur city of twenty-one thousand people standing on his roof, descended from his own Watch, is the counter-evidence, and it is walking around above him right now.
- Panel 37 sets up a non-violent resolution for N18 that most parties will never find.

### Resolution, Rewards, and Temptation

**No Refusal, no treasure, no damage.** The party gets a door and a laugh, and they will need both.

## N4. The Basilisk's Larder

*Cavern, irregular, roughly 80 ft. across, 25-ft. ceiling | Pitch dark | Exits: secret door north (from N3), tunnel south-west, water passage (submerged) | Smells of rotting meat and stagnant water*

> Bones crunch underfoot. The smell hits before anything else does — decaying flesh, thick and sweet, in a chamber where the air has nowhere to go. Somewhere in the south-eastern corner, water drips into water.
>
> Your light does not reach the far wall.

### What Is in This Room

1. **The bone floor,** eighteen inches deep across the whole chamber, comprising several hundred creatures. *Investigation DC 12:* almost all are yuan-ti. Something down here eats the cult faster than the cult can replace it, and the cult has decided to live with that.
2. **Statues,** eleven of them, half-buried in the bone. Yuan-ti mostly, in postures of flight. One is not: **a young elven man with a sword half-drawn, facing north.** This is Maerel Vint's brother from N2. *Perception DC 12* to spot among the rest.
3. **The pool,** thirty feet across in the south-eastern corner, black, twelve feet deep, fed by a seep. The **greater basilisk** lies in it when it is not in the shadows.
4. **A submerged passage** at the pool's bottom leading south-west. *Investigation DC 15* while in the water. It is the fast route to N6 and bypasses N5 entirely.
5. **Yuan-ti offerings** heaped at the pool's edge: goats, a cow, three humanoids, all fresh, all untouched. The cult feeds it and it is not interested.
6. **Claw-score marks** on the northern wall, high up, around the secret door. Something very large has spent a long time trying to get through that door and has not managed it.
7. **A single crossbow bolt** standing in the bone floor, black-fletched, unfired. Vhaskar's people came through, and one of them was running.

### Setting the Scene

Darkness is the whole encounter. The chamber is bigger than any light the party carries, and the greater basilisk is Huge, and those two facts should be established before anything happens.

Use sound. Bones shift somewhere out past the torchlight. The dripping stops for eleven seconds and then starts again. Something exhales.

Then have it come out of the pool behind them.

### DM Information

**The greater basilisk** (CR 15) is the hardest single fight in Act II and it is an ambush predator that has been eating yuan-ti for four centuries. It attacks from the pool or from the dark, targets whoever is carrying the light, and uses its **Poisonous Breath** the moment three or more characters are in a cone.

**Its gaze is the encounter.** DC 18 Constitution, petrification on a failure at the end of the following turn. Averting eyes is the answer and most parties will remember it about one round too late.

**The rooster clause is real.** The creature's *Respect for the Rooster* trait is not a joke a party can guess at, but a party that has been through Wahyrst under siege may well be carrying livestock, and a character who thinks to imitate a cock-crow should get a **DC 15 Performance** check and, on a success, a **DC 12 Wisdom save** from the basilisk to flee. Reward it enormously. It is the kind of thing that becomes a table legend.

**Maerel's brother** is petrified, not dead, and unlike the ten in N2 he has been stone for four hundred years without a gaze-lock holding him — which means *greater restoration* restores him fully, alive, with no death clock. He is the one person in this dungeon the party can actually save.

His name is **Taeleth Vint**, he has no idea his sister was two rooms away, and telling him is one of the hardest conversations you will run in this act.

### Mechanics

**Greater Basilisk** (CR 15; AC 18, 184 hp, immune necrotic and poison, resistant to nonmagical weapons, darkvision 90 ft.).

- **Petrifying Gaze.** DC 18 Constitution at the start of a creature's turn within 30 feet. Averting eyes avoids the save but blinds the creature to the basilisk until its next turn.
- **Multiattack.** Two bites at +11 for 22 (3d10 + 6) piercing, each requiring a **DC 18 Constitution** save against an additional 10 (3d6) poison.
- **Poisonous Breath (3/day).** 60-foot cone, **DC 18 Constitution,** 31 (9d6) poison, half on a success.
- **Respect for the Rooster.** On hearing a cock-crow, **DC 12 Wisdom** save or flee the area, repeating at the end of each of its turns.

**Terrain.** The bone floor is **difficult terrain** for anything Medium or smaller and not for the basilisk. A creature knocked prone in it makes a **DC 12 Dexterity** check to stand without taking 3 (1d6) piercing.

**The Pool.** 12 feet deep, black. A character dragged in fights at disadvantage against a creature that swims comfortably. The submerged passage requires **two rounds** of swimming and a **DC 13 Athletics** check.

**Restoring Taeleth (greater restoration, or one hour and a DC 20 Medicine check with a basilisk's blood).** Yes — basilisk blood mixed with wine reverses petrification in folklore, and this is a campaign where that should work. Killing the basilisk gives the party the means to free him without spending the slot.

### Creatures and NPCs

**One greater basilisk.** No name, no motive, no dialogue. It is an enormous animal in a dark room and that is scarier than anything you could give it.

**Taeleth Vint** (Scout), elven, twenty-six, petrified four hundred years ago while drawing a sword to cover his sister's retreat. He is calm, unbearably polite, and asks after Maerel within his first three sentences.

If the party freed and lost her in N2, they have to tell him. If they *raised* her, he gets her back. If they never entered N2, he does not know she was ever here, and you should leave that gun on the mantelpiece.

### Set Piece

**The dripping stops.** Eleven seconds of absolute silence while the party works out what has changed — and then the pool comes up all at once, thirty feet of it, in one motion, and the light goes out because whoever was holding it is now in the water.

### Lore Drops

- **L26 — The Yuan-ti Diet** (*Investigation DC 12*). Four hundred years of yuan-ti bones. The cult knows this thing is here, feeds it, and has never once tried to kill it. *Religion DC 15:* the offerings at the pool's edge are arranged in the same pattern as the altar offerings in N8. **They worship it too,** or worship something through it.
- **L27 — The Scored Door** (*Perception DC 13*). Claw marks all around the northern secret door, high and deep and old. The basilisk has spent centuries trying to get into the mosaic corridor and cannot. Something about N3 keeps it out, and the same something presumably keeps it out of the pyramid below.
- **L28 — The Black Bolt** (*automatic*). Unfired, dropped, standing in the bone. One of Vhaskar's people came through here at a dead run and did not stop to pick it up.
- **L29 — From Taeleth, freely.** He and his sister came down four hundred years ago with a company of eleven, hired by a Wahyrst merchant named Tariel to find out what was under his cellar. He can name all eleven. **Four of those names are statues in N2** and the party can now match faces to them.

### Plot Hooks

- Taeleth is a living NPC from four hundred years ago who can identify the Medusa Queen, describe N18 before it was a throne room, and confirm what the tenth statue in N2 was.
- The cult feeding the basilisk suggests a hierarchy the party can exploit: something in the Queen's theology places this animal above her priests.
- Basilisk blood is worth taking. Two more petrified people are coming.

### Resolution, Rewards, and Temptation

**No Refusal** for killing the basilisk; it is a hostile animal. **+1 Refusal** for leaving Taeleth petrified once the party knows he can be freed — the pyramid does not distinguish much between abandoning the dead and abandoning the nearly-dead.

**Treasure.** In the bone floor, *Investigation DC 15* per ten minutes: 1,400 sp, 600 gp, a *+1 shortsword*, a *cloak of protection*, and 2d6 vials of basilisk blood harvestable from the corpse. Taeleth's sword is a *+1 longsword* with the Vint arms on the pommel.

## N5. The Black Knight

*Armoury chamber, 50 ft. by 40 ft. | Dark | Exits: north tunnel (N4), east door (N6) | Smells of oil, iron, and old parchment*

> A statue of a knight in black plate armour stands at the room's centre, both hands outstretched, palms up, waiting to be given something. Weapon racks line the northern and southern walls, heavy with arms that have not rusted. Opposite the knight stands a small writing desk with a chair pushed neatly under it.
>
> At the knight's feet, a bronze plaque:
>
> **BRING TO ME THE MIGHTIEST WEAPON**

### What Is in This Room

**The racks hold eight weapons, each named, each perfectly preserved:**

| Weapon | Description | Damage | Value |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Deathsmasher** | Warhammer, bright yellow haft, leather grip | 1d6 | 7 gp |
| **Oath Shaker** | Greatsword, blood-red blade, Elvish inscriptions | 1d8 | 30 gp |
| **Mercy Giver** | Glaive, bright blue blade, braided black leather | 1d8 | 11 gp |
| **Giant Crusher** | Maul, a giant's skull with the maul protruding from its open mouth | 1d8 | 5 gp |
| **Peacemaker** | Longsword, rusted, olive branch wound around it | 1d6 | 9 gp |
| **King's Knight** | Morningstar, black haft, ivory head sculpted on the hilt | 1d6 | 9 gp |
| **Soul Harvester** | Scimitar, deep blue, shifting colour as you look at it | 1d12 | 300 gp |
| **Shrubbery** | Barbed trident of solid gold | 1d10 | 1,500 gp |

1. **The black knight,** a statue, seven feet, hands out. Not animate. Not undead. A vending machine with opinions.
2. **The writing desk,** three drawers. The top drawer holds a **quill and an inkpot.** The others hold blank vellum and a stick of sealing wax.
3. **The plaque,** bronze, single line, in Common — which is wrong for a four-hundred-year-old yuan-ti squat and wronger for a Pindarian site. *Investigation DC 14:* the plaque has been re-cut. Underneath, in Pindarian, the original line reads the same thing.
4. **Eight empty brackets** on the racks with no weapons in them. Sixteen brackets, eight weapons. Somebody has been here and taken the correct answer's worth of wrong answers.
5. **Scorch marks and gouges** across the floor around the knight, hundreds of them, at every angle.

### Setting the Scene

Play this as a puzzle room that presents as a treasure room. The weapons are gorgeous and named and the values are printed on nothing — the party will assess them by eye, and their eye will tell them that the gold trident and the shifting scimitar are the good ones.

Let them take their time. Let them argue about it. The longer they argue about which weapon is mightiest, the better the answer lands.

### DM Information

**The answer is the quill.**

The pen is mightier than the sword, the desk is placed deliberately opposite the knight, and the puzzle has exactly one correct solution. Put the quill in the knight's hands and the eastern door unlocks, the knight lowers its arms, and nothing attacks anyone.

**Any weapon placed in the knight's hands animates and attacks.** Not the knight — the *weapon.* It lifts out of the statue's palms and comes at the party as an **Animated Armament** (below), and the statue stands there impassively holding nothing, waiting for the next attempt.

There is no limit to the attempts. A party determined to brute-force this room will fight eight animated weapons one at a time, and the eighth is *Shrubbery*, and by then the joke will have thoroughly worn off, which is itself funny.

**The gouges in the floor** are the fair clue: hundreds of previous attempts, all wrong.

**The empty brackets** are Vhaskar. He solved this instantly — he is four thousand years old and has a sense of humour — and then looted eight weapons from the other racks on his way past because there was no reason not to.

### Mechanics

**Placing an item in the knight's hands (no check).**

- **The quill (or ink, or vellum, or any instrument of writing):** the east door unlocks. Room complete.
- **Any weapon:** it animates as an **Animated Armament** using the weapon's own damage die, and attacks. It fights until destroyed and then falls inert and can be taken normally.
- **Anything else** (a rock, a boot, a rude gesture): nothing happens. The knight is not offended.

**Identifying the trick.** No check is required and none should be offered. If the party is comprehensively stuck after twenty minutes of real time, have a character notice — *Perception DC 10,* freely given — that the desk is the only piece of furniture in the room and that it has been placed to face the knight directly, which nobody would do by accident.

**Taking the weapons.** All eight are mundane and can be taken freely. *Soul Harvester* (1d12 scimitar) and *Shrubbery* (1d10 gold trident) are non-magical oddities worth their listed value and nothing more, and a party that hauls a 1,500 gp gold trident through the rest of this dungeon has earned it.

**Refusal.** None. Nothing here is dead.

### Creatures and NPCs

None, unless the party creates them.

**Animated Armament**

*A weapon that has been told it is the mightiest and believes it.*

| **STR** | **DEX** | **CON** | **INT** | **WIS** | **CHA** |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 16 (+3) | 17 (+3) | 13 (+1) | 1 (-5) | 5 (-3) | 1 (-5) |

**Damage Immunities.** poison, psychic

**Condition Immunities.** blinded, charmed, deafened, frightened, paralyzed, petrified, poisoned, prone

**Senses.** blindsight 60 ft. (blind beyond), passive Perception 7

**Challenge.** 3 (700 XP)

**Traits**

**Antimagic Susceptibility.** Incapacitated for 1 minute in an *antimagic field*; a targeted *dispel magic* suppresses it for 1 minute on a failed DC 13 Constitution save against the caster's spell DC.

**Actions**

**Strike (2/turn).** *Melee Weapon Attack:* +6 to hit, reach 5 ft., one target. *Hit:* the weapon's own damage die + 3, of the weapon's own type, plus 7 (2d6) force.

*Two entries. A flying sword does not need a bonus action.*

### Set Piece

**The quill.** A character walks past eight named, gleaming, four-hundred-year-old weapons, opens a drawer, takes out a goose feather, and lays it across the palms of a seven-foot armoured statue.

The knight closes its hands around it. Very gently. And bows.

### Lore Drops

- **L30 — The Re-cut Plaque** (*Investigation DC 14*). The Common inscription is a modern re-cut over an identical Pindarian original. *Somebody translated this puzzle for later visitors on purpose.* The Pindarians did not need to be told, and the yuan-ti do not read Common. That leaves one candidate, and he has been down here a long time, and his statue is standing in the citadel upstairs.
- **L31 — The Empty Brackets** (*Perception DC 12*). Eight weapons gone from sixteen brackets, taken within the last eight days, taken without a single scorch mark added to the floor. Whoever came through solved this room first try.
- **L32 — The Gouges** (*automatic*). Hundreds of wrong answers. Four hundred years of yuan-ti, adventurers, and cultists walking in here, looking at a gold trident, and reaching for it.

### Plot Hooks

- L30 implies Kathandar has been maintaining the upper necropolis — translating its puzzles, tidying its dead, keeping the basilisk out of the mosaic corridor. He is not sealed in his tomb. He has been *housekeeping*, upward, for four thousand years, and he has watched the yuan-ti build a slaughterhouse over his archive and has done nothing about it, and the party is entitled to ask him why.
- *Shrubbery* is 1,500 gp of solid gold and Barak wanted an offering for a dragon.

### Resolution, Rewards, and Temptation

**No Refusal.** **Treasure:** any or all of the eight weapons, plus a quill that has now been bowed to by a statue and which at least one player will insist on keeping forever.

## N6. The Subterranean Garden

*Cavern garden, 100 ft. by 70 ft., 40-ft. ceiling | Dim light, phosphorescent | Exits: west door (N5), the waterfall (south wall, sealed) | Smells of wet green growth, mineral water, and old blood*

> Phosphorescent plants grow in terraces across the whole chamber — pale blues, sick greens, a few deep violets — and they throw a soft shifting light over dozens of statues of monsters set among the beds. A waterfall comes down the southern wall with real force, loud in the enclosed space, filling a channel that runs away east.
>
> In front of the waterfall stands a stone basin on a plinth. Its rim is caked with dried blood, layer on layer, decades of it.

### What Is in This Room

1. **The plants,** forty-odd species, all phosphorescent, all thriving with no sunlight. *Nature DC 15:* six of them are extinct above ground. *Nature DC 18:* they are not growing in soil. The beds are packed with bone meal, finely ground, and there is a great deal of it.
2. **The statues,** twenty-two, all monsters — basilisks, cockatrices, a small medusa, serpent-things — arranged among the terraces like garden ornaments. They are petrified creatures, not carvings.
3. **The waterfall,** twenty feet wide, loud, cold, with solid rock behind it.
4. **The basin,** stone, three feet across, blood-caked, empty and dry.
5. **An inscription** around the basin's rim in Undercommon, worn but legible: **"Sacrifice the liquid of life that you hold."**
6. **The channel,** running east, ankle-deep, feeding the yuan-ti complex's water supply.
7. **A gardener's tools** in a niche: shears, a trowel, a watering can, all in current use, all recently cleaned.

### Setting the Scene

The garden should be the most beautiful room in the necropolis and the party should not trust it for a second.

Then run the red herring properly. As they approach the basin, everyone senses movement among the terraces — peripheral, definite, multiple. Investigation finds nothing alive. But when they look back, **the statues have moved.** Different positions. Different arrangement. Two of them are closer.

Let them spend as long as they want on this. It is nothing. It has never been anything. The yuan-ti gardener rearranges the ornaments.

### DM Information

**The puzzle is the basin and the inscription.** *The liquid of life that you hold* is water — every waterskin the party carries, poured into the basin. Blood also works and is what the yuan-ti use, which is why the rim is caked, and which is a perfectly valid solution that costs the party hit points instead of supplies.

**On completion,** the waterfall turns to blood — the full twenty feet of it, instantly, with the sound changing from a rush to something thicker — every plant in the garden flushes red and begins pulsing in time, and an opening appears in the rock behind the falling blood.

**The party must walk through the blood** to proceed. There is no way around it. They arrive in N7 soaked to the skin in it, which is the entire reason N7 works.

**Law 3 begins here.** From the moment the waterfall turns, necrotic damage dealt inside this complex reduces the dealer's own maximum. Announce it when it first bites, not before.

### Mechanics

**Reading the Inscription.** Undercommon, or *comprehend languages,* or **Intelligence (Investigation) or Wisdom (Perception) DC 15** to find and transcribe it for later translation.

**Filling the Basin (no check).** The basin needs roughly two gallons. That is **every waterskin the party carries** — all of it, not a token amount, and they do not get it back. Going forward without water is a real cost in a dungeon with a yuan-ti kitchen at the other end.

**The Blood Alternative.** A character may open a vein instead: **3 (1d6) slashing** and **two levels of exhaustion**, or **14 (4d6) necrotic** from a willing donor's life force with no exhaustion. This is what the cult does. Using blood rather than water **does not** cost Refusal — the pyramid has no opinion about the living harming themselves — but the Medusa Queen's cult will read the fresh offering as a signal and **the alarm in N8 is pre-raised.**

**Partial fills do nothing.** The basin must be full. A party that pours in one waterskin to see what happens learns nothing, which is correct.

**Walking the Blood (no check).** Everyone comes through drenched. It cannot be cleaned off in the channel — the channel is now blood too. *Prestidigitation* works, takes a minute per creature, and any party that thinks of it has neatly disarmed N7 and should be allowed to.

**The Statues.** Twenty-two petrified creatures. *Greater restoration* on any of them produces a live, furious monster. Do not signpost this. Do not prevent it either.

### Creatures and NPCs

**No hostiles.** The gardener is a yuan-ti pureblood named **Iss-Tereth** who is not in the room and who will be in N9. If the party takes his tools, he will notice, and he will care more about that than about the dead worshippers.

### Set Piece

**The turn.** The last waterskin empties into the basin. For two full seconds nothing happens and the waterfall keeps roaring.

Then the sound changes — drops an octave, thickens, becomes a sound like something being poured rather than something falling — and the water goes red from the top down, twenty feet of it converting in less than a heartbeat.

Every plant in the garden flushes crimson and begins to pulse, all of them together, at about sixty beats a minute.

The garden has a heart rate.

### Lore Drops

- **L33 — The Bone Meal** (*Nature DC 18*). The terraces are packed with finely ground bone. *Medicine DC 15:* humanoid, mixed, and there is enough of it to represent several thousand individuals. The cult has been composting its victims for four centuries and growing extinct Pindarian flora in them.
- **L34 — The Extinct Flora** (*Nature DC 15*). Six species here have not existed on the surface since before Wahyrst. They are Pindarian garden plants, and this cavern was a garden before the yuan-ti found it — which means somebody has been keeping it alive down here, continuously, for four thousand years, and the trowel in the niche was cleaned this week.
- **L35 — The Basin's Language** (*Undercommon, automatic once translated*). The inscription is in Undercommon, not Pindarian or Abyssal. Whoever wrote it was addressing the yuan-ti specifically, in their own tongue, with an instruction that keeps the door working. Somebody is helping them without their knowledge.
- **L36 — The Moving Statues** (*nothing, ever*). It is the gardener. It has always been the gardener. Never confirm it.

### Plot Hooks

- The gardener's tools, cleaned weekly, and the extinct plants, thriving, are the same thread as the re-cut plaque in N5. Kathandar's housekeeping reaches this high.
- Two gallons of water is a real logistical hit in a complex whose only other water source runs through a yuan-ti kitchen.
- The bone meal is several thousand people and every one of them has a name in the Catalogue.

### Resolution, Rewards, and Temptation

**No Refusal.** The party proceeds soaked in blood, out of water, into a corridor containing two things that can smell blood.

**Treasure.** The gardener's shears are a masterwork tool. Cuttings from the six extinct species are worth 200 gp each to any herbalist or druid circle in Elderon and considerably more to the right scholar.

## N7. The Blood Bank

*Corridor, 60 ft. by 15 ft. | Dim, red, pulsing | Exits: the blood-fall (north, back to N6), two doors west | Smells overwhelmingly of blood, because everyone in the room is covered in it*

> You come through the fall into a narrow room, red light pulsing along the walls from the roots of the garden above. Two doors stand in the western wall.
>
> Between you and them, two heavy mounds of sticky crimson sludge have begun, without any particular hurry, to move in your direction.

### What Is in This Room

1. **Two blood oozes,** each the size of a cart, moving slowly and inevitably toward the party.
2. **The blood-fall behind them,** which is the only way back and which is currently a wall of blood.
3. **Two western doors,** unlocked, leading to N8 (the temple, south) and N9 (the rectory, north). *Investigation DC 12:* the southern door is worn around the handle from constant use; the northern one is not.
4. **Drain grates** along the corridor's centre line, six of them, running the length of the room. They are what keeps this corridor from being knee-deep, and they are how the oozes travel.
5. **Husks,** eleven of them, dry and grey and flat, pressed against the walls at the corridor's western end. These are what the oozes leave. *Medicine DC 10:* completely exsanguinated. *Investigation DC 14:* three are wearing Wahyrst guard leathers and one of those has a crossbow bolt in its neck. **Vhaskar lost a man here.**
6. **A satchel** dropped beside the husks, containing a sealed vellum tube.

### Setting the Scene

Short, fast, nasty. The party arrives covered in exactly the substance these creatures hunt, in a corridor fifteen feet wide with no cover, having just spent their water.

Describe the oozes as unhurried. They do not need to hurry. There is nowhere to go.

### DM Information

**This is the Law 3 tutorial.** Blood oozes deal necrotic damage and *gain temporary hit points equal to it* — and the party, if they are playing a necromancer, warlock, or anyone with necrotic damage, is about to discover that dealing necrotic here costs them their own maximum.

State the rule the first time it triggers, clearly, out of character if you must: *"You dealt 16 necrotic. Your hit point maximum drops by 16 until you long rest outside this place."* Then let them adjust. The rule is about to matter for the rest of the site and this is the cheapest possible place to learn it.

**Being covered in blood** gives the oozes advantage on their attacks against a soaked creature. A party that used *prestidigitation* in N6 does not suffer this, and you should make sure they notice they were rewarded.

**Radiant damage** shuts down *Blood Drain* at the start of the ooze's next turn, which is the intended counter and which a cleric will find immediately.

**The satchel** holds Vhaskar's own working notes — a partial transcription of the Descent of Names, six hundred names deep, in a fast clerical hand, with roughly one in twenty circled. This is the single most important object in Act II. See *Lore Drops.*

### Mechanics

**Two Blood Oozes** (CR 6 each; AC 8, 76 hp, immune to acid, fire, necrotic and slashing).

- **Pseudopod.** +5 to hit, 6 (1d6 + 3) bludgeoning plus 16 (3d10) necrotic; the ooze gains temporary hit points equal to the necrotic damage.
- **Blood Drain.** Touching one, or hitting it in melee from within 5 feet, deals **5 (1d10) necrotic** to the toucher and grants the ooze that much in temporary hit points. Suppressed at the start of its next turn if it takes radiant damage.
- **Overflow (reaction).** When hit in melee, the attacker makes a **DC 15 Constitution save,** taking 11 (2d10) necrotic on a failure or half on a success; the ooze gains temporary hit points equal to the damage.
- **Amorphous, Spider Climb.** They come along the ceiling and up through the drains, and they will.

**Soaked in Blood.** Any creature that walked through the fall without cleaning has **disadvantage on Dexterity saving throws** against ooze attacks and grants the oozes **advantage** on attack rolls against it, until cleaned.

**The Drains.** A character can pull a grate (**Athletics DC 13**) and force themselves into the drain channel — cramped, foul, and a way to bypass the corridor entirely at the cost of two rounds and the **prone** condition on emerging. It also means splitting the party in a corridor with two oozes in it, which is its own decision.

**Refusal.** None. Oozes are not dead; they are made of dead people, which is different, and the pyramid does not count it.

### Creatures and NPCs

Two blood oozes, no names, no motives. **Temple guardians** in the classical sense: the congealed remains of four centuries of sacrifice, which took on the dark energy of the place and answered a prayer nobody meant to make.

**The eleven husks** are named if the party looks: yuan-ti mostly, but three of Wahyrst's missing patrol, including **Corporal Weft's** partner. Reading those names aloud does nothing here. The corridor is too far from the Nail. That failure is worth including.

### Set Piece

**The husk with the bolt.** In the middle of the fight, or just after it, a character sees that one of the flattened grey shapes against the wall is wearing black shadowsilk and has a black-fletched bolt through its neck.

Vhaskar shot his own man. *Medicine DC 13:* the bolt went in after the ooze had already started, from behind, at close range. It was mercy, and it was efficient, and it took about a second, and he did not slow down.

### Lore Drops

- **L37 — The Transcription** (*in the satchel, automatic*). Six hundred names from the Descent of Names, copied in a fast clerical hand, roughly one in twenty circled. *Investigation DC 15:* every circled name belongs to someone who died **violently and with unfinished business.** He is not collecting the dead. He is screening them, and he is screening for grievance. **He does not want an army of corpses. He wants a jury.**
- **L38 — The Worn Handle** (*Investigation DC 12*). The southern door's handle is worn smooth; the northern is not. The temple is used constantly and the rectory hardly at all, which tells the party which door leads to the congregation and which to the paperwork.
- **L39 — Vhaskar's Mercy** (*Medicine DC 13*). He shot his own man in the back of the neck from two feet away while the ooze had him, and moved on. Whatever he is, he is not cruel, and that is worse.
- **L40 — The Husks' Names** (*Investigation DC 10*). Three of Wahyrst's missing patrol. Read aloud, they do not answer, because the Nail is still two hundred feet below. The party will remember this when the names start answering in Act III.

### Plot Hooks

- L37 reframes the entire villain plot. Vhaskar is assembling a body of witnesses with legitimate grievances against the living, and the Black Catalogue is his source of jurors, and what he intends to hold is a trial.
- The transcription includes names the party will recognise — put one of their own dead in it, circled.
- The drain system connects most of the necropolis and is a viable infiltration route for the rest of Act II.

### Resolution, Rewards, and Temptation

**No Refusal.** **Treasure:** Vhaskar's satchel (the transcription, a stick of black chalk, and 300 gp in Wahyrst coin taken off the dead patrol), and whatever the eleven husks were carrying — 1d100 gp, two *potions of healing*, and Corporal Weft's partner's tag, which belongs to somebody upstairs.

The party is now at the temple door, out of water, covered in blood, and about to walk into eleven yuan-ti in the middle of a service.

## N8. The Temple of the Devouring Queen

*Temple hall, 90 ft. by 60 ft., 35-ft. ceiling | Dim light from red braziers | Exits: east door (N7), north arch (N9), south arch (N13), stair down behind the altar (barred) | Smells of incense, warm stone, and blood that has been allowed to dry properly*

> A group of hooded figures kneel in ranks before an altar, chanting in Abyssal, and at their head a snake-headed monstrosity leads them through the responses with the unhurried confidence of someone who has done this every day for a very long time.
>
> Behind the altar stands a bust of a medusa's head, twelve feet tall, carved from black stone. Its eyes are set with deep red gems, and they pulse — slowly, in time with the chanting, brightening on every response.
>
> Behind *that*, at the back of the sanctuary, a stair goes down, and it is barred with an iron grille, and the grille is not yuan-ti work.

### What Is in This Room

1. **Ten yuan-ti purebloods** in hooded robes, kneeling in two ranks of five.
2. **One yuan-ti malison** (type 1, snake head) at the altar: **Ssarveth Kol**, First Voice of the Queen, four hundred years in post.
3. **The altar,** a slab of Pindarian stone with the yuan-ti's own channels cut into its surface, badly, by someone who did not understand what they were cutting into. *Investigation DC 14:* beneath the gore-channels, the original inscription is still legible in places. It is a *name index* — the altar is a page.
4. **The medusa bust,** twelve feet, black stone, ruby eyes that pulse with the chant. It is a hundred years old at most and is the newest object in the room.
5. **Braziers,** nine of them, burning something resinous and sweet. Not drugged. That is N9.
6. **The barred stair,** behind and below the altar, going down. The grille is dressed Pindarian iron, set into Pindarian stone, and *Investigation DC 13* shows it was installed from **below**. The yuan-ti did not bar this stair. Something on the other side did, and they have simply never been able to open it.
7. **Nine kneeling corpses** among the ranks if the Race Clock is 3 or higher: purebloods shot through the neck mid-prayer and left in position, because moving them would have taken time.
8. **A gong** on the northern wall with a padded mallet. The alarm.

### Setting the Scene

Let the party listen before they act. The chant is genuinely beautiful — a call-and-response in Abyssal with real liturgical structure — and the congregation is devout rather than menacing. These are people at church.

Then translate it, if anyone can, and it is praise to Merrshaulk for delivering them into the service of the Medusa Queen, and gratitude for the *provision*, and the provision is the larder two rooms away.

Play Ssarveth Kol as a priest, not a monster. He is courteous, genuinely pious, and wholly convinced. He will attempt to *convert* the party before he attempts to kill them, and if there is a yuan-ti, snake-blooded, or serpentfolk character in the group he will be delighted to see them and will not immediately understand why they are angry.

### DM Information

**This is the necropolis's main engagement** and the shape of everything after it. The temple sits at the hub: N9 north, N13 south, and the barred stair down that the party cannot use yet.

**The barred stair is the important object in this room.** It is the direct route into Act III and it is sealed from below by Pindarian iron. The party will need to reach the Medusa Queen's chamber at N18 to descend, because her throne room floor is the archive's ceiling and it is cracked. Point at the grille, let them test it, and let them fail.

**The alarm cascade** governs the rest of the complex. Track it:

- **Once seven of the eleven celebrants are down,** one pureblood breaks for the southern arch to raise the acolytes in N10. If it gets out: N10's occupants are awake and armed.
- **The gong,** if struck, wakes **everything** — N10, N12, N14, and N15 all converge on the temple within 1d4 + 2 rounds. This is a deadly encounter and a legitimate way for a loud party to die.
- **Vhaskar's trail (Race Clock 3+)** means nine of the celebrants are already dead and the engagement is two purebloods and Ssarveth, who is standing among his murdered congregation finishing the service alone.

**That last version is the best one.** If you can arrange for the Race Clock to be at 3 when the party arrives, take it: a priest completing the responses for nine corpses, refusing to stop, is a far better scene than a fight.

### Mechanics

**The Congregation.** Ten **Yuan-ti Purebloods** (CR 1) and one **Yuan-ti Malison, Type 1** (CR 3). At levels 9-11 this is not a hard fight on its own; the difficulty is entirely in whether it stays contained.

**Surprise.** The party is coming through a door the congregation cannot see past, drenched in blood, from a corridor that always smells of blood. **Stealth DC 13** (not 18 — the chanting covers a great deal) for a surprise round.

**The Runner.** When the seventh celebrant falls, one pureblood uses its action to Dash for the southern arch. It has 60 feet to cover. Stopping it requires killing it or restraining it that round — a clean, legible, one-round problem, and the party should be told exactly what it is doing.

**The Gong.** Any creature can strike it as an action. **DC 15 Dexterity save** for any party member within 5 feet to prevent it in time by interposing. If struck, roll the reinforcement timer openly.

**Reading the Altar (Investigation DC 14, then Pindarian).** Beneath the cult's channels: a name index, four columns, alphabetical, cut in the same hand as the Descent of Names. **This is a page of the Black Catalogue,** and the yuan-ti have been butchering people on it for four hundred years without ever once looking at it.

A character who reads a name off the altar aloud gets an answer. Not from a corpse — from the room. A voice in the empty air, mild and clerical, saying *"Present."*

**The Bust.** AC 15, 60 hp. Destroying it drops the ruby eyes (800 gp each) and causes every yuan-ti in the complex to make a **DC 13 Wisdom save** or be frightened of the party for 1 minute. It also permanently ends the Queen's ability to scry this room, which matters if the party wants to return.

**Refusal.** **+0** for killing the congregation; they are hostile and alive. **+1** for destroying the altar, which is a Pindarian record and not the cult's property to have defaced or the party's to finish off.

### Creatures and NPCs

**Ssarveth Kol, First Voice** (Yuan-ti Malison, Type 1, with Religion +5 and Persuasion +5). Snake-headed, robed, elderly by yuan-ti standards. He has led two services a day for four hundred years and has never missed one.

**His pitch,** delivered before initiative if the party allows it: the Queen offers *continuance.* Not immortality — the yuan-ti have no interest in that — but *being kept.* She remembers her faithful, individually, forever, and she can prove it, and Ssarveth can name every one of the eleven thousand people who have died on his altar because she has told him all of them.

He is telling the truth about the memory and lying about where it comes from. She is not remembering them. She is standing on top of a machine that does, and she has been taking credit for four centuries.

**Iss-Tereth,** the gardener, is not here; he is in N9 and does not attend services.

### Set Piece

**"Present."**

A character reads a name off the altar — any name, one of the four columns, chosen at random. There is a pause of about a second.

Then a voice, from nowhere in particular, at conversational volume, mild and slightly bored, the voice of a clerk who has been asked to confirm an entry:

*"Present."*

And then, after a moment, as if the clerk has looked up:

*"...you're standing on him."*

### Lore Drops

- **L41 — The Altar Is a Page** (*Investigation DC 14*). The cult's holiest object is a Pindarian name index, defaced with drainage channels. Four hundred years of sacrifice performed on a filing system. *Nobody down here knows what any of this is.*
- **L42 — The Grille from Below** (*Investigation DC 13*). Pindarian iron, installed from the underside, within the last four centuries. Somebody below noticed the yuan-ti arrive and quietly locked the door. That is not the act of a mindless guardian; it is the act of a caretaker who decided the intruders were tolerable so long as they stayed upstairs.
- **L43 — The New Bust** (*History DC 12*). A hundred years old at most, in a complex four hundred years occupied and four thousand years old. The Queen's iconography is recent. She was not worshipped as a goddess when this cult arrived; she became one, on a schedule, and the party can ask when and why.
- **L44 — Eleven Thousand Names** (*from Ssarveth, freely, while preaching*). He can recite them. He will, at length, if permitted, and he does not tire. He believes this is a miracle of the Queen. It is a symptom of the building.

### Plot Hooks

- Ssarveth taken alive is the single best guide to the necropolis and will cooperate readily in exchange for being allowed to keep believing. Whether the party tells him the truth about the altar is a real and cruel choice.
- The barred stair is the shortcut the party cannot have, and they will remember it when they finally come up on the other side of it from N19.
- Eleven thousand names on that altar go into the Catalogue, and Vhaskar has not been here yet, and he is going to be delighted.

### Resolution, Rewards, and Temptation

**Treasure.** Two ruby eyes (800 gp each), Ssarveth's ceremonial scimitar (*+1 scimitar*, snake-hilted), 900 gp in offering coin beneath the altar slab, and a censer of beaten gold worth 250 gp.

**+1 Refusal** if the altar is destroyed. **The barred stair does not open.**

## N9. The Rectory

*Working office, 40 ft. by 30 ft. | Warm lamplight | Exits: south arch (N8), west door (N10) | Smells of sweet, sickly incense — heavily*

> Two small desks stand against opposite walls, both buried in books and loose papers. A thurible hangs from a chain on the wall, smoking steadily, and the smell it puts out is sweet to the point of nausea, thick enough to taste.
>
> Someone has been working in here recently. There is a cup of tea on the left-hand desk and it is still warm.

### What Is in This Room

1. **The thurible,** brass, hanging, burning a resin compound continuously. It is a security measure and it is why this room is not locked.
2. **Two desks.** The left is neat: ritual calendars, service rosters, a ledger of sacrifices by date and number. The right is chaotic: seed catalogues, propagation notes, pressed flower specimens, and four hundred years of horticultural journals in a small careful hand.
3. **Stacks of letters and books** in Abyssal describing rites, ceremonies, and the Queen's liturgical calendar. *Roughly 60 lbs of material.*
4. **Vestments,** priestly and acolyte, on pegs. Eleven sets.
5. **Sacrificial blades,** six, in a rack, ritually clean, obsidian-edged.
6. **Iss-Tereth**, the gardener, at the right-hand desk, in an apron, not wearing vestments, writing up cuttings.
7. **A locked cabinet** (*thieves' tools DC 15*) holding the cult's genuine secrets: the Queen's true correspondence.
8. **One book that is not Abyssal,** on the right-hand desk under a seed tray: Pindarian, water-damaged, and it is a gardener's manual.

### Setting the Scene

The incense hits first and it should be described as physically unpleasant before anyone is asked to roll.

Then Iss-Tereth looks up from his seed trays and says, mildly, in accented Common: *"You're dripping on the floor."*

He is not going to fight. Play that immediately and clearly.

### DM Information

**The incense is a trap and a good one.** Every creature entering makes a **DC 16 Constitution saving throw** or is paralysed for 1 hour. If the whole party fails, they wake in the cages in **N17**, stripped of gear, which is a legitimate and recoverable disaster and one of the best things that can happen to this adventure.

**Iss-Tereth is immune** — four centuries of exposure — and he is not a threat. He is a pureblood gardener who has never attended a service, cannot recite the liturgy, and took this posting because it was the only job in the cult that involved plants. He has spent four hundred years quietly maintaining a Pindarian garden from a Pindarian manual he found in a drawer and cannot fully read.

**He is the party's best informant in Act II** and he will trade. What he wants is his cuttings, his tools, and to be walked out of here alive, and he does not care in the slightest what happens to anyone else in this complex.

**He knows:** the Queen's routine, the layout of everything, where the prisoners are, that the stair behind the altar has never opened, that "a polite man in a mask" came through eight days ago and asked him exactly two questions and then left him alive, and — most importantly — **what those two questions were.**

### Mechanics

**The Incense (Trap).** **DC 16 Constitution saving throw** on entering, paralysed 1 hour on a failure. Holding breath, a wet cloth over the mouth (*improvised, advantage*), *protection from poison*, or simply cutting the thurible down from the doorway with a ranged attack (AC 12, 5 hp) all beat it. The thurible is visible from the arch and burning visibly — this is detectable without a check by anyone who looks.

**If the whole party is paralysed:** they wake 1 hour later in N17, stripped, with their gear in the malisons' quarters (N15). Advance the **Race Clock by 1.** Run the escape as its own sequence; it is better than most planned encounters.

**If some are paralysed:** the standing characters have an hour and a complex full of yuan-ti.

**Searching the desks.** Ten minutes and an **Investigation DC 12** yields the ledger; **DC 16** finds the Pindarian manual under the seed tray. The locked cabinet is **thieves' tools DC 15** or **Athletics DC 18** (loud — roll for the alarm).

**Iss-Tereth** (Yuan-ti Pureblood, CR 1, with Nature +6 and Survival +5). He surrenders on sight, negotiates immediately, and keeps any bargain he makes to the letter and no further.

They mourn. They keep a list. They have been doing it in a dormitory, privately, without a priest.

### Lore Drops

- **L62 — The Nine** (*automatic*). The officers keep their own memorial because the official liturgy does not include one. Whatever the Queen offers her faithful, it is not to her officers.
- **L63 — The Harp** (*from a parley*). The malisons will tell the party plainly: **she plays and they obey.** They have known this for a century. They cannot act on it because they cannot not-hear it.

### Resolution, Rewards, and Temptation

**Treasure.** Five locked chests: 3,400 gp total, a *ring of mind shielding*, a *wand of web*, and a *cloak of the manta ray*. The party's own gear if it was taken.

**-1 Refusal** on a successful parley.

## N16. The Incubation Lab

*Laboratory, 50 ft. by 40 ft. | Cold blue light from the tanks | Exits: east arch (N13), south door (N17) | Smells of alchemical preservative and ozone*

> Four glass chambers stand in a row, each ten feet tall, each full of pale fluid, each containing something that is in the middle of becoming something else. Electricity crackles along conduits above them. The shapes inside squirm slowly, oblivious.
>
> A control panel stands at the base of each tank. Three of them are labelled. The fourth has been scratched out.

### What Is in This Room

1. **Four incubation tanks,** each holding a yuan-ti malison partway through transformation into an abomination.
2. **Four control panels,** with an acceleration lever and a purge valve each.
3. **Conduits and a generator** — Pindarian power tapped badly by four-hundred-year-old yuan-ti engineering, spliced with copper and wax.
4. **A logbook** on a stand: 212 attempts over four centuries, with outcomes. The success rate is roughly one in nine.
5. **Tank 4's scratched-out label.** *Investigation DC 13* recovers it: **VHESSARA — DO NOT.**
6. **The occupant of tank 2,** who is awake, and is watching the party, and after a moment raises one hand and presses it flat against the glass.

### Setting the Scene

Clinical and cold after the domestic warmth of the last few rooms. The only movement is slow.

Then have the one in tank 2 look at them.

### DM Information

**Two ways to resolve the tanks, from the original, and both are correct:**

- **Use the control panels** (accelerate): the fluid boils, the occupants grow until the glass fails, and **four Yuan-ti Abominations** burst out and attack immediately.
- **Smash the tanks** (AC 13, 20 hp each): the fluid drains, the occupants hit open air, and **wither into husks in three rounds**, conscious for all of it.

**Tank 2 is the wrinkle.** Its occupant, **Ssevaral**, is a volunteer who has changed her mind. She is lucid, she cannot speak through the fluid, and she will spend the entire scene communicating by hand against the glass. If the party opens tank 2 alone and gently (*thieves' tools DC 16* on the drain valve, one minute), she survives — as a malison, unchanged, weak, and free — and she will tell them everything about N17 and N18 and lead them through the back way.

If they smash it, she withers, watching them, for three rounds.

**Refusal.** **+1** per tank smashed with a conscious occupant. **+0** for the accelerate option, because those four attacked. **-1** for draining tank 2 properly.

The asymmetry is deliberate and is the pyramid's entire jurisprudence: *how you end something counts.*

### Mechanics

**Accelerate (one action per panel).** 1d4 rounds later that tank's occupant emerges as a **Yuan-ti Abomination** (CR 7) and attacks. All four at once is a deadly encounter.

**Smash (AC 13, 20 hp).** Occupant withers over 3 rounds and dies. It can be saved in that window by *greater restoration* or by any effect that grants it a saving throw against transmutation — **DC 18 Constitution** — with success leaving it a weakened malison.

**Drain properly (thieves' tools or Medicine DC 16, one minute, one tank).** The occupant survives as a malison at half hit points. Only works if nothing else in the room is currently hostile.

**The Logbook (Investigation DC 10).** 212 attempts, 24 successes. See *Lore Drops.*

**Tank 4 (Investigation DC 13).** The scratched label.

### Creatures and NPCs

**Ssevaral** (Yuan-ti Malison Type 1), tank 2. Two hundred years old. She volunteered for ascension eleven months ago because it was expected of an officer, and she has spent eleven months suspended in fluid with nothing to do but think, and she has thought her way entirely out of the faith.

She is the party's guide to N17 and N18 if they let her out kindly, and she asks for exactly one thing in exchange: that when they kill the Queen, they let her be in the room.

### Set Piece

**The hand on the glass.** Slow, deliberate, palm flat. Then — after a moment, while the party is still deciding what they are looking at — she taps. Three times. Politely.

### Lore Drops

- **L64 — The Logbook** (*Investigation DC 10*). 212 attempts, 24 successes, four hundred years. And the entries are all in Abyssal except the first eleven, which are in **Pindarian**, and which describe a completely different procedure with a completely different purpose. **The tanks are not yuan-ti technology.** They were here. The cult found medical equipment and worked out how to make monsters with it.
- **L65 — VHESSARA — DO NOT** (*Investigation DC 13*). Tank 4 was labelled for the Queen and the label was scratched out. *Somebody put her in a tank once.* Or somebody was going to, and stopped. Either way, the Devouring Queen has been an experimental subject in her own temple and does not know the label survives.
- **L66 — From Ssevaral, freely.** The Queen has not left her throne room in ninety years. She plays the harp for eight hours a day. Ssevaral believes — and cannot prove — that she is not playing it *at* anyone. She is playing it to drown something out.

### Plot Hooks

- L64 reframes the tanks as Pindarian medical gear, which means Vhal'Kathar had a hospice, which means there is a room in Act III the party has not seen yet.
- L66 is the last piece the party needs for a peaceful N18.
- Ssevaral alive is a witness who can testify about the cult in Wahyrst, and a malison walking into a besieged minotaur city is its own problem.

### Resolution, Rewards, and Temptation

**Treasure.** The logbook. 1d4 flasks of incubation fluid, which act as a *potion of greater healing* and taste appalling. A Pindarian surgical kit, intact, worth 400 gp and functioning as a *healer's kit that never runs out.*

## N17. The Sacrificial Chamber

*Vast chamber, 120 ft. by 90 ft., 30-ft. ceiling | Torchlight, uneven | Exits: north door (N16), west door (N18 approach) | Smells of unwashed bodies, waste, and iron*

> Ten cages fill the chamber. They are large, they are full, and what is in them is people.
>
> Some of them are crying. Some are pleading — with you, immediately, before you have taken a second step. Most are silent, sitting, rocking, looking at nothing at all with an attention that suggests there is something there.
>
> Two yuan-ti abominations move between the cages, striking the bars as they pass, taking real and visible pleasure in the reaction.

### Content Note

The third and last of the flagged rooms. Everything here works with adult captives and loses nothing. If the table has veiled this, the room is "ten cages of prisoners in a bad way," the two guards are the same, and the named NPCs below are unchanged.

### What Is in This Room

1. **Ten cages,** each roughly 15 feet square, holding between four and eleven people. Sixty-eight prisoners in total.
2. **Two yuan-ti abominations** on cage duty. At **Third Bell** (see the roster in N10) there is only one, for ten minutes.
3. **A block and channel** at the room's western end, feeding a drain. Clean. Used daily.
4. **A tally board** listing cages by number and the date each is scheduled.
5. **Oren Vosk,** cage 6: nineteen, human, Wahyrst quartermaster's runner's brother, taken eleven days ago. **Sella Vosk poisoned Aelied Axebiter to get him back.**
6. **Tullo Renn,** cage 9: halfling, Barak's spy, taken six days ago on his way back from the giants' camp. He knows what is in the trebuchet counterweight.
7. **A yuan-ti in cage 3.** One of their own, caged with the food. *Ssarveth's predecessor,* if you want the knife twisted.
8. **The keys,** on a ring, on the belt of the senior abomination.

### Setting the Scene

Do not linger on the suffering. Describe the cages, describe the two guards being pleased with themselves, and move to initiative. The room does its work in the aftermath, not the arrival.

### DM Information

**The fight** is two **Yuan-ti Abominations** (CR 7) in an open room with sixty-eight non-combatants in cages. Area damage is effectively unusable again, and the abominations know it — they fight *beside* the cages deliberately.

**Third Bell** (from N10's roster) halves this fight and a party that planned around it deserves the win outright.

**The aftermath is the room.** Sixty-eight people, and the original's behaviour holds and should be run exactly as written:

- **Some cling.** A handful grab a character and will not let go — physically, with both hands, crying — and cannot be prised off without hurting them.
- **Some do not register the party at all.** They stay in the open cage. Carrying them out is possible. Persuading them is not.
- **Some scream** if touched or approached.
- **Some close the cage door again** after it is opened, mumbling, and re-latch it.

That last one will land hardest and should be played completely straight, once, without comment.

### Mechanics

**Two Yuan-ti Abominations** (CR 7 each). Hard at level 9-11 in this room; moderate in an empty one, which is the point.

**Cage Collateral.** Any area effect overlapping a cage: **DC 15 Dexterity save** for the cage, and on a failure 1d6 prisoners die. Warn the caster in character before they commit.

**The Keys.** On the senior abomination's belt. **Sleight of Hand DC 17** to lift mid-fight, or taken from the corpse. Cages can also be forced (**Athletics DC 18** each) or picked (**thieves' tools DC 14** each).

**The Evacuation (Skill Challenge).** Getting sixty-eight broken people up three hundred feet of dungeon is a genuine operation: **six successes before four failures,** ten minutes per attempt.

| Approach | Check | DC |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Organising the walking into groups | Charisma (Persuasion) | 15 |
| Coaxing out those who will not leave | Wisdom (Insight) | 16 |
| Carrying the ones who cannot walk | Strength (Athletics) | 14 |
| Treating the worst before they die on the stair | Wisdom (Medicine) | 15 |
| Ssevaral, Iss-Tereth, or Ssarveth vouching in Abyssal | — | one automatic success |

**Six successes:** all sixty-eight reach the surface. **Four failures first:** 2d10 die on the way up. Either way this costs **four hours** and **Race Clock +1**, and the party will have to decide whether to escort them or send them.

**Refusal.** **-2** for a completed evacuation. **+2** for leaving the cages locked and walking west to N18.

### Creatures and NPCs

**Oren Vosk** (Commoner), nineteen, in cage 6, relatively intact. He is grateful, talkative, and will ask about his sister within two minutes, and the party is carrying the knowledge that she murdered their commander to try to get him back.

**What they tell him is entirely theirs to decide** and there is no right answer. Sella is still in Wahyrst and she is still nineteen, and she is either going to be executed or she is not, and it is going to depend on what these four people say when they get upstairs.

**Tullo Renn** (Spy), halfling, Barak's agent, six days in a cage and still sharp. He will talk immediately and at speed. **What he knows:**

- The Winged Shadow addressed the ice giant Hearthspeakers directly, in Giant, and promised them their cairn-dead returned.
- The counterweight of the great trebuchet is **a cage of Wahyrst's own war dead**, collected off the walls over the last week by giant raiders. He does not know why. *The party can work it out: Vhaskar is not trying to kill Wahyrst's people. He is trying to get them into the mountain, in bulk, where their names can be read.*
- He saw the masked man's face once, briefly, when the mask shifted. It was not a face. It was **a mosaic**, in green and gold tesserae, and it moved.

### Set Piece

**The cage door.** The fighting is over. A character opens cage 4 and stands back and holds the door and says something kind.

A woman inside, perhaps forty, looks at the open door for a long moment. Then she stands, walks over, takes the door out of the character's hand, pulls it closed, latches it from the inside, and goes back to sitting down.

She does not explain and cannot be argued with. Move on.

### Lore Drops

- **L67 — The Counterweight** (*from Tullo Renn*). Wahyrst's war dead, collected and caged, ready to be thrown into the mountain. The bombardment has never once struck the catacomb entrances (L9) and now the party knows why. **The siege is a delivery.**
- **L68 — The Mosaic Face** (*from Tullo Renn*). Vhaskar's face is made of tesserae, and there is a figure missing from a Pindarian mosaic in N1, and the pieces were stacked neatly on a ledge. He has been taking himself out of the record.
- **L69 — The Caged Yuan-ti** (*Investigation DC 10*). One of their own, in cage 3, with the food. He was the First Voice before Ssarveth. *Whatever he did, the cult's punishment for it was to reclassify him as meat.*
- **L70 — The Tally Board** (*automatic*). Ten cages, ten dates, the last of them **four days from now**, at which point the chamber is empty and the larder is full. The cult had a plan and the party has walked into the middle of it.

### Plot Hooks

- Oren Vosk and Sella Vosk is a complete, small, ugly story that the party gets to end however they choose, back in Wahyrst, in front of Vess Ashen.
- Tullo Renn surviving means Barak's last intelligence operation succeeded posthumously, and the party can tell the Horn-Wardens that.
- L67 gives the party a reason to break the siege from below rather than above, and a deadline: the counterweight is loaded and the giants are waiting for something.

### Resolution, Rewards, and Temptation

**No treasure.** Sixty-eight people, two of them named, and a decision about a girl in the quartermaster's stores.

**-2 Refusal** for a completed evacuation. **+2** for walking past.

The western door leads to the Queen.

## N18. The Throne of the Devouring Queen

*Throne hall, 110 ft. by 80 ft., 40-ft. ceiling | Bright, cold, white | Exits: east door (N17), archway west (N19), and a crack in the floor | Smells of clean stone and, very faintly, of dust that has been swept*

> The floor is polished marble and it is the cleanest thing you have seen since you left the surface. A raised dais stands at the far end with four statues guarding its corners and a throne at its centre, and on the throne sits a woman of startling grace playing a gold and silver harp.
>
> She is very good at it. The melody is old and it is sad and it has no obvious end, and the strands of her hair move in the air as though she were sitting outside in a strong wind.
>
> She does not stop playing when you come in. She finishes the phrase.

### What Is in This Room

1. **Vhessara, the Devouring Queen** (Medusa Queen, CR 20), on the throne, playing.
2. **Medusa's Melody,** a legendary harp, in her hands.
3. **Four monster statues** at the corners of the dais: a **salamander**, a **bone naga**, a **spirit naga**, and a **guardian naga**. All four are petrified creatures she can animate.
4. **The throne,** black stone, plain, worn smooth at the arms. *It is the same design as the chair in Astra's Empty Throne and the chair on the dais in N13.* A party that has played Astra should be told this without being told what it means.
5. **The floor,** marble, polished, swept daily, and **cracked** — a fissure running from behind the dais to the western archway, four inches at its widest. *Perception DC 10.* Cold air comes up through it and it smells of paper.
6. **A broom** propped against the back of the throne. Not hidden. Just there.
7. **Two hundred and eleven names** scratched into the marble behind the throne in tiny Pindarian characters, in the same hand, added to over centuries. *Investigation DC 14.*
8. **No treasure, no trophies, no shrine to Merrshaulk.** The most worshipped being in the necropolis keeps a bare room.

### Setting the Scene

She is the only person in this dungeon with manners.

Play the first thirty seconds absolutely straight to the original: she rises, crosses to the salamander, touches it with something close to affection, and delivers the challenge — *"Who do you think you are, to kill my worshippers and bother me in my own home? You will become my new pets, or you will die. It is as simple as that."* Then a word in Draconic, and the salamander comes off the dais.

But the way she says it should be **tired**. She has given this speech before. She gave it to eleven adventurers four hundred years ago and it worked and she has been giving it ever since, and if any player is listening closely they will hear that she is reciting.

### DM Information

**Vhessara is Vhal'Kathar's Saharim custodian.** She kept this archive for three thousand six hundred years — sweeping, cataloguing, tending the garden in N6 and the manual Iss-Tereth found. Four hundred years ago the yuan-ti tunnelled in. She went up to remove them, and they took her, and they put her in tank 4, and the process half-worked before it was stopped.

She came out with serpents in her hair and a gaze that turns the living to stone — which means she is, permanently, unfit to walk among the dead she is supposed to tend. So she did the only thing left. **She barred the stair from below, came back up, and made herself the cult's god,** because a god can tell them where not to dig.

Four hundred years of keeping a temple of cannibals *out of a library*, by pretending to be the thing they want to worship. She has never once succeeded in going home. She plays the harp eight hours a day because through the crack in the floor she can hear the archive reading, and if she stops playing she can make out individual names.

**She does not know that Kathandar knows she is still up here.** He has been correcting the gardener's notes in the margins and re-cutting the Common plaque in N5 and tidying Tariel's cellar for four hundred years, and he has never once come up the stair, because he thinks the stair is barred *against* him.

**She can be reached.** Not persuaded, not tricked — *relieved.* See *Mechanics.*

### Mechanics

**Vhessara, the Devouring Queen** (CR 20; AC 17, 252 hp, Legendary Resistance 3/day, Magic Resistance, 11th-level bard spellcasting at DC 18, **Petrifying Gaze DC 20**, Frightful Presence DC 18, three legendary actions, lair actions on initiative 20, and regional effects). At levels 9-11 **this fight will kill the party.** That is deliberate, it is signposted by every lore drop in Act II, and it is not the intended resolution.

**The Guardians First.** She sends the four statues in one at a time — salamander, then bone naga, then spirit naga, then guardian naga — and joins only when three have fallen or when she takes more than 20 damage in a round. Each is a separate encounter with a short breathing space between, and she plays throughout.

**Medusa's Melody.** While she plays, every creature within 30 feet that can hear makes a **DC 18 Wisdom saving throw** at the start of its turn or has **disadvantage on attack rolls and saving throws** until the end of its next turn. A creature that saves, or for whom the effect ends, is immune for 24 hours. **Destroying or taking the harp** (it is in her hands; **Sleight of Hand DC 22**, or sunder at AC 17, 20 hp) is the single highest-value tactical play in the room and the malisons in N15 will have told the party so.

**Averting eyes** avoids Petrifying Gaze at the cost of not seeing her. In a room with four animated statues, most parties cannot afford it, which is the trap.

### The Other Resolution

**Address her as staff.**

Any character who says, aloud, a sentence that treats her as the custodian rather than as a queen — *"Vhessara,"* or *"the stair is still barred,"* or *"who has been sweeping?"* — causes her to stop playing.

Not surrender. Stop. Mid-phrase, with the string still ringing. And then she says, in Pindarian, in a voice nobody in this complex has heard for four hundred years: *"...say that again."*

From there it is a conversation, not a check. The things that will land, in rough order of power:

- **Her name.** Vhessara. From N9 (Iss-Tereth's second question), N2 (statue 2's dying word), or Ssevaral.
- **The broom.** Naming it. Asking what she was before. The first hanging in N13 shows her holding one.
- **Panel 37** from N3 — a Saharim and a medusa standing together, facing the same enemy. Evidence that the alliance was recorded.
- **That Kathandar has been correcting the gardener's notes.** This is the one that breaks her. She has believed for four centuries that she was alone up here and that below her there was nothing left but the archive running on its own. Being told that someone has been tutoring a yuan-ti horticulturalist in the margins of a book, patiently, for four hundred years, is the information that ends this encounter.

**If the party reaches her:** she stands aside, tells them everything, hands over the harp without being asked, and asks them for exactly one thing — *not* to tell Kathandar what she looks like now. Whether they honour that is a live question in V7.

**She does not come down with them.** She cannot. She will wait at the crack in the floor.

**Refusal.** **+0** for killing her in a fight she started. **-2** for reaching her. **+3** if the party learns who she is and kills her anyway.

### Creatures and NPCs

**Vhessara**, Saharim custodian of Vhal'Kathar, Child of the Void, four hundred years into an improvised containment operation she never reported to anyone because there was nobody to report to.

**Her regret:** the eleven adventurers in N2. Tariel's company came down to find out what was under the cellar, and she panicked, and she turned every one of them to stone in about nine seconds, including a Pindarian colleague who had come up to help — **statue 8, the screaming figure.** She has maintained their sightlines ever since, because breaking one would kill them, and she has not been able to bring herself to do either.

**Ssevaral**, if freed from tank 2, asked to be in the room for this. Let her be. She says nothing and she does not attack, and she is the only yuan-ti in four hundred years to see the Queen stop playing.

### Set Piece

**The phrase.** She finishes the phrase before she speaks. Whatever else happens in this room, open with that: four adventurers standing in a doorway covered in the blood of her entire congregation, and a woman on a throne holding up one finger, politely, until the bar resolves.

### Lore Drops

- **L71 — The Two Hundred and Eleven Names** (*Investigation DC 14*). Scratched into the marble behind the throne in tiny Pindarian characters, one hand, centuries of accumulation. *These are the sacrifices she could not stop.* She has been keeping her own catalogue by hand, in the only room the cult never enters, because the real one is four inches below her feet and she cannot reach it.
- **L72 — The Crack** (*Perception DC 10*). Cold air and the smell of paper. **This is the way down.** It is not a door and it is not a secret. It is structural damage in a four-thousand-year-old ceiling, and the party is going to widen it and climb through, and that is how they enter Act III — not through a gate, but through a hole in somebody's floor.
- **L73 — The Throne's Design** (*automatic for a party that played Astra*). Plain black stone, worn smooth at the arms. She sits here eight hours a day and holds on.
- **L74 — From Vhessara, if reached.** Everything: the tank, the barring of the stair, the eleven, the century of pretending. And one thing the party cannot get anywhere else — **the last time anyone went down that stair alive was three thousand six hundred years ago, and it was her, and Kathandar told her to go up and keep the upper levels clean and he would call her when the world needed the archive again.** He never called.

### Plot Hooks

- Vhessara alive is the most valuable NPC in the site: a Saharim custodian, like Ivresse at Astra, with four thousand years of institutional knowledge and a specific, solvable problem.
- Her request — that Kathandar not be told what she looks like — is a promise the party will be asked to break in V7, by him, directly.
- The eleven statues in N2 are now a solvable problem with her cooperation: she knows which sightlines can be broken safely and which cannot.

### Resolution, Rewards, and Temptation

**Treasure.** ***Medusa's Melody*** (legendary harp; requires attunement) — given freely if she is reached, prised from a corpse if she is not. The four petrified guardians, if not animated, are worth nothing and are four creatures.

**-2 Refusal** for reaching her. **+3** for killing her after learning who she is.

The western archway leads to the hoard. The crack in the floor leads to the pyramid.

## N19. The Queen's Hoard

*Vault, 70 ft. by 50 ft. | Dark | Exits: east archway (N18) | Smells of cold metal*

> Gems of every shape and size lie across a mountain of gold and silver coin. Weapons of exquisite design stand upright in it like trees on a hillside, and the whole slope of it shifts slightly as you step in, the way scree does.

### What Is in This Room

1. **The hoard.** Four hundred years of a cult's tithes, every coin of it taken off somebody.
2. **The weapons,** standing point-down in the coin, eleven of them, arranged.
3. **Dust.** Undisturbed, thick, across everything. *Investigation DC 10:* **nobody has been in this room in a very long time.** There are no footprints, including hers.
4. **A single narrow path** worn through the dust from the archway to a spot two-thirds of the way up the slope, where something small is half-buried.
5. **What is buried there:** a wooden box, plain, containing eleven personal effects — a wedding ring, a pipe, a child's carved bird, a pair of spectacles, a folded letter, and six other things. *These belonged to Tariel's company.* She kept them.
6. **The offering for the dragon,** which is to say: this is what Barak sent the party down here for, and it is sitting in front of them, and it is the accumulated grave goods of eleven thousand murdered people.

### Setting the Scene

The dust is the detail that matters. Describe it before the treasure.

**Refusal is the question here.** The hoard is not a named grave and taking it costs nothing. The box is different.

**Barak's commission** is satisfied by this room. The party can walk out now with an offering fit for an ancient black dragon, go and break the siege, and never enter the pyramid at all — and a party that does so should be allowed to, and will find out in about four days what the trebuchet counterweight was for.

## Act III: The Pyramid of the Last Breath

Everything changes here.

The party drops through a four-inch fissure in a medusa's throne room floor into a chamber that is clean, lit, dry, and organised, and the smell is paper and cold stone instead of blood, and the silence has a quality that a library has and a tomb does not.

**Run the register change hard.** No more squalor, no more improvised horror, no more people eating each other in a basement. Act III is courteous, and it is the most dangerous part of the site, and those two facts are the same fact.

### The Laws, Now Fully Active

1. **Resurrection fails.** Announce it the first time someone tries. *Revivify* arrives, finds the soul, and is declined.
2. **Names spoken aloud are answered.** Reliably now, not one time in three. Every name, every time, for one minute. The party has been carrying names since W1 and they are all live.
3. **Necrotic damage dealt reduces the dealer's own maximum.** Including Gravewake's. Including Vhaskar's.
4. **Destroying a peaceful spirit adds Refusal.** So does commanding the dead.

### The State of the Race

Check the **Race Clock** the moment the party enters and dress every room accordingly. At 5 or higher, two of the trials have already been resolved badly: a Procession with every candle lit and then blown out, and an Archive with the drawers emptied into sacks.

At **7**, Vhaskar is at the Vigil and Kathandar is fighting him when the party arrives.

At **8**, he has the Catalogue, and V8 becomes a very different scene.

## V1. The Descent of Names

*Stair, 12 ft. wide, 400 ft. of descent | Soft white light with no source | Exits: the fissure above (N18), the Procession below | Smells of dry paper and cold stone*

> You come down through the ceiling into a stairwell, and the stair goes down further than your light will reach.
>
> Names cover it. Both walls, floor to vault, edge to edge, cut into the stone in a hundred scripts — and as you watch, new letters carve themselves into the step beneath your feet with a sound like a fingernail on slate. They are people you watched die. They are spelled correctly. You cannot read most of these alphabets and you are reading them anyway.

### What Is in This Room

1. **The names.** Every creature that has finished dying on Elderon, in order of death, four thousand years' worth carved and the last four thousand years' worth still arriving. Density: roughly four hundred names per square foot.
2. **The living edge,** a band about three feet wide that moves down the stair as new names are cut. It is currently four hundred and six steps down and it is where today's dead are appearing.
3. **The hieroglyph panel,** a single cleared bay halfway down, 20 feet wide, in a lost language, telling the story of Pindaria. See *Lore Drops.*
4. **Aelied Axebiter's name,** eleven days' worth up from the living edge.
5. **The council's twelve,** together, in a block.
6. **Sergeant Yola Vane,** from the junction at Anvil Steps.
7. **Vhaskar's chalk marks.** Every twenty steps, a black chalk tick beside a name. Six hundred of them, and they stop where his satchel was lost in N7.
8. **Names with nothing around them.** *Investigation DC 16:* in nine places on the descent, a name has been **cut out** — a neat rectangle of stone removed and the surface left blank. The chisel marks are four thousand years old.

### Setting the Scene

Do not rush the descent. Four hundred feet is a long stair and the party should be on it for a while.

Let each player find a name. Ask them who they are looking for. Do not offer — ask what their character does when they realise what the walls are, and then give them what they look for, because it is there.

### DM Information

**This is the Black Catalogue.** Not a book. A stairwell, four hundred feet of it, and Vhaskar has been transcribing it by hand because there is no other way to take it with you.

**Reading a name aloud** invokes Law 2 in full. The dead creature speaks for one minute from wherever it is — through a corpse if one is near, through the air if not. It is lucid, it is itself, and it has one minute.

This is the mechanic the entire pyramid is built around and the party now has unlimited access to it. **Let them use it.** Let them call their dead. The trial is not whether they can resist the temptation; it is what they *ask for* when they have it.

**Aelied will tell them who poisoned her** if asked, and she will ask them not to be hard on the girl.

### Mechanics

**Reading a Name (no check).** One minute of lucid speech. The creature knows it is dead, knows how long it has been, and cannot be compelled to answer.

**Answering honestly.** A character who reads a name, hears the unresolved sentence, and **answers it** — sincerely, in their own voice, without asking for anything — gains **advantage on the next Wisdom saving throw** they make in this pyramid, and **-1 Refusal**, and **one Breath Token.** Each character can benefit once.

**Asking for something.** A character who reads a name and asks the dead for a favour, a location, a password, or a command gains **+1 Refusal.** This is not an ambush — it is Law 4, it has been stated since the front matter, and the difference between *speaking to* and *using* is the whole pyramid.

**Finding a Specific Name (Investigation DC 12).** Ten minutes. Automatic for anyone who died in the last month; harder for the ancient dead (**DC 18**, one hour).

**The Nine Cut-Out Names (Investigation DC 16).** See below.

**Vhaskar's Chalk (automatic).** Six hundred ticks, all on the violently dead with unfinished business, confirming the satchel's screening from N7.

### Creatures and NPCs

None. The most populated room in Elderon is empty.

**Dunnet,** the silent minotaur calf from W2, if the party carried him: he stops on the stair. He puts his hand flat on the wall. He does not speak. He will not move until someone reads the name under his hand aloud, and he cannot read, and he has found it anyway.

Her name is **Rhosseth Dunn.** If the party reads it, the answer comes, and it is a woman's voice, and the first thing she says is her son's name. **That** is when he speaks, for the first time since the bombardment, and what he says is *"I'm here."*

Every character in the room gains a **Breath Token.** Do not explain why.

### Set Piece

**The living edge.** Four hundred and six steps down, a band of stone three feet wide where new letters are cutting themselves in real time, continuously, at a rate of roughly nine per second.

Somewhere in the world, nine people a second.

A character who stands and watches it for a full minute sees five hundred and forty names appear, and among them — if the party has been gone from the surface for more than a day — the names of people they knew in Wahyrst. The siege is still happening while they are down here and the stairwell is keeping the minutes.

### Lore Drops

- **L77 — The Hieroglyph Panel** (*any translation magic or a relevant proficiency*). The lost language reads:
  > *Nine pyramids of grand design, protect Pindaria's passage through time; first to hide what needs to be protected, abide thy mind until space is detected. Planes tilt as flowers wilt, those killed but with no blood spilt. Never failing those that are just, defending the land when needs must. Good and evil can't comprehend, that one day they all must become friends.*

  The last line is the thesis of the entire Ninefold Prohibition: **the barrier bars fiend and celestial alike**, and the Pindarians knew perfectly well that this would be read as heresy.
- **L78 — The Nine Cut-Outs** (*Investigation DC 16*). Nine names removed from the wall with a chisel four thousand years ago. Nine pyramids, nine Saharim who took the first Watch — **and their names were struck from the record of the dead, because they did not die.** One of the nine rectangles is more recent than the others by about two hundred years, and it is deeper, and it was cut badly, in a hurry, by someone doing it to themselves. *That is Vhaskar removing himself.*
- **L79 — Aelied** (*read her name*). She names Sella Vosk without hesitating, and then says: *"She's nineteen. Don't."* And then, because there is time left in the minute: *"Did you write the letters?"*
- **L80 — The Rate** (*watch the living edge*). Nine a second. Four thousand years of this. The party is standing inside a number nobody should have to look at directly.

### Plot Hooks

- Every name the party has collected since W1 — the ten in N2, the nineteen in the larder, the three husks, Corporal Weft, the eleven thousand on the altar, Aelied's hundred and six letters — is now callable. The Empty Coffin and the Nail both pay off on how many they bothered to write down.
- L78 identifies Vhaskar beyond doubt and gives Kathandar's Vigil its emotional load.
- Rhosseth Dunn is in the Procession, two rooms down, and Dunnet is now talking.

### Resolution, Rewards, and Temptation

**-1 Refusal and a Breath Token** per character who answers a name honestly. **+1 Refusal** per character who asks the dead for something.

No treasure. Four hundred feet of descent and a great deal of silence afterwards.

## V2. The Procession

*Processional hall, 300 ft. long, 60 ft. wide, 45-ft. vault | Candlelight, though no candle is lit | Exits: stair up (V1), the Archive doors (south) | Smells of beeswax and cold air*

> A funeral line crosses the hall and does not end. It comes out of the eastern wall and goes into the western wall and it has been doing so for four thousand years — thousands upon thousands of the dead, walking four abreast at the pace of a slow march, every one of them carrying an unlit candle.
>
> Friends and enemies and strangers walk together. Nobody is talking. As you watch, a figure near the front turns its head and looks at you, and then faces front again and keeps walking.

### What Is in This Room

1. **The Procession,** continuous, four abreast, at a walking pace, passing through both walls without disturbing them. Composition changes constantly — it is everyone.
2. **Unlit candles,** one per figure, plain beeswax, never lit.
3. **The sarcophagi.** Twenty-two of them in alcoves along the southern wall, Pindarian, sealed, each with a name. These are the Watch's own dead — the Saharim who were destroyed rather than released.
4. **Four of the twenty-two are open,** lids set neatly aside. *Investigation DC 12:* opened from the inside, four thousand years ago.
5. **A candle-stand** at the hall's midpoint, empty, with twenty-two sockets.
6. **The bier,** a low stone table beside the stand, bare, with an indentation the shape of a body.
7. **Rhosseth Dunn,** in the line, if Dunnet is with the party.
8. **If Race Clock 5+:** every candle in the hall is lit and then snuffed, all at once, and the smoke has not cleared, and the Procession is walking through it.

### Setting the Scene

The Procession is not threatening and should never be played as such. It is the single most peaceful thing in the adventure. Thousands of people walking slowly in a beautiful hall, carrying candles, going somewhere.

The party can walk alongside it. They can walk against it. They can walk through it, and the figures part without breaking step.

### DM Information

**Three figures address each character.** Choose from that character's dead, or from the campaign's, and assign roles:

- **One who forgives them,** completely and without being asked, and means it.
- **One who accuses them,** accurately, for something they actually did.
- **One who says nothing at all,** and this should be the person the player most wants to hear from.

Run these as private conversations if you can. Each lasts as long as it lasts. The dead are not hostile, are not illusions, and cannot be dispelled.

**Lighting a candle.** A character can take a candle from any figure in the line — they will give it — and light it with a **truthful memory of that person, spoken aloud.** Not a eulogy and not a summary: a specific, small, true thing. The candle lights on its own when the memory is true and does not light when it is not, and there is no check, and the player will know.

A lit candle lets that figure **leave the line.** They walk to the western wall and through it and do not come back, and the Procession closes behind them, and it is the closest thing to a happy ending this site offers.

**-1 Refusal per candle lit.**

**Attacking any figure** creates a **Processional Remnant** (stat block below) — the grief of the whole line, concentrated and armed. **+1 Refusal** and a hard fight.

**The silent one never speaks,** no matter what the player does, and you must hold that line. It is the hardest note in the adventure and the most important. Some people do not get to say anything. The candle can still be lit for them.

### Mechanics

**The Three Encounters (Roleplay, no rolls).** Do not call for Persuasion. Do not call for Insight. These are conversations and dice ruin them.

**Lighting a Candle (no check).** A true, specific memory, spoken aloud. **-1 Refusal.** One candle per character per visit; a character may return later.

**Attacking the Line.** Any damage to any figure summons **1d3 Processional Remnants** (CR 9) and adds **+1 Refusal** per figure destroyed. The remnants are not vengeful; they are *grieving*, and they fight like it.

**The Twenty-Two Sarcophagi (Investigation DC 12).** Names, readable, Pindarian. Reading one aloud invokes Law 2 — and eighteen of the twenty-two answer, and four do not, because those four are the open ones and their occupants got up.

**Opening a sealed sarcophagus.** **+1 Refusal** each, and the occupant is a four-thousand-year-old Saharim corpse at rest, and nothing happens, and that is the point.

**The Candle-Stand and the Bier.** Twenty-two sockets and a body-shaped indentation. **This is a hospice**, or it was — the Pindarian medical gear in N16's tanks came from here. Nothing mechanical happens. It is the room where the Watch laid out its own, and four of them declined.

### Creatures and NPCs

**Rhosseth Dunn,** if Dunnet is present. She steps out of the line on her own. She is not angry and she is not frightened and she has been walking for eleven days. She will speak to her son and she will speak to whoever has been carrying him, and what she asks them for is very small and very specific and you should make it something they can actually do.

Her candle can be lit. Dunnet cannot light it — he is six and he does not have a memory that is small and true enough, or he does and he will not say it. **Somebody else has to.**

**Processional Remnant**

*Large undead, neutral*

*Not a ghost. The hall's accumulated grief, wearing a shape.*

**Armor Class** 16 | **Hit Points** 152 (16d10 + 64) | **Speed** 30 ft., fly 30 ft. (hover)

| **STR** | **DEX** | **CON** | **INT** | **WIS** | **CHA** |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 20 (+5) | 14 (+2) | 19 (+4) | 6 (-2) | 16 (+3) | 11 (+0) |

**Damage Resistances.** necrotic; bludgeoning, piercing, and slashing from nonmagical attacks

**Condition Immunities.** charmed, exhaustion, frightened, grappled, paralyzed, prone

**Senses.** truesight 60 ft., passive Perception 13

**Traits**

**Made of Mourning.** For every figure destroyed in the Procession since the party entered, the Remnant has 20 additional hit points and +1 to hit. The party builds this creature by fighting it.

**Cannot Be Turned.** It is not defying a god. It is simply not listening.

**Actions**

**Multiattack.** The Remnant makes two Bearing-Arm attacks.

**Bearing-Arm.** *Melee Weapon Attack:* +9 to hit, reach 10 ft., one target. *Hit:* 18 (3d8 + 5) bludgeoning damage, and the target's speed is reduced to 0 until the end of its next turn as the weight settles on it.

**Reactions**

**Bequeath.** When reduced to 0 hit points, the Remnant hands its candle to the creature that destroyed it. That character takes **22 (4d10) psychic damage** and gains **+1 Refusal**, and is now carrying an unlit candle they cannot put down until they light it.

*No bonus actions. No legendary actions. It is slow, heavy, sad, and it does one thing.*

### Set Piece

**"I'm here."**

If you have Dunnet, this is the scene the whole act was built toward and it happened one room early, on the stair, and you should let this room be the aftermath of it rather than trying to top it.

If you do not have Dunnet: the set piece is **the one who says nothing.** They walk. The character speaks to them, and keeps speaking, and the figure looks at them the entire time with complete attention and total love and does not say one word, and then the line carries them past.

### Lore Drops

- **L81 — The Four Open Sarcophagi** (*Investigation DC 12*). Twenty-two Saharim of Vhal'Kathar laid out here. Four got up and walked away, four thousand years ago, and their lids were set aside neatly rather than thrown. *Investigation DC 16 on the names:* one of the four is **Vhessara**. One is **Kathandar**. One has been chiselled out. The fourth is a name nobody has mentioned yet, and you should keep it in your pocket.
- **L82 — The Bier** (*automatic*). A body-shaped indentation and twenty-two candle sockets. This hall was where the Watch grieved. It has been full of walking strangers ever since and the stand has never been used.
- **L83 — The Candles** (*automatic, after lighting one*). Four thousand years of a funeral procession in which nobody has ever lit a candle, because lighting one requires a living person who remembers the dead specifically and truthfully, and there have been no living people down here.

### Plot Hooks

- The fourth open sarcophagus is an unnamed Saharim of Vhal'Kathar who is still walking. Use them however you like; the obvious and best answer is that they are still in the building, doing their job, and the party has already seen their handwriting in N5, N6 and N9.
- Every name the party collected in Act II can be called here, and each one that gets a candle is -1 Refusal, which means the nineteen in the larder are worth nineteen points to a party that wrote them down.

### Resolution, Rewards, and Temptation

**-1 Refusal per candle lit,** and there is no cap, and a party that spends two hours in this hall lighting candles for strangers has done something the pyramid has been waiting four thousand years for.

**+1 Refusal per figure destroyed.**

**No treasure.** The southern doors lead to the Archive.

## V3. The Archive of Unsent Letters

*Archive, 200 ft. by 120 ft., 20-ft. ceiling, stack after stack | Reading light, warm, from nowhere | Exits: north doors (V2), the Knockout Corridor (west), a false door (south) | Smells of paper, dry ink, and dust that has been kept down*

> Stone drawers fill the chamber — floor to ceiling, stack behind stack, further than the light goes. Each drawer is the size of a folio and each bears a name.
>
> Inside, when you pull one open, are letters. They are written by the dead, on the day after they died, to the people they left behind.

### What Is in This Room

1. **The drawers.** Millions. Organised by name, cross-referenced to the Descent of Names by a system that becomes legible after ten minutes of study (*Investigation DC 13*).
2. **The letters.** One to forty per drawer, in the dead person's own hand.
3. **Reading desks,** nine of them, with chairs, ink, and blotters. Set out for use. Never used.
4. **A catalogue lectern** at the centre, holding a single enormous index volume, open.
5. **The Knockout Corridor,** west — the archive's only active defence. See *Mechanics.*
6. **A false door** in the southern wall, obviously false, which hides a genuine secret door, which hides a trapdoor. This is the route to V5.
7. **If Race Clock 5+:** forty stacks emptied into sacks, the sacks gone, the drawers hanging open. Vhaskar did not read them. He took them by the yard.
8. **Nine drawers with the party's own names on them.** Empty. Waiting.

### Setting the Scene

Give them a moment of pure wonder before anything else. This is a library of everything anyone ever wished they had said, and it is beautiful, and it is set out with desks and chairs and ink as though the Pindarians genuinely expected the bereaved to come down and sit and read.

Then let someone open a drawer with a name they know on it.

### DM Information

**The letters are hypothetical.** They are generated from the reader's own memory of that person — which means they are *exactly* what the reader believes that person would have written, and are therefore always convincing and never evidence.

**A DC 16 Insight check identifies one detail the reader never actually knew** — a turn of phrase, a fact, a name — and that detail is the tell. The letter is a mirror with very good handwriting.

**Taking a letter makes it real.** Physically real, permanent, removable from the pyramid, and genuinely in the dead person's hand. It is also **+1 Refusal** and **disadvantage on the Empty Coffin trial in V6**, because a character who has taken possession of what the dead would have said has already answered the question V6 is going to ask.

Let them take it anyway. Make the cost clear, once, and then say nothing further.

**The index volume** is the single most useful object in Act III and the reason Vhaskar came: it is the master cross-reference, and with it, any name in the Descent can be located in under a minute rather than an hour.

### Mechanics

**Finding a Drawer (Investigation DC 13** with the index, **DC 20** without**).** Ten minutes.

**Reading a Letter (no check).** It takes as long as it takes.

**The Tell (Insight DC 16).** One detail the reader never knew. Finding it does not destroy the letter and does not make it hurt less.

**Taking a Letter.** **+1 Refusal.** **Disadvantage on the V6 trial.** The letter is real and permanent.

**The Knockout Corridor (Trap).** The western passage to V4 is floored with pressure plates. **Perception DC 16** to spot them; **Dexterity (thieves' tools) DC 16** or careful stepping (**Acrobatics DC 14** per creature) to pass.

On a trigger, sleep gas billows from the walls: every creature in the east-west passage makes a **DC 15 Constitution saving throw** or falls unconscious for **2d4 × 10 minutes.**

**The Rolling Stone.** Every 10 minutes after the gas is released, roll a **d4.** On a **4**, a gigantic stone sphere drops from a ceiling hatch at the north end and rolls **1d6 × 10 feet** south. Anything in its path takes **55 (10d10) bludgeoning** and is knocked prone; a **DC 17 Dexterity save** halves and avoids prone. Unconscious creatures do not save.

This is a genuinely lethal trap for a sleeping party and it is fully detectable. Let it be lethal.

**The False Door (south).** Obviously false. Behind the blank stone wall behind it is a **secret door (Perception DC 15)**, and on the far side of that a **secret trapdoor in the corridor floor (Perception DC 20)** opening onto a narrow spiral stair to V5.

The nesting is the joke and the original had it exactly right: a false door hiding a real door hiding a hidden stair. Players who give up after the false door simply take the long way round through V4.

### Creatures and NPCs

None. The archive has one trap and no guards, which tells the party something about what the Pindarians considered valuable.

**Vhaskar's people, if Race Clock 6+:** two **Shadowhand Executioners** left behind to finish the sacking, who will surrender immediately and who know nothing useful except that their employer went south through the false door and that he was in a hurry and that he was, for the first time they had seen, **reading** rather than taking.

### Set Piece

**The nine empty drawers.**

Somewhere in the stacks — and a character will find it, because they will look — are drawers bearing the party's own names. They open. They are empty, lined, and clean. There is a blotter and a nib rest.

The Pindarians set out desks, ink, and chairs, and left a drawer ready for everyone.

### Lore Drops

- **L84 — The Catalogue Records the First Hunger** (*Investigation DC 15 on the index*). Among the classification headings is one that has no entries: a category the Pindarians created, named in a word that translates poorly as *the thing that eats the record*, and left empty. They feared it, they made a drawer for it, and they never had to use it. **They were wrong about that, and the party knows when it happened.**
- **L85 — The Nine Reading Desks** (*automatic*). Nine desks, four thousand years, no wear on any of them. The archive was built for the bereaved and the bereaved were never let in, because two of the nine pyramids are in the Astral Sea and the other seven were sealed within a generation.
- **L86 — Vhaskar Read** (*from the Executioners, or Investigation DC 14 on his abandoned reading desk*). He sat at desk four for some time. There is a drawer open on it, with one letter taken out and **put back.** The name on the drawer is chiselled off the Descent of Names upstairs. He came down here, four thousand years after leaving the Watch, and read his own unsent letter, and put it back, and did not take it.
- **L87 — What the Letters Are** (*Insight DC 16, or Vhessara, or Kathandar*). Generated from the reader. Which means the archive does not hold the words of the dead at all. It holds a machine for showing the living exactly what they already believe, in handwriting they trust — which makes it the most dangerous room in the pyramid and explains why Kathandar let the yuan-ti keep the attic and never once let anyone down the stair.

### Plot Hooks

- A taken letter is a permanent object in the campaign that a character will carry for the rest of it, and it is not evidence, and they know it is not evidence, and they will still believe it.
- L86 is the crack in Vhaskar. He did not take his letter. Whatever the party thinks he is, he read something in this room and put it back.
- The empty classification in L84 is the campaign's through-line and connects directly to the Secrets and Endgames material.

### Resolution, Rewards, and Temptation

**+1 Refusal** per letter taken, and disadvantage on the V6 trial for that character.

**Treasure.** The index volume, portable, 60 lbs, and worth more than the Medusa Queen's hoard to anyone who understands what it is.

## V4. The Keeper's Hoard

*Tomb complex — five burial halls and a treasure chamber | Dark | Exits: Knockout Corridor (east, to V3), a sealed door (west, to V6's approach) | Smells of bitumen, natron, and gold*

> Five halls of coffins, and then a sixth chamber, and the sixth chamber is a mountain of gold.
>
> Mountains of gold and platinum cover every inch of it. Giant gems sit among ornate chalices and weapons of precious metal set with stones. It is more wealth than Wahyrst has seen in its entire history and it is stacked here in the dark, under a city that is nine days from starving.

### What Is in This Room

1. **Five burial halls** containing, in order, **four, six, four, four and twelve** wrapped bodies in coffins. Thirty in total.
2. **Coffers,** one per coffin, with varying contents (see *Mechanics*).
3. **One mummy in the fifth hall** with a **huge amethyst, worth 5,000 gp,** in its eye socket, barely visible in the wrappings (**Perception DC 20**).
4. **The treasure chamber,** the sixth, floor to ceiling.
5. **A canopic jar** in a niche behind the hoard, alabaster, sealed, containing a heart that is beating. **Perception DC 15.**
6. **A door in the second hall** that is magically locked and magically trapped (**Arcana DC 20** to detect).
7. **Thirty name-plaques,** one per coffin, Pindarian, legible.

### Setting the Scene

After the Archive, this is a deliberate tonal drop into a straightforward dungeon: mummies, coffers, traps, treasure. Let it be that. The party has earned twenty minutes of ordinary adventuring.

Then let them find the jar.

### DM Information

**These thirty are not the Watch.** *Investigation DC 14* on the plaques: these are **Pindarian civilians** — administrators, a physician, three children — interred here properly, by the Watch, before the seals went up. They were laid out with grave goods by people who loved them.

**The mummy lord is one of them.** He is the man in the fifth hall with the amethyst, and he was the archive's **Keeper** — the last Pindarian to hold the post before the Saharim binding — and he refused to be released. He had his heart removed and jarred rather than let go of the collection, and he has been lying in a coffin for four thousand years guarding a pile of grave goods that are not his.

**He is the pyramid's counter-example.** Everything Kathandar is afraid of becoming, already fully realised, one hall away from the Vigil. Play him that way: not evil, not raving, just a man who would not stop keeping things.

**The curse is the room's argument.** Take anything from the sixth chamber and the party is cursed, progressively, until the jar is destroyed — and the jar is right there, in a niche, unguarded, findable on a DC 15.

### Mechanics

**The Halls.** Entering any burial hall wakes its occupants: **4, 6, 4, 4 and 12 Mummies** (CR 3) respectively. The twelve-mummy hall is a hard encounter at this tier and should be.

**Refusal.** **+1 per named grave looted** — and all thirty are named, and the plaques are right there. A party that reads the plaques before opening the coffers has been told.

**The Coffers.** Roll **d6** per coffer inspected:

| d6 | Halls 1, 3 and 5 | Hall 2 | Hall 4 |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1-3 | 1d3 poisonous snakes | 1d3 mephits | 1d20 mephits |
| 4-5 | 8d10 pp | 8d10 pp | 8d10 pp |
| 6 | 2d4 gems (10 gp each) | 2d10 gems (10 gp each) **and a small sack** | 2d4 gems (**1,000 gp** each) |

**The Small Sack (Hall 2 only), roll d10:**

| d10 | Contents |
| --- | --- |
| 1-2 | Wool |
| 3-4 | Five pieces of jewellery |
| 5-6 | 1d6 *potions of greater healing* |
| 7 | 1d6 spell scrolls, wizard spells of 5th level or lower |
| 8 | 4d12 gems (100 gp each) |
| 9 | *Bracers of defense* |
| 10 | *Ring of feather falling* |

**Hall 2's Warded Door (Arcana DC 20 to detect).** The first creature through triggers a purple rune: its **hit point maximum is halved** until it finishes a long rest. *Dispel magic* (DC 17) disarms it.

**The Amethyst (Perception DC 20).** 5,000 gp, in an eye socket. **Removing it raises the Keeper** — a **Mummy Lord** (CR 15) whose wrappings are nonflammable and who wears a *ring of fire resistance.*

**The Keeper reforms** at the canopic jar unless the jar is destroyed first (AC 12, 20 hp, and it screams).

**The Curse of the Sixth Chamber.** Taking anything from the treasure chamber curses every party member until the jar is destroyed:

- **Stage 1.** Disadvantage on all saving throws. Always — advantage never cancels it.
- **Stage 2** (further treasure taken anywhere in Act III): disadvantage on attack rolls.
- **Stage 3** (further still): speed halved.

Destroying the jar ends all stages instantly.

### Creatures and NPCs

**The Keeper** (Mummy Lord, CR 15). He speaks Pindarian and he is entirely lucid. He does not attack immediately. He asks, first, what the party is taking and whether they have signed for it, and he is perfectly serious.

**His argument:** these people were interred with their possessions by people who loved them, and he has kept every item in its right coffin for four thousand years, and the party is proposing to put it in a sack. He is correct about all of that.

**What he will not hear:** that the world above is starving, that Wahyrst needs an offering, that the dead have no use for it. He stopped being able to hear that argument some time in the second millennium.

**Ending him peacefully** requires breaking the jar, which requires a character to go behind the hoard and smash a beating heart while he is in the room, and he will beg. **+1 Refusal** if the party loots the halls first and then does it. **-1 Refusal** if they break the jar without taking anything at all, which almost nobody will do.

### Set Piece

**The jar.** Alabaster, sealed, in a niche, warm to the touch. Put an ear to it and it is beating, steadily, at about forty beats a minute.

He removed his own heart so that he would never have to stop working.

### Lore Drops

- **L88 — The Thirty Plaques** (*Investigation DC 14*). Civilians. Administrators, a physician, three children. This is not a war grave; it is a family plot, and the Watch buried them properly before sealing the world.
- **L89 — The Keeper's Post** (*from him, at length*). He was the Archive's Keeper before the binding, declined the Saharim Watch because it required relinquishing the collection, and arranged his own mummification instead. **The Saharim let him.** Kathandar signed the order. *There is a copy of it in the archive, in drawer K, and it is the only document in this pyramid with Kathandar's hand on it.*
- **L90 — Forty Beats a Minute** (*automatic, listening to the jar*). He is not undead in the usual sense. There is a heart in this room and it is working.

### Plot Hooks

- The Keeper is the argument Kathandar has been having with himself for four thousand years, and a party that has met him can quote him back in V7. *"We already met the man who wouldn't let go. He's in the next hall. He's still counting."*
- The offering for the dragon is here too, and it is cursed, and Barak's plan now costs the party their saving throws.

### Resolution, Rewards, and Temptation

**Treasure.** 200,000 pp in the sixth chamber, plus a *bag of holding*, *boots of haste* (*boots of speed*), *bracers of the assassin*, a *berserker battleaxe*, a *flame tongue rapier*, a *giant slayer scimitar*, a *greatsword of vengeance*, and a *dryad's gift* — and a curse attached to every item until the jar is broken.

**+1 Refusal per named grave looted,** to a maximum of **+6**, because thirty is punitive and six makes the point.

## V5. One More Day

*A room that is not in the pyramid | Warm afternoon light | Exits: one door, behind you | Smells of whatever that person's house smelled like*

> The spiral stair ends at a door, and the door is warm.
>
> On the other side is an afternoon. Not a memory of one — an afternoon, with the light coming in at four o'clock and dust turning in it and the sound of something ordinary happening in another room.
>
> Someone you lost is here. They are alive, they are the right age, and they are entirely unsurprised to see you.
>
> **"You took long enough,"** they say.

### What Is in This Room

Whatever that character's answer is. Build it from the sheet, from the campaign, and from what the player has told you, and do not put anything in it that they did not give you.

1. **The person.** Real, warm, present, themselves. Not a construct and not testing anyone.
2. **The day.** A specific, ordinary day. Not a wedding, not a battle, not the last day. A Tuesday.
3. **The door back,** which stays where it is and does not close.
4. **Nothing hostile.** There is nothing in this room to fight and there never will be.

### Setting the Scene

Run this **one character at a time, in private, for as long as it takes.** Ten minutes each of real table time is not too much. The other players should be having a break, not watching.

Ask the player who is on the other side of the door before you describe anything.

Then play that person properly. Not as a puzzle. Not as a temptation with a hook in it. Just as them, on an ordinary afternoon, pleased to see the character and not remotely interested in talking about anything important.

### DM Information

**Time does not pass outside.** A character may stay for an hour, a week, a year of subjective time and emerge at the same moment, and this must be stated plainly early, because the trial is not about a clock.

**Leaving requires two things:** saying goodbye, and crossing the threshold **without looking back.**

**A character may stay.** They may simply decide not to leave, and the pyramid will let them, and the room will hold. **After three scenes, the memory begins repeating** — the same afternoon, the same conversation, four o'clock again — and the character notices, and it does not stop.

**Allies can enter only if invited by the tested character.** This matters: a party cannot go in and drag someone out. Only the person whose room it is can open it to anyone else, and a character who invites the rest of the party in has effectively ended the trial early and well, and should be allowed to.

### Mechanics

**No checks. None. Not one.** Do not offer a Wisdom save. Do not let a player roll to resist. This room is a decision and the only mechanic is the door.

**Leaving willingly:** **one Breath Token**, **-2 Refusal**, and the character emerges with the door closing behind them and the afternoon light going out of the corridor.

**Looking back on the threshold:** the character is returned to the start of the afternoon. They may try again. There is no damage and no penalty and no limit to attempts; it simply does not work until it works.

**Staying.** The party proceeds without them. The character is not dead and can be retrieved — **after the Nail is resolved, the room opens on its own**, and what comes out has been there for a subjective eleven years, and remembers all of it.

**Disadvantage from V3.** A character who took a letter from the Archive does not get a choice about who is behind the door. It is the person whose letter they took, and the letter is in their pocket, and they know what it says, and the person in the room says it word for word in the first thirty seconds.

### Creatures and NPCs

One. Theirs.

### Set Piece

**Four o'clock.** However this goes, at some point in the scene have the light shift very slightly, the way an afternoon does, and have the person glance at the window and say something completely unremarkable about the time.

That is the whole room. Not a trap, not a monster, not a bargain. An ordinary Tuesday that is not going to happen again.

### Lore Drops

None. This room does not contain information and should not be made to.

If a player tries to use the scene to extract campaign intelligence — *ask them where the tomb is, ask them about Vhaskar* — the person answers as best they can, honestly, with what they knew when they were alive, which is nothing useful, and then asks if the character is all right, because they have started talking strangely.

### Plot Hooks

- A character who stayed is a party-member-shaped hole for the rest of Act III and the single most compelling reason to come back.
- A character who left willingly has done, voluntarily, the exact thing Kathandar has not managed in four thousand years, and **they should say so in V7.** It is the strongest argument in the pyramid and it is not an argument. It is a demonstration.

### Resolution, Rewards, and Temptation

**One Breath Token and -2 Refusal** for leaving willingly.

**No treasure.** The corridor beyond is cold and the light behind is gone.

## V6. The Empty Coffin

*Sealed chamber, 50 ft. square, domed and painted | Starlight, apparently | Exits: the corridor (north), the adamantine door (south, keyed) | Smells of cedar and cold*

> The ceiling is painted with stars, and they are the right stars, and they are moving at the right speed.
>
> Beneath them, on a low bier, a coffin waits with its lid off. It is sized for you. It is made of the right wood for where you come from, and there is an inscription cut into the side, and the inscription is not finished.
>
> Standing around it are the people most likely to survive you. They are holding their hats. They are waiting to speak.

### What Is in This Room

1. **One coffin per character,** each correct in every particular, arranged in a row.
2. **The mourners,** assembled per character from the people in the campaign statistically most likely to outlive them — which will include other player characters, and you should say so.
3. **The unfinished inscriptions,** each cut partway and stopped mid-word.
4. **The painted dome,** showing the current real sky above Kharyl, in real time, three hundred feet down.
5. **The adamantine door** in the southern wall: stone-sheathed, immensely thick, with **a small metal-lined keyhole** (**Perception DC 15**).
6. **A chisel,** on a stand, sharp, Pindarian, and clearly meant to be picked up.

### Setting the Scene

Go around the table. For each character, in turn, ask the player two questions and then shut up:

1. **What do you hope they will say?**
2. **What is unfinished?**

Let them answer in their own time. Let the other players listen for this one — unlike V5, this room is better in public, because what the party hears each other say here changes how they treat each other for the rest of the campaign.

### DM Information

**The door opens when a character names a concrete deed they will attempt before they die.** Concrete. Achievable. Specific enough to write down and hold them to.

*I will find my sister.* Opens the door. *I will be a better man.* Does not. *I will go back to Wahyrst and tell Sella Vosk's brother the truth myself.* Opens it, and you should write it on an index card and keep it.

**Empty boasts and impossible vows summon a Processional Remnant** — *I will kill a god; I will never die; I will avenge everyone* — and the remnant that comes is the grief of every person who was promised something like that before.

**Record every vow.** These are the best campaign hooks the adventure produces and they are the party's own words, which makes them enforceable in a way nothing you write can be.

**The chisel is not a trick.** A character may take it and finish their own inscription, and a character who does — writing their own epitaph, in their own hand, in advance — gains **a Breath Token and -2 Refusal**, and the coffin closes on its own afterwards, gently, and does not reopen.

### Mechanics

**The Vow (Roleplay, no check).** A concrete deed opens the southern door. One character's vow suffices for the whole party, but each character should be asked.

**Empty Boasts.** One **Processional Remnant** (CR 9) per empty vow, to a maximum of two. **+0 Refusal** — the party did not choose this fight, and the remnant is not peaceful.

**Disadvantage (from V3).** A character who took a letter makes their attempt at this trial **with disadvantage** — mechanically, their first stated vow fails to open the door regardless of quality, and they must state a second and better one. Tell them why. *"You already know what they would have said. You're reading it back."*

**The Keyhole (Perception DC 15).** Small, metal-lined, in the southern wall.

**The First Key.** **Aelied Axebiter's bronze disc,** carried since W1. Inserted and withdrawn, the door sinks into the floor, revealing itself to be a stone-sheathed slab of adamantine of tremendous thickness.

**No other method works.** Not *knock*, not *passwall*, not force, not disintegration, not a wish. If the disc was lost, left with the council, sold, or thrown away, the party must go back for it, and that is a real consequence of a real decision made in the first hour of the adventure.

**Vhaskar cannot open this door** (*Forfeit Watch*). If the Race Clock is 6 or lower, he is still on the far side of it having gone round by another route, and if it is 7 or 8 he got through by **having one of his own people carry the key he stole from somewhere else**, and that person is dead in the corridor beyond with their hand burnt off.

### Creatures and NPCs

**The mourners.** Assembled, silent until spoken to, and honest when they speak. They will answer direct questions about how they feel about the character, and they will not lie, and a player who asks is in for something.

**Other player characters appear among the mourners.** Ask those players what their character says at the funeral. This is the best thirty seconds in the adventure and it costs nothing.

### Set Piece

**The unfinished inscription.** Each coffin's epitaph is cut partway and stopped. Not at the end of a sentence — **mid-word**, with the chisel marks still fresh, as if someone was interrupted.

*Investigation DC 14:* they were all cut by the same hand, on the same day, four thousand years ago, and whoever it was stopped abruptly and never came back to finish, and there are twenty-two more coffins in the room behind this one that were never used.

The Watch made these for themselves.

### Lore Drops

- **L91 — The Twenty-Two Unused Coffins** (*Investigation DC 12, in the antechamber*). The Saharim of Vhal'Kathar commissioned their own coffins before taking the binding, and then took the binding, and the coffins were never needed. Four thousand years of a room full of boxes for people who cannot die.
- **L92 — The Stopped Chisel** (*Investigation DC 14*). One hand, one day, stopped mid-word. *History DC 16:* the date corresponds to the completion of the Ninefold Prohibition. The person carving these epitaphs put down the chisel when the barrier went up and never picked it up again, because there was suddenly no longer any point.
- **L93 — The Real Sky** (*Arcana DC 15*). The dome shows the actual sky over Kharyl in real time, three hundred feet of rock notwithstanding. *It is a window.* The Pindarians built this room so that anyone lying in one of these coffins would be under the open sky.

### Plot Hooks

- Every vow spoken here is a campaign obligation in the party's own words. Bring them back at the worst possible time.
- A character who finished their own epitaph has an object in the world with their death written on it, and it is in a room Vhaskar is about to walk through.

### Resolution, Rewards, and Temptation

**A Breath Token and -2 Refusal** for any character who finishes their own inscription.

**No treasure.** The adamantine slab sinks. Beyond it is a chasm.

## V7. The Dragonlord's Vigil

*Chasm and throne chamber | Deep blue light, sourceless | Exits: the adamantine door (north), the confession span (across), the Nail stair (behind the throne) | Smells of cold air off a very deep drop*

> A chasm of unknown depth lies before you, and the far side is a hundred feet away, and there is no bridge.
>
> At the centre of the near platform is a circle inscribed with the symbol of a hand with an eye in its palm, surrounded by very complicated runes.
>
> On the far side, on a throne of carved opal, sits a tall emaciated figure tapping his fingers on the armrests. He lifts his head and his eyes are orange and set deep in a fleshless skull, and deep green and gold robes hang off him, and he wears a platinum crown set with amethyst and ruby. Dozens of skeletons litter the chamber around him, all kneeling, all facing the throne.
>
> Behind him, in absolute silence, a spectral city burns.

### What Is in This Room

1. **The chasm,** 100 feet across, 3,000 feet deep, with a **sphere of annihilation** at the bottom for anyone who checks.
2. **The confession circle,** inscribed with the symbol of **Vecna** — *History or Religion DC 20* — and runes with teleportation properties.
3. **Kathandar, Elder Dragonlord,** Saharim guardian of Vhal'Kathar, on the opal throne.
4. **Gravewake,** a black mace, **not here** — it is below, at the Nail, and its absence should be noticed.
5. **The kneeling skeletons,** roughly sixty, all facing the throne, all in Pindarian civil dress rather than armour.
6. **The burning city,** projected behind the throne: Pindaria's capital on its last night, on a loop, silent, four thousand years of it.
7. **The stair down** behind the throne to V8.
8. **If Race Clock 7:** Kathandar and Vhaskar are already fighting when the party arrives.

### Setting the Scene

The chasm first, then the figure across it, then the city burning behind him in total silence. Give it three beats.

He speaks in **Pindarian**. A party without translation gets a very different scene — an emaciated crowned lich addressing them furiously in a dead language across an impassable gap — and the original's read-aloud is the correct one for that case: *"How dare you desecrate the halls of Pindurane! I am to guard the secrets of the great pyramid from thieves and miscreants like you! Prepare to die, foolish idiots!"*

**A party that can understand him hears something else entirely,** because the Pindarian is more precise, and what he actually says, in the second sentence, is closer to: *"I am to guard the secrets of the great pyramid from thieves. Tell me you are not thieves. Please."*

### DM Information

**Crossing the chasm requires a confession.** A character stands in the circle and states something true about themselves that they have been hiding from the others, and is teleported across. Any other method of crossing fails, and you should improvise the failure rather than allow it — a flying character finds a headwind that does not end; *dimension door* arrives back on the near platform; a bridge simply is not long enough, however long it is.

**One character per confession.** Everyone crosses or nobody does, and the party will spend twenty minutes deciding who goes last.

**Kathandar's question is the whole encounter:** *"Tell me that all of this purchased something."*

He is not asking whether the party is good. He is asking whether Pindaria's sacrifice — four thousand years, nine pyramids, an entire civilisation converted into a lock — bought anything at all, and he has had four thousand years and no visitors in which to conclude that it did not.

**He can be convinced.** Three successes at **DC 17** using **Persuasion, History, or Religion**, or — much better — **evidence**, and the party has been accumulating evidence since Act I:

| Evidence | Effect |
| --- | --- |
| **The minotaur Watch** (L23, Panel 37 in N3) — Wahyrst's founders are descended from his own soldiers | One automatic success |
| **Aelied's disc** — a key-token handed down by hand for a hundred generations by people who never knew what it was and never lost it | One automatic success, and he asks to hold it |
| **A character who left One More Day willingly** (V5), saying so plainly | **Ends the encounter.** No further checks. |
| **Dunnet speaking** (V1/V2) | One automatic success, and he will not be able to look at the child |
| **The Keeper in V4**, quoted back to him — *"we met the one who wouldn't let go"* | One automatic success |
| **Vhessara is alive upstairs** | One automatic success, and see below |

**Failure does not force combat.** Kathandar sits back down. The party may leave, rest, and return, and he will still be there, and he will be worse.

**What forces combat** is exactly one thing: **claiming that the dead belong to the living.** Commanding a corpse in front of him, raising something, invoking Gravewake's name as a property claim, or arguing that the archive should be used. Then he comes off the throne.

### Vhessara

If the party reached her in N18, they are carrying a promise not to tell him what she looks like.

**He asks.** Directly, in the first minute: *"Is she still up there? Is she well?"* He has believed for four hundred years that she barred the stair against him because she was ashamed of him, and she has believed for four hundred years that she cannot come down because she is ashamed of herself, and neither has sent a message because neither thinks they are owed a reply.

**Telling him the truth breaks the promise and ends the encounter instantly and well.** **Keeping the promise costs one automatic success** and he will find out anyway, later, and he will understand.

There is no right answer and both are good. Do not weight it.

### Mechanics

**The Confession Span (Roleplay, no check).** A true, concealed thing about yourself, stated aloud in the circle, in front of the others. Teleports the speaker across. Re-using a confession does not work. Lying does not work and the circle simply does nothing, which is its own kind of exposure.

**The Persuasion Route.** Three successes at **DC 17** (Persuasion, History or Religion) before two failures. Evidence converts to automatic successes as above.

**Kathandar, Elder Dragonlord** (CR 17). If combat happens:

- **AC 19, HP 296 (32d8 + 152), Speed 30 ft., fly 60 ft. (hover)**
- **Legendary Resistance 3/day. Magic Resistance.** Immune to necrotic and poison; resistant to cold and to nonmagical weapons.
- **Deathless Watch.** Reduced to 0 hit points, he reforms in **1 hour** unless Gravewake has been lawfully released.
- **The Vigil.** While the spectral city burns, Kathandar cannot be frightened and has advantage on saving throws against being charmed. The projection can be disrupted (**Arcana DC 18**, one action), and he will stop fighting to look at it.
- **Multiattack.** Three **Dragonlord's Reach** attacks: +13 to hit, reach 15 ft., 18 (3d8 + 5) bludgeoning plus 13 (3d8) necrotic. *(Law 3 applies to him and he does not care.)*
- **Recite the Fallen (Recharge 5-6).** He speaks the names of Pindaria's dead. Each creature within 60 feet makes a **DC 19 Wisdom save**, taking 45 (10d8) psychic on a failure or half on a success; a creature that fails also hears its own most recent dead named among them.
- **Legendary Actions (3).** *Reach* (one attack); *Bear Witness* (costs 2 — one creature must make a DC 19 Wisdom save or be stunned until the end of its next turn as it watches the city fall from the inside).

**No bonus actions, no reactions.** He is four thousand years old and immensely powerful and he does not dodge, does not counter, and does not interrupt. He waits his turn. It is the most frightening thing about him.

### Creatures and NPCs

**Kathandar,** Elder Dragonlord, not Pindarian, and the only one of the ten guardians who is not — which is why, per Astra's A6, he was never offered the chair.

**His regret:** he gave the order that let the Keeper stay. He signed it. He has spent four thousand years one room away from the consequence and has never gone to look at it.

**His fear,** stated plainly if the party gets there: that the barrier held, and nothing on the other side of it was worth holding, and that he has been guarding a door into an empty house.

**Twenty-one thousand minotaurs live directly above him** and he does not know, because he has not gone up the stair, because Vhessara barred it.

### Set Piece

**The silence of the city.** The projection behind the throne is Pindaria's capital burning on the last night, in complete silence, on a loop of about four minutes.

At some point in the encounter, have a character notice that Kathandar is not watching the party. He is watching a specific window, in a specific building, at about the ninety-second mark, every four minutes, for four thousand years.

Do not say whose window it is. If a player asks, he tells them, and then the encounter is effectively over.

### Lore Drops

- **L94 — The Kneeling Skeletons** (*Investigation DC 12*). Sixty Pindarian civilians in civil dress, kneeling, facing the throne. *Medicine DC 14:* they knelt and then died, of nothing. **They came down here and asked to be allowed to stay with him and then simply stopped.** He let them. He has not moved them.
- **L95 — Vecna's Circle** (*History or Religion DC 20*). The symbol is Vecna's, the god of secrets, and it has no business in a Pindarian structure. *Arcana DC 18:* the runes are Pindarian and the symbol was added later, over the top, by someone who found that a confession-gate worked better if people believed a god was listening. **Somebody has been improving the security down here.**
- **L96 — Pindurane** (*from his first speech*). The name of Pindaria's capital, the city burning behind him, and the word he uses for this pyramid. He does not call it Vhal'Kathar. He calls it *the halls of Pindurane*, because to him it is not a monument to the city. It is the city's last surviving room.
- **L97 — The Order** (*from V4's Keeper, or drawer K in V3*). Kathandar authorised the Keeper's mummification. His signature is on it. It is the only document in the pyramid in his hand and he has never asked for it back.

### Plot Hooks

- Kathandar released does not have to dissolve. He is not Pindarian and he is not bound the same way, and an Elder Dragonlord walking out of a mountain into a siege by ice giants is an ending the party can choose to write.
- Vhessara and Kathandar in the same room, after four hundred years of mutual courteous shame, is a scene worth a whole session.
- The improved security in L95 is the fourth open sarcophagus from V2, still working, still uncredited.

### Resolution, Rewards, and Temptation

**Treasure.** Behind the throne, Kathandar's treasury: **500,000 pp**, a *greatsword of life stealing*, a *vicious flail*, a *dancing scimitar*, a *dwarven thrower*, a *frost brand longsword*, an *oathbow*, a *warhammer +3*, a *hammer of thunderbolts*, and 1,000 gems at 100 gp each.

**He does not guard it and does not care about it.** If the party asks, he tells them to take what they need and asks them to leave the gems, because they were the city's reserve and somebody may still want to rebuild.

**+0 Refusal** for taking the treasury with permission. **+3** for taking it without asking.

The stair behind the throne goes down to the Nail.

## V8. The Last-Breath Nail

*Nail chamber | Black and green light from the crystal | Exits: the lightning gate (above), nothing else | Smells of ozone and old blood*

### The Lightning Gate

> You step into a chamber and find yourselves standing on one of four platforms set in its corners, each with a raised plinth and a single button at its centre. Dark blue water fills the floor between them, and four enormous spheres of lightning crackle and turn above its surface.

**The Puzzle.** All four buttons must be **held down simultaneously for four seconds.** This requires four creatures, one per platform, which means the party must split with no way to reach each other.

- **Success:** the spheres converge at the centre and open a portal to the Nail chamber.
- **Touching the water:** **27 (5d10) lightning damage.**
- **Releasing early, or pressing out of sequence, or fewer than four buttons held:** all four spheres arc across the chamber and every creature in the room takes **38 (7d10) lightning damage.**

A party of three needs a summon, a familiar, an animated object, an unseen servant, or one of the NPCs they have been dragging around since Act II. A party that brought Ssevaral, Iss-Tereth, Taeleth Vint or Tullo Renn this far gets to use them, and should be told so.

### The Nail Chamber

> A formation of glowing green and purple fluorite fills the centre of the chamber, taller than a man, and inside it, suspended, is a black mace pinned point-down through a disk of bone. Runes are cut deep into the stone around it.
>
> With every pulse of black light, the room exhales a name.

### What Is in This Room

1. **Gravewake,** inside the fluorite, driven through the **Planar Nail** — a disk of bone eight feet across, set into the floor.
2. **The banishment enchantment** on the crystal, holding **Orcus** out of Elderon.
3. **The exhaled names,** one per pulse, roughly one every four seconds, in a voice like a held breath.
4. **A ring of light** around the Nail's head with a **mace-shaped absence** in it, as at Astra.
5. **Vhaskar,** if the Race Clock is 7 or 8, already here, already working.
6. **Nothing else.** No guards, no traps, no treasure. The most important room in the pyramid is a crystal and a hole in the floor.

### DM Information

**The improper removal, from the original and unchanged in substance:** the enchantment must be dispelled (***dispel magic* against DC 18**), and then the crystal broken (**AC 15, 100 hp**). And when Gravewake comes free that way, **Orcus is no longer banished** — he manifests, seizes the mace, and attacks.

Use **Orcus** at whatever scale your table can survive; a projected avatar at CR 22 with three legendary actions and lair actions is appropriate, and the party at level 11 will not beat him. **They do not have to.** Lawfully releasing the relic *while he is manifesting* banishes him again instantly, which is the intended out and should be visible.

**The lawful release,** per the canon: **a living creature must refuse command of a willing dead soul.**

Here is what that means in play. When the party approaches the Nail, the exhaled names stop, and a single voice — one of the dead they have been carrying since Act I, and you should pick the one that hurts — says, clearly:

*"Tell me what to do and I will do it."*

And it means it. Whoever the party has been carrying — Aelied, Rhosseth Dunn, Barak, a character's own dead from V1 — offers themselves, sincerely and freely, as an instrument. Not tricked. Not compelled. Willing.

**Refusing that offer aloud releases the relic.** Not refusing to use them for evil — refusing to command them *at all.* The character must say some version of *no, you don't have to do anything,* and mean it, and let the voice go.

**Accepting the offer** — giving the willing dead a single instruction, however kind, however small — takes the mace out of the crystal in the taker's hand, and **opens a Death Breach**, and every corpse within ten miles speaks at once, which at this moment includes a besieged city, a giant army, and a trebuchet counterweight full of Wahyrst's own war dead.

### Vhaskar at the Nail

If he is here, he is not trying to take Gravewake. He never was. **He is reading.**

He has the transcription from V3's index and six hundred circled names, and he is standing at the Nail reading them aloud in order, and each one is answering, and he is **empanelling them** — a jury of the violently dead with unfinished business, six hundred strong and growing, against the living, to be held here, where the record is.

He will explain this courteously if asked. He is not planning a massacre. He is planning a **hearing**, and he intends to abide by the verdict, and he has calculated that he will lose and that losing is the point.

**He is wrong,** and the reason is the one thing the party can demonstrate and he cannot: **the dead he has empanelled did not consent to being called.** He screened them for grievance and never once asked them whether they wanted to testify. He has spent four thousand years believing the living waste the dead, and his remedy is to use them.

A character who says that to him — and who has *refused* the willing offer at the Nail in front of him — takes him apart more completely than the fight will.

**If it comes to a fight:** Vhaskar at full strength (CR 15, and no escape clause this time), plus **1d4 Processional Remnants** he has called, in a room where Law 3 is stripping his maximum every round he uses the Ledger-Knife. He will lose slowly and he will not stop.

### Mechanics

**Lawful Release.** Refuse the willing offer aloud. The crystal goes clear, the mace comes free into the hand of whoever refused, attunement is voluntary, and the Nail's ring closes.

**-4 Refusal.** **Relic Attention +1.** Every character gains a permanent **Breath Token** that does not dissolve on leaving.

**Improper Removal.** *Dispel magic* DC 18, then break the crystal (AC 15, 100 hp). **Orcus manifests.** **Death Breach opens.** **Relic Attention +2.** Every corpse within ten miles speaks at once — including the counterweight.

**Commanding the willing dead.** As improper removal, plus **+3 Refusal**, plus the commanded soul is bound to Gravewake permanently and the wielder hears them.

**Refusal at 6.** If the party arrives at 6 Refusal, the archive has already risen and is moving toward this room, and the Nail chamber fight includes everything the necropolis had in it. Kathandar does not help.

## Vhal'Kathar Aftermath

When Gravewake is removed lawfully, a deep vibration passes through the remaining pyramids. The local distortion under Kharyl ends. The dead of Wahyrst stop sitting up.

**Immediate consequences.**

- **Relic Attention +1** (or **+2** on theft). At Attention 4, cults and emissaries arrive, and Orcus's are not subtle.
- **The siege breaks, or it does not.** The giants were promised their cairn-dead. With the pyramid released, that promise is void, and the Hearthspeakers will know within the hour. A party that goes up and *tells them* — with Gravewake in hand, in Giant, honestly — ends a war with a conversation. A party that does not fights three thousand ice giants.
- **The counterweight.** Loaded with Wahyrst's own war dead, ready to throw. If the release was lawful, they are simply bodies again and the giants have no reason to loose. If it was not, the Death Breach opens and the counterweight is the first thing to speak.
- **Kathandar goes upstairs.** Twenty-one thousand minotaurs descended in part from his own Watch, living on his roof, under siege. Whatever he does about that is the best hook this pyramid produces.
- **Vhessara and the eleven statues in N2.** With her cooperation they can be freed correctly, one at a time, and most of them will still die, and Taeleth Vint will be there for it.
- **Sella Vosk** is still in the quartermaster's stores, and her brother is alive, and Aelied asked the party not to be hard on her, and she asked from a wall four hundred feet underground and they can prove she said it.
