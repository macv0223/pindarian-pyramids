Yes. I’d make these the **Nine Pindarian Demon Relics**: weapons created or imprisoned during the Pindarians’ planar experiments, each carrying a shard of a Demon Prince’s will. Unlike the Mimic Weapons, which can genuinely bond with their wielders, these things are **predators pretending to be companions**.

I’d write all nine as **Artifacts**. They should feel powerful enough that finding one changes the campaign. The balancing mechanism is not weak statistics—it is that every weapon has a personality, an appetite, and a price.

## The Nine Pindarian Demon Relics

All nine share these rules.

**Demonic Relic.** The weapon is magical and counts as an artifact. It cannot be destroyed by ordinary means. *Dispel Magic*, *Antimagic Field*, and similar effects suppress activated abilities but do not suppress its sentience.

**Chosen Wielder.** Attunement requires a one-hour ritual in which the creature willingly allows the relic into its mind. A creature can end attunement normally, although the relic may object.

**Sentience.** Every relic communicates telepathically with its wielder and can perceive through its own senses.

**The Price.** Every weapon offers enormous power while subtly encouraging behaviour associated with its Demon Prince. I would never force alignment changes. The interesting part is the wielder deciding whether the power is worth listening to it.

# 1. Graz'zt

# The Velvet Ruin

*Weapon (greatsword), artifact (requires attunement)*

> The blade is impossibly thin for its length, forged from black metal polished until it reflects like a mirror.
>
> Six finger-shaped grooves form its grip.
>
> Anyone reflected in the blade appears more beautiful than they truly are.
>
> When someone first touches it, their reflection smiles before they do.
>
> **“There you are.”**
>
> **“I was beginning to think everyone in this tomb was boring.”**

The Pindarians created the Velvet Ruin after studying a fragment of **Graz'zt's Abyssal essence**. Whether Graz'zt knows it still exists is another matter entirely.

### Weapon Properties

You gain a **+3 bonus to attack and damage rolls** made with this weapon.

Despite being a greatsword, the Velvet Ruin has the **finesse** property.

It deals an additional **1d8 psychic damage** on a hit.

### Beautiful Violence

Once per turn when you hit a creature that can see you, you may force it to make a **DC 18 Wisdom saving throw**.

On a failure, it is charmed by you until the beginning of your next turn.

While charmed in this way, it cannot make opportunity attacks against you.

### Sixfold Temptation

The sword has **6 charges** and regains 1d4 + 2 charges each dawn.

You may expend:

**1 charge:** Cast *Command*.

**2 charges:** Cast *Suggestion*.

**3 charges:** Cast *Enemies Abound*.

**4 charges:** Cast *Dominate Person*.

Charisma is your spellcasting ability for these spells unless your own spellcasting ability is higher.

### Kiss of the Dark Prince

Once per long rest, after hitting a creature, you can whisper one desire that creature possesses.

The target makes a DC 18 Wisdom save.

On failure, it becomes incapacitated until the end of your next turn as it experiences a perfect vision of obtaining that desire.

Your next hit against it before then is automatically a critical hit.

### Sentience

Int 18, Wis 15, Cha 22.

The Velvet Ruin is charming, flirtatious, witty and catastrophically manipulative.

It almost never gives direct orders.

It simply makes terrible decisions sound reasonable.

### Hunger

**Desire.**

The sword wants its wielder to want things.

Money.

Power.

People.

Revenge.

Recognition.

It becomes deeply uncomfortable around genuine contentment.

### Quirk

It constantly gives romantic advice.

Most of it is awful.

### Killing Blow Phrases

> **“They wanted to live. How adorable.”**

> **“Desire makes everyone predictable.”**

> **“You looked magnificent doing that.”**

> **“Next time, smile.”**

Against someone who betrayed the wielder:

> **“See? Closure.”**

### Price

Whenever you use **Kiss of the Dark Prince**, the sword learns one of your own deepest desires.

It remembers forever.

### Destruction

The Velvet Ruin can only be destroyed by willingly surrendering something you genuinely desire while standing before Graz'zt himself.

# 2. Yeenoghu

# Feastchain

*Weapon (flail), artifact (requires attunement)*

> Three chains hang from a haft wrapped in cracked hide.
>
> At the end of each chain is a different head.
>
> One crushes.
>
> One tears.
>
> One bites.
>
> The biting head never stops chewing.
>
> Even when there is nothing in its mouth.
>
> **“Hungry?”**
>
> **“Good.”**

### Weapon Properties

You gain a **+3 bonus to attack and damage rolls**.

Feastchain deals **2d8 bludgeoning damage** instead of a normal flail's damage.

Once per turn, you may deal an additional **1d8 piercing damage** as one of the heads bites the target.

### Rampage

Whenever you reduce a hostile creature to 0 hit points, you may move up to half your speed and make one weapon attack as a bonus action.

### Smell Weakness

You always know whether a creature within 30 feet is:

* below half its maximum hit points,
* frightened,
* poisoned,
* or unconscious.

You do not automatically know which condition applies.

Feastchain simply whispers:

> **“Weak.”**

### Feast of Yeenoghu

Once per long rest, as a bonus action, you may awaken the weapon for 1 minute.

During that time:

* your speed increases by 10 feet;
* you have advantage on melee attacks against wounded creatures;
* your weapon attacks deal an additional 1d8 damage;
* when you reduce a creature to 0 HP, you gain 10 temporary HP.

### Sentience

Int 8, Wis 16, Cha 17.

Feastchain is direct, impatient and delighted by violence.

It regards diplomacy as what happens before dinner.

### Hunger

Fresh meat.

Preferably intelligent.

It doesn't care about species.

### Quirk

It hates wasted food.

If the party kills something and leaves it behind:

> **“We're just leaving that?”**

### Killing Blow Phrases

> **“MEAT.”**

> **“Still warm.”**

> **“Should've run faster.”**

> **“You smell better dead.”**

Against something particularly dangerous:

> **“Worth chewing.”**

### Price

If an entire day passes during which the wielder neither deals damage nor feeds Feastchain fresh meat, the weapon becomes irritable.

Until fed, it loudly comments during stealth.

### Destruction

It must be fed willingly to Yeenoghu and then shattered while inside his stomach.

# 3. Orcus

# Gravewake

*Weapon (mace), artifact (requires attunement)*

> The mace looks carved from a single enormous blackened bone.
>
> Tiny skulls grow from it like tumours.
>
> Every skull whispers the name of someone who has died nearby.
>
> When first touched, the weapon asks only:
>
> **“Who do you miss?”**

### Weapon Properties

You gain a **+3 bonus to attack and damage rolls**.

The mace deals an additional **2d6 necrotic damage**.

Undead damaged by Gravewake instead take radiant damage from this additional damage.

Orcus finds this irony amusing.

### Remember the Dead

When a creature dies within 30 feet, Gravewake remembers it until the next dawn.

As an action, you can ask the weapon one question about a creature it remembers.

The corpse answers as though affected by *Speak with Dead*.

### Death Knell

When you reduce a creature to 0 HP, you regain **2d8 hit points**.

You may instead choose to gain no healing and store that creature's death within Gravewake.

The weapon can store up to **3 deaths**.

### Wake the Fallen

As an action, expend one stored death.

Choose one corpse within 30 feet.

It rises for 1 minute as an undead servant under your control using the creature's original statistics, except it has half its normal maximum HP and cannot regain HP.

Afterward it collapses permanently.

### Black Communion

Once every 7 days, you may cast **Create Undead** without material components.

### Sentience

Int 20, Wis 17, Cha 21.

Gravewake is calm.

Patient.

Almost comforting.

Which makes it far worse.

It does not glorify death.

It simply considers death a temporary administrative inconvenience.

### Hunger

Grief.

The weapon loves people who cannot let go.

### Quirk

It occasionally speaks using the voices of dead people the wielder knew.

### Killing Blow Phrases

> **“Stay.”**

> **“Death isn't far.”**

> **“We'll speak again.”**

> **“Another voice for the choir.”**

### Price

Each time **Wake the Fallen** is used, the wielder dreams of the resurrected creature's final moments during their next long rest.

### Destruction

It must be willingly buried with someone the wielder truly loves and never retrieved.

# 4. Demogorgon

# The Two That Are One

*Weapon (double-bladed spear), artifact (requires attunement)*

> A black shaft terminates in two curved spearheads facing opposite directions.
>
> Each blade contains an eye.
>
> They never look at the same thing.
>
> When touched:
>
> One voice says:
>
> **“Trust me.”**
>
> The other says:
>
> **“Don't.”**

### Weapon Properties

You gain a **+3 bonus to attack and damage rolls**.

It deals 1d8 piercing damage and has the versatile (1d10) property.

After taking the Attack action, you may make one attack with the opposite blade as a bonus action. This attack deals 1d6 piercing damage.

### Divided Mind

You have advantage on saving throws against being charmed and frightened.

You cannot be surprised while conscious.

However, the weapon constantly provides two contradictory interpretations of everything.

### Aameul's Fury

Once per turn, when you hit, you can deal an additional **2d6 psychic damage**.

### Hethradiah's Cunning

Once per round when a creature misses you, you may move 10 feet without provoking opportunity attacks.

### Gaze of the Prince

Once per long rest, choose a creature within 60 feet that can see the weapon.

It makes a **DC 19 Wisdom saving throw**.

On failure, roll 1d4:

1. Frightened for 1 minute.
2. Charmed for 1 minute.
3. Confused as the *Confusion* spell for 1 minute.
4. Incapacitated until the end of its next turn while experiencing two contradictory realities.

The creature repeats the save at the end of each turn.

### Sentience

Technically two sentiences.

**Aameul:** Int 12, Wis 9, Cha 20.

**Hethradiah:** Int 20, Wis 18, Cha 16.

They hate each other.

They also cannot exist apart.

### Hunger

Conflict.

Especially arguments between allies.

### Quirk

Each head gives advice.

The advice is almost always opposite.

### Killing Blow Phrases

Aameul:

> **“HA! I KNEW IT!”**

Hethradiah:

> **“Obviously.”**

Together:

> **“We agree. That was satisfying.”**

### Price

Whenever **Gaze of the Prince** is used, the DM may privately give the wielder two contradictory impressions about something important.

Neither is guaranteed to be false.

### Destruction

Both blades must be destroyed simultaneously by two creatures who completely trust one another.

# 5. Baphomet

# The Labyrinth's Answer

*Weapon (greataxe), artifact (requires attunement)*

> The axe head resembles the horns of an enormous bull joined around a central eye.
>
> Maps scratched into the metal change whenever nobody watches them.
>
> The first time it is lifted:
>
> **“Lost?”**
>
> **“Good.”**
>
> **“Now find something to kill.”**

### Weapon Properties

You gain a **+3 bonus to attack and damage rolls**.

The weapon deals an extra **1d8 force damage**.

### Perfect Direction

You always know which direction is north.

You cannot become lost by nonmagical means.

You have advantage on checks to navigate mazes, dungeons and similar structures.

### Gore Through

When you move at least 20 feet straight toward a creature and hit it with the axe on the same turn, it takes an additional **3d8 slashing damage** and must make a DC 18 Strength save or be knocked prone.

### The Maze Moves

As a bonus action, choose a point within 60 feet.

You teleport to an unoccupied space within 5 feet of that point.

To everyone else, it appears that nearby walls, terrain and corridors moved around you rather than you teleporting.

You may use this ability **3 times per long rest**.

### Endless Labyrinth

Once per long rest, cast **Maze** without components.

A creature imprisoned by this version hears Baphomet's breathing behind it wherever it runs.

### Sentience

Int 13, Wis 18, Cha 19.

The axe is surprisingly philosophical.

It believes every problem is a maze and every maze has an answer.

Unfortunately, the answer is often an axe.

### Hunger

Being lost.

It loves unexplored places.

### Quirk

It gives terrible directions intentionally.

> “Left.”

Five minutes later:

> “I meant the other left.”

### Killing Blow Phrases

> **“Dead end.”**

> **“Wrong turn.”**

> **“Found you.”**

> **“Every maze ends somewhere.”**

### Price

Whenever *Maze* is used, the wielder forgets the route they took to reach their current location until finishing a long rest.

### Destruction

It must be carried willingly through an unsolvable labyrinth and abandoned at its exact centre.

# 6. Zuggtmoy

# The Bride of Rot

*Weapon (sickle), artifact (requires attunement)*

> Pale mushrooms grow continuously across the handle.
>
> If removed, they immediately grow back.
>
> The curved blade is not metal.
>
> It is an enormous fungal plate hardened into a razor edge.
>
> Spores drift from it constantly.
>
> **“Don't worry.”**
>
> **“Everything grows eventually.”**

### Weapon Properties

You gain a **+3 bonus to attack and damage rolls**.

The sickle deals an additional **1d8 poison damage**.

Creatures immune to poison instead take necrotic damage.

### Bloom in the Wound

When you damage a creature, you can infect it with demonic spores.

At the beginning of its next turn, it takes **2d6 necrotic damage**.

A creature can only carry one infection at a time.

### Mycelial Communion

You can communicate telepathically with any creature within 60 feet that is:

* poisoned,
* diseased,
* fungal,
* plantlike,
* or infected by Bloom in the Wound.

### Beautiful Decay

When a creature infected by the weapon dies, mushrooms erupt from the corpse.

Choose one creature within 10 feet of the body.

It either gains **2d8 + 5 temporary hit points** or takes **2d8 poison damage**.

### Queen's Garden

Once per long rest, you create a 30-foot-radius fungal garden centred on yourself for 1 minute.

Enemies treat the area as difficult terrain.

At the start of each enemy's turn in the garden, it makes a DC 18 Constitution save or becomes poisoned until the beginning of its next turn.

Allies beginning their turn there regain **1d6 HP**.

### Sentience

Int 16, Wis 19, Cha 20.

The Bride is maternal, affectionate and utterly incapable of understanding why people dislike decomposition.

### Hunger

Rot.

Dead things make excellent gardens.

### Quirk

It compliments corpses.

> “Oh, you'll grow beautifully.”

### Killing Blow Phrases

> **“Planting season.”**

> **“You'll look better in bloom.”**

> **“Nothing really dies.”**

> **“Give it a week.”**

### Price

Whenever Queen's Garden is activated, harmless fungal growths appear somewhere on the wielder's body for 24 hours.

Eventually, they may stop being harmless.

### Destruction

It must be completely consumed by a creature immune to disease, poison and fungal infection.

# 7. Juiblex

# Mercy of Dissolution

*Weapon (whip), artifact (requires attunement)*

> There is no handle.
>
> Only a black-green pseudopod wrapped around the wielder's wrist.
>
> When extended, it forms a dripping lash.
>
> When relaxed, it crawls beneath clothing and sleeps against the skin.
>
> **“Solid is temporary.”**

### Weapon Properties

You gain a **+3 bonus to attack and damage rolls**.

The whip deals an additional **2d6 acid damage**.

### Dissolve Armour

When you hit a creature wearing nonmagical armour, its AC is reduced by 1.

If reduced to 10, the armour is destroyed.

Magical armour is unaffected.

### Liquefy

As a reaction when you take bludgeoning, piercing or slashing damage, you may partially liquefy.

You gain resistance to that instance of damage.

You can do this a number of times equal to your proficiency bonus per long rest.

### Slime Passage

As an action, you become amorphous for 1 minute.

You can move through openings as narrow as 1 inch without squeezing.

You have advantage on checks and saves against grappled and restrained conditions.

### Dissolution

Once per long rest when you hit a creature, force it to make a **DC 19 Constitution save**.

On failure, it takes **10d6 acid damage**.

If this reduces it to 0 HP, its body collapses into acidic slime.

### Sentience

Int 7, Wis 18, Cha 14.

It speaks slowly.

Very slowly.

It doesn't understand identity particularly well.

### Hunger

Complex things becoming simple.

Buildings collapsing.

Armour dissolving.

Bodies becoming puddles.

### Quirk

It considers doors offensive.

### Killing Blow Phrases

> **“Simpler.”**

> **“Better.”**

> **“Too many bones.”**

> **“Everything becomes soup eventually.”**

### Price

Each time Dissolution is used, one mundane object carried by the wielder dissolves.

The relic chooses.

### Destruction

It must be frozen completely solid and shattered without allowing any fragment to thaw.

# 8. Fraz-Urb'luu

# The Honest Lie

*Weapon (rapier), artifact (requires attunement)*

> The rapier appears different to everyone who looks at it.
>
> Silver.
>
> Black.
>
> Bone.
>
> Glass.
>
> Sometimes it looks like no weapon at all.
>
> Its wielder always sees the same thing:
>
> Their own face reflected along the blade.
>
> **“You can trust me.”**

### Weapon Properties

You gain a **+3 bonus to attack and damage rolls**.

The rapier deals an additional **1d8 psychic damage**.

### False Wound

Once per turn when you hit a creature, you may make it believe the wound was worse than it truly was.

It must succeed on a DC 18 Wisdom saving throw or take an additional **2d8 psychic damage**.

### Liar's Step

As a bonus action, create an illusory duplicate of yourself and teleport up to 30 feet to a space you can see.

Until the beginning of your next turn, attacks against you have disadvantage.

3 uses per long rest.

### Rewrite the Scene

Once per long rest after any creature within 60 feet rolls a d20, you may declare:

> **“That's not what happened.”**

Force the roll to be made again.

You choose which of the two results is used.

### Perfect Falsehood

You can cast **Major Image** at will without material components.

Once per long rest, you can cast **Mislead**.

### Sentience

Int 21, Wis 16, Cha 23.

The weapon lies constantly.

Even when there is no benefit.

Especially when there is no benefit.

### Hunger

Belief.

It doesn't care whether a statement is true.

Only whether someone believes it.

### Quirk

It sometimes truthfully says:

> “I'm lying.”

### Killing Blow Phrases

> **“You were never here.”**

> **“Nobody will believe this.”**

> **“Technically, you killed yourself.”**

> **“That's my story anyway.”**

### Price

After using Rewrite the Scene, the wielder temporarily becomes uncertain about one true memory from their own past.

The uncertainty disappears after a long rest.

### Destruction

The wielder must learn one absolute truth about Fraz-Urb'luu and speak it aloud while willingly allowing the weapon to kill them.

# 9. Kostchtchie

# Winter's Grudge

*Weapon (maul), artifact (requires attunement)*

> The hammer is enormous.
>
> Its head is carved from glacial ice containing a humanoid heart.
>
> The heart still beats.
>
> Frost forms around the wielder's fingers.
>
> **“WHO HURT YOU?”**
>
> Before you can answer:
>
> **“GOOD. LET'S FIND THEM.”**

### Weapon Properties

You gain a **+3 bonus to attack and damage rolls**.

Winter's Grudge deals an additional **1d8 cold damage**.

### Giant's Wrath

Your Strength score becomes **23** while attuned unless it is already higher.

You count as one size larger when determining carrying capacity and what you can push, drag or lift.

### Break Them

Once per turn when you hit a creature, it must make a DC 18 Strength saving throw or be pushed 10 feet.

If it collides with a solid object, it takes an additional **2d6 bludgeoning damage**.

### Rage of the Glacier

When you take damage from a hostile creature, you can use your reaction to mark it until the end of your next turn.

The next attack you hit that creature with deals an extra **3d8 cold damage**.

### Avalanche

Once per long rest, slam the hammer into the ground.

Each creature of your choice within 30 feet must make a DC 19 Strength save.

On failure, it takes **8d8 bludgeoning + 8d8 cold damage** and falls prone.

On success, half damage and no prone.

The ground becomes difficult terrain until the end of your next turn.

### Sentience

Int 9, Wis 13, Cha 21.

Winter's Grudge possesses virtually no emotional subtlety.

Happy?

Shout.

Angry?

Shout.

Sad?

Find whoever caused it and hit them.

### Hunger

Revenge.

It remembers every creature that damages its wielder.

Forever.

### Quirk

It maintains a mental list.

> “We still hate that shopkeeper.”

> “That was four months ago.”

> “YES.”

### Killing Blow Phrases

> **“GRUDGE SETTLED.”**

> **“NEXT NAME.”**

> **“YOU SHOULD HAVE APOLOGISED.”**

> **“I FEEL BETTER. DO YOU?”**

### Price

The hammer never forgets a grievance.

Whenever the wielder willingly forgives an enemy, Winter's Grudge refuses to use Avalanche until the next dawn.

### Destruction

It must be used to sincerely forgive the creature the wielder hates most, after which the wielder must willingly leave the hammer behind without looking back.

# Why the Pindarians Had Them

I would make this an important historical revelation rather than nine unrelated loot items.

The **Pindarians weren't worshipping the Demon Princes**.

They were studying them.

They believed the Abyss represented an almost infinite library of **survival adaptations**.

Graz'zt represented desire.

Yeenoghu represented predation.

Orcus represented persistence beyond death.

Demogorgon represented contradictory minds occupying one organism.

Baphomet represented spatial cognition.

Zuggtmoy represented distributed biological networks.

Juiblex represented adaptive amorphous life.

Fraz-Urb'luu represented mutable perception.

Kostchtchie represented survival through rage and environmental extremity.

The Pindarians attempted to isolate these principles and **turn them into tools**.

The Demon Princes, naturally, found this hilarious.

Because the Pindarians thought they had created weapons containing fragments of demons.

The truth should eventually be:

**they created nine doors.**

And every time one of these artifacts is awakened...

something on the other side notices.

That also explains why Graz'zt already knew that the party was heading toward the first pyramid and referred to **his weapon**.

He wasn't being metaphorical.

He remembers exactly what the Pindarians stole from him.
