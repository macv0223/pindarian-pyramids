# Gor-Mazaal: Pyramid of the Wandering Path - Site Guide

*Levels 11-13 | Children of the Void | Confusion into Purpose | Relic: The Labyrinth's Answer | Guardian: Marshal Korveth Anor, the Blind Cartographer*

A mountain fold whose shadow is a perfect pyramid at every hour of the day.

## Running Gor-Mazaal

The pyramid cannot be solved by memorising routes. Its geometry answers **intent**: characters who know why they are moving arrive, and characters who chase the centre get lost.

That is the whole site and it must be enforced literally, at the level of what players say out loud. A party that says *"we go north"* is navigating and will fail. A party that says *"we're looking for somewhere to put the wounded man down"* is stating a purpose and the building will take them there.

**Do not draw this dungeon.** Do not give the players a map, do not let them draw one that works, and do not maintain one yourself beyond a list of the seven keyed areas. Gor-Mazaal has no floor plan. It has a set of rooms and a rule about how you get between them, and the rule is that you have to mean something.

This is uncomfortable for exactly the kind of player who is best at dungeons, and that discomfort is the content.

### Pindarian Scale

**Pindarians are Large.** All of them, always — the Solaari, the Lunari, the Veylari, the Ilyri, every
Saharim guardian and every soldier in every rank. Eight to ten feet, long-boned, and long-lived, with a
reach and a stride to match: **10-foot reach on their melee attacks and a 10-foot space.**

This is not a variant or a subtype. It is what the species is, and it is worth showing rather than stating —
Pindarian doorways are twelve feet high, their stairs have deep treads, their chairs do not fit anyone else,
and a party of Medium adventurers spends nine pyramids using furniture built for people half again their
size.

Human, elven and dwarven NPCs met in and around the sites are Medium as usual. The contrast is the point.

### The Laws

State all four at the Blind Gate, through the Void Warden if you use one.

1. **A map records rooms but never connections.** Any map made inside Gor-Mazaal — drawn, magical, remembered, or divined — accurately shows every chamber and shows no corridor between any of them. *Find the path*, *clairvoyance* and similar all return a room and no route.
2. **Teleportation moves nearby architecture instead of the traveller.** *Misty step*, *dimension door*, *teleport*: the caster does not move. The room does. This is not a failure and it consumes the slot, and it is occasionally extremely useful.
3. **Arguing about direction for more than a minute advances the Maze Stalker one stage.** Use a real timer at the table. Say so before you start it.
4. **A character stating a clear purpose can fix one doorway until their next rest.** A fixed doorway leads where it led. It is the only stable thing in the building.

### Bearings

The track, visible, 0 to 6. It measures how lost the party is, and higher is worse.

| Event | Bearings |
| --- | --- |
| Arguing about direction for more than a minute | +1 each time |
| Moving without any stated purpose | +1 per transition |
| Moving *toward the centre*, stated as such | +1 each time |
| Consulting a map, chart, or *find the path* and acting on it | +1 |
| Backtracking to a room you have already left | +1 |
| Casting a teleportation effect | +1 |
| Stating a clear purpose and moving on it | -1 each |
| Naming somewhere you do not want to go (G4) | -1 |
| Anchoring to a companion rather than the structure (G3) | -1 |
| Filling a pursuit segment (G5) | -1 |
| Answering Korveth coherently (G6) | -1 each |

**At 3.** The Maze Stalker wakes and begins hunting. It is not in the room. It is one wall away, and it will be one wall away from now on.

**At 6.** Gor-Mazaal stops offering exits. Every doorway in every keyed area leads to the Returning Room (G4) regardless of purpose, until a character declares a Purpose and holds to it through two full transitions. Nothing else works — not force, not magic, not the relic.

### Declared Purpose

At any time, a character may state a **Purpose**: one sentence, present tense, about what they are trying to *do* rather than where they are trying to *be*.

- *"I want to get Hesh out of here alive."* Valid.
- *"I want to find out what happened to my brother."* Valid.
- *"I want to reach the centre."* **Not valid.** The centre is not a place.
- *"I want to go north."* Not valid. That is a direction, not a reason.

A character with a Purpose:

- **Fixes one doorway** until their next long rest. Mark it. It stays put.
- Gains **-1 Bearings** each time the party moves on that Purpose.
- May **change it at any time**, freely, but must say so aloud. An unacknowledged change does nothing.

Every character can hold one Purpose at a time. A party of four with four Purposes can cross Gor-Mazaal almost at will, and working that out is the site's real victory.

### The Maze Stalker

A four-stage clock, run in the open.

| Stage | What the party experiences |
| --- | --- |
| 0 | Nothing. |
| 1 | Sound on the far side of walls. Horn-scores in stone that were not there. |
| 2 | It is in rooms shortly after the party leaves them. Heat. Breath. |
| 3 | It is in rooms shortly before the party arrives. It has begun anticipating. |
| 4 | Contact. G5 begins wherever the party is standing. |

**Stages advance** on a minute of directional argument (Law 3), on three failures at the Turning Stair (G3), and at your discretion when the party is being aimless in a way the table is not enjoying.

**Stages never retreat** except by filling pursuit segments in G5.

### Pacing and Shape

Seven keyed areas and **no fixed order after G2**. G1 and G2 are the entrance; G6 and G7 are the end; G3, G4 and G5 happen in whatever sequence the party's Purposes produce, and a party may hit the Returning Room three times or never.

Two to three sessions. Resting is safe and costs nothing but time, and time in Gor-Mazaal is not reliable — see the hooks.

### Safety at This Table

Nothing here is distressing by subject. What it can be is **frustrating**, in a way that lands differently on different players: some people find a dungeon that refuses to be mapped delightful and some find it maddening, and the maddened one is usually the player who is best at dungeons and is used to being useful.

Watch for it. If a player is visibly locking up, have a Void Warden or Hesh Ollun state the rule to them plainly, in character, in one sentence: *"Stop asking where. Start saying why."* It is not a spoiler. It is the instructions, and the instructions were always meant to be given.

### Continuity

**Vharo Sellick is inside, and he is lost.**

He has measured three Nail sockets, he has the spacing, and he came here to triangulate the fourth — with charts, instruments, and a folding chair. He has been in the Returning Room for eleven days. He is the only being in this campaign with a plan precise enough to be completely useless here, and he is not enjoying it.

He will be polite about it. He will also, eventually, ask the party for help, and it will cost him something to do that.

## G1. The Blind Gate

*A doorway standing alone in a mountain fold | Open daylight | Exits: the valley behind, the pyramid through the door | Smells of cold rock and juniper*

> A doorway stands by itself in the mountainside. No wall, no frame beyond its own, no building behind it — you can walk around it, and there is nothing on the other side but more mountain.
>
> Its surface is carved with a map of the valley, and the map is perfect, and it is accurate to the boulder.
>
> The pyramid is shown on it. The pyramid is shown **behind you.**

### What Is in This Room

1. **The gate**, a freestanding Pindarian doorway of black stone, eleven feet high, carved on both faces.
2. **The valley map** on the outer face: accurate, current, and showing the pyramid at the party's back rather than in front of them. *Survival DC 12:* the map is not wrong. It is showing where the pyramid is **from the doorway's point of view**, which is not the same place it is from theirs.
3. **The mountain's shadow**, a perfect pyramid, cast at every hour regardless of the sun's position. *Perception DC 10*, and there is nothing casting it.
4. **Hesh Ollun**, a blind pilgrim, sitting against the gate's base with a staff across his knees. He has been waiting four days. He has a map tattooed across his back and he has never seen it.
5. **Eleven walking staffs** stacked against the mountainside beside him, of different heights and ages, none of them his.
6. **A Pindarian Void Warden**, which is not visible and will make itself so the moment somebody insists on seeing before acting.
7. **Cart ruts** approaching the gate and stopping fifteen feet short. Nothing has ever been driven through it.

### Setting the Scene

Open on the shadow, not the gate. Establish that the mountain fold throws a perfect pyramid across the valley floor and that there is nothing up there to throw it.

Then the doorway alone on the slope, then the map, then — after somebody has walked around it twice — Hesh, saying *"You'll not get in like that,"* without turning round.

### DM Information

**Closing your eyes makes the door tangible.** That is the whole of it. A creature that approaches with eyes shut, blindfolded, or otherwise not looking finds solid stone under its hands and a doorway that opens.

**A sighted creature that touches the door is teleported 60 feet backward**, gently, landing on its feet, facing the gate. No damage, no save, no exhaustion. It is not an attack; it is the building declining.

Most parties work this out in three attempts and one of them will try it eleven times first, which is what the eleven staffs are.

**Blindfolded entry grants advantage on the first navigation check** — in practice, on the party's first Bearings-relevant decision inside, which will be at G2.

**The Void Warden** challenges anyone who insists on seeing before acting. It does not attack first; it stands in front of the gate, visible, and asks one question:

> *"What do you expect to see that would change what you do?"*

A character who answers honestly — including *"nothing"* — is let past. A character who refuses to answer, or who tries to push past, fights a **Pindarian Void Warden** (see *Original Creatures*), which does not pursue beyond the gate and which stops the moment the question is answered.

**Hesh Ollun** is the tutorial, the moral, and a party member for as long as they want him.

### Mechanics

**Entering (no check).** Eyes closed, blindfolded, or blinded. The door is solid and it opens.

**Touching It Sighted.** Teleported 60 feet back. No damage. Repeatable indefinitely.

**Blindfolded Entry.** Advantage on the first Bearings-relevant decision inside.

**The Valley Map (Survival DC 12).** It is accurate from the gate's perspective. *Arcana DC 16:* this is the first demonstration of Law 1 — the map records a room (the valley) perfectly and tells you nothing usable about how to get from here to there.

**Hesh's Tattoo (Investigation DC 10, with his permission).** A map across his shoulders and back in Pindarian ink, drawn by someone with an excellent hand. It shows **seven chambers and no corridors.** It is accurate. It is also, by Law 1, useless, and Hesh knows this and carries it anyway because Korveth asked him to.

**The Eleven Staffs (automatic).** Eleven pilgrims before Hesh. He knows all their names and will give them if asked, slowly, and he stops at the seventh for a moment.

### Creatures and NPCs

**Hesh Ollun**, blind pilgrim (use **Acolyte**, MM, with Survival +4 and Insight +6). Human, sixty-ish, dry, patient, entirely unimpressed by adventurers and fond of them anyway.

He walked here from four hundred miles away on a rumour. Korveth met him at the gate — **outside**, which should be noted and not explained — sat him down, tattooed a map on his back over two days, told him he would not need it, and sent him in. He came out eight months later convinced he had been in for one afternoon.

**He has been back six times.** He has never reached the centre. He does not want to; he wants to know why the map does not work, and he has almost got it, and he is one conversation from having it.

**What he will do:** come with the party, cheerfully, and state a Purpose within the first ten minutes without being asked, because he has one, and it is *"I want to understand the map on my back."*

He is worth an entire pyramid and he costs the party nothing but the obligation of keeping a blind sixty-year-old alive in a maze with a minotaur in it.

**The Void Warden.** One question, one answer, no grudge.

### Set Piece

**The seventh staff.**

If a character asks about the eleven staffs, Hesh names them in order, evenly, without emotion, and stops at the seventh.

*"Ollun,"* he says. *"Same as me."*

Then he goes on to the eighth.

He will not elaborate unless directly and kindly asked, at which point: his son came here four years before he did, and came out, and came back in, and did not come out, and the staff is the one Hesh carved for him.

That is Hesh's actual Purpose, and it is not the one he declares, and **he will change his declared Purpose in G6 in front of everybody**, and that is his whole arc.

### Lore Drops

- **L1 — The Shadow** (*Perception DC 10, Arcana DC 15*). A perfect pyramid at every hour, cast by nothing. *The shadow is the anchor.* The stone fold is incidental; the Nail is under the shadow's apex, and the shadow's apex moves.
- **L2 — The Map That Points Behind You** (*Survival DC 12*). Accurate from the door's perspective. Law 1, demonstrated before the party has entered the building.
- **L3 — Hesh's Tattoo** (*Investigation DC 10*). Seven chambers, no corridors, drawn by a master. *History DC 15:* the hand is Pindarian military cartography, Veylari school, and it is **the finest example anyone has ever seen**, and it is on a blind man's back where nobody will look at it.
- **L4 — Eight Months** (*from Hesh*). He was inside for one afternoon. He was inside for eight months. Both are true and he has stopped arguing about it, and the agricultural season outside had turned twice.

### Plot Hooks

- Hesh's son went back in. He is findable — put him in the Returning Room with Vharo Sellick, four years lost, and let the party decide whether to tell Hesh before or after.
- Korveth met Hesh **outside the pyramid**. The Blind Cartographer leaves. That is not true of any other guardian in this campaign and somebody should notice.
- The eleven staffs are eleven names for the Black Catalogue, if the party has been to Vhal'Kathar.

### Resolution, Rewards, and Temptation

**No Bearings movement.** The track starts at 0 inside.

**Advantage on the first navigation decision** for anyone who entered blindfolded.

**Treasure.** Nothing. Hesh has 4 gp and a waterskin and he offers both.

## G2. The Map Without Walls

*Chart chamber, 50 ft. square, low-vaulted | Dim bronze light from the sheet itself | Exits: four archways, none of which are where they were | Smells of hot metal*

> A bronze sheet fills the far wall, twenty feet by twelve, and it displays every chamber in this pyramid in perfect detail — elevations, dimensions, contents, the lot.
>
> No corridor connects any of them.
>
> A dot marked **YOU** appears in three places at once, and none of the three is this room.

### What Is in This Room

1. **The bronze sheet**, warm, humming faintly, displaying seven chambers in isometric projection with no connective tissue whatsoever.
2. **Three YOU dots**, moving independently, none in the chart chamber. *Investigation DC 14:* they are not errors and they are not the party's positions. They are where the party would be if they had entered with three different intentions.
3. **Four archways**, one per wall. *Perception DC 12:* there were three when the party came in.
4. **A copying desk** with vellum, ink, and a bronze stylus, set out for use.
5. **Eleven completed copies** stacked on the desk, made by previous visitors. *Investigation DC 10:* **every one of them depicts a different building** — a farmhouse, a temple, a tenement stair, a ship's cabin.
6. **The absent centre.** Seven chambers are shown. The chart has an eighth position, marked, framed, labelled, and **empty**.

### Setting the Scene

Let them approach the chart with relief. It is the first thing in Gor-Mazaal that looks like a solution, and it is beautiful, and it is genuinely complete.

Then let somebody trace a route with a finger and find that there is nothing to trace.

### DM Information

**The map predicts rooms according to declared intent.**

**Each character may ask the chart where to find one thing.** The answer arrives as a **symbol**, burnt briefly onto the bronze: an image, never a direction.

- *"Where do I find water?"* → a cupped hand, and a horn-score across the knuckles.
- *"Where is my brother?"* → a closed door with light under it.
- *"Where is the axe?"* → the chart goes blank. Every time. For everyone.

Prepare three or four symbols in advance and improvise the rest. They should be interpretable and never unambiguous.

**Copying the map causes the copy to depict the copier's childhood home**, accurately, including rooms the copier had forgotten. This is not a trap and costs nothing and is the single best character beat available in this pyramid. Let every player who tries it have the full moment.

**The centre is absent because it is a decision, not a location.** *Arcana DC 17* on the empty eighth frame: the position is defined, dimensioned and load-bearing, and it has no contents, and the chart is not malfunctioning — there is genuinely nothing there until somebody decides something.

**This room is where the party learns the site's rule.** If they leave without understanding it, the Returning Room will teach them the hard way, which is also fine.

### Mechanics

**Asking the Chart (one question per character, no check).** A symbol, not a direction. Acting on an interpreted symbol counts as **moving on a Purpose**: **-1 Bearings**.

**Asking About the Relic.** The chart blanks. Always. **+1 Bearings** if the party keeps asking, which they will.

**Copying (Intelligence, cartographer's tools or Calligraphy, no DC).** The copy shows the copier's childhood home. It is permanent, portable, and accurate, and it is worth nothing and everything.

**The Fourth Archway (Perception DC 12).** There are four. There were three. Nobody saw it arrive. *This is Law 2 working in reverse:* somebody outside the room cast a teleportation effect at some point in the last four thousand years and the architecture moved instead of them, and it has stayed moved.

**Leaving.** The archway a character takes is determined by their stated Purpose, not by which arch they walk through. A party that leaves with no Purpose at all arrives in **G4, the Returning Room**, every time, and should.

### Creatures and NPCs

None. Gor-Mazaal's second room is an empty library of a building and it is the only quiet the party gets.

### Set Piece

**The childhood home.**

Whoever copies the chart first: describe their house. Ask the player two questions about it — what room did you avoid, and who is in the next room — and then put both of those things on the vellum, accurately, in Pindarian bronze-stylus lines.

Then let them notice that the copy has the same defect as the original. Every room. No corridors.

### Lore Drops

- **L5 — The Three YOUs** (*Investigation DC 14*). Not errors. Three positions the party would occupy under three different intentions, tracked simultaneously and updated in real time. **The building is modelling their possible selves and has been since they entered.**
- **L6 — The Eleven Copies** (*Investigation DC 10*). Eleven previous visitors, eleven different houses. One of them is a Pindarian officer's quarters in a barracks complex, drawn with the same hand as Hesh's tattoo. *Korveth copied the chart once, and got his own home, and kept it.*
- **L7 — The Empty Eighth** (*Arcana DC 17*). Dimensioned, framed, load-bearing, and containing nothing. The centre of Gor-Mazaal is a decision that has not been made yet.
- **L8 — What the Chart Is For** (*History DC 16*). Veylari planning instrument. It was never a wayfinding tool — it is a **logistics chart**, used to answer *what do we need and where does it live*, and it was built by people for whom "how do I get there" was a solved problem because they could simply fold the distance.

### Plot Hooks

- Korveth's copy of his own quarters is in the stack, and he will want it back, and he will not ask.
- The empty eighth frame is the Nail chamber and the party will not connect those until G7.
- The three YOU dots can be watched. A party that sits and observes them for an hour (*Investigation DC 16*) sees one of them stop moving, which means one of the party's possible selves has arrived somewhere and stayed, and you should never explain that.

### Resolution, Rewards, and Temptation

**-1 Bearings** per character who acts on an interpreted symbol. **+1** for pressing the chart about the relic.

**Treasure.** A copy of your own childhood home in Pindarian bronze-stylus on Pindarian vellum, worth nothing, and the bronze stylus, which is worth 150 gp and writes on anything.

## G3. The Turning Stair

*Stairwell, 200 ft. of run in a shaft with no consistent floor | Grey light from all four directions | Exits: the stair's two ends, which are both up and both down | Smells of dust falling*

> The stair climbs the wall, crosses the ceiling, and descends through its own underside. You can see all three of those happening from where you stand and you can see that it is one stair.
>
> Dust falls in four directions.

### What Is in This Room

1. **The stair**, 200 feet of tread in a shaft where gravity has four opinions and changes them on a schedule nobody has written down.
2. **Four gravity zones**, rotating irregularly, each claiming a different wall as the floor. A creature is subject to whichever zone its centre of mass occupies.
3. **Falling dust**, in four streams, all of it landing somewhere.
4. **Handholds**, carved into the treads at intervals — grips, not decoration. *Perception DC 10.* Somebody expected people to hold on.
5. **A rope**, Pindarian, still sound, running the full 200 feet, anchored at both ends, **with eleven loops tied into it at waist height.** It is a climbing line for a roped party.
6. **Scoring on the ceiling-section treads**, deep, curved, in pairs. Horns.
7. **One body**, partway up, or partway down, wearing Pindarian gear, with its hand still through a loop in the rope.

### Setting the Scene

Play the disorientation once, properly, and then move on to the mechanics — a whole scene of *which way is up* gets old fast, and the room's actual content is the rope.

Describe the dust falling in four directions first. It is the cheapest and best image in the pyramid.

### DM Information

**A group challenge, DC 16**, using **Acrobatics, Arcana, Athletics or Investigation** — whatever each character justifies. The party succeeds if half or more succeed.

**On a failure, gravity turns** under that character: **14 (4d6) bludgeoning** and they end prone on a surface that was a wall.

**Three failures across the ascent awaken the Maze Stalker** — advance the clock to Stage 1, or advance it one stage if already running.

**Anchoring to another character rather than the structure grants advantage.** Two characters roped together, holding on to each other, gripping a companion's arm instead of a handhold: **advantage on the check, both of them, -1 Bearings each.**

The rope with eleven loops is sitting right there and it is the answer. Some parties will use it immediately. Some will individually free-climb a 200-foot shaft with rotating gravity while a perfectly good Pindarian safety line hangs beside them, and those parties will make three failure rolls and meet a minotaur.

**The body in the rope** is the lesson. Somebody was roped in, and fell, and was held, and the rope did its job, and they died anyway of something else entirely — *Medicine DC 13:* they starved, still in the loop, unable to work out which way to go.

**Being anchored is not the same as having a purpose.** The rope solves this room and nothing else, and the site is making that point deliberately.

### Mechanics

**The Ascent (Group Challenge, DC 16).** Three checks across 200 feet: Acrobatics, Arcana, Athletics or Investigation.

| Result | Effect |
| --- | --- |
| Group success | Progress. Nothing else. |
| Individual failure | **14 (4d6) bludgeoning**, prone on a new surface |
| Three failures total | **Maze Stalker advances one stage** |

**Roping In (no check, one minute).** Two or more characters tied to the line or to each other. **Advantage on all their checks. -1 Bearings each, once.**

**Hesh Ollun** does not need to make any of these checks. He has been up this stair six times and he cannot see the problem, and he says so, and it is the funniest and most pointed moment in the site.

**Teleporting.** Law 2. The caster does not move; the stair does. This actually helps here — it randomises the gravity zones and grants the whole party advantage on the next check — at the cost of **+1 Bearings** and an immediate Stalker stage if the party then argues about what happened.

### Creatures and NPCs

None unless the Stalker arrives, in which case G5 begins on a staircase, which is a fine place for it.

### Set Piece

**The dust.**

Four streams, from four surfaces, falling in four directions, all of them perfectly steady. It has been falling like that for four thousand years and there is a cone of it at the bottom of each of the four floors.

Two of the cones are enormous. One is moderate. **One has a footprint in it.**

### Lore Drops

- **L9 — The Rope** (*automatic*). Eleven loops, Pindarian, sound, anchored at both ends. **This stair was climbed by roped parties as standard practice.** Gor-Mazaal was not a mystery to the Veylari; it was a facility with a safety protocol.
- **L10 — The Body in the Loop** (*Medicine DC 13*). Held by the rope, died of starvation in it. *Investigation DC 14:* they had eleven days of food and a full waterskin and did not use either after the fourth day. They stopped.
- **L11 — The Horn Scoring** (*Perception DC 12*). Deep paired curves on the ceiling treads. **The Maze Stalker uses this stair**, comfortably, in whatever gravity it likes, and has for a long time.
- **L12 — The Footprint** (*Perception DC 14*). One bare human footprint in the fourth dust cone, recent — days, not centuries. Somebody has been down here this week and was not wearing boots. *This is Hesh's son.*

### Plot Hooks

- The footprint is four years old in Hesh's understanding and four days old in the dust, and Gor-Mazaal's relationship with time is the reason, and the party now has a live rescue.
- The roped-party protocol implies a Veylari garrison. Where they went is Korveth's answer to give.

### Resolution, Rewards, and Temptation

**-1 Bearings** per character who ropes in. **+1** for teleporting. **Maze Stalker +1 stage** at three failures.

**Treasure.** The rope (Pindarian, 200 feet, functions as *rope of climbing* while inside a Pindarian structure). The body carries 90 gp, a *potion of superior healing*, and a Veylari gate-sigil that Korveth will recognise.

## G4. The Returning Room

*Chamber, 40 ft. by 30 ft., one arch in and one arch out | Cold brazier-light that is not lit | Exits: two arches, both of which lead here | Smells of cold ash and, faintly, of somebody else's cooking*

> You pass through the arch and find the same room waiting.
>
> The same extinguished brazier. The same chalk mark on the third flagstone. The same footprints approaching from the wrong door — from the door you are about to leave by, walking toward the door you just came in through, and they are yours.

### What Is in This Room

1. **The brazier**, cold, full of ash, never lit within memory.
2. **The chalk mark** on the third flagstone: a tally in five-bar gates. *Investigation DC 10:* it is being added to. Not by anyone the party can see.
3. **The footprints**, approaching from the exit arch toward the entrance arch, and they match the party's boots, and there are the right number of them.
4. **Vharo Sellick**, six-fingered, immaculate at the start of eleven days and considerably less so now, sitting on a folding chair beside a theodolite, with charts weighted down by his own shoes.
5. **Hesh's son, Aden Ollun**, in the corner, four years in and four days in, awake, quiet, and not lost — **he lives here now, and he is fine, and that is the horror.**
6. **A cooking fire** in the corner, small, in use, with a pot. Aden has been eating.
7. **Walls that whisper**, from behind rather than within: **The Labyrinth's Answer**, three rooms away, giving directions. All of them are wrong. All of them are confident.

### Setting the Scene

The party will arrive here at least once and possibly four times. **Run the first arrival straight** and let the loop land.

Second and subsequent arrivals: skip the description entirely. Say *"You're back."* Nothing else. Let the table's reaction do the work.

### DM Information

**Walking toward the goal loops.** Any transition made with the intention of progressing, advancing, or reaching the centre delivers the party here. That includes clever ones. It includes tunnelling through a wall.

**A creature that deliberately names somewhere it does not want to go exits into the Heart of Bearings (G6).** Out loud, sincerely, and meaning it: *"I don't want to go back to the stair."* *"I don't want to see my father's face when I tell him."* The arch opens onto G6.

**-1 Bearings** for each character who does this.

**Random movement leads to a memory maze and costs one Hit Die.** A party that rolls dice, flips coins or picks arbitrarily spends twenty minutes walking through rooms from their own pasts — described briefly, one per character, one sentence each — and arrives back here, tired.

**The relic whispers from behind the walls.** Directions, delivered in a severe, patient, faintly contemptuous voice, all of them wrong. **It is not lying.** *The Labyrinth's Answer* genuinely believes every problem is a maze and is genuinely trying to help, and it has been giving directions to a building that does not use them for four thousand years, and it has not yet drawn the obvious conclusion.

**Vharo Sellick** is the party's mirror. He arrived with a perfect method. He has filled eleven days of charts. He has established, to three decimal places, the position of a thing that is not in a position.

He will trade: **everything he has** — three Nail sockets measured, the spacing of the network, his employer's name, the schedule he is working to — **for being walked out.** He means it, he will keep it, and it is the best intelligence the party can acquire in this campaign.

Whether they take a cambion in Graz'zt's service out of a labyrinth is up to them.

**Aden Ollun** is the room's real content. He is not trapped, not charmed, not undead. He worked out in his first month that the pyramid gives you where you mean to be, decided he did not mean to be anywhere, and has been here ever since. He is calm, lucid, well-fed and entirely without purpose, and he has no intention of leaving.

His father is standing in the doorway.

### Mechanics

**The Loop (no check).** Any purposeful-toward-the-goal transition returns here. Repeat as often as needed; it costs nothing but morale.

**Naming Somewhere You Don't Want To Go (no check).** Sincere, aloud. The next arch opens onto **G6**. **-1 Bearings.**

**Random Movement.** One Hit Die per character, a memory maze, and back here. Describe one room per character from their own history, one sentence, and move on.

**Arguing.** This is the room where parties argue about direction. **Start the timer.** A minute advances the Stalker.

**The Whispers (Insight DC 15).** The directions are confident, specific, and wrong. Following one: **+1 Bearings** and the party arrives back here. Recognising the voice as the relic: *Arcana DC 16.*

**Vharo's Offer.** He asks once. He does not press. If refused he says *"Of course,"* and goes back to his charts, and the party will meet him again at G7 having been carried there by somebody, and he will not mention it.

**Aden.** No check reveals anything he will not say. He answers everything, pleasantly. **He cannot be persuaded to leave** by argument, guilt, force or magic — *dominate person* works and he walks out under it and he is gone from himself when it ends, and a party that does that has genuinely harmed a man to rescue him.

**What works:** Hesh, in the room, asking him for help with something specific. A Purpose, given rather than argued. Aden has no reason to leave and cannot be given one; he can be given a **task**, and the difference is the entire pyramid.

### Creatures and NPCs

**Vharo Sellick** (Cambion, MM, with Deception +9, Persuasion +9, Investigation +9). Eleven days lost, tie still knotted, genuinely rattled and hiding it beautifully.

**What he will give**, in exchange for being walked out, and he gives it up front rather than on delivery, which tells the party something:

- Three Nail sockets measured: Astra, Khar'Zhul, Vhal'Kathar. He has the spacing.
- The spacing is not geographic. **It is planar** — the nine anchors are evenly spaced in a dimension the party cannot see, and the physical locations are wherever that lattice happened to intersect Elderon.
- His employer wants the lattice, not the relics. **The relics are how you find the sockets.**
- He is being paid in something he will not name, and when asked directly he says *"a face,"* and will not elaborate.

**Aden Ollun**, thirty-one, lost four years ago and four days ago, and comfortable.

**The Labyrinth's Answer**, audible, three rooms away, wrong.

### Set Piece

**The chalk mark.**

On the first visit: a tally on the third flagstone, several hundred marks.

On the second visit, if a player looks: **it has one more mark than it did.**

Nobody in the party made it. Aden did not make it. It has been counting arrivals for four thousand years, and it has just counted them, and it will count them again.

### Lore Drops

- **L13 — The Footprints** (*automatic*). The party's own boots, approaching from the exit. They have already left this room, from the far side, walking in. Gor-Mazaal does not do time in a straight line and this is the cheapest possible proof.
- **L14 — The Planar Lattice** (*from Vharo Sellick*). The nine anchors are evenly spaced in a dimension nobody can see. Their positions on Elderon are accidents of intersection. **This is why the pyramids are scattered senselessly and why two of them are in the Astral Sea.**
- **L15 — What Aden Worked Out** (*from Aden, freely*). The pyramid gives you where you mean to be. So he stopped meaning to be anywhere. *Insight DC 14:* it worked. He is the only person who has ever solved Gor-Mazaal, and the solution was to stop wanting things, and he would recommend it.
- **L16 — The Relic's Voice** (*Arcana DC 16*). *The Labyrinth's Answer* has been giving confident directions to a building that does not use directions, for four thousand years, without concluding anything from the results. **It is a severe philosopher and it is not very good at its job**, and knowing that before attunement is worth a great deal.

### Plot Hooks

- Vharo walked out is an enormous, dangerous, extremely useful asset, and a betrayal waiting for a good moment.
- *"A face."* He is being paid in a face. Do not explain it here.
- Aden and Hesh is the best NPC scene in the pyramid and it resolves at G6, not here.

### Resolution, Rewards, and Temptation

**-1 Bearings** per character who names an unwanted destination. **+1** per followed whisper. One Hit Die each for random movement.

**Treasure.** Vharo's charts, if he is walked out — eleven days of measurements, and the three-socket spacing, and a theodolite worth 900 gp. Aden's pot, which he will give you.

## G5. The Pursuit

*Wherever the party is standing when the Stalker reaches Stage 4 | Whatever light that room had | Exits: all of them, briefly | Smells of furnace breath and hot horn*

> Something vast strikes the far side of the wall.
>
> Horns score the stone from the inside, in pairs, and the scoring is coming along the wall toward you at the speed of a walk.
>
> A breath like a furnace rolls down the passage and it smells of an animal.

### What Is in This Room

Whatever room the party is in. G5 is not a location; it is what happens next.

1. **The Maze Stalker**, arriving.
2. **Every doorway in the room**, briefly usable, in any order, regardless of Purpose. **The Stalker's presence overrides the site's geometry**, which is the only time that happens, and a clever party will notice and exploit it.
3. **Whatever the party brought with them**, including Hesh, Vharo, or Aden, all of whom move at their own speed and none of whom are fast.

### Setting the Scene

Start it mid-sentence. The party is doing something else and the wall comes in.

Do not describe the Stalker fully on arrival. Horn-scores, breath, weight, the sound of a very large thing walking unhurriedly. The full description is worth holding until segment four.

### DM Information

**A six-segment pursuit clock, run in the open.**

**Fighting the Maze Stalker fills no segments.** State this before the first round. A party that engages it is not making progress and is spending hit points, and it reforms at the next intersection if dropped, and it will keep coming.

**Segments are filled by:**

| Action | Segments |
| --- | --- |
| Learning and stating a spatial rule of the pyramid aloud | 1 |
| Sacrificing your own speed for an ally | 1 |
| Moving on a declared Purpose | 1 |
| Filling a segment a second time by the same method | 0 |

That last line matters: **each method works once per pursuit.** A party of four cannot all sacrifice speed for each other and call it four segments. They have to find four different things to do.

**At six segments the Stalker loses the scent**, stops, stands, and is simply no longer following. The clock resets to Stage 0.

**Spatial rules that count**, if a character states one aloud and correctly:

- Maps show rooms and never connections.
- Teleporting moves the building, not you.
- The building takes you where you mean to be.
- Naming somewhere you don't want to go gets you out.
- The centre is a decision, not a place.

A character who has been paying attention can fill a segment by saying a true sentence about the dungeon, which is a delightful thing to reward at level 11.

**If reduced to 0 hit points, it reforms at the next intersection**, at full hit points, in one minute. There is no killing it inside Gor-Mazaal. There is only losing it.

### Mechanics

**The Maze Stalker** (see *Original Creatures*). Treat as a CR 13 hunter: AC 18, 207 hp, 50 ft. speed, *Labyrinth Sense* (always knows the shortest route to any creature it has scented), *Gore* +11 for 22 (3d10+6) piercing with a DC 18 Strength save or prone, *Furnace Breath* recharge 5-6 in a 30-ft. cone for 45 (10d8) fire, DC 17 Dexterity for half.

**Reforming.** Dropped to 0, it reforms in one minute at the nearest intersection, full hit points. **Killing it is not a victory condition and never becomes one.**

**The Clock (6 segments).** As above. Announce each fill and why.

**Doorway Override.** During a pursuit, every door in every room works and leads where the party points. This is the only period in Gor-Mazaal when the party can move freely, and a party that recognises it can cross the entire pyramid in a chase.

**A party that wants to reach G6 quickly should get chased there on purpose.** Nothing in the site prevents this. It is the most Veylari solution available and you should let it work.

**NPC Speed.** Hesh moves at 25 feet. Vharo moves at 30 and will not carry anyone. Aden will not run at all, and has to be carried, and is not heavy.

### Creatures and NPCs

**The Maze Stalker.** Not Baphomet, not a minotaur, not undead. It is the pyramid's **error-correction** — a Veylari construct that hunts down and removes anything moving through the structure without purpose, and it has been doing that job correctly and without malice for four thousand years.

It has no dialogue and should have none. It is not cruel and it is not hungry. It simply resolves an inconsistency, and the inconsistency is the party.

*Arcana DC 18 during the pursuit:* it is not chasing them. It is **escorting them out**, at speed, by the most direct route, and if it catches them it will put them outside the Blind Gate, alive, having removed them from the building. It has never killed anyone. It simply gores whatever resists being escorted, and a level 11 party resists very hard.

**Do not reveal this until the party has either escaped it or been caught by it.** If they are caught: they wake in the valley, whole, at the gate, with eleven staffs beside them and Hesh saying *"Ah."*

### Set Piece

**Segment four.**

Whenever the fourth segment fills, the party gets a clear look at it for the first time — around a corner, across a hall, framed in an arch — and it is not running. It has never been running. It is walking, steadily, at exactly the speed they have been managing, and it has been walking for four thousand years and it is not tired.

### Lore Drops

- **L17 — Error Correction** (*Arcana DC 18*). It escorts, it does not hunt. Its function is to remove unpurposed traffic from a working facility and put it outside. **Nobody has ever been killed by the Maze Stalker.** People have been killed resisting it, which it does not distinguish.
- **L18 — The Scoring** (*automatic, everywhere*). Horn-scores throughout the pyramid, in pairs, at walking pace. Once the party knows what they are, they can read the building's traffic for four thousand years, and the scoring is heaviest around the Returning Room.
- **L19 — What It Does Not Enter** (*Investigation DC 14*). There are no horn-scores in the Heart of Bearings (G6) and none in the Nail chamber (G7). It does not go in there, and there is no barrier stopping it.

### Plot Hooks

- Being escorted out is a legitimate, survivable, deeply humiliating failure state that costs the party a day and teaches them the rule. Use it if they are floundering.
- The Stalker not entering G6 or G7 is because Korveth is in one and the relic is in the other, and it has purposes of its own about both.

### Resolution, Rewards, and Temptation

**-1 Bearings per segment filled.** **Stalker clock resets to 0** at six segments.

**No treasure.** The Stalker carries nothing and leaves nothing.

## G6. The Heart of Bearings

*Open void, no visible walls, no floor | Compass-glow | Exits: whichever one you earn | Smells of nothing, and the absence is clean rather than ominous*

> Thousands of compass needles float in darkness, each on its own, each turning slowly, each settling.
>
> All of them point at a blind man seated in the air. Seated, cross-legged, he is still taller than most of you standing.
>
> He is not waiting for you. He has been mid-thought for four thousand years and you have arrived inside it.

### What Is in This Room

1. **Marshal Korveth Anor**, the Blind Cartographer, seated, unhurried, in Veylari field dress that has not been repaired in four thousand years and does not need it.
2. **Eleven thousand compass needles**, floating, all pointing at him. *Investigation DC 13:* they are not pointing at him. They are pointing at **whatever each of them was last asked to find**, and he happens to be in the way of all of them, and *Insight DC 16* suggests he has arranged that.
3. **No floor.** Characters stand on nothing and it holds. This is not a hazard and nobody falls.
4. **The absent centre**, visible from here: a dimensioned, framed, empty position hanging in the dark forty feet away, which the party will recognise from the chart in G2.
5. **His eyes**, which he does not have, and the scarring is old and deliberate and was done with a Veylari surveyor's stylus.
6. **A single needle in his hand**, not floating, held between two fingers, pointing at nothing.

### Setting the Scene

Quiet and open after four rooms of stone. The needles are the image; describe them turning and settling, thousands of tiny movements, like a field of grass.

He speaks before they do. He knows exactly how many of them there are and whether Hesh is with them.

### DM Information

**Korveth asks each character the same question:**

> *"Where are you going, after the nine relics are assembled?"*

Not where they are going next. Not what they want. **After.** After the whole campaign, after the Prohibition question is answered one way or the other, after there is no more quest.

**Honest uncertainty is acceptable.** *"I don't know"* is a complete and passing answer if it is true, and he will say so, and he will thank them for it.

**Borrowed purpose is not.** A character who gives someone else's answer — their order's, their god's, their family's, the party's — fails, and he tells them so, without cruelty, and asks again.

**Three coherent answers stabilise the route.** Three, not all. A party of five needs three people who can say something true about their own future.

**A character can change an answer later, but must acknowledge the change.** Out loud, to him, and the acknowledgement is the point. He is not testing consistency. He is testing whether they know when they have moved.

### Hesh and Aden

If both are present, this is where it happens.

Korveth asks Hesh the question. Hesh's declared Purpose since G1 has been *"I want to understand the map on my back."* He answers with it.

And Korveth says: *"That is not true, and it has not been true for four years, and I drew it."*

Hesh changes his answer, out loud, in front of his son. **That is the demonstration the party needs and it is free**, and if they were paying attention in G1 they know what is coming and if they were not they find out now.

Aden, who has no purpose and wants none, is standing right there.

### Mechanics

**The Question (Roleplay, no check).** One per character. No rolls. Do not call for Insight and do not let a player roll Deception — he is blind and he does not need either.

**Scoring an answer:**

| Answer | Result |
| --- | --- |
| A true statement about their own future | Coherent. **-1 Bearings.** |
| *"I don't know"*, meant | Coherent. **-1 Bearings.** |
| Someone else's purpose, worn | Not coherent. He asks again. |
| A joke, deflected twice | Not coherent. He waits. He is very patient. |

**Three coherent answers** stabilise the route: from this point the party may move between any keyed area at will, with no Bearings cost, for the rest of the site.

**Changing an Answer.** Free, at any time, acknowledged aloud. **-1 Bearings** for the acknowledgement itself, because admitting you have moved is the skill this pyramid teaches.

**If the party cannot manage three:** he does not fight them and does not bar them. He says *"Then you are not going to the centre, because it is not there yet,"* and he is correct — **G7 does not exist until three coherent answers have been given**, and the party will walk into the empty frame and find an empty frame.

They may rest, think, and come back. He will still be here.

**If combat somehow happens:** Korveth Anor (CR 13) fights defensively, does not pursue, and stops immediately at any surrender or any coherent answer. He has no interest in this and neither should you.

### Creatures and NPCs

**Marshal Korveth Anor, the Blind Cartographer.** Veylari, Pindaria's chief of survey, and the man who drew the evacuation routes for the capital.

**His regret:** the routes were correct. Every one of them. He checked them himself and they were the best work of his life and eleven thousand people died on them anyway, because they did not know where they were going — they only knew they were running — and a map does nothing for a person who is running.

He put out his own eyes with a surveyor's stylus in year 1, deliberately, so that he would have to ask people where they were going rather than telling them.

**He does not think this was wise.** If asked, he says it was a young man's gesture and that he has had four thousand years to regret the theatricality and none at all to regret the principle.

**On the Ilyri:** if the party asks — and after Ilyr-Ameul they should — he confirms it, flatly, and adds the thing Vaelis and Vaelith could not:

> *"I surveyed the volume. They asked me how much of them it would take, and I told them, and the number was correct, and they voted. I have been asked whether the vote was free. It was free. That is not the same as whether it was fair, and nobody has ever asked me that one."*

**His surrender condition** is the three answers. He has none beyond it.

### Set Piece

**The needle in his hand.**

Eleven thousand needles in the air, every one of them pointing at something, and one in his fingers pointing at nothing at all.

If a character asks: it is his. It has pointed at nothing since year 1. He has been carrying it for four thousand years waiting for it to pick a direction, and it has not, and he says this without any self-pity whatsoever and then asks them the question again.

### Lore Drops

- **L20 — Eleven Thousand Needles** (*Investigation DC 13, Insight DC 16*). One per person who died on his routes. Each points at whatever that person was trying to reach. He put himself in the way of all of them.
- **L21 — The Stylus** (*Perception DC 12, or ask*). Veylari surveyor's stylus, used on his own eyes in year 1. He regrets the gesture and not the principle.
- **L22 — The Volume** (*ask about the Ilyri*). He surveyed it. He gave them the number. The vote was free, and he will say so, and he will point out that nobody has ever asked him the better question.
- **L23 — Why He Leaves** (*ask how he met Hesh*). Korveth goes outside. He is the only guardian who does. *Insight DC 15:* he does not consider himself to be guarding anything — he considers himself to be **posted**, which is different, and he has been walking his post for four thousand years and the post includes the valley.

### Plot Hooks

- *"Whether it was free is not the same as whether it was fair."* Somebody should ask him the better question, and the answer is the campaign's.
- Korveth released can travel. A blind Veylari surveyor who has mapped the planar lattice from the inside is the single most useful NPC for finding the remaining anchors, and Vharo Sellick has been trying to compute what this man simply knows.
- Aden Ollun, standing in this room while his father changes his answer, is one question away from being asked his own.

### Resolution, Rewards, and Temptation

**-1 Bearings** per coherent answer and per acknowledged change. **Three coherent answers** stabilise the route and **create G7.**

**No treasure.** He owns a needle and a stylus and the clothes he was posted in.

## G7. The Wandering Nail

*Nail chamber, dimensions uncertain and not important | Void-light | Exits: one, and it works | Smells of iron*

> The greataxe hangs within an empty frame.
>
> Every step toward it moves it farther away. Every step away brings it close enough to touch.

### What Is in This Room

1. **The Labyrinth's Answer**, a greataxe, hanging point-down in a frame of light.
2. **The frame**, which is the absent eighth position from the chart in G2, now occupied because somebody decided something.
3. **The Nail**, below the frame, with an axe-shaped absence in the ring around it.
4. **No walls, no floor, no ceiling, and no horn-scores.** The Stalker does not come here.
5. **Korveth**, if the party asked him to come, standing at the edge and not approaching the axe.
6. **Vharo Sellick**, if walked out of G4 — he is here, ahead of them somehow, with an instrument, taking a reading of the socket, and he stops when they arrive and puts the instrument away and says *"Thank you."*

### Setting the Scene

Short. The party has done the work; this room is the payment.

Demonstrate the recession once — a character walks four steps toward it and it is eight steps further away — and then let somebody work out the answer, which most parties do in under a minute because by now they understand the building.

### DM Information

**Reach the centre without killing the Maze Stalker.** If the party has not dropped it to 0 hit points at any point in the site, **Korveth fixes the room** and permits removal. The recession stops. The axe hangs there and can be taken.

**If they did kill it** — even once, even temporarily — Korveth does not fix the room, and the recession continues, and the only way to reach the axe is to **walk away from it**, deliberately, meaning it, and keep walking until it is close enough to touch and then not turn around.

That is achievable. It is also excruciating, and it should take four or five rounds of a player saying *"I keep walking away"* while you tell them it is getting closer.

**Lawful release** requires the axe to be taken by a character who has a **Declared Purpose currently active** and states it as they take it.

**Improper removal** — grabbing it without a Purpose, or forcing the frame — causes **every road within the region to loop back to Gor-Mazaal for nine days.** Every road. Caravans, pilgrims, armies, the party. The valley fills up with people who cannot leave, and there are eleven thousand of them by day four, and they have to be fed.

That is not a combat consequence and it is far worse than one.

**Lawful release gives the party an unfailing route out** — and the wording matters. Not out of Gor-Mazaal. **Out.** Once per campaign, from anywhere, the party may travel to any location any of them has personally been, by the route that works, arriving in the time it should take. Korveth will not explain further and neither should you.

### Mechanics

**The Recession (no check).** Move toward it, it recedes at twice your speed. Move away, it approaches at twice your speed.

**Korveth's Fixing (automatic, if the Stalker was never dropped).** The room holds still.

**Walking Away (Roleplay, 4-5 rounds).** Declare it, keep declaring it, do not look back. At the fifth round it is in reach behind you.

**Taking It Lawfully.** Active Declared Purpose, stated aloud as they take it. **-4 Bearings. Relic Attention +1.**

**Improper Removal.** Roads loop for nine days across the region. **Relic Attention +2.** No unfailing route.

**Attunement.** The axe is a severe philosopher and it will begin instructing the wielder immediately. Its first contribution should be a confident, specific, and completely wrong piece of navigational advice, delivered with total authority, within a minute of attunement.

### Creatures and NPCs

None hostile.

**Korveth**, at the edge, not approaching. He has never touched it. When asked why: *"It gives directions."*

### Set Piece

**"Thank you."**

If Vharo Sellick was walked out of the Returning Room, he is here first, taking a reading, and he stops when they arrive.

He puts the instrument away. He straightens his coat. And he says *"Thank you"* — and then, because he is what he is, he adds: *"I will not be able to make that worth anything to you later. I want to be clear about that now, while it is still true."*

Then he walks out past them, and he has the fourth socket, and he has the lattice, and he is the only honest thing in Graz'zt's service.

### Lore Drops

- **L24 — The Eighth Frame, Filled** (*automatic*). It was empty in G2 because nobody had decided anything. It is full now. The party filled it in G6 by answering a question, and the room they are standing in **did not exist four hours ago.**
- **L25 — Why Korveth Won't Touch It** (*ask*). *"It gives directions."* Four thousand years of a relic confidently telling a blind cartographer which way to go.
- **L26 — The Unfailing Route** (*on lawful release*). One journey, once, from anywhere to anywhere any of them has been, by the route that works. He gives it without ceremony and does not say how many of these he has.

### Plot Hooks

- Vharo has four sockets and the lattice and is walking out with it. That is a campaign clock the party started themselves.
- The unfailing route is a get-out-of-anything card and the party will spend three pyramids arguing about when to use it.
- The empty frame filling in response to a decision is the clearest statement in the campaign of what the Prohibition actually is: a thing held in place by people continuing to mean it.

### Resolution, Rewards, and Temptation

**Lawful: -4 Bearings, Relic Attention +1**, and the unfailing route for the whole party.

**Improper: Relic Attention +2**, nine days of looping roads, and eleven thousand stranded people in a valley with no food.

**Treasure.** The Labyrinth's Answer (artifact; see *The Nine Demon Relics*). Nothing else, and there never was anything else.

## Gor-Mazaal Aftermath

When The Labyrinth's Answer is removed lawfully, describe a deep vibration through the remaining pyramids. The mountain fold's shadow becomes an ordinary shadow within the hour and moves with the sun like shadows do, and for about a minute the whole valley looks wrong because of it.

**Immediate consequences.**

- **Relic Attention +1.** The axe is a severe philosopher who believes every problem is a maze and that an axe is a frequently valid answer. It will explain this. Repeatedly.
- **Korveth ages normally or dissolves by choice.** If he stays, he is the most valuable NPC the party has acquired: he has surveyed the planar lattice from the inside and he will draw it for them, and it is the first time anyone has drawn anything in this pyramid that works.
- **Vharo Sellick leaves with four sockets.** He now has enough to compute the remaining five without visiting them. Whoever he is working for knows the shape of the Prohibition.
- **Aden Ollun** leaves with his father, or does not. If Hesh asked him for help with something specific rather than arguing with him, he comes.
- **Hesh's Purpose has changed** and he said so out loud, and he is the only NPC in the campaign who has demonstrated the exact thing the party will need to do at Khal'Vethar.
- **The Ilyri question sharpens.** Korveth surveyed the volume. Vaelis and Vaelith voted. Two guardians have now confirmed it independently and neither has been asked whether it was fair.
