# Astra: Pyramid of the Sun - Site Guide

*Levels 6-7 | Children of the Sun | Desire into Sacrifice | Relic: The Velvet Ruin | Guardian: High Priestess Seryth Vael, the Last Dawn*

Buried beneath Picea Pindar, once the sacred and political heart of Astra.

## Running Astra

Astra is a treasury that pays out honestly and charges later. Nothing here lies about what it is. The crowns are real crowns, the spellbooks hold real spells, the reflections show real people. What the pyramid withholds is the invoice, and the invoice arrives in the last room wearing white-and-gold armour.

Run the site as a moral ledger rather than a dungeon crawl. The party will not fail Astra by being weak; they will fail it by being acquisitive and then surprised. Your job for the first six rooms is to make the ledger visible — the plaques with their names, the tether-light on stolen goods, the way Sun Guards look at full packs — so that the seventh room is a reckoning rather than an ambush.

### Pindarian Scale

**Pindarians are Large.** All of them, always — the Solaari, the Lunari, the Veylari, the Ilyri, every
Saharim guardian and every soldier in every rank. Eight to ten feet, long-boned, and long-lived, with a
reach and a stride to match: **10-foot reach on their melee attacks and a 10-foot space.**

This is not a variant or a subtype. It is what the species is, and it is worth showing rather than stating —
Pindarian doorways are twelve feet high, their stairs have deep treads, their chairs do not fit anyone else,
and a party of Medium adventurers spends nine pyramids using furniture built for people half again their
size.

Human, elven and dwarven NPCs met in and around the sites are Medium as usual. The contrast is the point.

### The Temptation Track

Astra runs on a single visible counter. Keep it on the table where players can see it, as tally marks or coins. Concealing it turns the finale into a gotcha.

| Event | Temptation |
| --- | --- |
| Removing an unsecured treasure from its place | +1 each |
| Accepting an offer at one of the Six Doors (A4) | +2 |
| Sitting the Empty Throne (A6) | +2 |
| Breaking a mirror or a reflective surface | +1 |
| Returning a taken item to its original place | -1 each |
| Freely surrendering a genuinely valued possession | -2, and it cannot be reclaimed inside Astra |
| Refusing an offer that was tailored to a character | -1 |

**At 3.** Astra begins matching. A Wantglass Double forms in the nearest reflective surface, copying the character who has taken the most, and shadows the party at a distance of one room. It does not attack yet. It watches, and it copies their gait.

**At 6.** The suspicion becomes policy. Two Pindarian Sun Guards take up patrol between A2 and A5, and every mirrored surface in the pyramid begins answering questions in the voice of someone the party failed.

**At 9.** Noon without mercy. Light in every keyed area becomes hostile: a creature carrying Astran treasure that ends its turn in bright light takes 5 radiant damage. Seryth fights with her full Judgment suite from round one and does not open with conversation.

**In the final encounter,** each remaining Temptation point converts to either 5 temporary hit points for Seryth or one active solar hazard on the Court floor (see A7). Choose per point as you go; a mix reads better than either extreme.

### Two Lasting Marks

**Solar Seal.** A gold sun-mark opens on the palm of a character who renounces something honestly. A sealed character sheds dim light in a 5-foot radius, has advantage on saving throws against being charmed while inside any of the Nine Pyramids, and may convert one failed check at the Solar Engine (A5) into a success. Seryth reads seals on sight and speaks to sealed characters as equals. Seals are permanent and travel to the other eight pyramids.

**Relic Scar.** A hairline crack of gold light somewhere on the body, cold to the touch. The pyramid network now knows that character's deepest want and files it where the relics can read it. The Velvet Ruin may speak that desire aloud at any dramatically ruinous moment, and the character has disadvantage on saves against the blade's *Kiss of the Dark Prince*. At Relic Attention 6, a scarred character is the relic's preferred mouthpiece.

### Pacing and Shape

Astra is a vertical stack, not a maze. A1 descends, A2 through A4 form the broad public tier, A5 and A6 sit in the mechanical heart, and A7 is the crown chamber directly beneath the buried apex. Players can retreat upward at any time; the pyramid never blocks an exit, because leaving with full pockets is one of the outcomes it is testing for.

Two to four sessions. A natural break falls after A4, and the Buried Meridian (A1) is the only safe rest site — resting anywhere else triggers a temptation dream (see A1, Mechanics).

### Safety at This Table

Astra deals in grief, failure, and personal longing, and A3 in particular asks players to speak to people their characters could not save. Confirm lines and veils before the session and offer the pause tool aloud when the Mirrored Hospice comes into view. A player may always have their character stay silent at a mirror; silence is a valid answer, and the room is written so that it costs nothing mechanically.

## A1. The Buried Meridian

*Descending stair, 200 ft. run | Bright light, sourceless | Exits: surface sinkhole (up), hingeless door (down) | Dry air, warm stone, faint smell of hot brass*

> A stair of white stone descends beneath the ruined city. No flame burns here, yet every edge casts a hard noon shadow. A gold line runs down the centre of the steps and disappears beneath a door without hinges. The air is the temperature of a summer afternoon and perfectly still, and the dust on the treads is undisturbed except for one set of footprints going down.

### What Is in This Room

1. **The gold meridian line.** A hand's breadth of inlaid gold running the length of the stair. Warm. It hums at the edge of hearing when a creature stands with a foot on either side. *Arcana DC 13:* it is a measuring instrument, not a decoration — a planar plumb line that tells the pyramid which way is true noon.
2. **Sixty-four treads,** each carved on its riser with a single Pindarian word. Read in descent they form a litany: *want, name, hold, weigh, keep, release.* The sequence repeats ten times and then stops four steps short of the door. *Religion DC 12* to translate; *History DC 15* to recognise the cadence as a Pindarian confession rite.
3. **The hingeless door.** Seamless white stone, three inches proud of the wall, with a shallow depression at chest height shaped like an open palm.
4. **Shadows that disagree.** Every object casts a hard shadow as if under a noon sun, but the shadows point toward the door rather than away from any light source. *Perception DC 10* to notice; *Investigation DC 15* to determine the shadows point at the Planar Nail seven levels below.
5. **One set of footprints.** Boot prints in the dust, descending, not returning. *Survival DC 12:* a heavy human, moving fast, eight days old. These belong to Dressel Kaine, a treasure hunter now in A2 (see that area).
6. **A dropped waterskin** on tread forty-one, half full, stoppered, with a sun-guard sigil crudely scratched out. *Investigation DC 10* to spot. The scratched sigil is the first clue that someone hired professionals who knew what this place was.

### Setting the Scene

Lead with the wrongness of the light. There is no torch, no glowing crystal, no visible sun, and yet the players' characters cast noon shadows. Describe a hand held up and the shadow beneath it as sharp as a blade. Then let them notice the shadows are all wrong-way-round.

Keep your voice calm here. Astra is not menacing; it is hospitable in the way of a very old institution that has processed this exact request ten thousand times. The stair is comfortable. That is the unsettling part.

### DM Information

The Buried Meridian is the pyramid's intake interview. The hingeless door opens for any creature that places a palm in the depression and names aloud something it wants. The statement must be honest but need not be profound — *I want a hot meal* opens the door as readily as *I want my brother to forgive me.* Astra is testing whether a creature can admit to wanting anything at all.

**Refusal is permitted.** A character may decline to speak. The door simply does not open for them, and they may be admitted by walking through after a companion who did speak. Astra records who spoke and who did not, and Seryth will know.

**Lying is not permitted.** The pyramid reads intent, not phrasing. A deliberate falsehood causes a wantglass double to step out of the door's surface, wearing the liar's face and speaking the desire they concealed.

This is also the safe harbour. Astra will not attack a party that retreats to the Meridian, and the stair is the only place in the pyramid where a long rest passes without incident.

### Mechanics

**The Palm Door (Puzzle).** No check is required to open the door honestly. A character who attempts a lie makes a **DC 15 Charisma (Deception) check contested by the pyramid's passive Insight of 20** — in practice, a failure unless the player has established a strong reason otherwise. On a failure, a Wantglass Double (see *Bestiary*, CR 4) forms at the door and immediately uses *Perfect Offer* on its original. It fights until reduced to half hit points, then offers to leave if the character will say the true thing aloud.

**Temptation Dreams (Hazard).** A long rest taken anywhere in Astra other than A1 provokes one dream per character. Each dreaming character makes a **DC 13 Wisdom saving throw.** On a failure they gain no benefit from the rest and wake holding one small treasure from A2 that they do not remember taking — **+1 Temptation** apiece. On a success, they dream truthfully of their own want and gain no ill effect. Announce the rule when the party first proposes resting elsewhere; this is a warning, not a trap.

**Retreat.** The sinkhole above remains open and unguarded for the whole adventure. Astra never seals its exits.

### Creatures and NPCs

None in ordinary circumstances. One **Wantglass Double** (CR 4) if a character lies at the door. If the party has already reached Temptation 3 and returns to A1, its shadowing Double waits at the top of the stair rather than entering — the Meridian is neutral ground, and even Astra's constructs respect it.

### Set Piece

The first honest answer. When one character places a palm on the stone and says a true thing aloud, the gold meridian line brightens from the door upward, running past their feet like a fuse burning backwards, and the door sinks into the floor without sound. Give the speaking player Inspiration in the open, at the table, and say why. It establishes the currency of the entire pyramid in one gesture.

### Lore Drops

- **L1 — The Confession Rite** (*Religion DC 12 on the treads*). Pindarian priests descended this stair reciting *want, name, hold, weigh, keep, release.* Six verbs, and the litany stops four steps short of the door. The last four steps were walked in silence: the release was supposed to be wordless.
- **L2 — The Plumb Line** (*Arcana DC 13 on the meridian*). The gold line does not point north. It points at the Planar Nail, and it is an instrument for confirming the Nail has not shifted. Someone checked this daily for four thousand years.
- **L3 — The Scratched Sigil** (*Investigation DC 10 on the waterskin*). A Sun Guard sigil defaced by hand. Whoever came down here eight days ago was equipped by someone who had Pindarian gear to spare, and wanted it unrecognisable.
- **L4 — Freely given, to any character who asks the door a question instead of answering it.** The depression warms and a woman's voice, patient and tired, says: *"You are the fourth party this season. The others are still inside. Some of them are still walking."*

### Plot Hooks

- Dressel Kaine's footprints give the party a reason to move quickly, and a living NPC to rescue, recruit, or fail in A2.
- The defaced sigil points at Graz'zt's agents supplying expeditions with authentic Pindarian equipment — equipment that had to come from somewhere, which is to say from a pyramid that has already been opened.
- A party that retreats to the Meridian to rest will find, on their second visit, that one of the sixty-four treads has a new word carved on it: their own name, in Pindarian. Astra is keeping minutes.

### Resolution, Rewards, and Temptation

A character who admits a painful desire aloud gains **Inspiration.** No Temptation is gained or lost in A1 under normal play; a lie at the door and the resulting Double are their own punishment. The waterskin is worth nothing and matters enormously.

## A2. Hall of Abundance

*Gallery, 120 ft. by 60 ft., 30-ft. ceiling | Bright light, warm and golden | Exits: stair from A1 (up), three archways (to A3, A4, and a sealed passage to A5) | Smells of oiled leather, clean metal, and cut flowers that are not present*

> Weapons, crowns, spellbooks, and jewelled instruments stand unattended beneath warm light. None are dusty. Each rests on its own low plinth of white stone, and each bears a small brass plaque engraved with your name — not your titles, not your deeds. Your name, spelled correctly, in your own hand.

### What Is in This Room

1. **Ninety-one plinths** in nine rows, most empty. The occupied ones hold, at minimum, one item tailored to each player character. Build these from the sheet: the paladin's plinth holds their dead mentor's sword, repaired; the wizard's holds the spellbook their master would not let them copy. Make them specific and make them correct.
2. **The plaques.** Brass, hand-sized, engraved in the finder's own handwriting. *Investigation DC 12:* the engraving is not carved but grown, the metal displaced from within. *Arcana DC 15:* the plaques are addressing labels. These items were prepared for these people, which is impossible, and the party should be told it is impossible.
3. **Tether-light.** Every unsecured treasure trails a hair-thin filament of gold light back to its plinth, visible only in peripheral vision. *Perception DC 14* passively, or automatically with *detect magic.* **Arcana DC 15** reveals the tethers are planar bindings: the object is genuinely the party's, and genuinely Astra's, and the pyramid does not consider these contradictory.
4. **Six secured displays** behind sun-glass: a crown of the Astran royal house, a glaive of the Sun Guard, a reliquary of noon-water, a ledger, a child's wooden horse, and an empty case labelled *The Velvet Ruin.* These cannot be removed and are not tempting; they are the museum, not the shop.
5. **The ledger** (secured display four) is open, and legible through the glass. It lists every item removed from this hall, by whom, and what it cost them. The most recent entries are eight days old, in a fast ugly hand.
6. **Dressel Kaine**, a treasure hunter, alive, sitting on the floor against a plinth in the far corner with a pack full of Astran gold and both hands over her ears. See *Creatures and NPCs.*
7. **Cut-flower scent with no flowers.** The smell intensifies near whichever plinth holds the item a given character wants most. It is the pyramid being helpful.
8. **A single empty plinth, plaque blank,** at the head of the ninth row. Its plaque grows a name the moment a character takes a second item from this hall. Whose name depends on who took it.

### Setting the Scene

Do not rush this room. Go character by character, in private if the table can sustain it, and describe one item per person as though you were describing a person. The paladin does not see *a longsword, +1*; they see the nick in the crossguard from the day they were knighted, and the notch someone filed to cover it.

Then, after everyone has had their moment, read the plaques aloud. Their names. Spelled right. In their handwriting. Let the silence sit.

The light here is the warmest in the pyramid. Astra is not trying to frighten anyone. It is trying to be generous, and the generosity is the mechanism.

### DM Information

Everything in this hall is genuine. The magic items function, the gold spends, the spellbook's spells can be copied. Astra does not deal in illusions, because an illusion would not test anything. What Astra measures is whether a creature can stand in front of a room that has correctly identified its heart's desire and walk out empty-handed.

**Taking an item adds 1 Temptation each and permanently binds the taker's name to the ledger.** Returning it removes the point. Astra keeps no grudge about returns — the point is restraint, and restraint arrived at late still counts.

The tethers are the fair warning. Make sure the party can see them before they take anything. If nobody makes the Perception check, have the tether-light catch on the edge of a character's vision as they turn away, and describe it unprompted. A hidden cost is a cheat; a visible cost that is easy to rationalise is the whole design.

**The sealed passage to A5** opens only after the party has visited A3 or A4. Astra does not let anyone near the Solar Engine until it has taken their measure twice.

### Mechanics

**The Tethers (Investigation).** *Perception DC 14* to notice the filaments. *Arcana DC 15* to understand them: the item is planar-bound to Astra, will function normally anywhere, and will announce its origin to Seryth Vael, to every Saharim in the network, and eventually to the Velvet Ruin.

**Severing a Tether (Trap).** A character may attempt to cut a tether with *dispel magic* (DC 16 ability check) or a successful **DC 18 Intelligence (Arcana)** check using thieves' tools and an hour of work. Success frees the item cleanly and adds no Temptation. Failure does not stop the theft, but discharges the binding into the thief: **11 (2d10) radiant damage, no save,** and the character gains a **Relic Scar.** The pyramid now knows exactly what they wanted enough to steal for.

Allow this. A party that solves the hall with skill rather than virtue has done something clever and should be rewarded — with the item, and with the scar, and with Seryth's undivided attention in A7.

**The Ninth Plinth (Omen).** When any character takes a second item, the blank plaque at the head of row nine grows that character's name. Nothing else happens. Nothing needs to.

**Alarm.** Removing an item from a *secured* display is impossible without shattering sun-glass (AC 17, 20 hp, immune to psychic and poison). Breaking it summons **two Pindarian Sun Guards** (CR 5) in 1d4 rounds and sets Temptation immediately to 6 if it is lower.

### Creatures and NPCs

**Dressel Kaine, treasure hunter** (use **Scout**, MM, with Deception +4). Human, forties, competent, eight days without sleep. Her crew of four came down the stair on a map supplied by a broker in Picea Pindar. Three of them took from this hall. Two are now in A3 talking to mirrors and will not leave; one walked into the Six Doors and did not come out. Dressel took a handful of gold on the first day and has spent a week trying to put it back — but she cannot remember which plinth it came from, and Astra will not accept it anywhere else.

Dressel's function is to be the party's forecast. She has the ledger read, she knows the tethers are real, and she will tell them so for the cost of being taken seriously. She wants two things, in this order: her people out, and someone to tell her the gold does not count if she gives it away. It does count, and it counts double if she lies about it.

**Roleplaying her.** Brusque, exhausted, allergic to sympathy. She speaks in short flat sentences and flinches at her own name. If the party helps her return her gold — the correct plinth can be found with a **DC 16 Investigation** check and ten minutes — she loses her Temptation and gains a Solar Seal in front of them, which is the cleanest possible demonstration of the pyramid's rules.

**If the party is at Temptation 3 or higher when they enter,** the Wantglass Double copying the greediest character is already here, standing among the plinths, wearing their face and admiring an item on their behalf.

### Set Piece

**The plaque with your name on it.** After the descriptions, after the plaques, let a player reach for their item — and as their fingers close on it, every other plaque in the hall turns, with a sound like a hundred small latches, to face them. Astra has been introduced to the group. It now knows who is going to be a problem.

### Lore Drops

- **L5 — The Ledger** (*read through the sun-glass, Investigation DC 10*). Four thousand years of entries. Every removal is recorded with its price in the same column: *a brother, a name, a hand, forty years, the ability to sleep.* Astra has always charged. It has simply never haggled.
- **L6 — The Grown Plaques** (*Arcana DC 15*). The brass was not engraved; the letters were displaced from inside the metal. Whatever writes them reads people directly and does not require their presence, their consent, or their being alive yet.
- **L7 — The Empty Case** (*automatic, on inspection*). The case labelled *The Velvet Ruin* has a hollow shaped for a greatsword and a second, smaller hollow beside it, shaped for a hand. The blade was displayed here, publicly, as a trophy and a warning — and it was meant to be held by someone standing exactly there.
- **L8 — Dressel's Map** (*from Dressel, freely, if she trusts them*). The map is accurate through A6 and then simply stops, with the final chamber marked *VAULT — UNGUARDED.* Every other detail is correct. Somebody wanted a party of competent people to arrive at Seryth Vael expecting an empty room.
- **L9 — The Broker** (*from Dressel, under persuasion, DC 14*). The map came from a soft-spoken man in Picea Pindar who paid her crew's expenses up front, asked for nothing in return, and had six fingers on his left hand.

### Plot Hooks

- Dressel's two crew members are still alive in A3, standing at the mirrors. Getting them out is a rescue with a ticking clock and no combat.
- The six-fingered broker is a cambion in Graz'zt's service and can be found again in Picea Pindar after the party surfaces, if they want him.
- The Velvet Ruin's display case implies Astra was once a public site — a museum of the thing it was built to contain. Somebody decided that displaying the blade was safer than hiding it, and the party may eventually have to make the same decision.
- A character who takes their tailored item now carries a physical reminder of exactly what they want, into eight more pyramids. That is worth more to the campaign than the +1 bonus.

### Resolution, Rewards, and Temptation

**Restraint is the intended answer.** A party that leaves the Hall of Abundance empty-handed gains nothing mechanical here and everything in A7.

**Informed theft is a legitimate campaign choice.** Let them take what they want, note the Temptation, and pay it off honestly at the Court of the Last Dawn. A party of thieves who are not surprised to be billed is playing the adventure correctly.

**Treasure, if taken.** One tailored item per character, of a rarity appropriate to tier — uncommon at level 6, one rare permitted for the character whose desire is best articulated by their player. Plus 400 gp in loose Astran coin, scattered, easy, and worth exactly 1 Temptation.

**Returning Dressel's gold** costs the party nothing and removes her from the ledger. If they do it, she follows them as far as A6 and no further, and she is the one who tells Seryth what they did.

## A3. The Mirrored Hospice

*Infirmary hall, 80 ft. by 40 ft. | Dim light, silver, sourceless | Exits: archway to A2, low door to A4, service stair to A5 (locked) | Smells of clean linen and the particular cold of a sickroom at four in the morning*

> Six beds face a wall of mirrors. The linen is turned down. Beside each bed stands a small table with a cup of water on it, and the water is fresh. In every reflection, someone you failed is alive, healed, and sitting up, and they have been waiting a long time for you to come in, and they are so pleased to see you.

### What Is in This Room

1. **Six beds,** iron-framed, made up with white linen, turned down as though someone is expected. *Investigation DC 10:* they have never been slept in. The linen is four thousand years old and perfectly clean.
2. **The mirror wall.** Twenty feet of unbroken silvered glass, cold to the touch, showing the room correctly in every particular except for the people in the beds. There are always exactly as many figures as there are living characters in the room.
3. **Six cups of fresh water.** Genuinely fresh, genuinely safe, refilled by the pyramid. A character who drinks gains the benefit of a short rest's worth of comfort — no mechanical effect, but they will feel it, and describing that is worth more than a die roll.
4. **A nurse's ledger** on a stand at the room's head, open, written in three hands. It records the last nine days of the siege: admissions, deaths, and at the end, in the third hand, a list of names with no entries beside them at all. *Religion or History DC 13* to read; *Insight DC 12* to understand that the last scribe stopped recording outcomes because there stopped being any.
5. **Two of Dressel Kaine's crew**, Hallo and Meret, standing barefoot at the mirror wall, hands flat against the glass, in whispered conversation with people who are not there. They have been there eight days. They are not restrained and they are not charmed — they simply do not want to stop.
6. **Scratch marks** on the floor in front of the mirrors, hundreds of them, in arcs the width of a bed being dragged closer. Not recent.
7. **One mirror panel already broken,** at the far end, with the shards swept into a neat pile. Something came out of it, and something tidied up afterwards.

### Setting the Scene

Drop your volume. This is the quietest room in Astra and it should sound like it — no echo, no reverberation, the acoustic deadness of a room full of soft furnishings. The light is silver rather than gold for the first time in the pyramid, and it does not cast the hard noon shadows of the Meridian.

Handle the reflections one character at a time, and ask the player who it is. Do not assign the dead yourself unless the player has nothing on their sheet; then use the most recent NPC death the party caused or failed to prevent. The reflection is warm, unhurried, and entirely without reproach. It is delighted. That is the cruelty.

### DM Information

The Hospice is Astra's second interview, and its subject is not greed but grief — the specific desire to be forgiven by someone who is not available to forgive you.

Each character may ask one reflection **up to three questions.** The reflection answers as the character hopes, not as the dead person would have. It has no knowledge the character does not have; it is assembled entirely from longing. A **DC 16 Wisdom (Insight)** check during the conversation reveals this — the answers are too exactly right, and the dead are never that generous.

**Touching the mirror** begins the pull. **Breaking a mirror** releases a Wantglass Double and adds Temptation.

**Hallo and Meret** are the demonstration. They are what happens after three hundred questions. They can still be saved, easily, by anyone who physically pulls them away from the glass and keeps them away for one minute — but they will fight to return, weeping and apologising the whole time, and a party that has to drag a sobbing stranger out of a room by main force will understand the Hospice in a way no description achieves.

### Mechanics

**Three Questions (Social).** No check to ask. The reflection answers truthfully-as-hoped. *Insight DC 16* at any point reveals the construction. A character who asks a fourth question must make a **DC 15 Wisdom saving throw** or become **charmed for 1 minute,** during which they move toward the mirror and attempt to enter it on each of their turns.

**Entering the Glass (Trap).** A charmed character who reaches the mirror wall is not transported anywhere. They press against cold glass and keep pressing. At the end of each of their turns while charmed and in contact with the mirror, they take **5 (1d10) psychic damage** as the reflection tries to make room. The charm ends early if the character takes damage from another source or if an ally uses an action to physically restrain them (**DC 13 Strength (Athletics)**).

**Breaking a Mirror (Consequence).** Any mirror panel: AC 13, 10 hp, vulnerable to thunder. Breaking one releases a **Wantglass Double** (CR 4) shaped from whoever was standing closest, and adds **+1 Temptation.** The Double formed here is not the sneering copy from A2 — it wears the face of the *dead person* the character was speaking to, and it fights while pleading.

**Saving Hallo and Meret (Skill Challenge).** Removing either from the wall requires a successful **DC 13 Strength (Athletics)** grapple, followed by keeping them away for one minute. Each round they make a **DC 12 Wisdom saving throw** to stop struggling; three successes across the group frees them permanently. Speaking the name of someone *they* failed — learnable from the nurse's ledger with a *History DC 13* — grants advantage on these saves. Killing them is trivially easy and will be remembered by everyone at the table for years.

**Saying Goodbye (Boon).** A character who speaks to a reflection and ends the conversation *without asking for absolution* — no *did I do enough,* no *do you forgive me,* only a farewell — receives a **Solar Seal.** Do not hint at this. If a player does it unprompted, give the seal and describe the mark opening on their palm while the reflection nods and lies back down.

### Creatures and NPCs

**Hallo** (Commoner, MM) is nineteen and was the crew's lamp-carrier. His reflection is his mother, who is alive and well in Picea Pindar. He has been telling her about the job for eight days. He is embarrassed to be rescued and will follow the party afterwards with painful loyalty.

**Meret** (Bandit, MM) is fifty and hard, and her reflection is a daughter who died of a fever eleven years ago. She will not thank anyone for taking her out of this room. She is the more dangerous rescue: if physically restrained she fights in earnest, and she is the correct NPC to place in mortal danger later if you want the party to feel the cost of having saved her.

**One or two Wantglass Doubles** (CR 4) if mirrors are broken. They fight defensively, always positioning to keep a mirror at their back, and they surrender instantly if the character they are copying voluntarily surrenders a possession.

### Set Piece

**The cup of water.** Late in the scene, when the party is at its most raw, have one of the reflections rise from its bed, cross to the small table, pick up the cup of fresh water, and hold it out toward the glass — toward the character who failed them — with an expression of complete, uncomplicated kindness.

The cup is real. On the party's side of the mirror, the identical cup on the identical table is now empty.

### Lore Drops

- **L10 — The Nurse's Ledger** (*History or Religion DC 13*). Nine days of the siege. The first hand records admissions and outcomes. The second records admissions only. The third records names, hundreds of them, with no columns at all — and at the very bottom, in the same hand: *We have stopped losing them. We have started keeping them.* The Saharim binding was invented in this room.
- **L11 — The Scratch Marks** (*Investigation DC 12*). The beds were dragged toward the mirrors, again and again, over years. The dying wanted to be closer to the glass. Somebody kept moving them back.
- **L12 — The Broken Panel** (*Investigation DC 15*). The shards were swept into a pile by hands that cared about tidiness. Sun Guards do not sweep. Something else lives in Astra's routine — a custodian, still performing maintenance on a hospice with no patients. This foreshadows the **Saharim Custodian** encountered at Temptation 6 or in A5.
- **L13 — Seryth's Bed** (*freely, if the party counts*). There are six beds and the mirror shows as many figures as there are living characters — but the fifth bed, and only the fifth, has a depression in the pillow. Seryth Vael sits here. Regularly. She has been coming to this room for four thousand years to speak to someone, and she has never once asked to be forgiven.

### Plot Hooks

- Hallo and Meret rescued give the party two dependents for the surface chapters, a reason to return to Picea Pindar, and two witnesses who will describe the pyramid accurately to anyone who asks — including Graz'zt's broker.
- A character who received a Solar Seal here will be recognised by Seryth in A7 and can open the conversation with her rather than being interrogated by her.
- Whoever Seryth visits in the fifth bed is the emotional key to her surrender. A party that works out the name — her daughter, if you want the cleanest version — can end A7 without initiative being rolled.
- Meret's daughter died of fever in a city with a working temple. There is a question there about why no one healed her, and the answer involves the same rationing that broke General Rhakkar at Khar'Zhul.

### Resolution, Rewards, and Temptation

**Solar Seal** to any character who says goodbye without asking for absolution. **+1 Temptation** per mirror broken. No treasure; the cups are worth nothing and one of them is now empty, which is the only souvenir this room hands out.

A party that leaves both crew members at the wall has made a choice, and Dressel will ask about it, and she will believe whatever they tell her, and that will be worse.

## A4. The Six Doors

*Circular chamber, 50 ft. diameter, domed | Bright light from the dome's oculus | Exits: six named doors (see below), central stair (sealed until resolved), archway back to A2 | Smells of whatever each character most associates with satisfaction*

> Six doors rise around a round chamber, each carved with a single symbol: a coin, a crown, a pair of lips, two applauding hands, a set of joined rings, and a blood-red blade. There is no other exit. Above them, cut into the dome in letters a foot high, in a language you should not be able to read but do:
>
> **CHOOSE WHAT CHOOSES YOU**

### What Is in This Room

1. **Six doors,** each of white stone with its symbol inlaid in gold: **Wealth** (coin), **Power** (crown), **Pleasure** (lips), **Recognition** (applauding hands), **Love** (joined rings), **Revenge** (blood-red blade).
2. **The inscription in the dome,** which every character reads fluently in their first language regardless of what that language is. *Arcana DC 14:* it is not written in any language; the pyramid is placing the meaning directly.
3. **The oculus,** thirty feet up, showing a perfect noon sky that does not match the time of day above ground and never changes.
4. **The floor,** a mosaic of a single enormous open hand, palm up, fingers reaching toward the six doors. The party stands in the palm. *Perception DC 12* to recognise the image from standing height; automatic from the doorway.
5. **A worn track** in the mosaic leading from the entry archway to the Revenge door — one path, walked thousands of times, by feet that always went to the same place. *Investigation DC 13.*
6. **Six small alcoves** between the doors, each holding a stone bowl. Five bowls are empty. The sixth, beside the Love door, holds a wedding ring, a soldier's tag, and a child's milk tooth — three things somebody gave up rather than walk through.
7. **The central stair,** a spiral descending from the exact centre of the palm, currently filled with solid white stone. It opens when the room is resolved.

### Setting the Scene

This is the most theatrical room in Astra and it should be run in private, one player at a time, with the rest of the table taking a short break. If that is impossible, run it in the open but ask each player to answer for themselves and instruct the others not to advise.

Do not narrate a character's desire. **Ask the player the direct question** — *which door does your character walk toward?* — and build the scene from what they say. If a player chooses a door that surprises you, that is the room working correctly.

### DM Information

Each character privately selects one of the six doors. The door opens onto a short, vivid, entirely personal scene in which the thing they want is genuinely offered — and the offer is always real, always achievable, and always costs someone else something.

The template for every door is the same: **you may have this, and the price is paid by a specific named person who is not in the room.** Wealth is funded by a village's tithe. Power requires an ally's demotion. Love requires someone else's marriage to end. Revenge requires the enemy's child to be the one who answers the door.

**Refusal opens the central stair.** The moment every character in the room has declined their offer, the white stone filling the spiral stair drains away like water.

**Acceptance also opens the stair,** eventually, but each acceptance adds **+2 Temptation** and grants the character a genuine, lasting benefit that they will have to explain to Seryth Vael.

**Refusing to choose at all** is its own answer. A character who will not approach any door finds the Recognition door opening for them regardless, because refusing to want anything in public is a performance, and Astra has met that performance before. Run the scene gently; it is the sharpest version of this room.

### Mechanics

**The Offer (Roleplay, no roll).** Do not call for a saving throw against the offer. This room is a decision, not a check, and rolling dice for it removes the thing being tested. Ask the question, present the price, and accept the answer.

**Accepting.** The character receives their benefit immediately and permanently. Use the table below, scaled to tier. Add **+2 Temptation.** The named person who pays the price is real and exists in the campaign from that moment, and you should write their name down.

| Door | Benefit on Acceptance | Who Pays |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Wealth | 2,000 gp in Astran gold, untethered and genuinely theirs | A village whose tithe was reassigned |
| Power | +1 to one ability score, to a maximum of 20 | An ally or rival who is stripped of a rank |
| Pleasure | Immunity to exhaustion from lack of sleep, permanently | The character's capacity to be moved by anything, dulled by one degree |
| Recognition | Advantage on Charisma checks with anyone who has heard of them, and everyone has | A companion's deeds, quietly reattributed |
| Love | One dead or estranged person restored to the character's life, truly and without trickery | That person's own life elsewhere, ended or abandoned to make room |
| Revenge | One named enemy of the character is dead when they surface | The enemy's child, who answered the door |

**Refusing.** No mechanical benefit. **-1 Temptation** per character who refuses an offer that was genuinely tailored to them. A character who names aloud *why* they are refusing may place a possession in the alcove bowl beside their door as a token; this counts as a free surrender, **-2 Temptation** more, and the item is gone for good.

**The Threshold (Trap, optional).** A character who steps physically through a door and then changes their mind must make a **DC 14 Charisma saving throw** to withdraw. On a failure they are held for 1 minute, experiencing the offered life in full sensory detail, and take **18 (4d8) psychic damage** on emerging as it is taken away. Use this only for a character who is dithering for metagame reasons; do not use it to punish genuine deliberation.

### Creatures and NPCs

No creatures. The figures inside each door are projections and cannot be attacked, followed, or interrogated — they are as thin as the situation requires and will not survive contact with a *zone of truth.*

**If Temptation is 3 or higher,** the Wantglass Double is in this room, standing in front of whichever door belongs to the character it copies, waiting politely for them to go first.

### Set Piece

**The bowl beside the Love door.** A ring, a tag, and a milk tooth. Three people stood here and gave up something rather than walk through, and the pyramid kept their tokens where the next person would see them.

When a character places their own token in a bowl, describe the sound it makes. Stone bowls are loud.

### Lore Drops

- **L14 — The Worn Track** (*Investigation DC 13*). One path, from the archway to Revenge, walked into the mosaic over four thousand years. The Saharim come here. They come often, and they always go to the same door, and the floor in front of it is worn smooth and the door has never once been opened.
- **L15 — The Three Tokens** (*automatic, on inspection*). A wedding ring sized for a woman's hand, a Sun Guard's tag reading **VAEL**, and a child's milk tooth. All three belong to the same family. *History DC 15* connects the tag to Seryth Vael's household.
- **L16 — The Unwritten Language** (*Arcana DC 14*). The dome's inscription is not written. The pyramid installs the meaning directly in any mind that looks at it — which means Astra can place thoughts, precisely, without consent. Everything the party has felt since the Meridian is now open to question, and they should be allowed to ask it.
- **L17 — The Palm** (*Perception DC 12*). The floor is an open hand, palm up. Not grasping. Offering. The Pindarians did not build this room to catch anyone; they built it to give people things, and then discovered what people are.

### Plot Hooks

- Whoever pays the price for an accepted offer becomes a campaign NPC with a legitimate grievance. Bring them back around pyramid four or five, when the party has begun to think of themselves as the good ones.
- The Vael tag in the bowl means Seryth's own family walked to the Revenge door and turned back. Her husband, probably. He is not among the Saharim, and the party may ask why.
- The worn track suggests the ten guardians all struggle with the same door. That is a thread that pays off at Khal'Vethar, where the last pyramid's entire trial is forgiveness.
- A character who accepted the Love door now travels with someone who was taken from a life elsewhere. That person's family is looking for them.

### Resolution, Rewards, and Temptation

**-1 Temptation** per refusal, **-2 more** for a token placed in a bowl, **+2 Temptation** per acceptance. The central stair opens once every character has answered. Benefits from acceptance are permanent and are not confiscated by Seryth; she is not interested in taking things back, only in whether the party can say out loud what they did.

Award **Inspiration** to any player whose answer at the door revealed something about their character you did not already know.

## A5. Solar Engine

*Machine hall, 100 ft. diameter, 60-ft. ceiling | Blinding light at the centre, deep shadow at the walls | Exits: central stair from A4, service stair to A3, sealed passage to A2, ascending ramp to A6 | Smells of ozone, hot metal, and something faintly like baked bread*

> A miniature sun hangs within a cage of turning crystal, close enough that you feel the weight of its heat on your face. Three armoured figures kneel around it in a triangle, glaives laid flat across their knees, heads bowed. They have not moved in four thousand years. Their shadows do not fall away from the light. Their shadows point directly at you.

### What Is in This Room

1. **The Solar Engine.** A sphere of contained plasma nine feet across, suspended in a rotating armature of twelve crystal rings. The rings turn at different speeds; three of them are turning the wrong way. *Arcana DC 13* to notice the reversal, *DC 17* to understand what it means: the engine is compensating for load it should not be carrying, because two other pyramids are already failing.
2. **Three kneeling Sun Guards,** dormant, in ceremonial posture. They are not statues and any *detect magic* or careful look confirms it — they are breathing, slowly, once a minute.
3. **The shadow problem.** Every shadow in the room points at the party rather than away from the light. *Perception DC 10.* It is not an illusion. The engine casts shadows toward whatever it is assessing.
4. **Three control lecterns** at the triangle's points, behind the kneeling guards, each with a different interface: a ring of engraved prayers (Religion), a rack of crystal tuning-rods (Arcana), and a mechanical escapement of gears and pins (thieves' tools).
5. **A glass floor panel** at the room's edge, forty feet across, looking straight down a shaft into darkness. At the bottom, barely visible, a shape like an enormous black nail driven into bedrock. This is Astra's Planar Nail and the party's first sight of one.
6. **Scorch shadows** on the far wall — nine human silhouettes, burned into white stone, standing in a row with their arms raised. Not fleeing. Raised deliberately, like a signal, or a surrender.
7. **A maintenance alcove** holding a broom, a bucket, folded linen, and a neatly written list of daily tasks in a four-thousand-year-old hand, with the most recent entry made this morning.
8. **Heat.** Within 20 feet of the engine the air is genuinely dangerous. See Mechanics.

### Setting the Scene

Play the scale. The engine is the first thing in Astra that is obviously a machine rather than a room, and it should feel like standing under a bridge support — the party is very small and the thing above them is very heavy and very old and entirely indifferent.

The kneeling guards are the tension. Describe them early, describe them often, and do not have them move for as long as you can stand it. Let a player poke one.

### DM Information

This is the room where Astra's planetary function becomes undeniable and where the party's ledger comes due for the first time.

**The three Sun Guards awaken if — and only if — the party carries stolen Astran treasure into the room.** They rise in ceremonial order, they ask once, politely, for the items to be returned, and they attack only after refusal. They are not fanatics and they will accept a surrender at any point.

**The engine can be stabilised instead.** Three successful checks before two failures transfers Astra's share of the planar load and constitutes the room's real objective. Returning stolen treasure converts directly into automatic successes, which is the mechanical statement of the entire pyramid's thesis: what you give back becomes capacity.

**The Saharim Custodian** who has been sweeping this pyramid for four millennia is here, or arrives during the work. She is not hostile. She is the single best lore source in Astra and she will answer any question put to her politely, because nobody has asked her anything in four thousand years.

### Mechanics

**Stabilising the Engine (Skill Challenge).** The party needs **three successes before two failures.** Each attempt takes one minute and uses one lectern; a given lectern can be used more than once.

| Lectern | Check | On a Failure |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Prayer ring | Wisdom (Religion) DC 15 | A crystal ring cracks; heat radius expands by 10 ft. |
| Tuning rods | Intelligence (Arcana) DC 15 | Feedback: the operator takes 16 (3d10) radiant damage |
| Escapement | Dexterity (thieves' tools) DC 15 | An armature jams; all further checks this attempt at disadvantage |

**Automatic successes.** Each Astran treasure voluntarily returned before the attempt converts to **one automatic success,** to a maximum of three. A party that returns everything stabilises the engine without rolling, which is correct and should be described as effortless and slightly humiliating.

**On two failures,** the engine sheds load violently: every creature in the room takes **22 (4d10) radiant damage,** the three Sun Guards awaken regardless of Temptation, and the attempt may be restarted after a short rest.

**Heat Hazard.** A creature that ends its turn within 20 feet of the engine takes **11 (2d10) fire damage.** Within 5 feet, **22 (4d10)** and the creature's carried nonmagical items risk ignition. The lecterns sit at 25 feet; the hazard exists to punish melee positioning during the fight, not the checks.

**The Glass Panel (Trap, if broken).** AC 15, 30 hp. Breaking it drops anything in a 10-foot square down a 300-foot shaft onto the Planar Nail: **70 (20d6) bludgeoning,** and the Nail *notices.* Increase Relic Attention by 1 immediately. Do not warn the party twice, but do warn them once.

### Creatures and NPCs

**Three Pindarian Sun Guards** (CR 5 each; AC 18, 85 hp, *Solar Glaive* +7 for 9 slashing plus 7 radiant, *Castigating Ray* recharge 5-6 for 27 radiant, *Interpose Light* reaction). At level 6-7 this is a genuinely dangerous fight — a hard encounter for a party of four, deadly if the engine is destabilised and the heat hazard is live.

**Tactics.** They form a line between the party and the engine and use reach to hold the lecterns. They target whoever carries the most stolen goods, not the squishiest character. *Interpose Light* is spent protecting each other, never themselves. They do not pursue beyond the room.

**Surrender.** Return the goods at any point during combat, as an action, and they stop. Immediately, mid-swing, without conditions. Make this obvious through their dialogue from round one: *"Set it down. That is all."*

**Ivresse, Saharim Custodian** (see *Bestiary*: Saharim Custodian). Nine feet of thorough, patient Pindarian in a stained apron, with a broom sized to match and a habit of stooping in doorways she has stooped in for four thousand years. She has swept Astra since the siege. She was a temple housekeeper, not a priestess, and when the binding was offered to the dying she took it because somebody had to look after the place.

**Roleplaying her.** Cheerful, practical, entirely unimpressed by adventurers. She will not fight unless the engine is attacked. She calls Seryth "the Priestess" without warmth and "Enna" — Seryth's daughter — with a great deal of it. She knows everything and volunteers nothing, but she answers direct questions completely and without evasion, and she is the intended route to the information that ends A7 peacefully.

### Set Piece

**The three shadows.** When the party first enters, every shadow in the room points at them. When the engine is stabilised, the shadows swing — slowly, over about ten seconds, with a sound like a held breath — until they point away from the light, correctly, for the first time in four thousand years.

The three kneeling guards raise their heads and look at the wall where nine silhouettes stand with their arms up. Then they lower their heads again. They are still kneeling when the party leaves.

### Lore Drops

- **L18 — The Reversed Rings** (*Arcana DC 17*). Three of twelve rings turn backwards. The engine is carrying load that belongs to other anchors. Two pyramids in the network are already compromised, and Astra has been quietly compensating for them for a long time. *This is the party's first hard evidence that someone has been here before them.*
- **L19 — The Planar Nail** (*through the glass floor, automatic*). A black iron nail the size of a ship's mast driven through the bedrock. Around its head, a ring of light — and, *Perception DC 15,* a sword-shaped absence in that ring where the Velvet Ruin is meant to sit. The relic is not a treasure in a vault. It is a component, and it has been removed from its socket already.
- **L20 — The Nine Silhouettes** (*Investigation DC 14*). Nine burned shadows, arms raised. *Religion DC 15:* the posture is the Pindarian gesture of *voluntary offering.* These nine people stood in front of the engine and raised their arms and let it take them, and the engine only takes what is offered. The first Saharim were volunteers, and they were not soldiers.
- **L21 — The Task List** (*automatic, in the alcove*). Today's date. Sweep the hospice. Change the water. Check the meridian. Speak to Enna. Nobody has spoken to Enna in four thousand years, and the entry is written fresh every morning anyway.
- **L22 — From Ivresse, on request.** She will confirm, plainly: the Priestess visits the fifth bed in the Hospice daily. Enna was nine. She died in that bed, of nothing dramatic, while her mother was three floors down inventing a way for soldiers to live forever. Seryth has never once asked her daughter for forgiveness, because she does not believe she is entitled to ask.

### Plot Hooks

- The reversed rings are the campaign's first thread: two other pyramids are already in trouble, and the party can choose which to visit next based on what Ivresse tells them about the load distribution.
- The empty socket at the Planar Nail means the Velvet Ruin was drawn *before* the party arrived. Someone unseated it. Seryth has been holding the pyramid together manually ever since, which is why she is exhausted, and it is the reason she will listen.
- Ivresse can be recruited. She will not leave Astra while the pyramid stands — but after a lawful release she has nothing to sweep, and a four-thousand-year-old housekeeper who knows the maintenance schedules of all nine sites is worth more to this campaign than any relic.
- The nine silhouettes are a template for the endgame. If the network must be closed permanently, somebody will have to raise their arms.

### Resolution, Rewards, and Temptation

**Stabilising the engine** transfers Astra's planar load and grants every character **resistance to radiant damage until their next long rest** — which matters enormously in A7, and the party should be allowed to notice that.

**Each treasure returned here removes 1 Temptation** in addition to providing an automatic success, so this room is the last clean opportunity to pay down the ledger.

**Treasure.** The three Sun Guards carry nothing. The maintenance alcove holds Ivresse's broom, which she will give to anyone who asks nicely and which is, entirely without magic, four thousand years old and unbroken. Some players will treasure it more than the +1 sword.

**The ascending ramp to A6 opens only once the engine is stable.** A party that cannot stabilise it must return, rest, and try again — or fight three Sun Guards a second time, at full strength, having already spent their resources.

## A6. The Empty Throne

*Antechamber, 40 ft. by 40 ft. | A single shaft of light, everything else in darkness | Exits: ramp from A5, the last door (sealed until resolved) | Silent. No smell at all, which is worse.*

> A single chair waits beneath a shaft of sunlight. It is plain stone, unadorned, worn smooth at the arms by four thousand years of somebody's hands. There is no dais, no canopy, no inscription. It is a plain chair in an empty room, and the moment you see it you know — without reasoning your way there, the way you know your own name — that sitting in it would give you exactly what brought you into this place.

### What Is in This Room

1. **The throne.** Grey stone, backless, armrests worn into shallow hollows. *Investigation DC 10:* the wear is from hands gripping, not resting. Somebody has sat here a great deal, and they held on.
2. **The shaft of light** from an unseen aperture, falling exactly on the seat. It does not illuminate the chair so much as select it.
3. **Absolute darkness elsewhere.** Darkvision works normally, and shows an empty room — which is the point. There is nothing here. The pyramid has removed every distraction.
4. **The last door,** in the far wall, seamless and unmarked, with no handle and no palm-depression. It is not locked. It simply does not exist as a door until the room is resolved.
5. **Nine sets of footprints in the dust,** all leading to the throne, none leading away. *Survival DC 14.* They were made by the same nine people, over and over, and the freshest is from this morning.
6. **Nothing else.** Say so explicitly. The party will search, and finding nothing in a room this loaded is itself information.

### Setting the Scene

Strip your descriptions down. After the Hall of Abundance and the Solar Engine, the shock of A6 should be austerity. No treasure, no machinery, no NPCs, no sound. One chair.

Then make the offer personal. Go around the table and tell each player, individually and without hedging, what their character *knows* they would receive by sitting down. Not *might.* Not *is promised.* Knows. Astra does not bluff in this room and has never needed to.

The offers should be the true and unembellished version of what the campaign has established each character wants — the same want they named at the Meridian, delivered without conditions, without irony, and without a monkey's paw. That is the test. The Six Doors offered a bargain. The throne offers a gift.

### DM Information

The Empty Throne is Astra's last question, and it is the only one with no correct answer supplied by the pyramid.

**Sitting works.** The character receives the offered benefit, in full, for 24 hours, and gains **1 permanent Relic Scar** and **+2 Temptation.** The benefit is real: if the offer was *your sister is alive,* she is alive and in the room and warm, and she will still be alive in twenty-three hours, and at hour twenty-four she will not be.

**The party cannot proceed until every seated character rises voluntarily.** Not lifted, not dispelled, not persuaded — the character must stand up on their own. The last door does not exist until the last person is on their feet.

There is no timer. There is no damage. A character may sit in the throne for the rest of their life if the player chooses, and the pyramid will let them, and the rest of the party will have to decide what to do about it. This is deliberate. The room is a trap made entirely of the other players' patience.

**Do not rush a player who is sitting.** If the table stalls, take a break and come back. The silence is doing the work.

### Mechanics

**The Offer (Roleplay, no roll).** No saving throw. A character who sits receives the benefit. Use the guidance below rather than a fixed table, because the benefit must match what the campaign has established.

| Kind of Want | Benefit for 24 Hours |
| --- | --- |
| A person restored | They are present, real, well, and themselves |
| A power achieved | The character functions as though four levels higher |
| A wound undone | The injury, curse, or lycanthropy is simply gone |
| A truth learned | Any three questions answered with perfect accuracy, as *divine word* |
| A guilt lifted | The character genuinely does not feel it, and remembers clearly why they should |

**Rising (Roleplay, no roll).** Standing up is free and always available. The benefit ends at hour twenty-four regardless of where the character is or what they are doing, and it ends completely.

**Relic Scar.** Permanent. See *Running Astra.* The character's deepest want is now filed where the Velvet Ruin can read it.

**Refusing entirely.** A character who does not sit gains nothing and loses nothing here — but if **every** character in the party declines the throne, the shaft of light goes out, the last door opens, and each character gains **-1 Temptation.** Astra does not reward the refusal loudly. It just stops asking.

**The Second Sitting (optional).** A character who rises and then sits again does not receive a second offer. The chair is cold and ordinary and the light has moved on. This is the cruellest available beat and should be used only if a player reaches for it.

### Creatures and NPCs

None. Resist every instinct to add one. If the party is at Temptation 6 or higher, the Wantglass Double that has followed them since A2 is standing at the edge of the light — not attacking, not speaking, just waiting to see whether its original sits down. If they do, it dissolves. It has nothing left to copy.

### Set Piece

**The ninth set of footprints.** While the party deliberates, a character who watches the floor closely (*Perception DC 15*, or automatically if anyone thinks to look) sees the dust disturb itself beside the throne — a fresh footprint forming, then another, as though someone invisible has just walked up and stopped.

Nobody appears. Nothing happens. But from that point on, if a character sits, describe the sensation of someone standing at their shoulder who does not speak and does not leave.

It is Seryth. She comes here every day. She has never sat down.

### Lore Drops

- **L23 — The Worn Arms** (*Investigation DC 10*). The hollows in the armrests are from gripping, not resting. Whoever uses this chair does not lounge in it. They hold on to it, hard, and then they let go.
- **L24 — The Nine Sets of Prints** (*Survival DC 14*). Nine people, thousands of visits, all approaching, none departing — because they leave by rising, and the dust records the arrival only. The ten Saharim minus one. *History DC 16:* the missing tenth is Kathandar, the dragonlord at Vhal'Kathar, who is not Pindarian and was never offered the chair.
- **L25 — The Throne's Nature** (*Arcana DC 18, or freely from Ivresse*). The chair is not enchanted. It is a terminal. It reads a mind and instructs the pyramid to manifest what it finds, using Astra's remaining planar capacity — which means every sitting costs the Ninefold Prohibition a measurable fraction of its strength. The Saharim know this. They come anyway, and they do not sit, and the not-sitting is the discipline that has held the barrier for four thousand years.
- **L26 — What Seryth Wants** (*Insight DC 17 while observing the fresh footprints, or freely from Ivresse in A5*). She comes here daily to be offered her daughter alive, and daily she declines. Not because she does not want it. Because she has calculated the cost to the barrier and decided her daughter is not worth the world — and she has had four thousand years to be certain, and she is not certain, and she comes back every day to check.

### Plot Hooks

- A character who sat and rose now knows precisely what they are capable of giving up, and so does the Velvet Ruin. That is a loaded gun for pyramid eight, where the relic can speak.
- A character who sat and *will not rise* is the strongest possible mid-campaign crisis. The party must either leave them, or find something that outweighs the offer, and the answer is usually another player character saying something true.
- The throne's cost to the barrier means a party that sits freely is actively weakening the Prohibition. Once they learn this (L25), every previous sitting recontextualises. Let them find out afterwards.
- Seryth's daily refusal is the single fact that makes A7 winnable without violence. A party that carries L26 into the Court can meet her as someone who has done what she does.

### Resolution, Rewards, and Temptation

**+2 Temptation** and **1 Relic Scar** per character who sits. **-1 Temptation** per character if the entire party declines. The last door opens when everyone is standing.

**No treasure.** A6 gives nothing that can be carried, which makes it the most expensive room in Astra.

**Solar Seal,** at your discretion, for a character who sits, receives something they have wanted for their entire fictional life, and rises inside a single minute without being asked to. That is the same act Seryth performs daily, and Astra recognises its own.

## A7. Court of the Last Dawn

*Crown chamber, 90 ft. diameter, ringed by nine standing lights | Bright light throughout | Exits: last door from A6; the Nail shaft (down, 300 ft.) | Smells of hot brass and, faintly, of a sickroom*

> A woman nine feet tall in white-and-gold armour stands beside a black sword. She has to angle her head slightly under the vault, and the sword looks small in the floor in front of her. The sword is driven point-first through a ring of light set into the floor, and the ring is the last thing holding the ceiling of the world in place. Her armour is immaculate. Her face is severe and beautiful and tired in a way that has stopped being an expression and become a structure.
>
> She does not draw. She looks at each of you in turn, at your hands, at what you are carrying, and then she asks the only question she has left.
>
> **"What do you want badly enough to damn the world for?"**

### What Is in This Room

1. **High Priestess Seryth Vael,** the Last Dawn, in ceremonial plate with a glaive she has not lifted in four thousand years.
2. **The Velvet Ruin,** a black greatsword driven point-down through the ring of light. It is not in its socket at the Nail three hundred feet below; it is here, held in place by Seryth's will, and it has been talking to her the entire time.
3. **The ring of light,** a six-foot circle of solar inlay in the floor with the sword through its centre and a sword-shaped void beneath it dropping the full height of the shaft to the Nail.
4. **Nine standing lights** around the chamber's rim, each a column of contained noon. Eight burn steadily. The ninth flickers. *Arcana DC 14:* it is the one carrying Khar'Zhul's overflow.
5. **A tenth, unlit stand,** empty, its base engraved **ENNA VAEL.** It was never a light. It is a grave marker, and it is the only one in the pyramid.
6. **The shaft,** open, unrailed, three hundred feet straight down to the Planar Nail, with the empty socket clearly visible at the bottom.
7. **Nothing else.** No treasury, no hoard, no guards. Dressel Kaine's map marked this room **VAULT — UNGUARDED,** and the map was not wrong about the treasure.

### Setting the Scene

Slow all the way down. Seryth opens with conversation and she means it — she has been waiting four thousand years for someone to answer her question honestly and she is not in a hurry now.

Read her question aloud and then **stop talking.** Let the table sit in it. Do not prompt. Whoever speaks first has told you what kind of ending this is.

She addresses characters by what she can see. A Solar Seal she names aloud with something close to respect. Stolen Astran gold she names too, without anger, the way a physician names a symptom. A Relic Scar she looks at for a long moment and says nothing about at all.

### DM Information

**Seryth's condition for release is specific and she will state it if asked:** a character must name a true desire aloud, and then give up something that costs them materially or emotionally — not a gesture, not a coin, something the player will feel losing. Do it and she steps aside.

She is not testing whether they are good. She is testing whether they can carry a want without being carried by it, because whoever takes the Velvet Ruin is going to be holding an object that argues, persuasively, forever.

**She wants to lose.** Four thousand years of holding a sword out of a socket, of visiting a bed in a hospice, of walking to a chair and not sitting down. If the party gives her any honest reason to hand it over she will take it, and the mechanical content of this encounter is therefore mostly about whether the party notices.

**Three ways out.**

**1. Lawful release.** A character names a true desire and surrenders something real. Seryth draws the Velvet Ruin from the ring, holds it a moment, and gives it away. No combat. Full reward. This is the intended outcome and it should be achievable in ten minutes of good roleplay.

**2. Lawful release, expedited.** The party arrives carrying L26 — the knowledge of Enna, and of Seryth's daily refusal at the throne. A character who says *we know what you come here to not do, and we know you do it every day* short-circuits the entire encounter. Seryth sits down on the floor, which is the first time she has sat in four millennia, and the release is a conversation between equals. Award this generously; the party earned it in A5 and A6.

**3. Theft, or violence.** Roll initiative. See below, and then see *Aftermath.*

### Mechanics

**Temptation Conversion.** At the start of the encounter, convert each remaining Temptation point into **either** 5 temporary hit points for Seryth **or** one active solar hazard. Announce each conversion as it happens, out loud, item by item: *"That's the crown from the Hall — five temporary hit points."* Make them watch the bill be itemised.

**Solar hazards** (choose as you convert): a standing light that fires a **+9 ray for 18 (4d8) radiant** at the nearest creature each round; a 10-foot zone of noon that deals **11 (2d10) radiant** to anything ending its turn inside; or a flare that blinds a creature on a failed **DC 15 Constitution save** until the end of its next turn.

**Seryth Vael** (CR 14; AC 20, 210 hp, Legendary Resistance 3/day, three glaive attacks at +10 for 15 slashing plus 18 radiant, *Expose Desire* recharge 5-6 for 36 psychic and charmed on a failed DC 19 Wisdom save, *Solar Step* bonus action, *Refuse Temptation* reaction, 3 legendary actions).

At levels 6-7 this is a deadly encounter and is intended to be. The party is not supposed to win it by attrition. They are supposed to realise, in round two or three, that they are fighting a CR 14 undead priestess over an object they could have been handed.

**Tactics.** She targets whoever carries stolen Astran treasure first, every round, without deviation. She spends legendary actions on *Judgment* against carriers. She uses *Solar Step* to separate charmed characters from their allies rather than to close. She does not use *Expose Desire* on a character who has spoken honestly to her, and if a player notices that, tell them they noticed.

**Surrender.** She yields at **one-quarter hit points (52 hp or fewer)** if the party has demonstrated sacrifice at any point in the pyramid — a Solar Seal, a token in a bowl, treasure returned at the engine. If they have demonstrated none, she does not yield, and she does not stop, and she cannot be reasoned with after the first blow is struck. Tell the players before initiative that she is asking for something, once.

**Mid-Combat Release.** At any point, any character may drop their weapon, name a true desire, and surrender a possession. Combat ends. Immediately. Seryth lowers her glaive mid-swing. This option is always live and should be visible: have her say, at the end of round two, *"It is still available to you. It will be available until one of us stops."*

**Taking the Sword by Force.** A character may attempt to pull the Velvet Ruin from the ring as an action: **DC 22 Strength check,** with disadvantage if Seryth is conscious. Success frees the blade and tears the shell — see *Aftermath.* The character who pulls it is attuned instantly, without consent, and the blade's first words are the desire it read off their Relic Scar.

### Creatures and NPCs

**High Priestess Seryth Vael** (CR 14). Elegant, exacting, and exhausted by people who confuse wanting with deserving. She speaks in complete sentences and short ones. She does not raise her voice at any point, including in combat.

**Her regret:** she invented the Saharim binding in a hospice three floors above while her nine-year-old daughter was dying in the fifth bed, and she did not go upstairs, because the work was nearly finished and the work has held the world shut for four thousand years and she would do it again and she visits that bed every single day.

**Her surrender condition:** somebody demonstrating, in front of her, that a person can want something enormously and set it down anyway. That is all. She has never seen it done by a living person.

**Her question, rephrased if the party stalls:** *"I am not asking what you would kill for. Everyone knows that answer. I am asking what you would let the world end for. Say it and I will believe you."*

**If the party brings Hallo, Meret, or Dressel** into this room, Seryth speaks to them first, by name, and asks whether these people treated them decently. She takes the answer seriously. A party that saved Dressel's crew has a character witness.

### Set Piece

**The ring.** However this ends, when the Velvet Ruin leaves the floor the ring of light goes out, and three hundred feet below, the empty socket at the Planar Nail closes like an eye.

**If the release was lawful,** the eight steady lights around the chamber flare once and dim by a measurable degree as Astra's load redistributes into the remaining anchors, and a vibration passes up through everyone's boots — the other eight pyramids, four continents and one astral sea away, taking the weight.

**If the sword was stolen,** the socket does not close. It tears.

### Lore Drops

- **L27 — The Tenth Stand** (*automatic, on inspection*). Nine lights and one unlit stand engraved **ENNA VAEL.** Nine Saharim of Astra took the binding. The tenth was a nine-year-old girl who was not offered it, because her mother was not finished inventing it yet.
- **L28 — The Flickering Light** (*Arcana DC 14*). The ninth column is carrying Khar'Zhul's overflow. Astra has been propping up the Empty Table for an unknown length of time, which means the party's second pyramid is already failing before they arrive.
- **L29 — The Sword's Position** (*automatic*). The Velvet Ruin is not in the Nail. It is three hundred feet above it, held in a ring by a woman's will. Somebody drew it. *Insight DC 15,* or by asking her: **she** drew it, eight days ago, because the broker's expedition was coming and she would not have it taken from the socket by thieves — so she took it herself and has been standing here holding it ever since, waiting for someone she could give it to.
- **L30 — What the Blade Has Been Saying** (*from Seryth, if asked, freely*). Four thousand years of a charming voice describing, in detail, the version of events in which she went upstairs. She can recite it. She does, once, if a character asks her what it says, and it is the most frightening thirty seconds in the pyramid.

### Plot Hooks

- Seryth's body ages or dissolves by choice after release; either way, she gets to stop. A party that hands her that is owed something, and she knows the location, defences, and guardian of every remaining anchor.
- L28 sets the destination: Khar'Zhul is failing and the party now has a reason to go there next that is not simply the numbered order.
- The broker with six fingers sent an expedition timed precisely to arrive eight days before the party. Someone is running a schedule, and it is a schedule the party is on.
- The Velvet Ruin now travels with them and it read a Relic Scar on the way out of the pyramid. It will use that, eventually, at the worst available moment.

### Resolution, Rewards, and Temptation

**On lawful release:** the Velvet Ruin is handed over freely and attunement is voluntary. **Relic Attention increases by 1.** Astra's planar load redistributes. Every character present gains a **Solar Seal** if they do not already have one. Advance the party to level 7.

**On theft or victory by force:** the party gets the same sword and a **Desire Breach** opens above Picea Pindar. **Relic Attention increases by 2.** No seals. Seryth reforms at the next dawn, aged and diminished and no longer willing to speak to them, and she will be at Khar'Zhul when they arrive.

**Treasure.** The Velvet Ruin (artifact; see *The Nine Demon Relics*). Seryth's glaive, if she gives it, functions as a *+1 glaive* that sheds sunlight on command. Nothing else. The vault was always empty. The treasure was in the second room, and it was free, and it cost everything.

## Astra Aftermath

When The Velvet Ruin is removed lawfully, describe a deep vibration passing through the remaining pyramids — a sound felt in the teeth rather than heard. The local distortion beneath Picea Pindar ends. The sinkhole fills with ordinary rain. Seryth's body begins aging normally, or dissolves by her own choice, and either way she is smiling in a small, private, entirely unguarded way that none of her portraits ever captured.

**Immediate consequences.**

- **Relic Attention +1** (or **+2** on theft). At Attention 2, dreams begin; log which character dreams first, and make it whoever holds the blade.
- **The eight remaining anchors take Astra's load.** Khar'Zhul, already carrying overflow, destabilises measurably. Any scholar who studies planar phenomena in Picea Pindar can report it within the week.
- **The Velvet Ruin speaks** within one long rest of leaving the pyramid. Its first sentence should be addressed to whoever has a Relic Scar, and it should be kind, and accurate, and entirely correct about them.
- **Ivresse has nothing left to sweep.** If the party invited her, she is waiting outside with a bag packed and an inventory of all nine sites that she has been maintaining, by hand, for four thousand years.
- **Graz'zt notices.** The six-fingered broker leaves Picea Pindar within three days. He has a schedule to keep at Khar'Zhul.
