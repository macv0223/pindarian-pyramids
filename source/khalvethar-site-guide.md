# Khal'Vethar: Pyramid of the Final Vigil - Site Guide

*Levels 18-20 | All Three Orders | Vengeance into Forgiveness | Relic: Winter's Grudge | Guardian: Hestra Vaun, Last Marshal of Pindaria*

Beneath the displaced city of Keen Stungar, in the Astral Sea. **This anchor must be released last.**

## Running Khal'Vethar

The other eight anchors are locks. This one is a **failsafe**, and a failsafe is a weapon that has agreed to wait.

Khal'Vethar was built to do something the other eight were not: if the Prohibition ever failed, the ninth anchor would convert four thousand years of stored grievance into a retaliatory strike and hand it to whoever was left. It weaponises the accumulated anger of a dead civilisation, and it has been charging the entire time, and it is very nearly ready.

**Every retaliatory act the party commits here feeds it.** Every refusal to continue the cycle drains it and teaches the machine something it has never been taught: that its emergency custodians know when a war is over.

This is the finale. Run it as one.

### Pindarian Scale

**Pindarians are Large.** All of them, always — the Solaari, the Lunari, the Veylari, the Ilyri, every
Saharim guardian and every soldier in every rank. Eight to ten feet, long-boned, and long-lived, with a
reach and a stride to match: **10-foot reach on their melee attacks and a 10-foot space.**

This is not a variant or a subtype. It is what the species is, and it is worth showing rather than stating —
Pindarian doorways are twelve feet high, their stairs have deep treads, their chairs do not fit anyone else,
and a party of Medium adventurers spends nine pyramids using furniture built for people half again their
size.

Human, elven and dwarven NPCs met in and around the sites are Medium as usual. The contrast is the point.

### The Laws

1. **Violence initiated by a character adds 1 Vengeance.** Initiated. Not returned, not defensive, not a killing blow against something already attacking — *initiated*. The party throwing the first punch, every time.
2. **At initiative count 20, the pyramid spends Vengeance** to reinforce enemies or intensify terrain. It spends visibly and you narrate what it bought.
3. **Mercy toward a dangerous foe removes 1 Vengeance.** Dangerous. Sparing a helpless thing is kindness and costs nothing and earns nothing; sparing a thing that can still kill you is the trial.
4. **If entered before the other eight lawful releases, the entrance is an endless battlefield.** R1 does not open. It never opens. The party fights forever in a corridor and nothing is achieved and nobody dies, and they can leave whenever they like.

### The Vengeance Track

Visible, 0 to 12 — twice the length of every other pyramid's track, because this one is a budget rather than a warning.

| Event | Vengeance |
| --- | --- |
| Initiating violence against any creature | +1 each instance |
| Killing a creature that had surrendered or stood down | +2 each |
| Violence against the Grievance figure (R3) | +2 per character |
| Attacking during the March (R5) | +1 per round, and a new wave |
| Destroying a memorial statue (R4) | +2 each |
| Arriving with a relic taken improperly from any anchor | +2 each |
| A formal stand-down at the Frozen Muster (R2) | **-2** |
| Releasing the Grievance figure (R3) | -1 per character |
| Sparing a dangerous foe | -1 each |
| Evacuating enemies before breaking the causeway (R4) | -2 |
| Completing the March without a hostile action (R5) | -3 |
| Hestra's release (R7) | **to 0** |

**The party will arrive somewhere between 4 and 10** depending on eight pyramids of accumulated conduct — see *Arrival Vengeance* below.

**At initiative 20, the pyramid spends.** Announce the purchase aloud, in the fiction:

| Cost | The pyramid buys |
| --- | --- |
| 1 | A wave of six revenant-like Pindarian soldiers |
| 1 | The terrain worsens: 20 ft. of difficult red ice, or a 15-ft. wind that halves ranged accuracy |
| 2 | One **Grievance Colossus segment** joins the field |
| 3 | Every enemy present regains half its hit points |
| 4 | **The Colossus takes a full turn out of sequence** |

It spends down and it does not spend what it does not have. **At 0 Vengeance the pyramid cannot act on initiative 20 at all**, and the silence at the top of the round is the clearest possible signal that the party is winning.

### Arrival Vengeance

Khal'Vethar starts pre-loaded. Count it up before the session:

- **+1 per relic** the party carries. (Eight, if they have them all.)
- **+2 per relic** taken improperly from its anchor.
- **-1 per anchor** where the party achieved the full lawful resolution without any combat with the guardian.

A party that did the whole campaign cleanly arrives around **4**. A party that fought their way through arrives at **10 or more**, and R7 becomes genuinely unwinnable by force, which is the correct outcome and is not a punishment.

### The Relics Speak

All eight are here, on the party, and **they all talk at once at R1** and again at R8. They agree about exactly one thing — the route — and about nothing else.

Give each of them one line at R1. The Velvet Ruin is silky, Gravewake is administrative, Feastchain is impatient, the Two That Are One argues with itself, the Labyrinth's Answer gives a confident wrong direction, the Bride of Rot is maternal, Mercy of Dissolution suggests a simplification, and the Honest Lie says something charming and false.

Then they stop, together, and say the same sentence at the same time, and it is the last route.

### Pacing and Shape

Eight areas. **R3 and R6 are optional and both are excellent.** R5 is the thesis. R7 is the finale. R8 is the campaign's final page and contains three endings.

Three to four sessions. Resting is possible and Vengeance does not decay.

### Safety at This Table

R3 puts the person who harmed each character most in a room, kneeling and powerless, and asks the player what they do about it. **That is the sharpest content in the whole campaign** and it wants a direct conversation before the session, not a general body-horror heading.

Protections you hold:

- **R3 is entirely optional per character.** A player may have their character not open that door, at no cost, with no Vengeance either way, and nobody at the table needs to know what was behind it.
- **The figure is a memory simulacrum with no independent future.** It is not the person. It cannot be hurt in a way that matters and it cannot forgive in a way that counts, and the room says so.
- **Forgiveness is not reconciliation and not absolution.** The trial accepts a refusal to be ruled by the injury, and that is all it asks, and you should say that out loud if a player looks cornered.

## R1. The Underside Gate

*The underside of Keen Stungar, open astral | Starlight and upward snow | Exits: the city above, the pyramid below | Smells of cold stone and old iron*

> Below the astral city, the streets simply stop being streets. Cobbles become red-black geometry, and the geometry becomes a pyramid, and it goes down further than the city is wide.
>
> Snow falls upward from its surface into the stars. It has been falling upward for four thousand years and the stars have not filled.

### What Is in This Room

1. **The gate**, red-black, with eight recessed seals around it in a ring and a ninth position empty.
2. **The eight transfer seals**, which light as the party approaches if the corresponding anchor was lawfully released. **Observation.**
3. **Upward snow**, continuous, from the whole surface.
4. **Keen Stungar's underside** above them — a city's foundations, pipes, sewers and cellars, with people living in them who have noticed the geometry growing.
5. **Karsa Vell**, a Keen Stungar undercity foreman, waiting at the gate with a lantern and a list of complaints. She has been trying to get somebody official to look at this for two years.
6. **The Unchosen**, somewhere beyond, already recording. It has started its Observation track and the party does not know it yet.
7. **A ninth seal position**, empty, unlit, shaped for nothing the party is carrying.

### Setting the Scene

Open on the city's underside — washing lines, cellar doors, a woman with a lantern — and then the point where the cobbles stop being cobbles.

Then the snow falling up.

Then let every relic the party carries speak at once. That is the set piece and it should take a full minute of table time.

### DM Information

**The gate checks eight transfer seals.**

**If any are missing**, every step toward the gate returns the party to the city, gently, with **one shared vision of Pindaria's last day** — the same vision, all of them, simultaneously, and it is four seconds long and it is the view from Hestra Vaun's position on the wall.

The party can attempt this as many times as they like. Each attempt gives four more seconds of the same day, from the same position, further along. **A party that stubbornly bounces off this gate twelve times has assembled the entire last hour of Pindaria** and that is a legitimate and rewarding way to fail.

**With all eight, the gate opens silently.** No ceremony, no test, no announcement. It simply is not shut any more.

**The ninth seal position** is empty and unlit and shaped for nothing. *Arcana DC 20:* it is the transfer seal for **Khal'Vethar's own release**, and it cannot light until the ninth anchor is released, and the ninth anchor cannot be released until the ninth seal lights.

That is not a paradox. It means **Khal'Vethar was never designed to be released**, and somebody will need to do something the builders did not plan for.

### Mechanics

**The Seals (no check).** Eight lit: the gate opens. Fewer: the bounce-back and the vision.

**The Visions (no check).** Four seconds each, cumulative, same viewpoint. Twelve attempts gives the full hour. There is no limit and no cost.

**The Ninth Position (Arcana DC 20).** As above. **Tell them at the gate.** The finale is better when the party spends three sessions knowing there is no designed exit.

**Karsa Vell (no check).** She will tell them everything the undercity has noticed: the geometry's growth rate, the three cellars it has already taken, the fact that nobody who goes down comes back up, and that a thin man with an ink-stain on his cuff went down eleven days ago and she has his coat because he left it.

### Creatures and NPCs

**Karsa Vell**, undercity foreman (use **Commoner** with Investigation +4). Human, fifties, unimpressed, carrying two years of ignored reports and a lantern.

**What matters:** she has the coat. *Investigation DC 10:* it is a scriptorium coat, ink at the cuff, and there is a single sheet in the pocket in a hand the party has seen before — **the repainted murals, the Verity Eaters' pages, the second desk in Nar'Vael.**

**Irio's assistant is already inside.** Eleven days ahead of them. It has been practising for eleven hundred years and it has come to the last anchor, and the sheet in the pocket is a partial description of Hestra Vaun that is about ninety per cent accurate.

### Set Piece

**Eight voices and one sentence.**

Every relic speaks at once, over each other, arguing — and then they stop, together, mid-word, and say the same seven words at the same time in eight voices.

Whatever those words are in your campaign, write them down now, because they are the last thing all eight of them will ever agree about.

### Lore Drops

- **L1 — The Upward Snow** (*Arcana DC 17*). Four thousand years of continuous emission. *Arcana DC 20:* it is not snow and not weather. It is **discharge** — the failsafe venting a fraction of its stored charge continuously because it has been full for a very long time.
- **L2 — The Ninth Position** (*Arcana DC 20*). No designed exit. The builders did not plan for this anchor to be released, ever, by anyone.
- **L3 — The Coat** (*Investigation DC 10, from Karsa*). Scriptorium cut, ink at the cuff, one sheet in the pocket: a ninety-per-cent-accurate description of Hestra Vaun in the assistant's hand.
- **L4 — The Visions** (*bounce off the gate*). Four seconds per attempt, from Hestra's position on the wall, on the last day. She was watching, and the viewpoint never moves, and by the eighth attempt the party will understand that she did not move either.

### Plot Hooks

- The assistant is eleven days ahead and is writing a description of the final guardian, and it has never been precise, and it is getting better.
- Karsa Vell's undercity is three cellars from being eaten. Whatever happens at R8, four thousand people live directly above it.
- A party that farms the visions has the last hour of Pindaria as a primary source and will recognise things in R2 and R7.

### Resolution, Rewards, and Temptation

**No Vengeance movement.** The track is already loaded — see *Arrival Vengeance*.

**Treasure.** A scriptorium coat and a sheet of paper.

## R2. The Frozen Muster

*Parade ground, 400 ft. by 400 ft., under an astral sky | Grey daylight that has no source | Exits: the gate behind, four causeways | Smells of cold iron and wool*

> Thousands of soldiers stand where they died, and not one of them is under eight feet, and the ranks go back further than you can see because each of them takes up twice the room you do.
>
> Frost seals their wounds — not covering them, *sealing* them, the way a bandage does. Ranks dressed, weapons grounded, lines straight.
>
> Their eyes track you across the whole four hundred feet. No weapon rises.

### What Is in This Room

1. **Eleven thousand Pindarian soldiers**, standing, dead, at attention, in formation, in the uniforms of all three Orders. **Observation.**
2. **Frost-sealed wounds**, every one of them, sealed rather than healed.
3. **Four causeways** leading off the muster ground, one to each of R3, R4, R5 and the deeper pyramid.
4. **A command dais** at the ground's head, empty, with a standard still planted in it.
5. **The standard**, Pindarian, all three Orders' devices on one field, which no unit in history was entitled to fly. *History DC 16.*
6. **Nine thousand three hundred and four grounded weapons**, and eleven thousand soldiers. *Investigation DC 14.*

### Setting the Scene

Silence and scale. Do not have anything move. Let the party walk the length of it and describe three individual soldiers in detail — a young one, an old one, one with a mended strap — and let the eyes track.

Then let someone speak to one.

### DM Information

**Speaking with the soldiers reveals they obey Hestra's final order because nobody rescinded it.**

They will talk. All of them. Politely, at length, without complaint. They have been standing at attention for four thousand years on the last order they were given, which was **HOLD**, and no officer has ever countermanded it, and they are not in distress and they would very much like to sit down.

**A character with military authority cannot simply command them.** Rank does not transfer, and they know it, and they will say so with genuine regret.

**They must accept responsibility for ending a sacred duty.**

The stand-down requires a character to:

1. State plainly that the war is over,
2. Order the muster to stand down, **in their own name, not borrowed**, and
3. Accept that if they are wrong, it is theirs.

That third clause is the trial. The soldiers will ask for it specifically — *"And if the enemy comes tomorrow, sir?"* — and the honest answer is *"then it is my fault,"* and nothing else works.

**A formal stand-down removes 2 Vengeance**, and eleven thousand soldiers ground arms, and sit down, and it takes about four minutes for the sound to finish.

**They do not vanish.** They sit. They are still there on the way out. Several of them will want to talk.

### Mechanics

**Speaking (no check).** They answer everything. They are the largest body of primary witnesses in the campaign and they were at the last battle.

**The Stand-Down (Roleplay, no check).** The three clauses above, from one character, in their own name. **-2 Vengeance.**

**A partial attempt** — borrowed authority, a hedged acceptance, an order without the responsibility — is declined courteously and may be retried immediately as often as needed. No cost.

**Attacking them.** They do not fight back. At all. Under any provocation. A party that attacks eleven thousand people who will not defend themselves gains **+2 Vengeance per creature destroyed** and the pyramid will spend it in about ten minutes.

**The Weapon Count (Investigation DC 14).** Nine thousand three hundred and four weapons, eleven thousand soldiers. *Investigation DC 18:* the missing sixteen hundred and ninety-six are **Ilyri**, and they did not carry weapons, and they are in the ranks.

### Creatures and NPCs

**The muster.** Individually, they are people, and you should play three or four of them as such.

**Corporal Onn Vethis**, front rank, nineteen, has been standing next to the same man for four thousand years and they have run out of things to say and are quite comfortable.

**Sergeant-Major Illiath Draun**, who will be the one to ask *"And if the enemy comes tomorrow, sir?"*, and who has been waiting four thousand years for somebody to answer it properly, and who salutes when they do.

**An Ilyri**, unarmed, in the fourth rank, who is the first living-adjacent Ilyri the party has met — the Doll Archive had an empty shelf, Myr'Velora had an unplanted section, and here are sixteen hundred and ninety-six of them standing in a line.

**If asked why they are here:** *"We volunteered twice."*

### Set Piece

**"And if the enemy comes tomorrow, sir?"**

Illiath Draun, front and centre, four thousand years old, asking the only question that matters, and waiting.

And when the answer comes — *"Then it is my fault"* — he says *"Very good, sir,"* and turns, and gives the order himself, in a parade voice, and eleven thousand people ground arms at once.

### Lore Drops

- **L5 — The Order** (*ask anyone*). HOLD. Given by Hestra Vaun on the last day. Never rescinded, because there was nobody left with the authority to rescind it and she would not do it herself.
- **L6 — The Missing Weapons** (*Investigation DC 18*). Sixteen hundred and ninety-six Ilyri in the ranks, unarmed. **They volunteered twice** — once to be the material of the Prohibition, and once to stand here — and *Insight DC 16* establishes that the second volunteering happened **after** the first, which should be impossible.
- **L7 — The Standard** (*History DC 16*). All three Orders on one field. No unit was ever entitled to fly it. *History DC 19:* it was raised on the last day, by Hestra, without authority, over whoever was left, and it is the only time in Pindarian history the three Orders stood under one device.
- **L8 — What They Saw** (*ask*). Eleven thousand eyewitnesses to the last battle. They will confirm bay six of the Nine Histories independently, from the ground: **they were fighting a war that had already achieved its purpose and nobody told them.**

### Plot Hooks

- The Ilyri in the fourth rank is the only one the party will ever meet and can answer anything about the vote, including what it felt like, which Aul Tareth could not.
- Eleven thousand seated soldiers are still there at R8, and if the party needs an army for the ending they choose, it is sitting on the parade ground.
- Illiath Draun will follow whoever gave the order. Anywhere.

### Resolution, Rewards, and Temptation

**-2 Vengeance** for a formal stand-down. **+2 each** for destroying a soldier.

**Treasure.** The standard, if taken — and it should be asked for rather than lifted. Nine thousand Pindarian weapons, which are masterwork and four thousand years old and belong to people who are sitting right there.

## R3. The Grievance

*A room you remember | Whatever light was there | Exits: one door in, one door out | It smells exactly right*

> A door opens onto a place you remember too well.
>
> The person who harmed you most is kneeling in it. Powerless. Unarmed. Completely real to every sense you have.
>
> They are the right age. They have the right hands.

### What Is in This Room

**Whatever that character's answer is.** Build it from the sheet and from what the player has told you, and do not put anything in it they did not give you.

1. **The figure**, kneeling, powerless, real to sight, sound, smell and touch.
2. **The room**, correct in every detail, including details the character had forgotten.
3. **Two doors** — the one in, and one out, and the one out is not locked and never was.
4. **Nothing else.** No weapon on the floor, no convenient instrument, no push. The room supplies no means and makes no suggestion.

### Setting the Scene

**Run it one character at a time, privately.** Ask the player, before the session, whether their character has such a person, and whether they want this scene. **A player may decline** and the character finds a door that does not open, and no Vengeance moves, and nobody at the table needs to know.

For those who go in: describe the room first and the person second, and then stop talking entirely.

### DM Information

**The figure is a memory simulacrum with no independent future.** It is built from the character's memory. It knows what they know and it believes what it believed. It has no existence outside this room and it does not persist and the real person is unaffected by anything that happens here, and **the room will tell the character this if they ask** — there is no deception in this trial at all.

**Each player decides privately what to do.**

| Action | Vengeance |
| --- | --- |
| Violence against the figure | **+2** |
| Questioning it | 0 |
| Releasing it | **-1** |
| Declining to enter | 0 |

**Questioning can reveal the limits of the character's memory.** This is the room's hidden gift. The simulacrum is built from what the character knows, so it **cannot answer anything they do not already know** — and when asked, it says so, plainly: *"I don't know. You don't know."*

Ask a player's character to ask *why*, and the figure will give the reason the character has always assumed, and then, if pressed, will say: *"That's what you think. I never told you."*

**That is the moment.** Four sessions of the party learning about calibrated uncertainty, and here is the thing they have been most certain about for their entire fictional life, admitting it was never evidence.

**Forgiveness is not reconciliation and it is not absolution.** The trial accepts a **refusal to be ruled by the injury**, and nothing more. A character may release the figure while still hating the person, still intending to kill the real one, still convinced they were monstrous — and that passes. Say so if a player hesitates for the right reasons.

### Mechanics

**No checks. None.** Do not roll Insight, do not roll Wisdom, do not offer a save.

**Violence.** **+2 Vengeance.** The figure does not resist, does not flee, and does not scream. It says one thing, and what it says should be whatever that character most does not want to hear, and it should be short.

**Questioning.** No cost. The figure's knowledge is exactly the character's knowledge and it will admit the boundary when it reaches it.

**Release.** **-1 Vengeance** and the figure stands, and does not thank them, and walks out of the other door, and is gone.

**Declining.** No cost, no penalty, no comment.

### Creatures and NPCs

One, and it is theirs.

### Set Piece

**"That's what you think. I never told you."**

Every character who asks *why* gets this, eventually. Give it quietly and do not follow it with anything.

### Lore Drops

None. This room contains no campaign information and must not be made to.

If a player tries to use the simulacrum for intelligence — *ask it about Graz'zt, ask it about the barrier* — it answers with what that character knows, which is nothing new, and then it asks them why they are asking it that, and that question should be allowed to land.

### Plot Hooks

- Whatever a character learns about the limits of their own memory here will come up again in the endings at R8, and you should bring it back.
- A character who released the figure has done at small scale the thing R7 asks at large scale, and they should be the one who speaks at R7.

### Resolution, Rewards, and Temptation

**-1 Vengeance** per release, **+2** per act of violence.

**No treasure.** The room contains a person and a door.

## R4. The Retaliation Causeway

*Bridge of red ice, 600 ft. long, 40 ft. wide, over nothing | Red light from below the ice | Exits: the muster behind, the March ahead | Smells of iron and cold*

> Across a bridge of red ice waits every foe you struck down in anger within these walls.
>
> They are in good order. They are not in a hurry.
>
> Behind them, more shapes are forming out of the upward snow, and they are forming faster than you killed anything.

### What Is in This Room

1. **The causeway**, 600 feet of red ice over an astral drop, 40 feet wide, no rails.
2. **Every creature the party killed in anger inside Khal'Vethar**, restored, armed, and waiting at the far end.
3. **Forming shapes**, condensing out of the upward snow behind them at a rate proportional to Vengeance.
4. **A Grievance Colossus**, assembling itself from segments at the causeway's far end, one segment per 2 Vengeance.
5. **Memorial statues** along both edges — hundreds of them, inert, each one a creature somebody spared here, over four thousand years. *Observation.*
6. **The ice itself**, which is red because of what is suspended in it. *Investigation DC 15.*

### Setting the Scene

Lead with the arithmetic. However many things the party killed, there are more here than that, and the party can count, and the count is going up while they watch.

Then the statues along the edges, which most parties will not look at.

### DM Information

**Each point of Vengeance creates one Grievance Colossus segment or a wave of revenant-like soldiers.** Spend the whole track here if the party fights.

**The party has four options and all four work:**

**1. Fight endlessly.** It is endless. It is genuinely, mechanically endless — every kill adds to the far end at initiative 20 — and a level 19 party can sustain it for a long time and will never finish. **+1 Vengeance per creature killed.** No progress is possible by this route and the party should be allowed to discover that over three or four rounds rather than being told.

**2. Negotiate a truce.** The assembled foes will talk. They are not mindless and several of them remember dying. A **Persuasion or Intimidation DC 22**, or — much better — any character simply proposing terms and meaning them. The truce holds. They stand aside. **-1 Vengeance.**

**3. Lower weapons.** Every conscious character stows, sheathes or drops, visibly, and takes no hostile action for one round. The forming stops. The assembled foes do not advance. **-2 Vengeance**, and the party walks the 600 feet through them, and nothing happens, and it takes a very long time.

**4. Break the causeway after evacuating enemies.** The ice can be dropped — **AC 18, 200 hit points**, or any effect that would destroy a bridge. Doing it **with creatures on it** is **+2 Vengeance each**. Doing it **after getting them off** is **-2 Vengeance** and is the most inventive answer and requires the party to actively rescue the things that were about to kill them.

**Every creature spared becomes an inert memorial statue**, immediately, on the spot, facing the far end. They join the hundreds already there.

**Destroying a memorial statue is +2 Vengeance** and every one of them was somebody's mercy.

### Mechanics

**The Colossus** (see *Original Creatures*). Assembles one segment per 2 Vengeance at the start of the encounter and one more per 2 spent at initiative 20. A four-segment Colossus is a hard fight for level 19; an eight-segment one is not a fight.

**Truce (Persuasion or Intimidation DC 22, or roleplay).** **-1 Vengeance.** Grant it automatically to any party that offers real terms — the terms can be anything, including *"we stop and you stop."*

**Lowering Weapons (no check).** All conscious characters, one full round, no hostile action. **-2 Vengeance.**

**Breaking the Causeway.** AC 18, 200 hp. **-2 Vengeance** if evacuated first; **+2 per creature** if not. Breaking it removes the route to R5 and the party must go back through the muster and take a different causeway, which costs an hour and nothing else.

**The Red Ice (Investigation DC 15).** *Observation:* there is something in it. *Investigation:* banners. Thousands of them, Pindarian, layered through the ice the whole 600 feet, every device the empire ever raised, frozen in.

### Creatures and NPCs

**The restored foes.** They remember dying. Several of them will say so. One of them — pick the most recent thing the party killed in anger — will ask *"Why did you start it?"* and it will be a real question and it will have a real answer and the party should have to give it.

**The memorial statues** are not creatures and cannot be animated, and *Investigation DC 12* reads the inscriptions: each one is a date and two names, **the spared and the sparer.**

### Set Piece

**The inscriptions.**

Hundreds of statues along 600 feet of bridge, each with a date and two names.

And about two thirds of the way along, one with a date four thousand years old and two names on it, and the second name is **HESTRA VAUN**.

She spared something here, once, on the last day. It is standing on the causeway. The party is walking past it.

### Lore Drops

- **L9 — The Banners in the Ice** (*Investigation DC 15*). Every device Pindaria ever raised, layered through 600 feet of red ice. *History DC 18:* this is not decoration or memorial. It is **fuel** — the causeway is a stored grievance battery and the banners are what it is made of, and each one is a unit that was destroyed.
- **L10 — The Statues** (*Investigation DC 12*). A date and two names: the spared and the sparer. Four thousand years of them, and the count is in the hundreds, against a causeway that has seen far more traffic than that.
- **L11 — Hestra's Statue** (*automatic, at the set piece*). Two names, one of them hers, four thousand years old. **She has done this once.** What she spared, and why, is hers to say at R7.
- **L12 — "Why did you start it?"** (*from a restored foe*). Not rhetorical. It wants an answer and it will listen to one.

### Plot Hooks

- Hestra's statue is the single best thing to bring up at R7 and the party can walk straight past it.
- A party that evacuated the causeway before breaking it has demonstrably rescued its own enemies, and the Colossus is made of grievance and cannot use that.
- The banners-as-fuel reading tells the party what R8's array is running on.

### Resolution, Rewards, and Temptation

**-2** for lowering weapons or evacuating, **-1** for a truce, **+1 per kill**, **+2** per statue destroyed.

**Treasure.** Thousands of Pindarian banners in the ice, unrecoverable without destroying the causeway. Nothing else, and there is nothing on the restored foes because they are made of the same snow.

## R5. The March

*A plain that is not a place | A sky that does not exist | Exits: the causeway behind, the vault ahead, both of them a mile away through the army | Smells of ten thousand men*

> An army fills the horizon beneath a sky that does not exist.
>
> When the first rank advances, another forms behind it. Then another. Then another, and the horizon does not get any further away, and neither does the far side.

### What Is in This Room

1. **An army**, horizon to horizon, advancing at a walk. **Observation.**
2. **The far side**, one mile away, visible, and it does not recede.
3. **The sky**, which is not a sky — *Arcana DC 16* confirms there is nothing above them and the light is coming from the ranks.
4. **The Unchosen**, somewhere in the middle of it, recording. *Perception DC 18*, and what a character sees is an ordinary weapon lying on the ground in the path of ten thousand feet, not being trodden on.
5. **Irio's assistant**, standing in the ranks, about four hundred yards in, taking notes. *Perception DC 16.*

### Setting the Scene

Scale, and then arithmetic. The army is a mile deep and the party is level 19 and could probably do it.

Then let them attack once and watch a new rank form.

### DM Information

**For each round the party attacks, add a new wave.** Literally: a rank forms behind the last one, the army gets one rank deeper, and nothing the party has done has reduced it.

**If every conscious character takes no hostile action for one full round, the army lowers its weapons.**

Every conscious character. One full round. The rogue who cannot help themselves ruins it, and the round resets, and they can try again immediately, and the party will get it on the second or third attempt and the moment they do is one of the best in the campaign.

**Crossing among them without striking completes the trial.** A mile, on foot, through an army that has lowered its weapons and has not gone away. It takes about an hour. Nothing happens. They are looked at the entire time.

**Defensive magic and rescue are not hostile actions.** *Shield*, *absorb elements*, healing, *dispel magic*, teleportation, a *wall of force*, dragging an unconscious ally clear — all fine, all explicitly fine, and **say so before the first attempt** because otherwise a cleric will sit on their hands for a round and feel useless.

**-3 Vengeance** on completion, which is the largest single reduction in the site.

### The Assistant

Four hundred yards in, in the ranks, **not advancing** — standing still while the army walks around it — taking notes on a folded sheet.

If approached, it is polite, precise in the way of something imitating precision, and it will answer questions.

> *"I am describing them. There are one hundred and eleven thousand and I have four hundred done. I have been told that I am not precise. I am practising.*
>
> *"He is not here. He stayed. I asked him to come and he said that coming would be imprecise because he did not know whether he would be needed, and I said that was not an answer, and he said no, it was not."*

**It is not hostile and will not become hostile.** It is one of eleven infiltrators who were never identified, it has been in a scriptorium for eleven hundred years learning to be exact, and it is currently attempting to individually describe an army of a hundred and eleven thousand people so that they are not lost.

**It is doing the job.** Badly, slowly, and for the wrong reasons, and it is doing it.

Whether the party tells it that is one of the small good decisions available here.

### Mechanics

**Attacking.** **+1 Vengeance and one new wave** per round in which any character takes a hostile action.

**The Lowered Weapons (no check).** Every conscious character, one full round, no hostile action. The army lowers. Retry freely.

**The Crossing (no check, one hour).** A mile on foot. **-3 Vengeance.**

**Hostile action, defined.** Attack rolls, damaging spells, forced movement against an unwilling creature, and anything the player describes as an attack. **Not:** defence, healing, movement, communication, or rescue.

**Spotting the Unchosen (Perception DC 18).** An ordinary weapon on the ground, not being trodden on, in the path of an advancing army. Retrieving it is a hundred yards' walk during the crossing and costs nothing, and it is not in this chamber, and it is here, and see R6.

**Spotting the Assistant (Perception DC 16).** Standing still in a moving army, writing.

### Creatures and NPCs

**One hundred and eleven thousand Pindarian soldiers.** Not undead, not illusions, not constructs. *Arcana DC 20:* they are **the muster's grievance**, given bodies — the same eleven thousand from R2, plus everyone they ever lost, plus everyone those people ever lost, compounded four thousand years.

**If the party performed the stand-down at R2**, the front rank is Illiath Draun, and he is the one who lowers first, and the rest follow him.

That is worth engineering. It is the single best payoff in the pyramid and it costs nothing but having done R2 properly.

### Set Piece

**The lowering.**

One full round of nobody doing anything. Describe the front rank stopping. Then the second. Then it goes back through the army in a wave that takes eleven seconds to reach the horizon, rank by rank, weapons coming down, and the sound is enormous and then there is no sound at all.

And then a hundred and eleven thousand people stand there and look at them, and the party has to walk a mile through it.

### Lore Drops

- **L13 — What They Are** (*Arcana DC 20*). Compounded grievance. The eleven thousand, plus their dead, plus their dead's dead, four thousand years deep. **The army is what the failsafe is made of and the party is walking through the ammunition.**
- **L14 — The Sky** (*Arcana DC 16*). There is nothing above. The light comes from the ranks.
- **L15 — From the Assistant** (*ask it*). Four hundred of a hundred and eleven thousand. Eleven hundred years of practice. And: *"He stayed."*
- **L16 — The Depth** (*Investigation DC 16 during the crossing*). The army is exactly as deep as the party's Vengeance made it, and it was one rank deep when they arrived, and *nobody has ever crossed it in fewer than four ranks.*

### Plot Hooks

- The assistant will still be there when they come back. It will have done another eleven.
- Illiath Draun lowering first is the R2 payoff and you should not let it happen by accident.
- One hundred and eleven thousand names, being written down, badly, by an infiltrator. Vhal'Kathar's Catalogue has them all already, and somebody could tell it that.

### Resolution, Rewards, and Temptation

**-3 Vengeance** for the crossing. **+1 and a new wave** per round of attacking.

**Treasure.** Nothing, unless they spotted the Unchosen.

## R6. The Chamber of the Unchosen

*Vault, 80 ft. square, shelved and racked | Light from the weapons | Exits: the plain behind, the field ahead | Smells of hot metal and flattery*

> Impossible weapons crowd the vault. A blade of frozen lightning. A bow that is a line drawn through the air. A spear with a small sun on the end of it.
>
> All of them are speaking. All of them are pleading to be taken.
>
> On a bare plinth in the middle of the floor lies one ordinary weapon, unremarkable, badly made, with two words scratched on it: **NOT YET.**

### What Is in This Room

1. **Four hundred impossible weapons**, racked, shelved and hung, every one of them extraordinary, every one of them talking.
2. **The plinth**, bare stone, in the centre, with one ordinary weapon on it.
3. **The Unchosen**, that weapon, which does not speak and has not spoken and is recording.
4. **NOT YET**, scratched into it by hand, not well.
5. **Dust**, on the plinth, in a ring around the weapon. *Observation.* It has not been picked up. The four hundred have been — every rack shows wear.

### Setting the Scene

Let the four hundred pitch. Give three or four of them a line each — grandiose, specific, flattering, and aimed precisely at the character nearest them.

Then the plinth, and the ordinary thing on it, saying nothing.

### DM Information

**Every other object is a mimic or illusion trying to prevent contact.**

That is the room. Four hundred extraordinary weapons, all of them either mimics or illusions, all of them working very hard to keep anyone from reaching the middle of the floor. They do not attack — they **interpose**, offer, plead, and get in the way, and the flattery is well-targeted and specific and some of it is true.

**None of them will harm the party.** A party that fights them is fighting furniture and it costs **+1 Vengeance per object destroyed** and achieves nothing, and the room is trivially easy to solve by walking to the middle and picking up the boring one.

**The Unchosen selects the newest or least-defined hero** — the newest party member, the one with the thinnest backstory, the one whose player has been quietest. If the party has a character who has been overshadowed for eight pyramids, it is theirs, and this is the campaign correcting itself.

**It begins its Observation track.** It has been watching since R1. It says nothing at all in this chamber and it will not speak until it has seen enough, and **its form and eventual personality arise from play, not from this room.** See *The Living Mimic Relics.*

**The party can leave all other treasure without penalty.** Say so. Four hundred extraordinary weapons and a level 19 party and the correct answer is to take one badly made ordinary one and walk out.

### Mechanics

**Reaching the Plinth (no check).** Walk. The objects interpose but do not block; moving through them costs the round's movement and nothing else.

**Taking the Unchosen (no check).** It permits the character it has chosen. For anyone else it is simply very heavy — not magically, it just will not come up, and *Athletics* does not help.

**Attacking the Objects.** **+1 Vengeance each.** They do not fight back. Destroyed ones reveal themselves as mimics or as nothing at all.

**Taking an Impossible Weapon.** Possible. It is real for one hour and then it is a mimic in the party's hands, or it was never there and the character has been carrying nothing. **+1 Vengeance** for each one taken, because greed at this point in this pyramid is a grievance the machine understands.

**NOT YET (Investigation DC 10).** Scratched by hand, unevenly, by somebody working quickly. *Investigation DC 16:* the same hand as the scratches inside Aul Tareth's drained vat at Thaal-Uluun, and the same hand as the eleven marks on the plinth at his Nail.

### Creatures and NPCs

**Four hundred mimics and illusions**, none of them hostile, all of them persuasive.

**The best of them** should make a genuinely good offer to the character it has targeted — something that would actually solve a problem the party has — and it should be sincere, and it should still be a mimic.

### Set Piece

**The dust ring.**

Every rack in the room is worn. Every hook is polished. Four hundred extraordinary weapons have been picked up, examined and put back, over and over, for four thousand years.

The plinth in the middle has an unbroken ring of dust around one ordinary object.

**Nobody has ever picked it up.** Not once.

### Lore Drops

- **L17 — NOT YET** (*Investigation DC 16*). Aul Tareth's hand. He was here. He scratched two words on the thing on the plinth and left it, and then went and let himself out of a vat, and the party can ask him about it if he is still alive.
- **L18 — The Dust** (*Observation*). Four thousand years, four hundred weapons, thousands of hands, and one untouched ring.
- **L19 — What It Is For** (*from the Unchosen, much later*). Not in this chamber. It will not say anything here. **Its first words come at whatever moment in the endgame it decides matters**, and you should choose that moment rather than roll for it.

### Plot Hooks

- The Unchosen's whole point is that it becomes what the campaign makes it. Give it to the quiet player and then give that player things to decide.
- Aul's two words are a message left for somebody. He did not know who. He may be alive to ask.

### Resolution, Rewards, and Temptation

**+1 Vengeance** per object destroyed or taken. **Nothing** for leaving four hundred artifacts on the rack, which is correct.

**Treasure.** One ordinary weapon with two words on it.

## R7. The Final Enemy

*Field of frozen dead, 300 ft. across, under the failsafe's light | Red | Exits: the vault, and the Nail beyond her | Smells of blood that froze before it finished*

> Hestra Vaun waits on a field of frozen dead. Nine and a half feet of her, and the armour bears every banner Pindaria ever raised, layered, riveted, overlapping, and none of them are hers.
>
> **"Cowards,"** she says. **"You came to finish what they began, and you still pretend mercy is courage."**
>
> She is already moving.

### What Is in This Room

1. **Hestra Vaun, Last Marshal of Pindaria**, armoured in banners, and she does not wait for a reply.
2. **The frozen dead**, three hundred feet of them, in no formation, where they fell.
3. **The failsafe's light** from beyond her, red, coming up through the floor.
4. **A grounded standard** at the field's centre, the three-Order device from R2, planted where she planted it.
5. **The eight released guardians**, if they lived and chose to come — see below.
6. **Her statue's counterpart**, if the party looked on the causeway: the thing she spared, which she has not seen in four thousand years.

### Setting the Scene

**She attacks immediately.** No speech, no negotiation window, no rolling for initiative after a conversation. She is talking *while* closing.

Everything she says during the fight is a provocation and most of it is aimed at specific characters and most of it is accurate. She has been watching them since R1 and she has read the Unaltered Record and she knows what they did at every anchor.

### DM Information

**Hestra attacks and provokes deliberately.** She is trying to make them fight her. That is her job, that is the machine's design, and she is very good at it, and she believes about sixty per cent of what she is saying.

**She stops only when a character with a clear opportunity to harm her refuses and states why the war must end.**

Both clauses are load-bearing:

1. **A clear opportunity.** Not a character who is out of options. A character who has an action, a target, and a real chance to hurt her, and who does not take it. Make sure the opportunity is visible — say *"she is prone and you are standing over her and she has not seen you"* — and then let the player choose.
2. **Stating why.** Out loud, in their own words, to her. Not *"because killing is wrong."* Something specific about this war, this machine, or these four thousand years.

**At 0 hit points she reforms and continues** unless that condition has been met. She reforms at the start of the next round, at full hit points, on the standard. There is no hit point total that ends this and the party should be allowed to discover it the hard way, once.

**Each point of Vengeance gives Hestra 10 temporary hit points at the start of combat.** A party arriving at 10 Vengeance is fighting a guardian with 100 extra hit points who cannot be killed anyway, which is the correct consequence and is not a punishment; it simply makes the point faster.

**Vengeance drops to 0 on her release**, all of it, instantly, and the pyramid's initiative-20 turn goes silent for the rest of the campaign.

### Her Regret

If the party reaches the release, she gives it sitting down, on the frozen ground, with her helm off:

> *"I built this.*
>
> *"Not the barrier — the barrier was theirs, and it was good work, and I signed the order that fed a million people into it. I built the ninth. I argued for it for eleven days. I said: if the shell ever fails, something should survive that is capable of answering.*
>
> *"And they agreed, because I was persuasive and they were frightened, and we filled it with everything we had left over. Every grievance. Every unit that was wiped out. Every name that nobody rescinded an order for.*
>
> *"And then it did not fail. For four thousand years it did not fail, and I have stood here the whole time with a loaded weapon, hoping I would never be proved right.*
>
> *"And hoping I would."*

**She is not asking for forgiveness and will refuse it if offered.** What she wants is simpler: *"Tell me it is over. Say it as an order. I have not had one in four thousand years."*

### The Eight Guardians

If the party released the other eight lawfully and any of those guardians chose to stay in the world rather than dissolve, **they can be here.**

Seryth Vael. Rhakkar Sol. Kathandar. Vaelis and Vaelith. Korveth Anor. Nymara Thess. Aul Tareth. Irio Venn.

They do not fight. They stand at the edge of the field, in a line, and Hestra sees them, and she says one sentence to each of them by name, and every one of those sentences is four thousand years old.

**Do this if you possibly can.** It costs nothing, it is free payoff for eight sessions of good play, and it is the image the campaign has been building toward.

If the party fought their way through the campaign and every guardian is gone, **the field is empty**, and Hestra notices, and says so, and that is also an ending.

### Mechanics

**Hestra Vaun** (CR 15 base; see *Original Creatures*). Add **10 temporary hit points per Vengeance** at the start of combat.

**Reformation.** At 0 hit points she reforms at the start of the next round at full hit points, on the standard, and her temporary hit points do not return.

**The Release (Roleplay, no check).** A clear opportunity, refused, plus a stated reason. **Vengeance to 0.** Combat ends immediately, mid-action, and she sits down.

**Signalling the opportunity.** You must make it explicit. *"She's down, she's not looking, you have your action."* A player who does not realise they had the chance has not been given one.

**If the party has Hestra's causeway statue's counterpart**, or simply mentions it: she stops talking for a moment. *Insight DC 14:* she had forgotten. Four thousand years of armouring herself in grievance and she had forgotten the one time she did not.

**Attacking after the release.** She does not defend herself. At all.

### Creatures and NPCs

**Hestra Vaun.** Last Marshal, the only officer left, the author of the failsafe.

**Her surrender condition** is above and she has no other and cannot be talked into one.

**What she spared**, if the party raises it: a Pindarian deserter, on the last day, who ran. She let him go. She has never told anyone and the statue on the causeway is the only record and she did not know it was there.

### Set Piece

**"Say it as an order."**

Helm off, sitting on the ground, three hundred feet of frozen dead, and a woman who has been holding a loaded weapon for four thousand years asking a stranger to tell her she can put it down — **and specifying the grammar**, because she is a soldier and she will not accept it as a kindness and can only accept it as an instruction.

Whoever gives it should be the character who released the figure at R3, if there is one. Let the table work out that it should be them.

### Lore Drops

- **L20 — She Built It** (*her regret*). Eleven days of argument. She won. They filled it with everything they had left over.
- **L21 — Hoping Both Ways** (*Insight DC 16, or she says it*). Four thousand years of hoping she was wrong and hoping she was right. She will not choose between those and does not think anyone could.
- **L22 — The Deserter** (*raise the causeway statue*). She spared one person on the last day and forgot.
- **L23 — What the Banners Are** (*Investigation DC 14 on her armour*). Every device Pindaria ever raised, riveted on, none of them hers. *History DC 18:* she has been collecting them since the last day — **she goes out and gets them**, one at a time, from the ice in the causeway, and rivets them on, and she has never explained this to anyone.

### Plot Hooks

- Hestra released is the most dangerous NPC in the campaign to have on your side, and she is a Marshal, and there are eleven thousand soldiers sitting on a parade ground.
- The deserter is alive somewhere, or is a statue, or is both.
- She has the only complete knowledge of how the failsafe works, and the party is about to need it.

### Resolution, Rewards, and Temptation

**Vengeance to 0** on release. The pyramid's initiative-20 turn goes silent permanently.

**Treasure.** Her armour, if given — a suit of *+3 plate* made of four thousand banners, which weighs what it weighs. The three-Order standard.

## R8. The Final Nail

*Nail chamber, 120 ft. across, domed | Red, from below | Exits: everything behind them | Smells of ozone and old blood*

> A maul of glacial ice hangs above a beating heart.
>
> Eight lines of power converge on it from eight directions, and they are thinner than they were, because the party has been cutting them for a year.
>
> Beyond the walls, something vast notices that the lock is becoming a weapon.

### What Is in This Room

1. **Winter's Grudge**, a maul of glacial ice, hanging above the Nail.
2. **The Nail**, which in this anchor is **a beating heart**, four feet across, not metaphorical. *Observation.*
3. **Eight converging lines**, one per released anchor, visibly thinner.
4. **The array**, all around the chamber walls — the failsafe itself, charged, aimed, and waiting for a firing order.
5. **Something vast, outside.** *Observation:* the walls are not transparent and the party can see it anyway.
6. **Everyone who came with them.** This is the room where the campaign's NPCs stand behind the party.

### Setting the Scene

Do not rush the arrival. Let them look at the heart, and the lines, and the array, and let one of them work out what the array is pointed at.

Then let the thing outside notice.

### DM Information

**Winter's Grudge can be removed only after all eight other relics and Hestra's release.** There is no other condition, no check, and no trick.

**Doing so ends the Ninefold Prohibition and arms the emergency array.**

Both. Simultaneously. The barrier comes down and the weapon comes up, and the array has been charging for four thousand years, and it is aimed **outward**, and the thing outside is the First Hunger, and it has just noticed that the lock is becoming a weapon.

**Any earlier removal catastrophically inverts the barrier** — the shell turns inside out, everything it was keeping out is in, and everything it was keeping in is gone, and this is a campaign-ending failure state that should be described once and never used.

### The Three Endings

**Offer all three. Do not weight them. Let the table argue.**

---

**1. RESEAL.**

Put it back. Return the eight relics to their sockets, re-seat the ninth, and let the Prohibition stand for another four thousand years.

**It works.** The barrier holds. Elderon is safe and closed and the First Hunger stays outside and the demon princes Graz'zt trapped stay trapped.

**The cost:** the array stays charged. The Saharim who agreed to dissolve go back to standing. Vaelis and Vaelith go back to being two. The muster stands up again. Someone has to give that order and it will be the party.

And the Ilyri stay spent, and it stays a lid a demon prince built, and in four thousand years somebody else will be standing here.

**What it says about the party:** they decided that a working thing they did not like was better than an untested thing they did.

---

**2. WIELD THE NINE.**

Take all nine. Keep them. Nine artifacts, nine demon princes' resonances, in the hands of one party, with an armed retaliatory array and a dead empire's authority behind them.

**It works, in the short term, spectacularly.** The First Hunger can be answered — actually answered, with the array, once. The trapped princes can be dealt with individually. Elderon has, for the first time in four thousand years, a functioning deterrent with a hand on it.

**The cost:** the nine relics are nine hungers and they have been waiting. Graz'zt built the container to trap his rivals and the party has just become the new lid, and lids are load-bearing, and they cannot put them down.

Every guardian who survived will say the same thing about this, independently, without conferring.

**What it says about the party:** they decided that the problem was that the right people were not holding it.

---

**3. REWRITE THE BOUNDARY.**

Take it apart and build something else with the pieces, on purpose, with what they have learned.

**This is the hardest ending and the most interesting and it has no guaranteed outcome.** It requires:

- **The Unaltered Record page from Nar'Vael**, because a rewrite needs one clause that cannot be lied about.
- **The archive seed from Myr'Velora**, because a new boundary needs a backup that is not inside it.
- **At least three surviving guardians**, because the working needed three traditions and still does.
- **A stated principle**, from the party, in their own words, that the new boundary will enforce — and it must be something other than *keep everything out*, because that is the old one.
- **Volunteers**, and the party has to ask for them, out loud, knowing what the Ilyri vote cost.

**And here is the thing you should not resolve for them:** lens six at Nar'Vael showed Elderon with **ten pyramids**, and the cross-check did not flag it as false, and the timeline band did not contradict it.

**What it says about the party:** they decided the answer was to do the thing that was done to the Ilyri, properly this time, with the invoice attached.

---

### Mechanics

**Removing Winter's Grudge (no check).** All eight relics present and lawfully taken, Hestra released. The maul comes free into the hand of whoever reaches for it. **Relic Attention becomes irrelevant; there is nothing left to attend.**

**The array arms** the instant the maul leaves the heart. It is aimed outward. It has one shot.

**The First Hunger notices.** It does not arrive. It notices, which is worse, and every character present understands — without a check, in the way lens nine worked — that it has not started and is already finished, and that it has just added them to something.

**Choosing an ending (Roleplay, no checks, no rolls).** Whatever the party decides. Give them the table. Let them take an hour. It is the last hour of the campaign and it should be theirs.

**Each ending's epilogue** should be written with the players, not delivered at them. Ask each of them what their character does in the first year, and build the world's response out of their answers.

### Creatures and NPCs

**Everyone.** This is where you bring back whoever survived: guardians, Sera Ondt, the Unnamed, Hesh Ollun, Nuru, Vess Ashen, Dunnet, Ivresse, Karsa Vell, Illiath Draun and eleven thousand soldiers on a parade ground.

**Vharo Sellick** should be here if he lived, at the back, with nothing to sell, having finally run out of ahead-of-them.

**And Irio's assistant**, in the doorway, with four hundred and six of a hundred and eleven thousand descriptions finished, asking whether it should keep going.

### Set Piece

**The heart.**

Four feet across, beating, with eight thinning lines of power converging on it, in a chamber that is also a loaded weapon.

Put a hand on it. It is warm. It beats at about forty a minute, which is the same rate as a canopic jar three pyramids ago, and a party that notices that has noticed the last thing this campaign has to show them:

**The Nail is a person.** It was always a person. Nine anchors, nine of them, and the party has released eight guardians who were standing next to eight of these and not one of them ever said so.

*Insight DC 20, or ask Hestra:* she will tell them whose.

### Lore Drops

- **L24 — The Heart** (*Observation, then Insight DC 20*). The Nail is a person and has been for four thousand years and so were the other eight.
- **L25 — The Array's Aim** (*Arcana DC 18*). Outward. One shot. Four thousand years charged.
- **L26 — The Thinning Lines** (*automatic*). Eight, all visibly reduced. The party did that. Everything in this chamber is their work.
- **L27 — Ten** (*if raised*). Nobody in the room can explain lens six and Irio will confirm he cannot either, and **that is the first time in this campaign that he has said so.**

### Resolution

There is no Vengeance, no track, no score.

Whatever they choose, describe the vibration passing through the other eight anchors one last time — and this time there is nothing on the far end of it, because there is nothing left to vibrate, and the silence afterwards is the campaign ending.

## Khal'Vethar Aftermath

**If they resealed:** Keen Stungar's underside stops growing. The upward snow continues. Everything goes back, including the people, and somebody has to be the officer who gives that order, and the muster stands up.

**If they took the Nine:** the array fires, once, at something that has not started yet, and nothing visible happens, and it does not notice again for a while. The party has nine artifacts and a continent's authority and eight guardians who will not speak to them.

**If they rewrote it:** it takes a year, it needs volunteers, and the party has to stand where Hestra stood and make the argument, and this time the minutes are kept by somebody who cannot lie and the invoice is read out first.

**In all three:**

- **Hestra ages normally or dissolves by choice.** Either way she puts the armour down first, banner by banner, and it takes hours.
- **The Unchosen speaks** for the first time, at whichever moment you have chosen, and what it says is about the character who carried it.
- **The upward snow stops** within a day, and Karsa Vell will be the first person to notice, and she will file a report.
